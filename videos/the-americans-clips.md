# The Americans — PDP-10, ARPANET, and ominous Pac-Man

Short excerpts from FX's *The Americans* (2013–2018), presented for **commentary, criticism,
and historical context** on the Will Wright Show For Food — specifically the PDP-10 / ARPANET
thread with [Lars Brinkhoff](../characters/lars-brinkhoff/) and the Cold War DC backdrop of
[Don's upbringing](../characters/don-hopkins/portrayal/upbringing.yml).

*The Americans* is a period spy drama created by former CIA officer Joe Weisberg: two KGB
illegals living as a suburban American couple in Reagan-era Washington, D.C. It is one of the
great series — and it takes its computing history seriously enough to build an entire episode
around ARPANET and a PDP-10.

claimed; watch the full series — it's worth it.

## S02E07 "Arpanet" (aired 2014-04-09)

### 1. "It's like God" — the professor, ARPANET, and the beast

**Watch:** *YouTube link coming (upload pending)*
· Scene: 11:30–14:53 · ~3.5 min

Philip and Elizabeth, posing as journalists, get an ARPANET explainer from computer science
professor Thane Rosenbloom (Geoffrey Cantor) — the post-office translation analogy, the
Interface Message Processor as "the interstate highway system through which all information
flows" — and the camera pans up to a **PDP-10**, "the beast." The professor rattles off its
operating systems: TENEX, TYMCOM-X, ITS, WAITS, TOPS-20. For anyone who grew up on that iron
(or is [resurrecting ITS from source](https://github.com/PDP-10/its) like Lars), this scene
is a love letter.

Full transcript (draft, being proofread word-by-word):
[`americans-arpanet-transcript.md`](../characters/lars-brinkhoff/sources/americans-arpanet-transcript.md)

Key exchange:

> **Professor:** And heaven, as we all know, is … the PDP-10! Or as we affectionately refer
> to it: "the beast." It can run multiple operating systems: TENEX, TYMCOM-X, ITS, WAITS,
> TOPS-20, etc., etc., etc.
>
> **Spy:** So the IMP is like a traffic cop?
>
> **Professor:** Traffic cop, facilitator, translator. Keeping information moving with no
> snafus, no tie-ups, on an endless ribbon of virtual highway.
>
> **Spy:** Going where?
>
> **Professor:** To the future.

### 2–3. Oleg coaches Nina for the polygraph

**Watch:** *YouTube links coming (upload pending)*
· Part 1: 14:52–16:51 (~2 min) · Part 2: 17:16–17:55 (~40 sec)

Oleg Burov (Costa Ronin) coaches Nina Krilova (Annet Mahendru) to beat an FBI polygraph —
culminating in the immortal countermeasure advice. From the clip captions:

> **Oleg:** Have you been providing information about Stan Beeman to the KGB?
>
> **Nina:** No. … I can't do this. I can't.
>
> **Oleg:** Squeeze your anus.
>
> **Nina:** What?
>
> **Oleg:** Squeeze your anus before you answer the question.
>
> **Nina:** Is this a joke?
>
> **Oleg:** No. Have you been providing information about Stan Beeman to the KGB?
>
> **Nina:** No. … It works?
>
> **Oleg:** It works.

Beating the machine by controlling the body: a lie detector is just another interface to
hack. (Polygraph countermeasures are a real, contested topic — good interview fodder.)

Casting footnote: Costa Ronin later plays Leonid "Lenya" Polivanov, the Soviet governor of
Happy Valley on Mars, in *For All Mankind* season 5. Same actor, different oath.

## S01E12 "The Oath" (aired 2013-04-24)

### 4. Ominous Pac-Man — Claudia at the arcade

**Watch:** *YouTube link coming (upload pending)*
· Scene: 10:07–12:06 · ~2 min

Elizabeth meets her KGB handler **Claudia** — Esteemed Character Actress **Margo
Martindale** — at an arcade, both in disguise. **Keri Russell**'s Elizabeth is the spy mom
who plays games too; Claudia eats without eating. Handler vs asset, maze vs trust:

> **Elizabeth:** You're good at games. I'll give you that.
>
> **Claudia:** I enjoy the maze part, the eating-without-eating part. Pac-dots are
> calorie-free. … The Center wants the meeting with the Colonel to proceed.
>
> **Claudia:** Don't get your panties in a bunch, Elizabeth. This isn't about you and me.
>
> *(next scene)* **Elizabeth:** I don't trust a word that bitch says. I mean, who knows
> what she could be up to.

Elizabeth's opening line lands with icy contempt — not a compliment, a cold acknowledgment
of a handler's manipulative skill. Between the Pac-dots and the trust rupture, Claudia
overrules Elizabeth's suspicion that the Colonel's SDI intelligence is an FBI trap
("US intelligence would never give up anything that valuable as part of a trap"), and
swats her down: *"Don't get your panties in a bunch, Elizabeth. This isn't about you and
me."* This scene is pivotal: it pushes the first domino in a long chain of tragic events
and hard-won i-told-you-so rights.

Period-detail footnotes (the show is set in May 1981):

- **Ms. Pac-Man** didn't launch until February 1982 — anachronistic.
- The cabinet behind it is **Centipede** (January 1981) — big centipede art down the
  cabinet side — borderline for May 1981.

Martindale's rap sheet — Claudia here, Mags Bennett in *Justified*, "Esteemed Character
Actress Margo Martindale" as a fictionalized fugitive version of herself in *BoJack
Horseman* — is documented in [Don's HN survey](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42905440).

run through everything from SimCity to The Sims.
