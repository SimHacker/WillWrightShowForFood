# The Wedding Playset went deep — paperwork as procedural rhetoric

*Design history from Don, August 2026. The machinery skeleton is in
[ORCHESTRATOR.yml](ORCHESTRATOR.yml); this is what the paper on the
dining table actually did, and what it was going to do next.*

## Documents as diegetic UI

The Wedding Consultant didn't open dialog boxes. She produced
**documents** — in-world objects that configured the simulation:

- **Magazines** — order services and objects from their pages, and
  configure the ceremony itself, including *who marries you*: priest,
  rabbi, clown. The officiant is a parameter, and the parameter lives
  in a magazine ad.
- **The invitation list** — worked like a phone that lets you pick any
  household or Sim in the game and *toggle* whether they're invited,
  instead of calling them one at a time. A guest list as a checklist
  over the whole neighborhood.
- **The marriage contract / license** — binds the two Sims together
  legally, and **enables the ceremony**. No license, no wedding: the
  document is a capability token, not decoration.

Interlocking dependencies throughout: configurable objects carrying
packets of parameters that all dovetail together to orchestrate the
wedding. The paperwork is the program. And note what the paperwork
*doesn't* contain: a gender check. The contract binds two Sims — which
is the whole inclusion argument, executed in bureaucratic silence, on
a form.

## The photography pipeline

You hire different **photographers** from the staff magazine, and
different photographers take different kinds of photos. The photos go
into the **Family Album** — the simulation documenting itself into the
storytelling layer as it runs.

## The generated-content pipeline (designed, unshipped)

The plan went further: import your wedding Family Album and
**generate custom personalized content automatically from the
after-effects of the simulation** —

- paintings and blown-up photos of your wedding to hang on your walls,
- a coffee-table illustrated storybook of your wedding,
- lots more — the ceremony as a content factory, your play session
  crystallized into furniture.

Personalized procedural content generated from *social simulation
after-effects*, designed circa 2002: your own emergent story, printed
and framed and placed back into the world that produced it. The
[emergence↔nuance slider](../../characters/ian-bogost/roles-not-characters.md)
running in reverse — the engine authoring keepsakes of what the player
improvised.

## The lawyer

And a **lawyer you could hire to get a divorce**. ;) The contract that
binds can be unbound — for a fee, through another professional, via
more paperwork. The institution modeled honestly, exits included:
procedural rhetoric that trusts you with the whole loop, not just the
aisle.

## Reading it now

Marriage in the playset is a *configuration*: officiant, guests,
staff, venue, license — parameter packets dovetailing into one
orchestrated event, enabled by a document and dissolvable by another.
That's the argument the playset makes about the institution itself,
never once stated in a cutscene. The generated-content pipeline is the
direct ancestor of the StoryMaker → ShowMaker lineage
([showmaker-network](../../process/showmaker-network.md)) and of what
[Soul City](../soul-city/README.md) revives: the simulation's
after-effects as publishable, personal, portable artifacts.
