## Who he was

Stanisław Lem was born in Lwów in 1921, studied medicine, and spent most of his life in Kraków. He
wrote novels, stories and essays about what minds, machines and the people who build them might
become, and he is among the most widely translated writers of science fiction.

*Solaris* (1961) is about scientists who fail to understand an alien ocean that understands them
too well. *His Master's Voice* (1968) follows scientists trying to decode a signal from space and
learning mostly about themselves. *Summa Technologiae* (1964) is a book of philosophy that
anticipated virtual reality ("phantomatics"), artificial intelligence ("intellectronics") and
search engines. *A Perfect Vacuum* (1971) is a collection of reviews of books that don't exist, and
*Imaginary Magnitude* (1973) a set of introductions to them. In *Golem XIV* (1981) a military
supercomputer lectures its makers and then stops talking to them.

[*The Cyberiad*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cyberiad) (1965), translated into English by
Michael Kandel in 1974, tells of Trurl and Klapaucius, two robot constructors who build machines for
kings and for each other. Trurl builds a machine that insists two and two make seven, and an
Electronic Bard that writes poetry on any subject, including a love poem in tensor algebra. In the
Seventh Sally, Trurl builds a deposed tyrant a miniature kingdom in a box, with citizens so
perfectly simulated that Klapaucius asks whether they suffer.

He died in Kraków in 2006.

## Why he's here

Will Wright has said that the Seventh Sally is what inspired him "through the years to build these
things" ([1996 talk](../will-wright/sources/1996-04-26-winograd-interfacing-to-microworlds/transcript.md)),
and he and Don have long called each other Klapaucius and Trurl. Lem's questions about simulated
people, machines that write, and machines that are confidently wrong are the questions this
project's AI characters raise. The [reading group](../mark-weiser/pkd-lem-ai-sf.md) reads him beside
Philip K. Dick.
