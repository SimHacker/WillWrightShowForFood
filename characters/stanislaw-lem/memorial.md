# In memoriam — Stanisław Lem (1921–2006)

*Memorial mode: we represent and discuss his work; we do not speak as him.*
[Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

Lem wrote about machines that think, write poetry, run simulated worlds and get things wrong, decades
before anyone built them. He was also funny about it. *The Cyberiad*'s constructors Trurl and
Klapaucius build and argue like engineers, and their stories hold up as arguments about what we owe
the things we make.

The Seventh Sally is the one this project returns to. Trurl gives a deposed tyrant a kingdom in a
box, and Klapaucius objects that if the little citizens suffer convincingly enough, they suffer.
Will Wright has said that story inspired the kind of thing he builds, and SimCity and The Sims are
kingdoms in boxes. The question Klapaucius asks becomes practical once the citizens are language
models.

A remembrance would read the Sallies aloud in Michael Kandel's translation, with Will and others who
care about them, and look at what they say about the simulations and AI characters people are
building now.

*This is a collaborative memorial, not a signed work. Translators, scholars and readers of Lem are
welcome to correct and add to it.*
