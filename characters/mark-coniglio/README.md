## Who is he?

**Mark Coniglio** is a composer, media artist, and programmer — co-founder of the pioneering
dance-and-technology company **Troika Ranch**, and creator of two important pieces of interactive-media
software: **Interactor** and **Isadora**.

As a composition student at **CalArts**, Coniglio was the strong programmer who hardcoded interactive
ideas for the composer **Morton Subotnick** — work that grew into **Interactor**, used in Subotnick's
1987 piece *Hungers*. Years later he built **Isadora**, the real-time interactive media/video
environment that became a staple of live performance (theatre, dance, installation).

## What he said (and why it matters here)

Coniglio is also a careful *historian* of the patch-cord lineage this repo cares about. On his
[Isadora history page](http://www.sdela.dds.nl/sfd/isadora.html), he credits **David Levitt's Hookup**
(at MIT) as the **first "patch-cord" visual language** — the metaphor that both **Max** and **Isadora**
inherit. His timeline:

- **1986** — Morton Subotnick used **Hookup** at MIT.
- **1987** — Coniglio's **Interactor** (from hardcoding Mort's ideas) debuts in *Hungers*.
- **1996** — a Troika Ranch **STEIM** residency, where he encountered **Image/ine** (Tom Demeyer +
  Steina Vasulka) → **Isadora** grew out of Interactor.

His distinction is worth keeping: Max, Pd, and Isadora all share Hookup's patch-cord metaphor, but
**Isadora surfaces parameter names/values and gives real-time graphic feedback per module** — a design
choice, not just a family resemblance.

That makes Coniglio the connective tissue between **David Levitt** (Hookup), **Miller Puckette**
(Max/Pd), the **STEIM / Image-ine** real-time-video world, and **Bounce / Body Electric**.
