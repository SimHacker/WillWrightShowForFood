## Who

**Sergio van Pul** is a Dutch game designer and educator based in Hilversum, with an **MA in Game
Design and Development from HKU** (Hogeschool voor de Kunsten Utrecht). As a freelancer he designed
interactive media and education projects for clients including the **Eduverse Foundation**, the
**University of Utrecht**, and the **Scratchweb Foundation**, where he coached workshops teaching
children programming and game design with **Scratch**. That work became **"Scratch 2.0 Game
Development Hotshot"** (Packt, 2014, with Jessica Chiang) — ten complete game projects spanning
most genres, teaching kids and beginners real game development. He went on to build games at Talpa
Digital and Gamica, and he paints and scratch-builds tabletop miniatures.

## The Micropolis years

Sergio was a core participant in the **Micropolis Eduverse Project** (2008–2010) — the
Kennisnet/IBM-backed push to bring the newly GPL'd, open source Micropolis (SimCity) into Dutch
education. He was one of the named regulars at the ABC Treehouse meetings in Amsterdam alongside
Don, Warner Dijkhuizen (IBM), Michael van Wetering (Kennisnet), and David van Gent (IBM) — there
at the very beginning of open source SimCity in education, before almost anyone else.

When that chapter wound down in 2010, Sergio's parting note to Don was candid, warm, and
characteristically forward-looking. He'd already turned the experience into something real:

> "The only lasting connection I took away from it was meeting Helen and the rest of the 9-box
> development team. We're currently testing the game on a small scale in schools, are developing a
> game design studio around the 9-box concept and educational games in general…"

And he signed off:

> "Have lots of stupid fun creating some crazy concepts with Will Wright. And perhaps we'll meet
> again sometime in the future."

Sixteen years later: it's the future, and the crazy concepts with Will Wright are a repo.

## Why we'd love to work with him again

- **He builds through adversity.** Out of a project that fizzled, he carried forward a team, school
  pilots of the 9-box game, and eventually a published book teaching game development to kids.
  That's the constructive spirit this project runs on.
- **He was right early.** Teaching children game design with Scratch, testing educational games in
  real schools, treating game-making as literacy — in 2010 that was niche; in 2026 it's the heart
  of the [educators' track](../../repo-shows/ideas/themes/educators-track.yml). Sergio has been
  doing what we're planning, for longer than we've been planning it.
- **He connects the worlds.** HKU game design, Scratch education (kin to the Snap!/Logo lineage of
  Brian Harvey and Jens Mönig), Dutch broadcast games, and hands-on craft — a rare combination,
  and exactly the mix the show celebrates.
