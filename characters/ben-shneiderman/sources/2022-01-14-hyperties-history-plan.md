# Ben Shneiderman's plan for a HyperTIES history (January 2022), and Don's reply (September 2026)

**Between:** Ben Shneiderman and Don Hopkins, with Catherine Plaisant in 2026  
**Dates:** 14 January 2022; Ben resent it on 6 February 2022; Don replied on 28 September 2026  
**Subject:** Re: Luck -vs- hard work

A public-safe digest. Addresses left out.

## What set it off (14 January 2022)

Don had bought Jakob Nielsen's *Multimedia and Hypertext: The Internet and Beyond* (1995) and
posted excerpts on HN. Nielsen's section on Hyperties covers:

- **Its origin as TIES**, "The Electronic Encyclopedia System", renamed for a trademark.
- **Cognetics' PC product** from 1987, on plain text screens.
- **Jump keys**: the arrows move straight to the next anchor. Ewing et al. (1986) measured them as
  a little faster than the mouse.
- **The definition window**: a link first shows a short definition at the bottom of the screen,
  and often that is enough.
- **Links to whole articles**, one phrase to one article, which keeps authoring simple for
  curators and historians.
- **Potter et al.'s touch-screen strategies** (1988, 1989): land-on, first-contact and take-off
  (lift-off). Take-off had fewer errors, and the best strategy depends on the task.

Don added what Nielsen left out of the Sun NeWS research version:

- pie menus, both built-in and user-defined;
- embedded PostScript applets with interactive widgets;
- scalable colour PostScript pictures and animations;
- pop-up cut-out graphical links;
- tabbed windows in the Emacs-based authoring tool.

## Ben's plan, in his words where it matters

Ben and Catherine had held three Zoom calls of an HCI history group around a shared document. He
proposed four things for him and Don to do together:

1. **One document of everything HyperTIES**, organised by topic: "early designs, testing, the blue
   link, Sun version, CACM July 1988, Museum versions, Hypertext on Hypertext book, impacts on
   other systems". Ben offered his photos of Don, Tim Berners-Lee, Ted Nelson and others, and his
   book of history and photos of 60 key people.
2. **A scholarly paper** "for something like the IEEE Annals of the History of Computing". It
   would be reviewed and take more than a year, but a draft could circulate first. "I have good
   connections to the Computer History Museum and Charles Babbage Institute", and "it would be
   great to get added to the CHM timeline".
3. **Wikipedia**: "I have done some of that, but more is possible."
4. **Medium**: the HCIL's own *Sparks* publication, run by Niklas Elmqvist.

"We really have to bolster our claims to this history."

## Where it stands (28 September 2026)

Don's reply to Ben and Catherine, copied to Brad Myers, Jakob Nielsen and Ted Selker: "I've been
documenting and reincarnating HyperTIES and PIXIE. What else should we document and reincarnate
and write and do?"

- [hyperties.org](https://hyperties.org/)
- [PIXIE live](https://hyperties.org/databases/pixie/pixie-live/)
- [Sun founders' big heads](https://hyperties.org/databases/playground/sun-founders-big-heads/)

He also introduced Roger Gregory's reincarnation of Xanadu in Rust. With Roger's permission, he
shared Roger's draft analysis of the code, asked for feedback, and hoped Roger and Ted Nelson
would publish: papers in journals, code on GitHub. See [Roger Gregory](../../roger-gregory/README.md).

reviewer attached, and they fit PIXIE and Xanadu as well as HyperTIES. Ben's CHM and Babbage
contacts are also a route for the PDP-7 questions in
