# HCIL — Human first, not Computer first

**People:** [Ben Shneiderman](../README.md) · [Brian Harvey](../../brian-harvey/README.md) ·
[Don Hopkins](../../don-hopkins/README.md)

---

## The hook

When Ben founded the **Human-Computer Interaction Laboratory** at UMD in **1983**, he insisted on
**human-computer interaction** — not ACM's **computer-human interaction**. Same field, opposite
word order: **CHI** vs **HCIL**.

## The accident both men named

| Institution | Phrase | Why |
|-------------|--------|-----|
| **ACM** | Association for Computing **Machinery** | Machines, not people (Brian Harvey, Snap!Con 2025 Karlstrom address) |
| **SIGCHI** | **Computer-human** interaction | So the conference acronym pronounces as **CHI** — computer still leads |
| **HCIL** | **Human-computer** interaction | Ben put the human first deliberately |

## Ben in his own words

From *ACM Interactions* — "30 Years at HCIL" (Sep/Oct 2013); echoed in his Software Psychology lineage:

> The term human-computer interaction (HCI) was still novel, but I insisted on putting the human
> first, as opposed to the ACM's choice of computer-human interaction to make a more pronounceable
> name, "CHI."

Thesis: much of computer science forgets humans exist — quest for abstract algorithmic purity.
Real power comes when computation **augments human thought**; you have to understand human thought
to do that.

## Don's orbit

Don worked at UMD HCIL with Shneiderman and Weiser — CHI'88 pie menus, HyperTIES. **CHI '88** is
SIGCHI's conference; the acronym preserves computer-first ordering even at the human-interface
conference.

Brian Harvey (Snap!Con 2025) notes ACM has been embarrassed since the 1960s that its name centers
machines; Shneiderman answered that embarrassment with a lab name, not a rebranding campaign.
