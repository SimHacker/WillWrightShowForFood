# Al Kossow — hooks

Possible threads for a conversation, if Al wants one. None of this is his until he says so.

1. **How bitsavers started**, and how it grew from manuals to bits. What gets scanned first, and
   what is still missing.
2. **Reading tapes.** Drives, heads, formats nobody remembers. How he tells `bar` from `tar` from
   `dump` from a raw dump, and what fails most often. The Turing Institute tape lost one 512-byte
   block in a 55 MB dump; how common is that, and where does it come from?
3. **The cabinet, from his scans.** Show him the PDP-7 and Type 340 running in a web page, built from
   the manuals he put online, and the 1969 PIXIE restored from its printed listing. Ask what he'd
   want an emulator to do for a museum.
4. **Software as museum objects.** The Software Preservation Group, what the Computer History
   Museum keeps and how, and what David Rosenthal's argument for emulation looks like from inside a
   collection.
5. **Formats as code.** A reader for every archive format a museum holds, in a language that runs in
   a browser, so a visitor can open a tape image in a page. `@wwsff/sunos-bar` is the first; which
   formats would he want next?
6. **Apple, 1986–2005.** Graphics, operating systems, CPU vendors: what he saw of the transitions.
