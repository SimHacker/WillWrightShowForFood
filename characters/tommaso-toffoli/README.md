## Who he is

Tommaso Toffoli was born in 1943 in Friuli and grew up in Rome, where he took his physics degree
in 1967. He came to the United States in 1969 to study self-reproducing programs and structures,
and his 1977 Michigan dissertation was titled "Cellular Automata Mechanics." In it he proved that
invertible cellular automata can be computation-universal: a world whose laws can run backward
can still compute anything.

From 1977 to 1995 he worked at MIT's Laboratory for Computer Science, where he led the Information
Mechanics Group. The Toffoli gate (MIT/LCS/TM-151, 1980) is a three-bit gate that is both
reversible and universal; it later became one of the basic primitives of quantum computing. With
Edward Fredkin he wrote "Conservative Logic" (1982), which describes a computer built from
idealized billiard balls bouncing off mirrors, losing no information and in principle dissipating
no energy. In 1981 he organized the Physics of Computation conference at MIT's Endicott House.

He also built machines. Starting with Squareland in 1982, he and Norman Margolus designed a series
of cellular automata machines. The CAM-6 (1985-86), a PC board produced by Systems Concepts, ran a
256 by 256 cellular automaton sixty times a second and was programmed in Forth. Their book,
[*Cellular Automata Machines: A New Environment for Modeling*](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262200608/cellular-automata-machines/)
(MIT Press, 1987), taught a generation to model physics with cellular automata: diffusion, gases,
spin systems, and the billiard-ball computer itself. The CAM-8 followed in 1992. Rudy Rucker's
[account of a CAM-6 demonstration](https://www.fourmilab.ch/cellab/manual/chap5.html) shows what
it was like to see one run.

At Boston University since 1995, he has worked on lattice gases, quantum gate speed limits with Lev
Levitin, entropy ("Entropy? Honest!", 2016), and the idea that physical action is a measure of
computation. He was named an IEEE Fellow in 2012.

## Why he's here

Don's [CAM6 simulator](https://donhopkins.com/home/CAM6/) is built from Toffoli and Margolus's book
and runs rules compatible with the Forth software they wrote for the CAM-6. Norman Margolus has
given permission to turn the book into interactive courseware, chapter by chapter.
