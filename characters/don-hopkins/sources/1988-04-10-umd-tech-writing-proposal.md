# UMD technical writing proposal — April 10, 1988 (the PIXIE-citation receipt)

**Document:** "Proposal to investigate interaction techniques and display styles appropriate
for the application of pie menus to window management." English 393 technical writing class,
University of Maryland, dated **April 10, 1988** — a month before CHI'88.
**Medium reprint (2019):** https://donhopkins.medium.com/proposal-to-investigate-interaction-techniques-and-display-styles-appropriate-for-the-application-837b013ed36f

## What it proves

The bibliography closes with:

> Wiseman, N. E., Lenke, H. V., & Hiles, J. O. (1969). PIXIE: A New Approach to Graphical
> Man-Machine Communication. In Proceedings of the 1969 CAD Conference, Southampton IEEE
> Conference Publication 51, pp. 463.

("Lenke" for Lemke — a period typo that itself vouches for hand-typed authenticity.)

**Don knew PIXIE and was citing it in writing by April 1988** — answering the charge, aired
in Buxton's later pushback on the pie menu history, that Don didn't know about or reference
PIXIE. Chain of custody: found the reference 2 Dec 1986 (self-email via Newman & Sproull,
seven months after the independent theta-menu invention) → cited in this April 1988
bibliography → piemenus.com references page (first entry, archived 2001) → every history
since. Buxton, by his own 17 May 2008 email, learned of PIXIE the same route (Newman &
Sproull 1st ed.) and read Wiseman's papers only in 2008.

## The bibliography's other receipts

The same page cites **three Buxton papers** — 1982 selection-positioning, 1983 lexical/
pragmatic input structures, and 1986 *Chunking and Phrasing* — plus Engelbart/English/Berman
1967 display-selection, Newman 1968 light-handle numerical input, and Newman & Sproull 2nd ed.
Don's scholarly conduct in 1988: cite the prior art, including Buxton's. The 1993 marking-menu
paper cited exactly one Hopkins-related item back.

## Mouse-ahead, muscle memory, chunking — April 1988

> Pie menus work very well for nested menu selection, especially for experienced users. They
> have a nice kinesthetic feel, and make good use of **muscle memory**. You remember a path
> through a tree of nested menus as a series of directions, somewhat like navigating from
> room to room. Since menu selection is defined by direction, and delimited by mouse clicks,
> you can rapidly **mouse ahead through familiar menus, without having to look at the
> screen**. The physical gestures of menu selection "chunk" together into higher level
> actions, which are easy for experienced users to perform automatically **(Buxton, 1986)**.

Another pre-1993 receipt for mouse-ahead and expert gesture chunking — with the chunking
insight *attributed to Buxton himself*. Don credited Buxton's theory while describing the
behavior; five years later the same behavior was published as "unique" to marking menus.

Also on record here: direction-selects/distance-modifies ("the distance of movement is
independent of the direction, so it may serve to modify the choice"), complementary items
opposite / orthogonal at right angles, compass and clock layouts, nested submenu paths as
remembered routes.
