# Why pie menus were never patented — Don → James Tobias (11 Nov 2007)

**From:** Don Hopkins · **To:** James Tobias, PhD (Digital Media Studies, UC Riverside)
**Context:** Tobias, researching pie menu history (saw them demoed at Apple as a design intern
in the early '90s; noticed them in Second Life), found patents *citing* pie menus but no patent
*on* them, and asked Don whether one was ever filed.

The cleanest contemporaneous statement of the no-patent decision, written mid-move to
Amsterdam/TomTom, eleven years before the Medium articles:

> The original pie menu was not patented, for various reasons. One reason is that **there was
> prior art dating back to the 60's (the PIXIE paper from 1969)**.
>
> Another reason… is that the patent system at the University of Maryland was set up to be
> **exploitive and only benefit a few lawyers** who would make it impossible for other people
> to use pie menus. **If I had patented pie menus, I would not have been able to use them in
> future jobs like SimCity, The Sims, etc.**
>
> Some people from Alias have filed some patents on **derivatives of pie menus they call
> "marking menus"**. But they were not aware that some of the assumptions that they made about
> what pie menus already did (vis a vis mouse-ahead and display pre-emption) were not accurate,
> so **their patent claims are questionable**. But I don't have the money to challenge their
> patents.
>
> Pie menus have been used in enough products that they are no longer remotely patentable (or
> you would have to lie your ass off on the patent application…), which means anyone can use
> the idea.

Same email announces **EA donating SimCity to OLPC** (Ars Technica link + Don's YouTube demo) —
the two threads of the ethic in one message: don't fence the technique, and give the game away.

## Why this receipt matters

- 2007, patent still recent memory: "derivatives of pie menus" framing predates the 2018
  articles by a decade — consistent account across 2001 (piemenus.com), 2007 (this), 2018
  (Medium), 2023 (Brad Myers letter).
- Names the two reasons in priority order: **prior art** (PIXIE) first, exploitative
  university patent pipeline second.
- The self-interested clincher — patenting would have locked Don out of using his own
  technique at Maxis/EA — is the practical argument for the commons that lands with anyone.
