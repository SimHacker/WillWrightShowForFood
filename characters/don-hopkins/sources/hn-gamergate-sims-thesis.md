## Connecting the dots (for roles-not-characters.md)

GamerGate's premise had empirical content: gamers are a coherent (young,
male) identity; games belong to them; inclusion is an imposition that
ruins games commercially. Every clause was falsified **in advance** by
the best-selling PC game franchise in history: a famously
majority-female player base, development teams that were "anything but
a boys club," creator communities led by women (Heather Castillo's
28-artist SimFreaks team; the SimFreaks/SimSlice/SimBabes federation),
and same-sex romance from day one — which didn't cost the franchise
anything; it *saved* it (the QGCon argument). The Sims wasn't a
counterexample that arrived after the harassment campaign; it had been
sitting at the top of the sales charts contradicting the premise for
fourteen years before the premise was shouted. GamerGate wasn't just
morally wrong. It was empirically refuted by the biggest game in the
industry, in advance.
