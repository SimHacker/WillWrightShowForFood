# SimCity as cellular automaton, then as visual programming language (Dec 1989 - Jan 1990)

Recovered from Don's mail archive, August 2026, via a 5 January 2007
compilation Don sent to Will Wright, John Gilmore, Charles Normann, and
Walter Bender ("Here's a message I posted to comp.theory.cell-automata
after first seeing SimCity..."), re-sent 3 November 2007 in the
[SimCity for OLPC QA sign-off thread](https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore).
These letters date Don's first contact with SimCity to the same two
weeks as his TinyMUD robot-protocol posts -- the two obsessions ran
concurrently, and the second letter says so explicitly.

## 1. The comp.theory.cell-automata post (26 December 1989)

Don, `don@brillig.umd.edu`, first impressions:

> I just got a chance to play SimCity! It's like a drawing program that
> lets you build cities... The simulation is actually running *while*
> you're constructing it! It acts like cellular automita with very high
> level rules -- it keeps track of each cell's population, land value,
> pollution, and many other factors, and the rules that govern how the
> zones develop are based on the state of neighboring zones, and other
> global factors (tax rate, etc). ...
>
> You may wonder what traffic copters have to do with cellular automita.
> You just have to play it yourself to understand.
>
> SimCity is absolutely the most amazing game I've seen on the Macintosh
> to date!

## 2. Daniel Rothman's reply (29 December 1989)

Georgia Tech, `gt0852c@prism.gatech.edu`: also fascinated, points Don at
multiplayer Empire, and asks for the thing everyone would keep asking
for over the next two decades:

> what i would also be very interested in is a copy of the rules which
> govern sim-city. Or better yet, the code for sim-city.

## 3. Don's reply: the visual programming language letter (1 January 1990)

The load-bearing artifact. Four days after his TinyMUD tagged-packet
posts, Don connects the two worlds:

> I share your longing for a taste of the Sim City sources! ... I have
> been playing around with tinymud (a multi user dungeon that the
> players can extend!). Sim City is a very interesting example of a
> visual programming language! As I pan over the cities I have created,
> I cannot help but thinking of those buildings as software modules and
> integrated circuits, mounted on a PC board. And in a sense, they
> really are! I would like to have the programming system that Sim City
> is built on top of actually embeded in the city, as buildings,
> software modules that I can hook up with systems of roads and mass
> transit! ... Think of putting all those factories to work, actually
> computing real stuff! A wire frame factory, connected to a 3-d
> transformation factory, to a ray tracing factory, then a railroad to
> take all the nice new ray traced objects to market in the city...
> A nuclear power plant to run them all (a remote computing server!).
>
> Sim City has a *wonderful* metaphore! It sure beats the desktop!
> I wonder how far it can be taken?

"I wonder how far it can be taken?" got a thirty-six year answer: the
HyperLook/NeWS port (1991), the DUX Unix port, SimCity for OLPC (2007),
Micropolis open source, and the
[Soul City plugin ladder and VPRL](../../../catalogs/soul-city/object-shops.md)
-- buildings as software modules is the literal architecture of the
object shops.

## 4. Sidelight: the bias critique (28 August 1990)

tane' tachyon on the Buckminster Fuller GEODESIC list, forwarded to Don:

> The complaint I have with SimCity is that it has a lot of, let's say,
> ideological biases built in. For instance, adding more police always
> lowers the crime rate. It would be nice if there were a way to edit
> the weights that are given to various factors yourself...

A procedural-rhetoric critique of SimCity written fifteen years before
the term existed, ending with the hope that the game "stimulates people
to think rather than getting them to blindly accept its built-in
biases." Editing the weights is exactly what Micropolis open source
eventually allowed.

## 5. Sidelight: SimEarth review to the elbows list (3 December 1990)

Don, one year later, now at Sun:

> I just picked up SimEarth, for a color Mac II. **WOW**!! It's the most
> amazing toy with the neatest user interface on the planet! Drag & drop
> amoebas, dinosaurs, jungles, ice meteors, nuclear warheads, and
> monoliths (to advance civilization)...

Simson Garfinkel replied the same day asking for the publisher's name
for a Christmas software article, offering to quote Don.
