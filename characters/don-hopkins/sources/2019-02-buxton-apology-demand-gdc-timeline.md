# The apology demand and the 66-point GDC timeline — February 2019

**Thread:** Don Hopkins → Bill Buxton and Gordon Kurtenbach, 9 Feb 2019, Cc Brad Myers,
Ben Shneiderman, John Callahan — "About that apology, and my guest lecture about the history
of pie menus for Brad Myers's 05-640: Interaction Techniques class." Written two days before
Don's guest lecture at CMU (11 Feb 2019), at Brad Myers's explicit request that Don cover the
Buxton/Kurtenbach dispute. Preceded by prep emails to Myers, Shneiderman, and Plaisant
(7–8 Feb) assembling the evidence chain.

## What the letter does

It is the formal, on-the-record closing of the loop: notice that the correspondence would be
published (cleared with **Mike Godwin** — first staff counsel of the EFF, ex-general counsel of
Wikimedia — who confirmed Don's right to publish Buxton's emails without permission), a direct
demand that Buxton apologize or produce evidence, and an explicit release for Kurtenbach:

> I am satisfied with the email that Gordon sent to me… I hold no grudges against him, since he
> has never exhibited any sociopathic behavior towards me, I accept his explanation, and I don't
> believe he owes me an apology. But I still believe you do owe me an apology and an
> explanation, Bill.

The specific challenge on the PIXIE accusation, put as questions Buxton never answered:

> If you have any evidence that I learned about Weisman from you, then please provide it. What
> was the date that I learned about Wiseman from you?… Otherwise, you owe me an apology for
> that baseless accusation.

No apology, retraction, or evidence ever came.

## The 66-point timeline (first sent to Kurtenbach, 16 May 2018)

The letter reproduces Don's numbered reconstruction of the GDC trade-show encounter and the
FUD sequence — written so that "you can understand the direction of time, cause and effect."
The load-bearing points:

- **1–6**: Don ships free ActiveX pie menus, knows *nothing* of any patent, uses 3D Studio Max
  daily at Maxis building The Sims animation pipeline, and approaches the Kinetix booth at GDC
  offering to integrate his pie menus **for free**.
- **7**: Kinetix recites the FUD, point by point: Alias patented marking menus; Buxton is behind
  it; *nobody at Kinetix had ever seen or read the patent*; customers had requested the feature;
  Kinetix refused it out of fear of suit.
- **8–19**: Minutes later at the Alias booth, a salesman blurts "OF COURSE THEY ARE!" when asked
  if marking menus are patented, weasels when pressed for specifics, refuses to connect Don to
  legal, and names Buxton as the man behind the patent.
- **20–23**: Don phones Buxton **from the trade show floor, within the hour** — establishing
  that he could not possibly have read a then-unpublished pending patent ("I DO NOT HAVE A TIME
  MACHINE").
- **24–39**: Buxton stonewalls: "there is no marking menu patent," plays the insulted victim,
  doesn't care that his competitor's users are harmed, claims to be on the users' side.
- **41–56**: The patent publishes; Don finds the two prior-art misstatements (submenu popup by
  distance; selection by location); concludes the errors served the novelty claim; identifies
  the leak — Buxton himself shot his mouth off to Alias marketing about the pending patent, and
  the FUD's success in killing pie menus for Max was "a textbook example of successful FUD."
- **57–61**: Post-acquisition, Kurtenbach's own conversation with the Max manager confirms
  Kinetix was deterred without ever reading the claims — the exact outcome Don reported and was
  called a liar for.
- **62–66**: The kicker: "Do you really expect everyone to read your patent, when you won't
  even bother reading the announcement and code that I wrote and published in 1987?" Dated the
  30-year anniversary of the CHI'88 presentation.

Kurtenbach's reply to this timeline contained the
["Max was the main rival" admission](2018-05-kurtenbach-retract-or-clarify-exchange.md) — the
concession that the clarifying sentence was withheld deliberately.

## The lecture-prep receipts (7–8 Feb 2019)

The prep emails to Brad Myers assemble the full PIXIE chain of custody in one place, with the
donhopkins.com/home/piepaper URLs: the 2 Dec 1986 self-email, the `pixie` file (4 Jun 1987, UMD
call number TA174.I47 1969 Folio), the `references` file (23 Mar 1988), the Apr 1988
[tech-writing proposal](1988-04-10-umd-tech-writing-proposal.md), and the `piepaper.comments`
CACM-draft TODO ("; pixie reference", 14 Feb 1989). Also recovered: **Mark Weiser's 2 Nov 1988
email** — CACM accepted the Pies paper "a year and half later!" wanting a minor rewrite; the
rewrite never happened (Weiser at PARC, everyone busy, too much had changed with NeWS/tabbed
windows/HyperTIES), which is why the DDJ 1991 article became the definitive publication instead.

And the reviewer-chill statement, plainly:

> My negative experiences with Bill Buxton have caused me to give up on publishing articles
> about pie menus in CACM or CHI or other academic journals, because he would probably be asked
> to review it.
