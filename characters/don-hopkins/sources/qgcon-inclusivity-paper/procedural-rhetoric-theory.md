## Bogost — argument in rules

**Procedural rhetoric**: argument encoded in rules, processes, and
interactive failure modes — not essays on a page. Craft laws and rules
within games to convey ideology.

> I developed my own design philosophy that I called procedural
> rhetoric, an unholy blend of Will Wright and Aristotle.

— Ian Bogost,
["Video Games Are Better Without Characters"](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2015/03/video-games-are-better-without-characters/387556/)
(The Atlantic, 2015). Coined and defined in
[*Persuasive Games*](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262026147/persuasive-games/)
(MIT Press, 2007) and "The Rhetoric of Video Games" (*The Ecology of
Games*, ed. Katie Salen, MIT Press, 2008).
[Full citation chain](../../../ian-bogost/sources/procedural-rhetoric-quote.md) ·

## Murray — the storytelling half

[Janet Murray](../../../janet-murray/README.md),

## Frasca — ludology's levels

Gonzalo Frasca,
["Simulation versus Narrative: Introduction to Ludology"](http://www.ludology.org/articles/simulationversus_narrative.html)
(2003) — the precursor that called it *simulation rhetoric*. His
distinction: **paidia** (SimCity, The Sims — no winner or loser, an
open-ended toy) versus **ludus** (Mario, Zelda — winners and losers
with social rules). And his four ideological levels of a game:
representations and events (skins, settings); manipulation rules (what
the player can do); goal rules (what the player must do to win); and
meta-rules (editors, modding, source) — the level where inclusivity
work in The Sims actually lived.

## Constructionism — the learning lineage

Jean Piaget, Seymour Papert, Alan Kay, Maria Montessori, Doreen
Nelson. Papert's **microworld**: a subset of reality whose structure
matches a cognitive mechanism — Logo geometry as the prototype, SimCity
as the descendant. Situated learning (Vygotsky, Dewey) and Doreen
Nelson's design-based learning close the loop back to the SimCity
lineage.
