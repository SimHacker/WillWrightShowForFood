# How Inclusivity Saved The Sims — and what saves the sims now

*The 2026 reframe of Don's QGCon paper: from franchise history to
player infrastructure. Machine-readable source data:
[soul-city-soul-saver-thesis.yml](soul-city-soul-saver-thesis.yml).*

## The thesis in two sentences

**Then:** inclusivity saved The Sims — not as a late patch or a
marketing slide, but as playability and
[procedural rhetoric](../../../ian-bogost/README.md): argument encoded
in SimAntics rules and Motive.c failure modes, masking (Scott McCloud),
constructionist microworlds (Seymour Papert), community tools (Family
Album, the Exchange, the Transmogrifier), and twenty years of
society ↔ game co-evolution across gender, race, sexuality, and
expression.

**Now:** when oligarch-owned publishers and censorship markets threaten
to repossess representation, the counter-move is **infrastructure**:
**Soul City**, a git-checked-in hub for round-trippable character
**soul-files**, and **Soul Saver** tooling to relocate player souls
from walled gardens into indie worlds. The metaphor is an
**underground railroad** — parallel rails the owners can't foreclose.

## Procedural rhetoric, extended to the platform

Bogost's insight is that mechanism argues, not decoration: the Wedding
Playset was inclusion agitprop, Dumbold was institutional farce, and
the same-sex relationship rules made their argument in processes —
[the full record of how they got in](../../../patrick-j-barrett-iii/same-sex-relationships-the-record.md).
Soul City extends the rhetoric from objects to **platform**: publish
gates, attribution, and bridge schemas are themselves arguments about
who gets recognized and where souls may travel.

## Not only LGBTQ+

The paper spans gender (binary → fluid → medical wearables in Create A
Sim), race and representation, the
[Patrick Barrett arc](../../../patrick-j-barrett-iii/README.md) on
relationships and preference, modding as player-authored inclusion
(Tanja Sihvonen's *Players Unleashed!*), safe-space storytelling via
the Family Album, and flying under hostile radar — the 2014 Russia
restriction as a badge of honor, fourteen years late.

## The namesake — carried carefully

Our Soul City is *not to be confused with, but inspired by*
[**Soul City, North Carolina**](../../../../repo-shows/will-wright-premiere/soul-city-namesake.md)
— Floyd B. McKissick's anti-racist planned town, funded by Nixon's HUD
for coalition math rather than conviction, cleared by audit, and killed
anyway by Jesse Helms's smears and the economy. The incubator built for
second chances houses a prison work complex today — the inverted coda
our project runs in reverse. Our Soul City is the liminal hub between
games where souls meet, mate, and **cannot be foreclosed**: GPL plus
git.

## Soul Saver — what it saves

**Soul Saver** is the mission; **SoulAngel** is the product (a
universal companion with per-game Soul Bridges). What it saves:
character souls locked in EA-owned saves, dead Exchange URLs, a Legacy
Collection shipped without sharing, and platforms subject to
sovereign-wealth or censorship pressure. Read and write soul-files;
publish to [Soul City](../../../../catalogs/soul-city/README.md);
bridge outward.

## The 2026 buyout, and what to do about it

EA is now private — Saudi PIF, Silver Lake, and Jared Kushner's
Affinity Partners, with the receipts and the indictment laid out in
[roles, not characters](../../../ian-bogost/roles-not-characters.md).
The Creator Network voices walked; the community praised
[Ellpeck](../../../ellpeck/)'s Tiny Life as "what we know and love."
The response, in order:

- **Soul City hub** — soul-files in git, federated catalogs (SimFreaks,
  SimSlice, SimProv lineage).
- **Soul Bridges** — Sims ⇔ Soul City ⇔ Tiny Life first; Stardew,
  Spore, and more to follow.
- **Exchange revival** via GitHub and Steam Community Guides — no EA
  dependency.
- **Archive resurrection** — Wayback the Family Album literature back
  to life.
- **Indie exodus as feature, not bug** — characters evacuate to worlds
  that still ship sharing and story.

## Paper structure

1. How inclusivity saved The Sims (1999–2020) — procedural rhetoric
   plus the timeline.
2. The Soul City NC namesake — Nixon, Helms, second chances; why we
   carry the name carefully.
3. The 2025–26 ownership change — community fears, Creator Network
   exodus, the Legacy Collection gap.
4. Soul City + Soul Saver — the mechanism for saving souls today, with
   the Tiny Life bridge as case study.

**Conclusion:** inclusivity saved the franchise once by making better
rules and tools. It saves players now by making **exit** — portable
souls, federated publishing, indie destinations — as real as the
original inclusion was in SimAntics.
