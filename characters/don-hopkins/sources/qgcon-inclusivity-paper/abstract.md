# Abstract (~350 words) — How Inclusivity Saved The Sims

*Don Hopkins, Ground Up Software. QGCon 2020 target (not submitted); reframed 2026 for Soul City / Soul Saver.*

**How Inclusivity Saved The Sims** argues that inclusivity did not arrive as a late patch or a press release — it **saved** the franchise by making the game more playable, more personal, and more durable across twenty years of cultural change. The mechanism is **procedural rhetoric** (Ian Bogost): persuasion encoded in rules and tools, not decoration on top of stereotype. SimAntics failure modes, Scott McCloud **masking**, Seymour Papert **constructionism**, and Will Wright's **Simulator Effect** let players imprint gender, race, sexuality, family, and story onto abstract dolls against realistic grounds. Don Hopkins insisted on same-sex relationships; Patrick Barrett implemented them; the **Family Album**, **Exchange**, and modding culture (Tanja Sihvonen) turned private identification into shared literature — inclusion across the full LGBTQ+ spectrum and beyond, on axes from binary gender to CAS medical wearables and safe-space storytelling.

That history matters again under new ownership. Public reporting on a ~$55B take-private deal (Saudi Public Investment Fund, Silver Lake, Affinity Partners — verify figures before citation) prompted Creator Network departures and fear that representation and user data may serve oligarch or censorship markets more than players. EA's **Legacy Collection** removed the Exchange while indie **Tiny Life** still ships share, story, and mods — the gap is procedural: who controls publishing rails.

This paper's second act names our response: **Soul City** — a git-checked-in hub for round-trippable character **soul-files**, *not to be confused with, but inspired by* Floyd McKissick's Soul City, North Carolina: a Black-led planned town HUD funded in 1972 with Nixon's controversial backing, cleared by audit, killed by smear and foreclosure while its industrial incubator became a prison. Our Soul City runs the arrow the other way: souls trapped in dead platforms get drained, carried, and given new lives. **Soul Saver** tooling (**SoulAngel**: DVR, album revival, per-game **Soul Bridges**) implements the freight layer — **Sims ⇔ Soul City ⇔ Tiny Life** as the first spoke for evacuating characters from walled gardens into indie worlds that still honor inclusivity in code and community.

Inclusivity saved The Sims once by making better rules. It saves sims now by making **exit** as real as the original wedding playset was in SimAntics — mechanism, not slogan.
