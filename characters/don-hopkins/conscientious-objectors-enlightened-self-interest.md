# Conscientious Objectors & Enlightened Self-Interest

*A two-term lexicon, 1967–2026 — how a draft-board phrase and an economics phrase became
load-bearing engineering ethics, traced through the people of this repo.*

Two phrases keep resurfacing across sixty years of this cast, each time meaning something
slightly bigger. This page collects the receipts in one place — for **David Ungar**, who was
in the room where one of them got its name, and for **Heinz Lemke**, who lived the literal
version before either of us borrowed it.

---

## 1967 — the literal term: Heinz refuses the army

Heinz Lemke was a **conscientious objector to German army service**, living in England by
personal choice only 22 years after the war. That status — plus his "unusual (and suspicious!)
hours" reading about computer modelling in the company library until 10pm — got him reported
as a suspected **German spy**. His manager Cook believed him, asked him to write up his
thinking (7 handwritten pages, dated 10 March 1967), and drove him to Cambridge to meet
**Maurice Wilkes**. The objection didn't end his career; it started PIXIE.

→ [`../heinz-lemke/sources/1967-back-to-the-roots-part1.md`](../heinz-lemke/sources/1967-back-to-the-roots-part1.md)

## 1969–1976 — Weizenbaum: the moral spine

Heinz met **Joe Weizenbaum** at MIT in 1969 while presenting PIXIE. Weizenbaum became a
visiting professor at Heinz's TU Berlin department in 1979 and spent his last three decades
largely in Germany — years of conversations with Heinz about modelling, HCI, and AI.
*Computer Power and Human Reason* (1976) is the book-length form of the objection: there are
things computers *can* do that they *should not* be asked to do. Heinz, 2026: "many of his
predictions have become true now."

→ [`../joe-weizenbaum/`](../joe-weizenbaum/) · [`../heinz-lemke/ideas.md`](../heinz-lemke/ideas.md) (hook 4b)

## Late 1980s — self-interest

The Self language mailing list was called **self-interest**. Don was on it from the early
days. The name was already a joke with a straight face: subscribing to Self *is* self-interest.

## Early 1990s, Kaleida — the meetup and the companion term

At **Kaleida Labs**, the **ScriptX object-system design team** and the **Self team**
(David Ungar and colleagues) held a recurring meetup that Don named the
**Conscientious Objectors** — conscientious about how we design our *objects*. Prototype-based
object systems from two directions, bouncing ideas off each other in good faith; not a
one-way lecture.

The companion phrase in the air back then was **enlightened self-interest** — the
self-interest pun completed: sharing your object-system ideas openly with the "competing"
team down the hall is not charity, it's the smartest thing you can do for your own system.
Ungar's whole career modeled it — papers, talks, *Self: The Movie*, the self-interest list,
and showing up at other people's labs to argue in good faith. What's good for the ecosystem
of ideas is good for your ideas.

## 1987 → 1995 — the objection you can read in the patent record

Don invented pie menus in 1986, found the PIXIE prior art himself in December 1986, credited
it forever after — and **chose not to patent**. Read against the Alias 1995 marking-menu
patent filings, the non-patent is the conscientious-objector move in engineering-artifact
form: consciously objecting to enclosing the commons you grew up in.

## July 2026 — the repossession, and Heinz closes the loop

In the PIXIE storyline thread, Heinz told the 1967 army story. Don replied (9 Jul):

> "I love and respect that position, and I like the term too, so I 'repossessed' it (like
> gays have reclaimed 'queer', but reframing … conscientiously objecting as a software
> development methodology) to mean software designers who are conscientious in how they
> design their objects, prototypes, and classes, and code. And of course consciously object
> to it being used for evil, too!"

Heinz answered the next day, extending the term to his present main activity — **AI model
trustworthiness** (CARS Nagoya sessions on Model Guided Medicine and AI in Surgery) — and
invoked Weizenbaum directly: it may be advisable to become a conscientious objector *"with
respect to blindly following an AI dominant world."* Then he asked Don for his thoughts on
a **model science**. Sixty years after the draft board, the term came home with interest.

## 30 July 2026 — the Latent Office

The 1987 non-patent got its lexicon entry too. Don, in an email to **Richard Stallman**
(who received one of Don's snail-mailed **Copyleft (L) stickers** back in 1984), noted that
because pie menus were never patented he stayed free to use them everywhere — NeWS Toolkit,
SimCity, The Sims, Micropolis — then coined the patent-side counterpart of Copyleft:

> **LATENT (L)** — All rights unreserved. This invention may already exist, but hasn't
> become obvious yet.
>
> **Latentleft** — publish the invention, conceal the monopoly.
> **Latent Pending** — the idea is out there somewhere.
> **Latent Troll** — remains dormant until an industry becomes profitable.

A Latent Office protects inventions *from* the people who claim to have invented them.
The conscientious-objector move, franchised.

## Now — what the two terms mean in this repo

- **Conscientious Objectors** (Kaleida sense): design your objects, prototypes, classes, and
  skills with intention — artisanal craft, not vibe slop — and consciously object when the
  work is pointed at evil. MOOLLM's constitution teaches the same ethic to LLM authors.
- **Conscientious objector** (Weizenbaum–Lemke sense): refuse blind obedience to an
  AI-dominant world; keep the human, not the machine, as the measure.
- **Enlightened self-interest**: share the ideas. Credit the lineage. The self-interest list,
  the Kaleida meetup, this repo full of invitations — same move, thirty-five years apart.

**Show hook (David Ungar episode):** what would the Conscientious Objectors teach LLM
authors today — and is prompting an LLM with credited, named lineage (k-lines, latent-space
inheritance) the current form of enlightened self-interest?
