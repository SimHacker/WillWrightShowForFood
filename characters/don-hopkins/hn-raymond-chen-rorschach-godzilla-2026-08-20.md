## HN comment (copy below the fence)

```
Same Rorschach energy in games, and it cost real money.

Raymond Chen's Windows 95 hologram baby had no pants because the photographer only framed him from the waist up. Some government imagined the rest. Maxis had the opposite problem: they put a generic stompy orange monster on the original SimCity Macintosh box -- red MONSTER disaster button on the Bakelite-appliance cover, a creature on the city screen that Maxis never called Godzilla. Not in the game. Not on the box. Jeff Braun (Maxis co-founder and CEO) told me what happened when I emailed him in 2024 while open-sourcing Yoot Tower, because The Tower II shipped Gamera movie tie-in maps and I did not want to step on anybody's trademark the same way twice:

Original SimCity Macintosh box art (1989) -- monster button, no Godzilla name anywhere:

https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/will-wright/media/simcity-macintosh-front-cover.jpg

Jeff Braun email, archived in the Will Wright Show for Food repo (17 Feb 2024):

https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/jeff-braun/sources/email-godzilla-toho-2024-02-17.md

jeffbraun> Maxis was sued by Toho. We never referred to the name Godzilla, our monster on the box cover was a T-Rex looking character, but... a few magazine reviews called the monster, Godzilla. That was all it took. Toho called it "confusion in the marketplace". We paid $50k for Godzilla to go away. In all honesty, Toho liked Maxis, they said $50k was the minimum they take for Godzilla infringement. I doubt you will need to worry about Toho, as long as there are no magazine reviews that call the monster Godzilla.

What I had asked him, earlier that morning:

https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/jeff-braun/sources/email-godzilla-toho-2024-02-17.md#don-hopkins-to-jeffrey-braun-prior-message-same-thread

"We will have to fictionalize all the trademarked plugins, to avoid the Godzilla problem that Maxis experienced. What happened with that anyway? How much did Maxis have to pay in the end? I noticed there are still some "God" prefixed function names and variables in the SimCity code, but Godzilla is never spelled out in full. ;)"

Micropolis (open source SimCity Classic) README quotes Jeff on the trademark -- call the game Micropolis, not SimCity, or EA's lawyers may come knocking:

https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore#the-story

Yoot Tower open source README -- same Jeff quote, plus the Gamera 3 Tower Kit maps we have to fictionalize for the browser port:

https://github.com/YootTowerManagement/YootTower

Raymond Chen, "Windows brings out the Rorschach test in everyone" (2003, still good):

https://devblogs.microsoft.com/oldnewthing/20030825-00/?p=42723

So Microsoft put a shirt and overalls on the hologram baby and dropped the arm animation because somebody imagined naked children. Maxis paid fifty thousand dollars because a few magazine reviewers saw Godzilla in a T-Rex-looking disaster button Maxis never named. Nature's Rorschach test, but with invoices.

I am still working on the open source Micropolis monster sound, which Will and I have taken to calling the "definitely not Godzilla" disaster. Last month I texted Will that it needs a better roar and suggested a player contest for mood-tagged monster roars. Will replied:

willwright> lol, try recording your cats meowing in slow motion

(I may take him up on that. Nelson is EXTREMELY LOUD though.)

Earlier I had told Will "Well on my todo list is letting you play as Godzilla!" and he answered:

willwright> Don't get him near a Monopoly set

Then I couldn't resist adding that maybe Toho only sued for $50,000 because if it were really Godzilla the monster would have been much angrier -- but fighting that in court would have cost a million in legal fees. That part is my joke, not Will's. Jeff's email is the receipt.

Lesson Raymond Chen didn't quite spell out: name your monsters yourself. Don't leave the Rorschach test to magazine reviewers.
```
