# ConnectedTV — Touch Tuning, Finger Pies, calm technology in the dark

**ConnectedTV** (ConnectedMedia, ~2002) — Palm PDA personalized TV guide + universal IR remote.
Built by **Don Hopkins + David Levitt**. Bundled on Sony CLIÉ. **HyperLook/HyperCard energy on the Palm,
for controlling devices remotely** in the living room: **use it in the dark, finger-only, no stylus to
lose in the couch.**

## Slogans worth reviving

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Touch Tuning** | Touch a show's *name* to tune — speed-dial for the remote; forget channel numbers. Up/down strokes browse time/channel; up sends channel number to TV; down dives into show info |
| **Finger Pies** | Directional strokes on finger-sized buttons — up/down/left/right/tap stack up to **five functions per button**. Pies **lead, follow, and get out of the way** — visible feedback Apple's/Android stroke gestures lack. (Beatles *Abbey Road* "finger pie" slang — intentional pun) |
| **Stroke vs poke** | One-handed **stroking** without stylus — before iPhone made touch mainstream |

## Calm technology (Mark Weiser's sense)

ConnectedTV is quintessentially **calm technology** in Weiser's original sense. It **coalesces, composes,
and optimizes** a whole pile of one-per-device IR remotes — each cluttered with buttons you never touch —
into a single **task-oriented, user-editable** interface. Lineage: **HyperCard + HyperLook**, now on the
Palm, controlling devices remotely.

- **Custom pages per activity** (watch TV, DVD, stereo) — not per device. A few pages of big buttons
  replace a coffee-table pile of remotes; each button stacks **up to five functions** (up/down/left/right/tap).
- **IR capture + community sharing** of device codes — build the remote for the gear *you* actually own.
- **XML custom-UI editor** in the HyperCard/HyperLook tradition — end-users author their own skins.
- **Never care about numbers again.** No more reading three digits off a guide and poking them one at a
  time (three-plus presses per channel). **Touch Tuning** abstracts the channel number away into named
  favorites, filtered custom channels, and time/channel browsing.
- **Designed for the dark living room.** You will lose the stylus in the couch, so it must work
  **finger-only** — which is exactly what the self-revealing pie menus and reversible strokes deliver.

### The up/down gesture pair — "throw out up / dive in down"

On a show, vertical strokes carry two opposite verbs with built-in mnemonic direction:

- **Up = throw it out / send it** — fling the channel number *up and out* to the TV to tune there.
- **Down = dive in** — pull the show *down toward you* to see more about it.

Stroke-back-and-forth **reverses** the action (undo/redo by direction), so exploration is always safe —
the pies **lead, follow, and get out of the way**, never hidden or undiscoverable.

→ Mark Weiser: [`../mark-weiser/`](../mark-weiser/) · calm tech thread

## Why it still matters

Honda-ditching-touchscreens HN ([22738415](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22738415)) and Norman's
elderly-UI threads echo the same lesson: **physical + self-revealing beats mystery meat.** ConnectedTV
was an existence proof on a 160×160 Palm screen.
