## Who he is

Richard Potter worked at the University of Maryland's Human-Computer Interaction Lab with Ben
Shneiderman. His first CHI paper, with Linda Weldon and Shneiderman (CHI '88), introduced the
take-off strategy for touch screens: select when the finger lifts, not when it lands, so you can
see and correct what you're about to choose.

He then built Triggers. Many Macintosh applications offered no way to script them, but they all
drew on the screen, so Triggers read the screen's pixels: rules matched visual patterns, such as a
selection handle or a particular icon, and played back recorded actions in response. With it he
added features to programs that lacked them, including a tool palette for MacDraw II. He described
it in two chapters of Allen Cypher's [*Watch What I Do*](http://acypher.com/wwid/) (1993):
["Triggers: Guiding Automation with Pixels to Achieve Data Access"](http://acypher.com/wwid/Chapters/17Triggers.html)
and "Just-in-Time Programming," about writing a program for a task you're already in the middle of.
His 1999 Maryland dissertation and the paper
["Pixel Data Access for End-User Programming and Graphical Macros"](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~ben/papers/Potter1999Pixel.pdf),
with Shneiderman and Ben Bederson, gathered the work.

## Why he's here

Don worked alongside him at HCIL while he was developing an early version of Triggers. Triggers is
the earliest ancestor of [Screen Angel](https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore/blob/main/apps/screen-angel/SCREEN-ANGEL.yml)'s
pixel path, and the PDP-7 cabinet's [manifesto](../../packages/cabinet/MANIFESTO.md) borrows its
idea: an emulator that helps a program and its user meet halfway.
