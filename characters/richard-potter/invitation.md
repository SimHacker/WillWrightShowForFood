# Repo Show invitation — Richard Potter

*Rich may edit, decline, delay, or request removal at any time — or take direct edit access. [Portrayal standards](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/schemas/portrayal-standards.md) — also covers quiet mode and onboarding.*
**Subject:** Triggers, thirty-five years on

---

Rich —

It's Don Hopkins, from Ben Shneiderman's lab at Maryland. I was lucky to be around while you were
developing an early version of Triggers, and it has stayed with me ever since. Reading the screen's
pixels to script applications that offered no other way in seemed like a trick then. It turned out
to be a principle, and I've been citing your "Triggers" and "Just-in-Time Programming" chapters in
*Watch What I Do* for years.

Two things I'm building descend from it:

- **Screen Angel**, an app that reads, scripts and extends other applications' interfaces from
  outside. It started as aQuery, a jQuery for accessibility APIs, which I proposed around 2013
  citing your Triggers paper. It reads accessibility trees and has a selector language and a
  click-through overlay; the pixel path, your half of the idea, is next
  ([SCREEN-ANGEL.yml](https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore/blob/main/apps/screen-angel/SCREEN-ANGEL.yml)).
- **The cabinet**, a PDP-7 emulator in the browser. Its
  [manifesto](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/packages/cabinet/MANIFESTO.md)
  credits Triggers for the idea that an emulator should help a program and its user meet halfway,
  the way Triggers gave MacDraw II a tool palette it lacked.

What's changed is that LLMs can now look at a screen and say what's on it. They can't yet say
exactly where, every frame, or be trusted to act without showing what they'll do. Triggers had
both: exact pixel patterns, and rules a person could read. I'd love to hear what you'd build now.

**Will Wright is in — he's signed on to do the [premiere](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/repo-shows/will-wright-premiere/README.md) and more.**

I'd like to invite you to a **Repo Show**: a recorded conversation whose stage is a public GitHub
repository, where what we discuss can be built afterward. Possible threads, your pick:

1. Triggers at HCIL: how it worked, what it could do, and what stopped it from spreading.
2. Just-in-Time Programming and LLMs: programming the task you're already doing, by asking.
3. Pixels and accessibility trees together, with Morgan Dixon and James Fogarty's Prefab.
4. *Watch What I Do* thirty years later, perhaps with Allen Cypher.

Remote is fine. No preparation needed.

— Don
