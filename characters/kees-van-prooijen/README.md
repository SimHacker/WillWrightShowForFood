# Kees van Prooijen 🎨🎼

**Computer artist, language designer, Spore prototype engineer.**
Pronounce "Kees" like "Case" — yes, like the Neuromancer protagonist.
Born The Hague, 1952; long since of California; his own Facebook bio
says it best: *"Retired (graphics) software developer. Now young
emerging artist."*

His artist statement is one sentence that belongs in this repo's
constitution: **"I make my art by programming in my own computer
language."** The generated images are printed strictly in editions
of 1/1.

- Wikipedia: [Kees van Prooijen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kees_van_Prooijen)
- Home: [kees.cc](https://www.kees.cc/)
- His language: [IL](https://www.kees.cc/il/)

## The scale that doesn't bear his name

In 1978, investigating continued fractions and generic equal
temperaments, Kees independently discovered what the world now calls
the **Bohlen–Pierce scale** — 13 tones in the *tritave* (3:1) instead
of 12 in the octave, built on odd harmonics ("A Theory of
Equal-Tempered Scales," *Interface* vol. 7, 1978). Bohlen found it,
Pierce found it, Kees found it — and Kees says the others "have done
such a wonderful job in studying and documenting this scale that it
is only just that it is known by their names." He did get a unit
named after him: the **Kees height**, an "expressibility" measure for
the complexity of just-intonation pitch classes, lives on in the
[Xenharmonic Wiki](https://en.xen.wiki/w/Kees_height).

The pedigree runs deep: the **Institute of Sonology** (then Utrecht),
studying under **Gottfried Michael Koenig** — the composer he credits
as a major influence on the principles underlying his art. In 1977 he
wrote a real-time synthesis engine in **PDP-15 assembly language**
and composed "De Onbesuysde Steenhoop" on it, named after the biggest
prehistoric megalithic stone grave in the Netherlands. Real-time
computer music, hand-assembled, on a machine this workspace only
knows through emulators.

## Two languages of his own

At **Electrogig** (Amsterdam, then San Francisco, 1991–96) — where he
was technology founder and principal architect — he designed a full
3D modeling/animation/rendering system, 3DGO, and then designed
**Intercol**, the language it was built *in*. The shading and
texturing language taught him lessons he folded into a second, freer
language: **IL** — "unification of image processing, (2-D and 3-D)
modeling, texturing, animation and sound, **based on intensional
logic**."

Sit with that phrase. Intensional logic is the logic where an
expression's value depends on the *context of evaluation* — the same
name denotes different things at different indices. That is the
[korz-prime](../david-ungar/korz-prime.md) move — slots whose meaning
is subject to the dimensions you evaluate them under — shipped as a
working art tool decades early. Kees didn't write a paper about
subjective programming; he grew three decades of paintings out of it.
He's still extending IL today: sound synthesis, mesh growth through
strictly local vertex operations, geometric-algebra multivectors,
dual quaternions, automatic differentiation, Oculus.

## Spore

At Maxis (2001–2007) Kees was one of the **three engineers on the
original Spore prototyping team**, working directly with Will Wright:

- The **creature editor** for articulated bodies.
- A **genetic algorithm evolving nervous systems** for those bodies,
  optimizing locomotion efficiency — prototyped in IL, his own
  language.
- Artificial **evolution of body plans** — L-systems with a linear
  genetic code and network-based developmental stages.
- In production: the **walking algorithm for creatures with any
  number of legs in random configuration** — the procedural animation
  Will demoed at GDC 2005, later reflected in the SIGGRAPH 2008 paper
  "Real-time motion retargeting to highly varied user-created
  morphologies."

So when a player's five-legged nightmare walks convincingly off the
creature editor's table: that gait came through Kees.

## The art

His technique is "biologically inspired algorithms, rather 'growing'
my models then 'sculpting'" — and the goal is explicitly *not*
algorithm visualization but "the expression of very personal
visions." Influences: Bacon, Kiefer, Duchamp, Kandinsky, Miró,
Tanguy, de Kooning. SIGGRAPH Art Show (Chicago '92), Sausalito Art
Festival by invitation ('97, '98).

Three delights for this repo's readers:

- The **Function series** is named after the movements of Alban
  Berg's *Lyric Suite* — "function" as a dedication to Koenig.
- The earlier **Enigma Variations** titles are Philip K. Dick titles
  run through a scrambler: "Our Friends Dream of the Untelectric
  Puppets," "A Scan's Hammer Clock World," "The Stigmate Truth."
  Procedural remixing of PKD — in 2000.
- The canvases' aspect ratio is the **Odd Golden Section**: the
  Tribonacci constant (~1.839), his generalization of the golden
  ratio to sums of three — chosen because he wanted a Fibonacci-like
  series containing only odd numbers.

And **Filter Farm**, his Photoshop plug-in, is interactive evolution
in miniature: "It is not a filter. It is an environment where filters
live their own life. They grow from genetic information." The user's
only control is judging each generation — the human as fitness
function.

## Why he's here

- **Spore reunion** — with [Will](../will-wright/) and
  [Chaim](../chaim-gingold/) (and [Chris
  Trottier](../chris-trottier/)): the prototyping era, when the
  creature editor was three engineers, a designer, and a genetic
  algorithm learning to walk.
- **Language design** — the [korz-prime](../david-ungar/korz-prime.md)
  conversation needs him: Self and Korz from David Ungar's side,
  Intercol and IL from the working artist's side. Two languages of
  his own, one based on intensional logic — context-dependent
  evaluation as a *practice*, not a proposal.
- **Music and theory** — Bohlen–Pierce, tuning as continued
  fractions, Sonology and Koenig, PDP-15 real-time synthesis; a
  natural voice alongside [Jerry Martin](../jerry-martin/) and the
  music shows, and the tuning-theory end of the robopoetry /
  instrument-building thread.
- **Evolving things** — GA-evolved nervous systems and Filter Farm
  sit right next to [Jim Crutchfield](../jim-crutchfield/)'s evolving
  cellular automata and the artificial-evolution lineage.
- **The Dutch thread** — The Hague, Sonology, Electrogig Amsterdam, a
  composition named after a hunebed. This repo's Amsterdam canal runs
  straight through his biography.

Don and Kees go back to the turn of the century, introduced by Howard
Penner in Berkeley — and it was Don who forwarded Kees's résumé to
Will Wright. It worked.
