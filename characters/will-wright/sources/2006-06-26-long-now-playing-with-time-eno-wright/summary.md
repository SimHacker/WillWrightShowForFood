## Elevator pitch

Two generative artists compare notes: **compress rules, decompress worlds**. Eno plants musical seeds;
Will builds simulation seeds. Live **Conway's Life** with generative score; full **Spore** demo; Q&A on
authorship, politics in SimCity, time perception, and what "play" means. The evening that later connected
Eno to Scott Draves's Electric Sheep.

## Quotes worth clipping (subject to transcript cleanup)

**Will on compression:**
> Games are just kind of the opposite process — we're trying to come up with very simple rules that we
> can decompress into these elaborate worlds.

**Brian on seeds:**
> What you're doing is making seeds rather than forests — letting the forest grow itself.

**Brian + Will on amphetamine ambient** ([Long Now summary](https://longnow.org/talks/02006-eno-wright/)):
> Eno: I work on them much faster than I end up releasing them… the simplest way is just by slowing
> it all down.
>
> Will: You should release an album with all the speeded up versions.
>
> Eno: *Amphetamine Ambient.*

**Will on mental models:**
> Those aren't the real models we're building — the real models we're building are in the player's head.
> The computer game is just a compiler for that mental model.

**Brian on culture:**
> Culture is everything you don't have to do.

**Audience on open-sourcing SimCity** (Dan Ancona + Mark Live):
> There are a lot of really interesting political assumptions embedded in the simple rules that
> drive SimCity — would you ever consider open sourcing a version of it so we could play with
> those assumptions?

**Will on arguing with the simulation:**
> When people play SimCity and they get to the point of starting to argue with the assumptions
> of the simulation — that means the game has been successful… I'm very open to the idea — it's
> probably harder than it sounds.

**Stewart on Space War:**
> A flawless crystal ball of things to come in computer science and computer use.
