## Elevator pitch

Will **lied about the title** so EA wouldn't know he was about to demo Spore. His real argument:
AAA content costs are winning a losing battle — **give players ownership and procedural leverage**
instead. Live demo climbs from tidepool to planet-buster, with creature editor, procedural mating,
and a **T-shaped** design (evolution ladder + sci-fi meta-games). The talk that launched a thousand
"what could have been" comments.

## Quotes worth clipping

**On the title lie:**
> This year when they asked me what I was going to talk about I just flat out lied.

**On the Care Bears rant:**
> Going back to Care Bears for a second… Which Care Bear are you? Turns out I'm Funshine Bear. Care-a-Lot, Forest of Feelings, Kingdom of Caring — Earth is the kingdom of people who don't give a shit. But I digress.

**On the microscope tangent:**
> This is just kind of Will's personal information you might find valuable… put your hand under this thing and you'll spend an hour just obsessing over your hand — and it's like a religious experience. Anyway, back to our story.

**On the demo:**
> It's very unfinished — kind of held together with baling wire and duct tape.

**On compression:**
> Something like a five thousand to one compression.

**On Care Bears geography:**
> They collectively refer to this as the kingdom of caring… Earth is the kingdom of people who don't give a shit.

**On planet-busting:**
> There go our friends, and there goes my reputation as a non-violent game developer.

**On imagination (founding advice — Don applied decades later):**
> As we were working on this game, the biggest obstacle I truly had was making myself believe that we could build the game. My own imagination was the biggest bottleneck. Once I truly believed, it was quite easy to convince my staff and the executives. If you've got some totally weird idea that's so far outside the box you think there's no way that would work — go back occasionally and revisit those ideas, because you just never know where they might lead to.

**On prototyping:**
> Prototyping has become a religion for us around this.
