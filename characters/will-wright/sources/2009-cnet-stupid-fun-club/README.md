# Source: CNET on Stupid Fun Club, April + October 2009

Two CNET pieces bracketing the founding year of the Stupid Fun Club —
the press record of what Will said the company was for, *before* the
output shipped. Key passages quoted; full text at the links.

## April 8, 2009 — "Will Wright to leave Electronic Arts" (Ina Fried)

<https://www.cnet.com/culture/will-wright-to-leave-electronic-arts/>

- Will leaves EA to head SFC, "a venture aimed at developing concepts
  that can serve as the nexus for video games, movies, TV shows, toys,
  and other entertainment products." EA invests; EA and Will each own
  equal percentages.
- Will's statement: "The entertainment industry is moving rapidly into
  an era of revolutionary change. Stupid Fun Club will explore new
  possibilities that are emerging from this **sublime chaos** and
  create new forms of entertainment on a variety of platforms."
- From his Web 2.0 Expo talk the week before: "Most people are very
  narcissistic. The more you can make the game about that person, the
  more interested, the more emotionally involved they will get."
- Housekeeping: Maxis co-founded with Jeff Braun 1989, EA bought it
  1997, Lucy Bradshaw takes over Maxis on Will's departure.

## October 7, 2009 — "Will Wright speaks about his Stupid Fun Club start-up" (Daniel Terdiman)

<https://www.cnet.com/culture/will-wright-speaks-about-his-stupid-fun-club-start-up/>

- The name's origin, Will to GameSpot: "This started many years ago
  actually, with friends I met doing **Robot Wars** together. That's
  when we originally coined the name, because it's kind of ridiculous
  to invest hundreds of hours building these things and then destroy
  them. But it's great fun, and it's really stupid."
- The company's entire public self-description, 12 people strong: "The
  Stupid Fun Club is an entertainment development studio. The ideas
  developed here can be manifested in video games, online
  environments, storytelling media, and **fine home care products**."
- To VentureBeat (Dean Takahashi): "Every product that we are working
  on has a web component. The web is like the connective tissue in
  entertainment today."
- And the thesis statement, October 2009: "There are all of these huge
  interesting web communities forming around traditional properties. I
  am interested in the online communities around popular TV shows. The
  stuff the participants are doing are very extraordinary. **The
  community around The Lost show on TV is one of my favorites. It's
  awe inspiring.**"

## Why this matters here

- **Bar Karma was announced in the press before it existed.** Will
  names community-written TV as the thing he's chasing in October
  2009; Bar Karma — a TV show written by its community through
  StoryMaker — aired on Current TV in February 2011, fifteen months
  later. Bogost's 2015 line that SFC's "output remains mysterious and
  largely hypothetical" reads differently against a stated thesis and
  a shipped show: see
  [roles-not-characters.md](../../../ian-bogost/roles-not-characters.md).
- **The narcissism quote is the tension Bogost's essay lives in.**
  Will diagnosed the identification hook in April 2009 — make the game
  about the person and they're emotionally involved — while spending
  his career building system protagonists. Both true at once; good
  show question.
- **Robot Wars origin** connects the name to the Slats/Dents robot
  theater lineage
  ([theme](../../../../bits/theme-stupid-fun-club-robots/theme-stupid-fun-club-robots.md)):
  build something for hundreds of hours, set it loose, delight in what
  happens. The One Minute Movies are Robot Wars with empathy instead
  of flippers.
- "Fine home care products" is the best corporate mission sentence
  ever filed, and should be read aloud on the show.
