## Pitch parallel (Sims ↔ Proxi)

> "This is a boat I've been in before. I'm kind of used to being in this position," Wright said.

> "Our story is about how hard it is getting brand new projects off the ground, particularly with larger publishers. There are parallels between Proxi and The Sims." — Lauren Elliott

> "I don't blame anybody, because it's very hard to describe an idea as a designer and expect the people you're pitching to reverse engineer that idea in their head," Wright said.

Focus group (mostly men): wouldn't play The Sims.

## Architecture → people (Sims origin)

> "It started primarily as an architectural thing, and then we started realizing how fascinating the people were," Wright said. "Then everybody had so much fun just messing with the people that we doubled down on that."

> A Pattern Language — church pew seating patterns as design input.

## Smart objects / SimAnt

> "The people actually ended up being roughly modeled on what we had with Sim Ant… their intelligence is distributed through the environment… simulate human intelligence by distributing the intelligence to the environment rather than making the Sims really smart."

> "The objects are advertising. If you're hungry, come to me."

## EA / Mattrick

> When Don Mattrick saw the Sims prototypes: "We need to buy Maxis for this, not for Sim City."

> "Don and I were like oil and water, and yet I really respect Don," Wright said.

EA acquired Maxis July 1997 for $125M. Sims shipped February 4, 2000.

## Proxi concept

> Simulation of your brain; real-life memories → generative AI scenes; editable 3D "memory palace"; each memory like a snow globe.

> Memories connect to shared memories of others; intersections; self-discovery.

> Organized as: people, places, time, feelings, things, activities.

> Scott McCloud — more abstract characters → easier identification.

> Kevin Lynch — five to ten major landmarks; hometown memory map.

> "I found that I was getting interesting results after just adding two dozen memories. When you get to 100 memories, it's really fascinating."

> Design horizon: game should last 100 years so people can always have their memories.

> Life trailer from top 10 memories.

## Funding (Apr 2025)

> Griffin Gaming Partners led $6M round (Aug 2022) — enough for prototype, not launch/marketing.

> "We're right at that point where the team is at the end of our funds." — Elliott

> Publishers need ~20M copies; small team (10–12) with huge upside hard to sell upstairs.

> Elliott: publishers away from new ideas; EA marketing budget ~$1B (+4–5% YoY) — "would have funded 10 new companies."

> Blockchain considered for memory ownership; rejected — transactions too slow.

> Platforms: PC, PS5, Xbox; Roblox explored lightly.
