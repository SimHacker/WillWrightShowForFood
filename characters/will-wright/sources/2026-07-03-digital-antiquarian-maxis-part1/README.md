## Why it matters

Maher's thesis: fan-preserved history (MobyGames/Retro Gamer) over-weights hardcore titles and
under-weights the games that actually sold to non-gamers — **SimCity** being the subtle example that
*grew* its sales year over year while subsidizing everything else Maxis did. Useful framing for the
Repo Show: **software toys, no win state, "it's all one game."**

## Highlights / show beats

| Beat | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **"All one game"** | 1990 GDC: Wright pitched an industry-wide standard file format so a SimCity road net could feed Vette! or Ultima VI — naïve but revealing his single-universe mindset |
| **SimEarth + Lovelock** | Stewart Brand introduced Wright to **James Lovelock**; Gaia hypothesis sim, nearly named *Gaia*, renamed by Jeff Braun to protect the Sim brand. Roots in Lovelock/Watson **Daisyworld** |
| **SimAnt + E.O. Wilson** | Inspired by *The Ants* (1991 Pulitzer); Wilson missed the collab letter, praised it after. Wright's first three Sims all model **distributed/communal sentience** (macro/micro/mid) |
| **SimLife (Ken Karakotsios)** | Artificial-life / Conway's Game of Life expansion; Langton: "awfully close to a useful research tool." Worst-selling Maxis release to date |
| **SimFarm (Eric Albers)** | "SimCity's country cousin" — ditches less exciting than roads, cows less than commuters |
| **SimCity 2000 (Fred Haslam)** | Braun had to "drag" Wright off the Dollhouse prototype (built on SimAnt algorithms) to ship the 1993 sequel — crunch, some quit. Isometric, 16→256 colors, real artists |
| **Dr. Wright / Miyamoto** | Miyamoto championed the SNES port; inserted Wright as advisor character "Dr. Wright" |
| **Lem / artificial life** | Stanislaw Lem's *Non Serviam* (personoids) quoted via Levy's *Artificial Life* — ties to Will's Lem/Trurl fascination (see 1996 Winograd clips) |
