Hello, Chuck!

I'd like to help, so I've done some digging and some typing, and I have come up with some great information for you!

I actually found my original contract with DUX, and (drum roll) a copy of DUX's original contract with Maxis! (I'm glad I asked for a copy -- it's interesting!)
Both contracts were successfully completed with no complications, and DUX's contract with Maxis expired in 2001, so we are in the clear.
I'll be glad to scan both of them in and send them to you, if you like. I have typed in the important parts below.

Here's the history as I know it:

On July 19, 1991, DUX Software Corporation and Sim-Business, a California Corporation, doing business as Maxis, signed a contract giving DUX the rights to develop a Unix version of SimCity, for a term of 10 years from the date of signing (so the contract expired on July 19, 2001).

On February 18, 1992, DUX Software Corporation and I signed a contract for me to develop SimCity for Unix.

I developed two versions of SimCity for various Unix platforms:

The first version was for HyperNeWS/HyperLook (a NeWS based user interface system that I helped develop at the Turing Institute in Glasgow Scotland).
http://www.donhopkins.com/home/catalog/hyperlook/SimCity.README
http://www.donhopkins.com/home/catalog/hyperlook/index.html

The second version was for X11 using the TCL/Tk scripting language and gui toolkit, which supports multiple players collaborating to vote and build over the network.
http://www.donhopkins.com/home/catalog/simcity/simcity-announcement.html

The HyperLook version of SimCity was reviewed in Unix World magazine, the April 1993 issue, on page 105.
http://www.donhopkins.com/home/catalog/simcity/simcity-review.html

The multi player X11 version of SimCity was awarded Outstanding Product of 1993 by Unix Review magazine, on page 52.

There is a huge amount of interest in SimCity, in the academic, commercial and open source communities:

I demonstrated multi player SimCity to commercial and academic computer human interaction researchers and developers, at the ACM InterCHI '93 Interactive Experience in Amsterdam, on two SGI Indigo workstations networked together.
http://www.donhopkins.com/home/catalog/simcity/simcitynet.html

I demonstrated multi player SimCity to the public at the "Electric Carnival" at Lollapalooza '94, which was a traveling tent of technology sponsored by Interval Research, that toured around the country with the concert.
An SGI Indigo and an NCD X Terminal were built into wooden kiosks networked together so kids could walk up and play with them.
http://www.donhopkins.com/home/catalog/don/electric-carnival.html
http://web.archive.org/web/19990422071458/web.interval.com/projects/ecweb/ec.html 
http://web.archive.org/web/19990422081201/web.interval.com/projects/ecweb/i.simcity.html 

I was invited as a guest speaker to demonstrate multi player SimCity and its collaborative building and voting interface at the MIT Media Lab's Digital Life Sponsor's Meeting, May 14-15, 2001.
The theme was "Public Process in the Digital Age", and the students, educators and corporate sponsored were quite interested in SimCity's educational applications.

I was invited to visit Columbia University Department of Earth and Environmental Engineering during November 11-15 2002, to discuss the educational applications of SimCity to project OPTIMUS, sponsored by the NSF.
The educators at Columbia University are excited by in the possibilities of opening up the simulator with scripting to enable SimCity to be used by students to perform experiments, stimulate them to think about complex systems, and inspire them to sign up for more earth science and environmental engineering courses.
Their express intent is to measure the effects of using simulations like SimCity on student's enrollment in hard science courses and their performance in those courses.

The SimCity source code already compiles and runs quite nicely on the latest version of Fedore Core, which is the Linux operating system the OLPC is using, so people could start playing with it immediately after it was released.
I can show you a demo and give you a copy of a VMWare virtual machine image, which you can run on Windows or Linux, including the source code, instructions for building it, and a roadmap I've written telling what all the files are for and how it works.

Making SimCity open source will have many tangible benefits to EA and the Sim franchise (and it'll make Will and a lot of other people happy, too).
I would be glad to discuss the benefits with you, and give you more references to people, studies and research papers that support that fact.

The OLPC project and the entire open source community will benefit in many ways from SimCity, both as an educational tool, a non-violent game that kids will be interested in, and a compelling concept proving "killer app" that will push the technology, and inspire more developers and educators to become involved with the OLCP project.

By enabling Columbia University to incorporate scriptable SimCity into their curriculum, and scientifically measure its effects on the educational process, it will prove SimCity and the Maxis brand of simulation games to be legitimate educational tools.

The positive publicity from open sourcing SimCity will be excellent for EA and the Sim franchise, will raise awareness of the Maxis brand and its educational qualities, and of course it will inspire many parents to buy the latest greatest version of SimCity for their kids.

It's interesting to note that when SimCity 2000 came out, the sales of the classic version of SimCity spiked too.
I think the positive publicity of making the classic version of SimCity open source will stimulate a lot of interest in the latest version of SimCity, and it will be great for EA's reputation in the educational and open source communities!

-Don
