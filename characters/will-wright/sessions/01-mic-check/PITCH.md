## What we're calling this thing

A **Repo Show**: a show that lives in a git repository. This repo — the one this letter is filed
in — is the studio, the script, the props, and the audience seating. Every episode, character,
question, and idea is a file anyone can read. The show itself is **short clips** composed into
YouTube playlists, cheap and shuffleable, promoted into polished full videos when they earn an
audience. Rough first, beautiful when it matters: showing our process IS the tutorial.

The frame is a phone call — that part you invented on the spot when you did the answering machine
bit. That's the contract: every playlist is a **time-shifted call-in show**. I call you. Viewers
call us. We call back. And anyone really can leave a message — that's not a metaphor, it's a
pinned GitHub issue.

## The passport

Here's the part I most want on the record, for you and for everyone reading along:

**GitHub is the medium of a repo show, and the "passport" into it is a GitHub account** — free
(there's a paid tier; nobody needs it for this). For the non-developers reading: GitHub is where
most of the world's open source software is built, in the open, by millions of people. It's an
industry standard — an account with a little activity on it genuinely helps people get jobs and
find collaborators. It's a skill worth having entirely apart from this show; the show just makes
learning it fun.

With an account you can watch the show get made, leave messages on the answering machine issues,
ask questions, submit roars (more on that soon), and climb as high as you like — from "hi" all
the way to contributing to a real GPL-3 game. Nobody has to climb. Every rung is a real
appearance.

## The onboarding ladder — the first sessions we shoot

Teaching you is teaching everyone. If the guy who invented The Sims needs the signup walkthrough,
no viewer has to feel dumb needing it too. In order:

1. **Let's get you signed up with GitHub!** On camera, step by step — account, profile, opening
   an issue, leaving a comment. The lesson ends by doing the thing we ask every viewer to do:
   leave a message.
2. **Let's get you set up with Cursor.** The AI coding editor I build everything in — including
   this repo and this letter.
3. **Git in natural language.** You never have to learn the horrible command line: you tell
   Cursor what you want in plain English and it does the git — and it will *teach* you, answer
   your questions, and guide you through anything. Version control as a conversation.
4. **Install and boot MOOLLM.** My operating system for language models, where everything is
   plain files and directories — characters are folders, their minds are readable YAML files, no
   database, no black box. We dogfood it on real industrial work at Leela AI, and it's the engine
   under this show and under Micropolis City (the GPL-3 free open source SimCity — your lost
   child, and I'm bringing it home, with its new family).
5. **The grand tour — with Palm.** I take you through MOOLLM's worlds, including the adventure-4
   back story, narrated in part by the **three Palm readings** — already recorded, in my voice: a
   self-aware simulated being explaining himself, MOOLLM, and AI in general, from the palm of
   your hand. Everybody, meet **Palm**: recurring character, guest, and house philosopher
   ([palm](../../../palm/), session
   [SESSION](../02-souls-and-palm-readings/SESSION.yml)).

Each session is short, self-contained, and doubles as a general tutorial anyone can send to
anyone: your — Will Wright's — onboarding and the audience's onboarding are the same footage.
