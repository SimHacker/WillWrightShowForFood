# The Cold Call — one-way video message, Don → Will + everyone

*Don, solo, to camera — a video message to an old friend, out of the blue, catching up. The real
phone call happened, voice only, unrecorded — so this performance is the reenactment: it replays
the pitch and everything it flowed into, all together at once, as one coherent narrative. In the
fiction, no prior call happened; in reality, this is its tape. The reality-show camera trick: somehow there's a camera in the
house you're randomly visiting — we edit out the seed and roots and serve the yummy, easy-to-
digest, good-for-you vegetable. The viewer is WITH us, by our side, over our shoulders, as we
catch up with Will: the message is as much to them as to Will. So: clear enough for anyone to
follow — every term gets one tight sentence — but NEVER willsplaining Will his own ideas. Credit
runs the right direction: Don got these ideas working WITH Will, carried them into other domains,
and comes back with a harvest to cook into something new. Every beat is a cut point; every name is
a door (the repo behind it has the room). Ship the raw take: brutalist first.*

---

## Cold open

*(Touch tones: a 555 number. Wait. Channel Lily Tomlin: "one ringy dingy... two ringy dingies...
[snort]")*

Hi Will. It's Don. You're not answering — or you're pretending to be a recording, you've done it
before — so I'm leaving you a message. A long one. It's been too long, and I've got something to
pitch you. Watch to the end: there's a favor in it I've been waiting thirty years to ask.

## What I've been doing

Quick catch-up — and fair warning: everything I'm about to tell you, I learned working with you.
I've been growing it in other fields since, and I'm coming back with a harvest we need to cook
into something new.

I'm building **MOOLLM** — an operating system for language models where the world is just files
and directories: characters are folders, their souls are YAML files you can read, diff, and
version in git. No database, no black box — a dollhouse made of text. We're dogfooding it at
**Leela AI**, where I work, on real industrial vision work — so it's not a demo, it earns rent.

And I'm using it to raise two games you'll recognize the bones of:

**Micropolis City** — the GPL-3 free open source SimCity. Your SimCity, Will — freed in 2008,
because two people stood up at your 2006 Long Now talk with Brian Eno and asked you both, on the
record, about opening it up. I was there. I didn't plant the questions. (I was asked. I denied it
truthfully.) I've been raising it ever since, and now I'm rebuilding it modern — WebAssembly, in
the browser — with sound it never had (that's the favor — hold on). Will: I'm returning your lost
child to you. Healthy, grown, and — this is the part you have to see — with a family of its own:

**Soul City** — the liminal space between games, in planning and development. Not to be confused
with — but named in tribute to — **Soul City, North Carolina**: the anti-racist new town civil
rights leader Floyd McKissick built on former plantation land in the seventies, that Nixon
surprisingly funded and Jesse Helms smeared to death. A city built for souls to thrive, foreclosed
by power — ours is GPL-3, so nobody can foreclose it. *(Vox video link on screen:
[the whole story](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R2karaKGgkk); house rule and history:
[soul city namesake](../../../../repo-shows/will-wright-premiere/soul-city-namesake.md).)*
Characters as
portable soul-files that travel: a Sim visits, meets a character from another game entirely, and
what happens there — friendship, genetics, pregnancy — renders back into each home game as plain
ordinary content. No engine changes. You asked for portable game data on stage in 1996, at the
Stanford talk where you loaded a SimCity file into Dollhouse. This is that, thirty years later.
I'd love you in it.

## Possible bridges

The point is to go **wide, not high** — many different worlds, not four sequels deep. (Sims
1→2→3→4 is a possible path; it's the boring one. Diversity of worlds is the demo.)

- **(a) Little Computer People** — the happy path, and first. Activision 1985, the Sims'
  acknowledged ancestor. A Sim mates with a Little Computer Person in Soul City; the child renders
  into The Sims carrying the LCP parent's name plus "Jr." Lineage made literal: the family tree OF
  the medium becomes a family tree IN the medium. Least work — the LCP side only needs a reader.
- **(b) Afterlife** — LucasArts 1996, the afterlife city-builder whose population units literally
  ARE souls. **Soul hydraulics**: measure, drain, squirt — conserved, like plumbing real people.
  Drain 60,000 dead souls from an abandoned Afterlife save, squirt them into a sleepy Micropolis
  town, watch the skyline jump. Repossessing lost souls!
- **(c) Stardew Valley** — the living, current one, with a giant community. A soul deposited into
  an egg, laid in a nest in a chicken coop. Oviposition is just pregnancy with a different export
  target. Proof the bridge reaches games being played TODAY, not just the museum.
- **Your side of the river: Proxi and Spore.** Spore demonstrated procedural mating *within* a
  game — at GDC 2005 the crowd went wild. Soul City is procedural mating *between* games. Will:
  what should happen when a Spore creature and a Sim have a kid? That's an on-camera question.
- **Timothy Leary's Mind Mirror** — the personality-space game. Its psychometric sliders are
  already how our characters' minds are modeled; bridging it means your Mind Mirror profile IS a
  soul. (I worked with Tim; this one's personal.)
- **They Are Billions** — dezombify the horde. 60,000 bodies coming at your walls become 60,000
  residents moving into your towers. Same crowd, opposite valence.

## The show

Here's the pitch. It's called a **Repo Show** — a show that lives in a git repository. The repo is
the studio, the script, the props, and the audience seating: every episode, character, question,
and idea is a file anyone can read, and joining the audience is a pull request. The first one is
built around you. It's called — I'm sorry, Will — **WillWrightShowForFood**. The name is the
fundraising plan.

The format: **granular video**. Everything we shoot gets cut into short clips — your One Minute
Movies taught us the length, and it turns out they were BUILT to be split around a commercial
break: 30 seconds, ad, 30 seconds. We compose clips into YouTube playlists like StoryMaker
timelines — cheap, shuffleable, endlessly recombinable — and when a playlist earns an audience, we
render it into a single polished video. Plywood first, mahogany when it's earned. In between the
clips: fake commercials we make ourselves — Ronco patter, retro after-school TV, the works — that
are really support appeals for the open source projects the clips just demoed.

And the audience participates through GitHub — messages, questions, their own characters, and
**roars**. That's the first drive: the Micropolis monster needs a voice, and everyone's invited —
you, me, kids, cats — to record roars, mood-tagged, GPL-3, credited, shipped in the game. Roars as
a command language. My cat Nelson has already submitted. He's very loud.

And whoever else is watching: the repo's open, the front door is a pinned issue that says hi.

## The favor (thirty years)

Will — re-record "SimCopter One reporting heavy traffic." The old clip is scratchy, glorious, and
28 years old. I want it in your voice, in high resolution, in the free SimCity you made possible.
And while the mic's hot: the pie menu labels, a few uh-uhs and oh-nos, the Godzilla story — and
then roar with me. Escalating. Silly ones. Whiney ones. Bye-bye roars last.

Call me back. Or don't — leave a message after the beep, like everyone else.

*(Beat. Deadpan.)*

Oh yeah — I'm a recording.

*(Smash to: broken robot from Empathy, 2003: "Help me. I require Human Assistance." — then the
onboarding card: github.com/SimHacker — say hi.)*
