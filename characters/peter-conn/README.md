# Peter Conn 🎬

Founded **HOMER & Assoc.** with **[Coco Conn](../coco-conn/README.md)** at
Sunset Gower. Built the **optical printer**. Wrote ***Flying Logos*** so a
demo reel could walk into SIGGRAPH as a story. Gifted the shop's papers to
Stanford.

## The story

Stanford BA Communications, 1971. A year at the Cinémathèque Française.
The finding aid says he invented **HOMER** — Hybrid Optical Montage
Electronically Reproduced — in **1973**, used it on a Levi's short in
**1974**, and founded the company with Coco in **1977**. Coco's public
account dates the console build **1978–1981**. Both sit here until he
says otherwise.

The desk mixed 16 slides and 4 movie projectors, live, like an audio
board. Peter did the optical-printer passes — 10–15 to IP film was
normal — until the composite was right. When the engineer who wired the
desk left for England, they still had no software. **[Paul Rother](../paul-rother/README.md)**
walked in. **[Charles Moore](../charles-moore/README.md)** came with the
Forth, Inc. contract and programmed the host.

Peter wanted to *see* the printer move. That errand became the CAT-700
paint system Paul got running in Forth, first used on *Abracadabra* and
*Atomic Dog*.

1988–89: SIGGRAPH stopped taking straight demo reels. Peter wrote
***Flying Logos*** — a salesman for an imaginary house walks the catalog
of chrome, then the client orders the cosmos. Coco: it was how they
sneaked the reel into the Electronic Theater. SIGGRAPH History Archives
catalog it as 1988 Film & Video Show; Coco, Peter's YouTube, and Stanford
say 1989. Director credit: Peter Conn. Voice (later consensus): Corey
Burton.

Complete records already in Paul's room — [sources/](sources/README.md)
maps them. Do not depend on the 2019 Stanford announcement blog; it is
404. The papers did not move.
