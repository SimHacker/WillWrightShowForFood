## What Dave has done

**Dave Ackley** (Prof. Emeritus, University of New Mexico) — **robust-first computing**
("a crashed system is infinitely wrong"; survive → heal → function → optimize), the
**[Movable Feast Machine](https://movablefeastmachine.org/)** (CA-descended spatial computing where
programs are living, self-repairing patterns), the **[T2 Tile Project](https://t2tile.com/)**
(indefinitely scalable hardware — bolt tiles edge-to-edge, no global clock, no single point of
failure), and, decades earlier, the co-authored **Boltzmann machine** paper (Ackley, Hinton &
Sejnowski, 1985).

## The hooks

### 1. Robust-first computing — survival before correctness
The reordering that changed how Don sees software: **survive, heal, function, optimize, adapt**.
Correctness and efficiency are luxuries; a crashed system is infinitely wrong. What does software
actually look like when you believe that all the way down?

### 2. The Movable Feast Machine, live — and break it on purpose
CA grown into an *architecture*: a program is a self-repairing **pattern** that diffuses, heals,
and reproduces. Run it live, corrupt it, watch it heal. This is the [Norman Margolus](../norman-margolus/README.md)
locality/reversibility worldview turned into a machine that keeps running while you attack it.

### 3. T2 tiles — indefinitely scalable *hardware*
Physical tiles that talk only to their neighbors, asynchronously — add tiles, add computer,
forever, with no global coordination. The physical answer to "what if the hardware were as local
as the CA rule?" Bolt-it-together computing you can hold.

### 4. The CA looping fest — architecture wing of the complexity worldview
Ackley + **[Margolus](../norman-margolus/README.md)** (physics-respecting, reversible CA) +
**[Wolfram](../stephen-wolfram/README.md)** (CA-as-computation): locality, reversibility, and what
happens when the hardware is as local as the rule. MFM is CA-as-*architecture* — the missing corner
of the triangle.

### 5. The SFI / artificial-life lineage
UNM↔Santa Fe proximity and the **Artificial Life II** paper (Ackley & Littman, 1991) put Dave in
the same soil as [Jim "Chaos" Crutchfield](../jim-crutchfield/README.md) (edge of chaos, evolving
CA) and [Reese Jones](../reese-jones/README.md) (agent-based modeling, ALife, SFI board trustee).
Robust-first is the *engineering conscience* of that complexity worldview.

### 6. Rules-and-art jam — MFM patterns as living organisms
MFM's self-repairing patterns are beautiful the way [Scott Draves](../scott-draves/README.md)'s
Electric Sheep and Don's [CAM6 artwork storytelling](../norman-margolus/the-cam6-demo-for-norman.md)
are beautiful — evolving generative life. Bring it to the cellular-automatists' rules-and-art jam.

### 7. Simulation humility — a crashed system vs. "no hope"
"A crashed system is infinitely wrong" rhymes with Will Wright's 1996 "simulating people is really
no hope — prop up a convincing illusion" and Reese Jones's "can't fold one protein in one neuron."
Three flavors of the same modesty: build for survival, not for an impossible correctness.

### 8. The long arc — Boltzmann machine (1985) → robust-first
From a founding neural-net paper (with Hinton & Sejnowski) to renouncing brittle determinism:
what he kept, what he threw away, and why the field's obsession with efficiency is the bug.
