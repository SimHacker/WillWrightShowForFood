# Christine McGavran — segment seeds

*Seeds, not a script. Her pick.*

## Two people who shipped SimCity

She led **SimCity 3000**; Don open-sourced the original and is rebuilding **Micropolis** for the
browser. Questions worth asking on air:

- What SC3K had to solve that the original never faced — art pipeline, memory, an audience that
  already knew the game.
- The Dynamic Zone Filter in the original was Shneiderman's dynamic queries in disguise. What survived
  into SC3K, and what got cut for being too strange?
- Producing versus engineering on the same title, from someone who has done both jobs.

## The map was always the game

Her own account of her career is the argument: SimCity taught her mapping and urban planning, and that
sent her into navigation. The obvious follow-up — what did the game get *wrong* about cities that the
real map data corrected?

## Getting a map into hardware that isn't yours

She pitched maps into car dashboards; it became Google Automotive Services. Compare with
[`bike-computer.md`](../../apps/ebike-safari/design/bike-computer.md):

- What actually closes an embedded deal, versus what engineers think closes it.
- Where the bike case is genuinely different — no OEM relationship, no dealer, a browser instead of an
  app store.
- The part Don keeps hitting: web-map terms of service assume a per-request server relationship, and a
  head unit on a long ride is a poor fit for that shape.

## Feeding the basemap

Thousands of feeds, cities and governments, cost per source. Then the honest question about amateur
signal: is [road roughness measured by a bicycle accelerometer](../../apps/ebike-safari/design/bumps-as-input.md)
worth anything to a real map operation, or is it a charming dead end? Don would rather hear no from
someone who has run the intake.

Adjacent: TomTom clipped the ends of trips and aggregated the middles so it could publish traffic
without publishing anyone's front door. That model is the basis of
[`privacy.md`](../../apps/ebike-safari/design/privacy.md). Does it hold up in 2026?

## Orbit to handlebar

Vantor's satellite tasking at one end, a phone camera on a handlebar at the other. One street, two
resolutions, and the question of what a fresh capture is worth versus a cheap one. Street-level
imagery (Panoramax) sits in the middle.

## The Tower

Maxis-side memory of SimTower, and the 2024 code drop she has already looked at. The open problems are
real and unglamorous: where the art assets live, what is Nintendo's and must not ship, and the
Japanese comments and strings — which is the help she volunteered to think about.
