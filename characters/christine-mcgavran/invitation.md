Christine —

You wrote on your own profile that **SimCity is what taught you mapping and urban planning**, and that
is what sent you into navigation. I have been building the thing that sentence describes, and I would
love to have you on to talk about it — from the side that actually shipped the maps.

Since the Yoot Tower thread, I have been making **Will Wright Show For Food**: a repo-native talk show
whose stage *is* a public GitHub repo — artifacts, correspondence and working code anyone can browse.
**Show, don't tell.**

**Will Wright is in — he's signed on to do the [premiere](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/repo-shows/will-wright-premiere/README.md) and more.**

**What I'd love to explore with you — your pick, any order, solo or across episodes:**

**1. Two people who shipped SimCity**
You worked on and led **SimCity 3000**; I open-sourced the original as **Micropolis** and am rebuilding
it to run in the browser. Twenty-five years apart, same city, completely different problems. I would
rather hear how SC3K really got made than hear the legend again.

**2. The map was always the game** *(the one I think you'd enjoy most)*
Your career is the throughline: urban simulation → mapping and navigation. I am arguing the same thing
from the other end with **eBike Safari** — OpenStreetMap as the world model, a bike as the cursor, and
the ride itself as the interaction. Ben Shneiderman's direct manipulation, on two wheels.

**3. Getting a map into hardware that isn't yours**
You pitched Google's maps into car dashboards and it became **Google Automotive Services**. The
bicycle cockpit is that argument with nobody standing in it: the rider already carries a better
computer than the head unit, and the incumbent app is rented back to them monthly. I have written up
why that market looks like the PND collapse I watched from inside TomTom — and where the analogy
breaks.

**4. Feeding the basemap**
Transit feeds, incident feeds, GIS, EV, Waze, and the city partnerships underneath. I want to know
what an amateur signal is actually worth to a map — because a bike measuring **road roughness through
its accelerometer** is exactly that kind of signal, and I would rather learn the honest answer than
guess.

**5. Orbit to handlebar**
Vantor's imagery and satellite tasking at one end, a phone camera on a handlebar at the other, the
same street at two resolutions. What survives the trip down the ladder, and what doesn't.

**6. The Tower**
You have already seen the Yoot Tower code drop. There is a revival room in the repo with Yoot Saito,
Patrick Hulin, Thomas Cherryhomes and Lars Brinkhoff in it — including the Japanese comments and
strings problem you offered to help think about.

**Format:** Remote is easy; in person if we overlap. **Zero homework — as much *homefun* as you feel
like.** Warm room, not gotcha-podcast. One episode or a few — you steer.
