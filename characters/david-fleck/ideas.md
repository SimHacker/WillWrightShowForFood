# Dave Fleck — conversation hooks

*Draft — consent not yet asked. [Portrayal standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md).*

## 1. Closing the eighteen-year loop (the opening beat)

In 2008 he asked Don for the 1969 PIXIE paper and wrote *"I just feel this need to see
the original."* The answer at the time was: nobody has it, and Buxton says the original
is unsuitable for scanning. The show opens by handing it to him on air: the digitized
paper, Heinz's PhD thesis, both 1969 films, [Flight of the PIXIE](../heinz-lemke/cambridge-films-flight-of-the-bumblebee.md),
the PDP-7 assembly + octal (transcribed, stepping in SIMH), plus notes, recollections,
articles, interviews, and discussion threads. His live reaction to finally seeing the
thing he went looking for eighteen years ago — that's the cold open. Then the reveal:
*his own forwarded PDFs* are what set the recovery in motion. He's not a spectator to
this story; he's the first cause.

## 2. The Wacom Radial Menu origin story

- What drove the radial design in 2007–2008? The patent's key move — wedges as
  **continuous variable controls** set by cursor angle, not just discrete commands —
  is a genuine extension of the pie menu idea. Where did it come from?
- The prior-art hunt: what did the patent lawyers make of PIXIE (1969), Wiseman's
  papers, Don's CHI'88 work, Kurtenbach's marking menus? How does a corporate patent
  process metabolize forty years of academic lineage?
- Shipping it: Radial Menu as a standard driver feature from Intuos4 on. What did
  artist telemetry / support feedback say? Do artists actually use it, and how?
- The Sims connection: Don's Sims pie menus and Wacom's Radial Menu are arguably the
  two largest radial-menu deployments in history. Compare notes on what each learned.

## 3. Twenty-six years inside the tablet

- 1993 → 2019 spans serial ArtZ tablets to Cintiq Pro and the web platform. Walk the
  eras: what was the hardest pen-input problem of each decade?
- The patent portfolio as autobiography: jitter filtering that varies with pen height,
  palm rejection, the finger-wheel stylus, the filament-sensing brush. Pick three
  patents and tell the story behind each.
- W3C Pointer Events: getting pen twist into the browser. What did standards work
  teach a hardware VP?

## 4. Prior art as a way of life

- He's the rare industry engineer who did his history homework — and got burned by the
  same one-way information flow Don did (Buxton's notes went to a list that excluded
  the person who connected everyone). His read on how the field treats its own history.
- Would he want to help with the PIXIE restoration itself? A pen-input engineer's eye
  on the light-pen-era interaction loop (PIXIE's stroke recognizer, the tracking cross)
  would be a real contribution, not a courtesy credit.

## Ensemble options

- **Solo**: pen input from ArtZ to Pointer Events, with the PIXIE cold open.
- **With Heinz Lemke**: the man who asked for the paper meets the man who wrote it.
  1969 light pen meets 2009 EMR stylus; two radial menu generations, forty years apart.
- **With Simon Schneegans**: the corporate radial menu vs. the open-source one —
  Wacom's driver feature and Kando, design constraints compared.
