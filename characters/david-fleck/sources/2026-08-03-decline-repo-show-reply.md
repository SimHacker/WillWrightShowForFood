Hello Don,

Wow - this is a blast from the past!

As a bit of background - I was asking about pie menus for my doctoral thesis. Since I was supposed to "extend the art", I chose an area that I was intimately familiar with, that is UI and pointing activities. I'm sure you know that anything an employee "invents" is owned by the company - so Wacom patented my thesis work. Being a hardware company they never shipped any product with pie menus in it (so that's a bit of a correction there). But since they foot the bill to patent the idea, I was happy for them to patent it and get my name on it.

So I'd like to respectfully decline your invitation. I did the research and got my degree, Wacom patented it (to add to their portfolio), and that was the end of it. So I feel my involvement in pie menus was quite short lived and minor. And it was long enough ago that I'm not sure I remember what thinking went behind it (although it was a learning experience - I think I have a good grasp of Fitt's Law now ;-). I retired in 2019. Before that I tried to keep my finger on the pulse of pen computing. I was somewhat active in USB and W3C standards and attended SIGCHI, UIST, AVI, and Table-Top/ISS to keep current and see what ideas I could bring to Wacom. Now that I'm retired I've left all of that behind (I don't even know what products Wacom currently ships and haven't been to an Apple Developer's Conference for many years).

Oh, and I think Second Life may have had a huge pie menu deployment, maybe the largest.

Thank you for all of the papers and video. Watching the pie menu demo excerpts from "All The Widgets" was fascinating. There is some really advanced techniques in there. It's a shame more of the ideas in there were never shipped commercially.

I tried to look at the Pixie movies at https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/library/archives.html but I get errors "https://sms.cam.ac.uk/media/3009795/embed is unreachable".

Getting tilt into W3C was a bit of a challenge. It seems some of the applications people and the "mathy" types want altitude and azimuth, but pretty much all pen hardware measures X and Y tilt. The problem comes when the pen is nearly vertical, and the resolution is one degree of tilt (or lower) so azimuth jumps wildly (one degree tilt with one degree of resolution only yields every 45 degrees in azimuth). Anyhow, they stuck both in and developers will figure it out. That being said, I'm not sure how many pen vendors even support tilt, or how many software developers utilize it (and perhaps none on the web).

So thanks for thinking of me. And I'd be glad to continue the conversation (assuming I know anything more of interest ;-)

Best,  
Dave
