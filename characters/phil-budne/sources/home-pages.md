## The XY display simulator

"A DEC XY display simulator for use with SIMH, standalone, or with other simulators. Currently
includes PDP-1 Type 30 and PDP-11 VT11 support." The longer resume lists it among his SIMH
contributions: "Calligraphic (X-Y) graphics simulator (simulates phosphor decay) with device
simulators for PDP-1 (Type 30), PDP-11 (VT11) and Type 340 Display Processor on PDP-6 with
support for X11, MacOS and Win32 graphics", and "Binary paper tape support for PDP-1 for MACRO
Assembler." So `display/display.c` is his design, not only his upkeep; Douglas Gwyn's
revisions and Douglas W. Jones's `vc8e.c` are in its header.

## The PDP-10

- High school: the BOCES/NCODE KA10 (serial 109) on Long Island, "via an ASR33 and an acoustic
  coupler". Then KL1282 at Stevens Tech, Hoboken.
- "I was also a tourist on the MIT ITS systems as BUDD (a typo that stuck)." That is the
  `budd` in `budd@bu-cs.bu.edu`.
- DEC Marlboro, FORTRAN-10/20. Then he managed Boston University Computer Science's 2060
  (KL3047), as `BUDD@BUCS20.ARPA`, "until it's demise in October 1986, when I became a
  full-time Unix person."
- "A great joy of using (and working on) DEC software was the focus on quality that simply
  doesn't exist in todays world."
- SAFE: a 64-bit, VAX-datatype-compatible RISC project Alan Kotok headed; Phil "briefly worked
  the code scheduler for VAST, the VAX to SAFE Translator". It became PRISM, and "some allege
  that the AXP stood for Almost eXactly Prism". Alan and Rosanne Kotok autographed his copy of
  *Twenty Years of 36-bit Computing with Digital 1964-1984* at Alan's retirement.
- The page keeps lists he maintains (TOPS-10 CALLIs, TOPS-10 and TOPS-20 evolution, PDP-6/10
  I/O codes, opcode chart, KL10 and KS10 microcode versions, old DEC option type codes), his
  tape readers `read20` and `backup10`, an `its-install` expect script for ITS under SIMH,
  and a history of the line from Whirlwind, TX-0 and the PDP-1 to the PDP-6 and the PDP-7. It
  links Lars Brinkhoff's PDP-10 pages.

## Finger

"FINGER and WHOIS hark back to a different time on the internet; a time when the net was a
small and friendly town, where people could leave their front doors unlocked." His Unix finger
is "inspired by MIT ITS and TOPS-20 finger programs" (comp.sources.unix vol. 25, c. 1990; v3(447),
1996); so is "Fido", an rwho watchdog named for an ITS program. The page keeps output from MC
in its last days ("The ITS machines are being shut down. Read the file SYS;GOOD BYE (last
updated 12 May 90)"), from SAIL around 1990, and from XKL's TOPS-20 machines in 2002.

## Since 2020

From the longer resume: Media Cloud (2022 on), a news search tool; XXL, "eXperimental
eXtensible Language", a JavaScript/Python hybrid with runtime syntax extension, closures and
continuations ([github](https://github.com/philbudne/xxl)); the PDP-7 UNIX replacement shell
[`pbsh.s`](https://github.com/DoctorWkt/pdp7-unix/blob/master/src/other/pbsh.s); a TMG compiler
written in TMG, and a B compiler in TMG
([notes](https://github.com/philbudne/pdp7-unix/blob/tmg/misc/tmg-notes.txt)); `libprom`, a
small C Prometheus library. His summary of himself: "Willing and able to wade into any
environment, read code, and be able to contribute at any layer within a week. I will create a
tool or little language for any job I need to do more than twice."
