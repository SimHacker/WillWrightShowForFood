# Correspondence: Phil Budne × Don Hopkins

Public-safe digest, not the full threads. Third parties' private replies are summarized
without names. Mailing-list posts (FIGIL, TUHS) were public.

**Span:** September 1986 to November 2026 · **Consent:** `not_asked`

---

## 1986: FIGIL, Forth for an Apple IIe

The FIGIL Forth mailing list, moderated from the Weizmann Institute over BITNET, reached Don
at `A2DEH@mc.lcs.mit.edu`. The August 1986 mailing (sent 12 September) carried a request from
Phil, then at Boston University's Distributed Systems Group, posted 6 September:

> I have a friend doing experiment control / data acquisition with an apple II but finds basic
> too slow (big surprise). What are some forths for the apple and their pros/cons/prices. Or
> alternatively a compiler for basic.

Replies in that mailing and the October one pointed him to Mountain View Press's public-domain
Forth-79, Paul Lutus's GraForth, and the UNIFORTH Sampler, whose author, Lee Brotzman at NASA
Goddard, answered in person.

## 1990: Rick Adams's FTP, and PizzaTool

- **16 March 1990.** Don, at the University of Maryland, wrote to uunet asking them to carry
  HyperNeWS and his NeWS collection, and mentioned that he'd got "a copy of Rick Adams brand
  FTP [tm] from Phil Budne" and was running it on `tumtum.cs.umd.edu`.
- **24 October 1990.** Don, now at Sun, posted about PizzaTool, Ben Stoltz's program
  for faxing orders to Tony & Alba's in Mountain View. A wave of people asked for copies. Phil,
  at Shiva, wrote: "Mind if I forward this?" Don forwarded the whole pile to Ben: "Me and my
  big mouth!"

## 2020: "Phil Budne did most of the work"

In March 2020 Don asked Lars Brinkhoff about SIMH's new PDP-7 Type 340 support. Lars: "Actually
Phil Budne did most of the work. He wrote the emulator for the PDP-6/10. I added the PDP-7
parts." Don, after reading it: "clean, sleek, well organized code", modular enough to compile
for the browser. Full thread: [Lars Brinkhoff correspondence](../lars-brinkhoff/correspondence.md).

## 2026: TUHS

On TUHS (6 May 2026) Phil asked: "Was the VAX 11/780 the first DEC system that came with an
RTC/TOY clock out of the box?" Clem Cole and Paul Winalski answered: the 780's clock wasn't
battery-backed except as an option, and the 11/730 had no battery at all, which led to Dick
Hustvedt's April Fools "SD730 Fixed Head Solar Horologue", a sundial with a photocell at noon,
plugged into the UNIBUS. [TUHS digest vol. 126, issue 5](https://www.tuhs.org/pipermail/tuhs/).

## November 2026: Facebook

Don and Phil became Facebook friends on 25 November 2026. Don's first message is drafted for
him to send: the browser PDP-7 and Type 340, whose 340 engine is ported from Phil's
`type340.c` and `display.c`; the Ultimate Hand demo; the Forth Day video; and PDP-7 UNIX from
the `pdp7-unix` restoration running in the same emulator.
