# Sourced quotes — Bill Joy

Public-safe excerpts only. No private addresses or phone numbers. Full 2006 correspondence
stays in the private Don Hopkins archive.

## Joy's law and the 1991 Sun talk

From Don Hopkins, [Bill Joy's Law: 2^(Year-1984) Million Instructions per Second](https://donhopkins.medium.com/bill-joys-law-2-year-1984-million-instructions-per-second-3a8c92165cfe) (Medium, 2018), summarizing a February 1991 talk:

> **wnj's law:** 2**(year-1984) performance is slightly behind, but will catch up with superscalar and multiprocessor technologies.

> **"C++++-= is the new language that is a little more than C++ and a lot less."** — Bill Joy

David Hough (Sun), on the same talk:

> It's vintage wnj. When assessing wnj-speak, remember **Eric Schmidt's comment that Bill is almost always qualitatively right, but the time scale is sometimes wrong.**

On open systems (same talk, via Medium article):

> **Open systems empower small organizations to do great things.**

> **Second law of technology: innovation will occur… usually somewhere else.** Don't bet against innovation.

## Educom 1988 — pie menus (public anecdote)

Don Hopkins, Educom 1988 (documented in public writing; see also [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17098824)):

At **Educom October 1988**, Don demoed NeWS, pie menus, Emacs, and HyperTIES to a visitor who turned out to be **Bill Joy** — he had shaved his beard. After several rounds of demos (including inflating Sun founders' heads via the optical mouse trick), Bill said: **"I used to use Emacs on ITS!"** Don had thought he was "more of a vi guy."

## 2006 — pie menus and UI (short excerpts)

Bill Joy reached out to Don Hopkins in **October 2006** about user-interface work. Quoted substance only:

> **"I thought of you and your great 'pie menus'."**

> **"Thanks for coming down last night. That was fun."**

> **"Original logo: Vaughan Pratt (Prof. Stanford). 45 degree rotation: Mark Anderson (P.A. Designer)."**

After Don shared the **SimFaux** demo:

> **"Fun. Thanks for the pointers."**

On **SimCity for OLPC** (December 2006):

> What is likely to happen with Open Source is that the game will be extended and appear in devices in ways you never imagine. Not only will this give lots of people exposure to the great educational experience, but it will whet people's appetite for other great similar things **and also perhaps inspire another Will!**
