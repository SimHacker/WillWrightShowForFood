# Repo Show invitation — Art Medlar

*Art may edit, decline, delay, or request removal at any time — or take direct edit access. [Portrayal standards](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/schemas/portrayal-standards.md) — also covers quiet mode and onboarding.*
**Subject:** WAIS, the early Archive, and the Connection Machine, on the record

---

Art —

We work together at Leela, and I've never asked you to tell these stories properly, with a recorder
running. I'd like to.

You were there when searching the Internet and archiving it both began: WAIS and the first version of
the Internet Archive, after programming Connection Machines at Thinking Machines. The Archive has
turned out to be the backbone of a lot of what I've been doing lately. The Wayback Machine gave back
Knuth's 2022 Adventure source, the Living Computer Museum's PDP-7 videos, and my own 2013 emails about
what became Screen Angel. And I've built a PDP-7 and Type 340 emulator in the browser that I'd like to
turn into proper Archive items, programs with their tapes and listings and scans
([the cabinet](https://hyperties.org/cabinet/) ·
[what that would take](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/packages/cabinet/PRESERVATION.md)).
I've written to Brewster too ([his page](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/brewster-kahle/README.md)).

**Will Wright is in — he's signed on to do the [premiere](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/repo-shows/will-wright-premiere/README.md) and more.**

A **Repo Show** is a recorded conversation whose stage is a public git repository; what we talk about
gets written down and linked, and stays. Possible threads, your pick:

1. The Connection Machine: programming massive parallelism in the late 1980s, and what it taught
   you about large systems.
2. WAIS: how it worked, who used it, and what the search logs showed people wanted.
3. The early Internet Archive: the first crawls, the machines, and what was hard.
4. PARC operating systems research in the 1990s.
5. Brightmail: from a napkin sketch to full-scale deployment and Symantec.
6. With Brewster, if you'd both like a WAIS reunion.

Whenever suits you. No preparation needed.

— Don
