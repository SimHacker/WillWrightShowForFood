# Don ↔ The Echo Nest, 2010–2013 — correspondence digest

*Primary source digest. Full threads in Don's mail archive; this file
keeps the load-bearing quotes and the timeline. Contact details
omitted.*

## The Stupid Fun Club lead-in (May 2010)

Don to Peter Swearengen and Will Wright, May 24, 2010, in a thread
about [sCrAmBlEd?HaCkZ!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eRlhKaxcKpA):

> I was looking at those links from Ge Wang, and discovered they're
> having an Music Information Retrieval conference in Utrecht in
> August ... How interesting does that sound to you?

Peter's reply lists the ISMIR 2010 tutorials, including:

> Tutorial 4: Ben Fields (Goldsmiths, University of London) and
> **Paul Lamere (The Echo Nest). Finding A Path Through The Jukebox —
> The Playlist Tutorial.**

That title has aged into a design principle: the moody city record is
a path through a jukebox the size of a city.

## First contact (June 2010)

Eric Swenson (Echo Nest bizdev) reached out June 14; Don's June 15
reply is the SFC-era snapshot:

> I'm developing music processing software for the Stupid Fun Club,
> which is Will Wright's "entertainment think tank" start-up company.
> ... I first heard of your work on reddit from the demo that detects
> regular drum machines, and then of course the wonderful swinger and
> cowbells demos too. ... It's lovely, easy to understand code.

Architecture described: Python "desktop back-end" web server for
content management and analysis, Unity3D front-end. Plus a shotgun
blast of questions — local analysis, fingerprinting beta, Spotify's
closed library versus Echo Nest's open platform — and the closer:

> Did you design the name of your company to subliminally contain the
> word "hoNest", or was that an "hoNest" co-incidence? (Of course who
> am I to tease you about the name of your company, when I work for
> the Stupid Fun Club?)

Skype call with Paul and Eric confirmed for July 8, 2010.

## Paul debugs Don's earworm (July 2010)

Don hit `EchoNestAPIThingIDError` missing from pyechonest while
running the earworm demo on Bohemian Rhapsody. Paul, July 19:

> Looks like you are seeing some version skew. Remix still requires
> an older version of Pyechonest ... if you start from a fresh
> install of remix, it all should work.

Don's reply: "I'm looking forward to visualizing the song analysis
and earworm analysis in unity!"

## ISMIR 2010, Utrecht (August 2010)

Eric connected Don and Paul for the conference (Will Wright and Peter
Swearengen cc'd). Don: registered, paid, signed up for Paul's
tutorial — "Paul's tutorial sealed the deal — that's precisely what
we're interested in." Phone numbers exchanged for the
Amsterdam→Utrecht rendezvous; Paul arrived Schiphol Saturday morning,
tutorial Monday (Aug 9). Don also offered Eric a canal boat cruise
during Pride weekend.

## The newsletter era (2010–2013)

Don stayed a registered Echo Nest developer through the platform's
whole arc, newsletter by newsletter:

- **Dec 2010 – Jan 2011** — API v4, Rosetta Stone (one artist ID
  mapped across every music service), personal catalogs, playlisting.
- **2011** — speechiness ("anything by William Shatner falls
  somewhere in the middle"), EMI sandboxes (Blue Note, Gorillaz),
  taste-profile playlisting with an 'adventurousness' knob, the
  Rdio/IDJ/Twitter partnerships, Paul's Google Instant Mix shootout.
- **2012** — Spotify Radio powered by Echo Nest; taste profile
  similarity ("user-to-room recommendations - find the best virtual
  listening room for me based upon my music taste" — a moody sentence
  if there ever was one); $17M round; **The Infinite Jukebox** (Music
  Hack Day Boston, Nov 2012: "thousands of folks who wanted to listen
  to Call Me Maybe 24 hours a day"); live/studio/christmas song
  types; genre radio.
- **2013** — acousticness, artist location and the **Roadtrip
  Mixtape** ("a mixtape for your roadtrip featuring artists from the
  cities you're passing through"), Every Noise at Once (Glenn
  McDonald), the Bonhamizer, MySXM personalization.

Spotify acquired The Echo Nest in March 2014; Paul went with it.

## Why this digest exists

The Echo Nest built the industrial version of moody's annotation
army: machine listening that ships per-song meaning over the net to
anyone's player. What it never had was the consumer — a world of
simulated people who could *feel* the data. The invitation
([`../invitation.md`](../invitation.md)) is about finally wiring the
two halves together.
