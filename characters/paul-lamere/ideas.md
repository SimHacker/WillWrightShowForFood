# Paul Lamere — show ideas

## §1 The Infinite Jukebox at 1:1 scale

The city record ([MOODY.md](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/designs/MOODY.md))
is his hack, scaled up: streets as parallel grooves, the bike as the
record needle, intersections as branch points, roundabouts as locked
grooves, a radio dial that tunes across nearby rides. Segment: open
the [Infinite Jukebox](https://musicmachinery.com/2012/11/12/the-infinite-jukebox/)
beat graph next to a city map with ride trails and let Paul react to
his own visualization wearing asphalt. Then ride it — the ebike
safari demo mode runs in a browser (drag the marker, click a
destination, tilt to steer).

## §2 Measuring vs meaning

The Echo Nest extracted what a song *is* — tempo, energy,
danceability, acousticness, valence. The moody track declares what a
song *means* — romantic 0.9 at t=45, the percussion event at the
crescendo. Ask Paul: how far can analysis get toward meaning without
the composer? Where does the hand-authored annotation beat the
extracted feature, and where is it the other way around? (He ran the
2011 playlist shootout; he has opinions with data.)

## §3 Taste Profiles as souls

Echo Nest Taste Profiles modeled the listener; Rosetta Stone mapped
one identity across every music service. Both rhyme hard with the
soul-file: one character, re-rendered per world. A short two-hander
with the Soul City material — what did music tech learn about
identity federation that life sims haven't yet?
