# Invitation — Paul Lamere · the machine-listening chair on the Moody panel

*Draft, Aug 2026 — not yet sent.*

Dear Paul,

Sixteen years ago you debugged my pyechonest version skew personally,
and I sat in your ISMIR tutorial in Utrecht — "Finding a Path Through
the Jukebox" — back when I was building music software for Will
Wright's Stupid Fun Club. I've been an Echo Nest fan since the
swinger and the cowbells, and I read every newsletter you sent from
speechiness through the Infinite Jukebox. This mail is about paths
through jukeboxes, so it's really your fault.

I'm building a Repo Show called **Will Wright Show For Food**, around
the Sims 1 content-creation stack. **Will is in — he's signed on to
do the premiere and more.** One of the episodes is one you belong in:

**Moody: MIDI for Mood** — the idea I pitched at Maxis while we were
building The Sims and never shipped: media that carries a
machine-readable *meaning* track alongside the audio, time-varying
emotional parameters that a room full of simulated people can feel.
In 2019 I told Sims composer Jerry Martin that moody "reminds me of
the music analysis stuff that Paul Lamere and his colleagues at The
Echo Nest was working on" — that sentence is now in the primary
sources, so you were on this panel before it existed:

- The design doc: [MOODY.md](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/designs/MOODY.md)
- The 1999 SimRadio email: [simradio-moody-1999-maxis-email.md](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/don-hopkins/simradio-moody-1999-maxis-email.md)
- The even earlier design-doc review that proposed "we hire monkeys to
  listen to well known CDs" and mark them up — the annotation army The
  Echo Nest actually built: [moody-design-doc-review.md](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/don-hopkins/moody-design-doc-review.md)

The panel question you'd own: **measuring versus meaning.** The Echo
Nest extracted what a song *is* — tempo, energy, danceability,
valence. Moody declares what a song *means* — romantic 0.9 at t=45, a
percussion event at the crescendo that triggers something in the
world. How far does analysis get toward meaning without the composer?
You ran the 2011 playlist shootout; I want your answer with data,
next to composers (Jerry Martin among the invitees) who'd hand-author
their own meaning tracks.

And one more thing, because I couldn't help myself: the design doc
has a section called **the city record**, and it is the Infinite
Jukebox at 1:1 scale. Streets as parallel grooves, a bicycle as the
record needle, intersections as branch points, roundabouts as locked
grooves, a radio dial that tunes across intersecting and nearby
rides so songs hop around the city one commute at a time. Your beat
graph, drawn in asphalt. I'm prototyping the recording side as an
ebike-safari app (place+time sync tracks: music, GPS trace, photos,
spoken notes) with a browser demo mode — I'd love to show it to you
and hear what the Echo Nest era knew that I'm about to relearn the
hard way.

What have you been up to since Spotify? Music Machinery's Substack
suggests the jukebox never actually ended — which is the most
on-brand possible outcome.

— Don
