# Paul Lamere 🎛️🔁

*Portrayal of a real invitee, written by Don — not Paul.*
[Portrayal standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md)

**Paul Lamere** ([Music Machinery](https://musicmachinery.com/),
[Substack](https://musicmachinery.substack.com/)) ran the developer
platform at [**The Echo Nest**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Echo_Nest),
the MIT Media Lab spinoff that industrialized machine listening —
tempo, energy, danceability, acousticness, speechiness, liveness, a
per-song meaning database served over the net — before Spotify bought
it in 2014. He's the maker of
[**The Infinite Jukebox**](https://musicmachinery.com/2012/11/12/the-infinite-jukebox/)
(Music Hack Day Boston 2012: graph a song's similar-sounding beats,
branch playback through the graph, and the song never ends), Boil the
Frog, the Bonhamizer, the Roadtrip Mixtape, and the 2011
playlist-engine shootout that toured the tech press. Before The Echo
Nest: music research at Sun Labs ("Search Inside the Music").

## Why this show

| Beat | Why it matters here |
|------|---------------------|
| **The Infinite Jukebox** | The [city record](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/designs/MOODY.md) is the Infinite Jukebox at 1:1 scale — beats become blocks, branches become intersections, the song graph drawn in asphalt |
| **Machine listening at scale** | The moody design's ["we hire monkeys to annotate CDs"](../don-hopkins/moody-design-doc-review.md) army, industrialized — everything except the consumer: a world of simulated people who can *feel* the data |
| **Roadtrip Mixtape** | An Echo Nest demo that playlists the cities your route passes through — the ebike safari's direct ancestor |
| **Taste Profiles** | The per-person reaction model the [1999 SimRadio email](../don-hopkins/simradio-moody-1999-maxis-email.md) asked for ("different people would react to the musical moods in different ways") |
| **Rosetta Stone** | One musical identity mapped across every service — rhymes with the soul-file, one character re-rendered per world |
| **The tutorial title** | "Finding a path through the jukebox" is literally the show's geometry — through a beat graph or through a city's grooves |
