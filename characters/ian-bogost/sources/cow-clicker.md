# Cow Clicker (2010) — satire that became a phenomenon

*Ian Bogost's procedural rhetoric as performance art. Machine-readable
source data: [cow-clicker.yml](cow-clicker.yml).*

## What it was

Bogost built [Cow Clicker](https://bogost.com/games/cow-clicker/) in
about three days for his July 2010 NYU seminar "Social Games On Trial,"
after coining "cow clickers" at GDC 2010 to criticize FarmVille winning
Best New Social/Online Game. It distilled Zynga-style mechanics to
their absurd essence: click a cow every six hours, earn clicks, buy
Mooney, invite friends, post to your feed. It was built to demonstrate
what social games abuse — not to ship a product.

The satire then compounded itself: a Cowclickification API, Cow Clicker
Blitz (with Bejeweled's Jason Kapalka), My First Cow Clicker on iOS,
Cow Clicktivism (an emaciated cow benefiting Oxfam), and the
Stargrazer Cow — a roughly $20 mirror-image cow. Leigh Alexander's
Kotaku piece on the "Rightward-Facing Cow" treated the mirror cow as
the life-changing luxury good it satirized.

## The Cowpocalypse

Bogost scheduled the game's death for its first birthday — July 21,
2011 — and let players fight it: each click deducted thirty seconds
from the countdown, and Facebook Credits could extend it (10 credits
bought an hour; 4,000 bought a month; players paid roughly $700 total
to delay doom). The cows were finally
[raptured on September 7, 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cow_Clicker#%22Cowpocalypse%22_event_and_conclusion),
leaving blank spaces — still clickable. Bogost, to a mourning fan:
"It wasn't very fun before."

## Reception

The joke escaped the lab: 50,000+ players by September 2010, to
Bogost's genuine discomfort — he continued it as layered satire. Jason
Tanz profiled the trap in Wired ("The Curse of Cow Clicker," December
2011); NPR's On the Media ran "If You Can't Ruin It, Destroy It";
researcher Nick Yee called it an "incredibly clear Skinner box";
Kapalka called it "ironic, satirical, self-referential — what Facebook
lacked." Bogost later judged Cookie Clicker (2013) Cow Clicker's
logical conclusion, and in 2018 wrote
"My Cow Game Extracted Your Facebook Data" for The Atlantic in the
Cambridge Analytica moment.

## Show use — respectful beats

- Satire first — the popularity was the second joke, not the business
  plan.
- The Cowpocalypse was honest about manipulation: scheduled death
  communicated upfront, pay-to-delay-doom as the argument.
- Contrast with Curiosity (2012): hype-by-accident versus
  criticism-by-design —
  [Don's Unite 2012 cow joke](../../don-hopkins/sources/hn-cow-clicker-curiosity-unite-2012.md).
