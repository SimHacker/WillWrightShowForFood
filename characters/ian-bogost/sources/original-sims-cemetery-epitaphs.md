# The Original Sims cemetery — an archaeology museum (Wayback, 2004–2006)

**Sources:**
[2004-11-12 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20041112005837/http://www.originalsims.com/cemetery)
(**364 tombstones**) and
[2006-06-12 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20060612225935/http://www.originalsims.com/cemetery)
(**2249 tombstones**) of originalsims.com/cemetery. The
[September 2005 article](../../will-wright/sources/2005-09-22-halloween-tombstones-original-sims/article.md)
recorded 1200 in between — the graveyard sextupled in nineteen months.

Users engraved tombstones (Halloween with ghosts, or Solemn with flowers),
uploaded photos of the deceased, downloaded the working Sims object, and
emailed links to friends. Epitaphs below are verbatim, spelling preserved —
patina is the point. Images are the actual personalized tombstone graphics,
served from the Internet Archive; click any stone to visit its page.

## The museum label

Every register the internet has, on one page, without a moderator: a
sister mourned in Bismillah, the first Sim to die of a broken heart, the
trans game designer The Sims is dedicated to and the father of
ubiquitous computing (both engraved by the toolmaker himself, testing
his own software), EA's crunch scandal as a mass grave, the whole
Romantic movement from memory, both sides of the 2004 election, a
Finnish cat, an ice cream cone, and "u smell." The worst thing in 2249
tombstones is playground spite — no slurs, no brigades, no doxxing.
Strangers wrote each other's grief with care and jokes on an open UGC
site with a free download button, and the community's own institutions
got headstones when they died.

That's the show beat: walk Ian through the wings, then ask what this
page knew that the platforms that came after didn't. The cemetery is
the counterfactual internet — and it's still standing in the Archive,
photos and all.

## Wing III — Industry history, engraved as it happened

<a href="https://web.archive.org/web/2006/http://www.originalsims.com/Objects/Personalized/Simslice-SolemnTombstone_227064334790.html"><img src="https://web.archive.org/web/20070512220935im_/http://www.originalsims.com/Objects/Personalized/Simslice-SolemnTombstone_227064334790.bmp" width="220" alt="Dani Bunten Berry's tombstone"></a>

> **Dani Bunten Berry** — The Sims is dedicated to Dani Bunten Berry
> (February 19, 1949 - July 3, 1998), a.k.a. Dani Bunten, born Daniel
> Bunten. An American game designer and programmer, known for the 1983
> game M.U.L.E (one of the first successful multiplayer games), and 1984's
> Seven Cities of Gold. Bunten was a transsexual, undergoing a sex-change
> operation in November 1992. She designed and directed development of 12
> original computer games for major publishers representing over $8
> million dollars in sales and 16 awards, including Computer Gaming World
> Hall of Fame. Among the games were ten multi-player games and 3 online
> games. Also included was the first modem-to-modem game by a major
> publisher (Modem Wars, Electronic Arts) and the first 4-player network
> game by a major publisher (Global Conquest, Microprose). Her best known
> titles were: Command HQ (Microprose), Seven Cities of Gold (Electronic
> Arts), and M.U.L.E. (Electronic Arts).

*The Sims 1 really is dedicated to Dani Bunten Berry — it's in the game's
credits. And this stone is the toolmaker's own: Don engraved it himself,
testing his own tombstone generator by carrying the game's dedication
into the fan cemetery — a proper Solemn stone for the pioneering trans
game designer, in a community GamerGate later insisted didn't exist.
Exhibit A for the [roles-not-characters](../roles-not-characters.md)
argument, signed by the sexton.*

<a href="https://web.archive.org/web/2006/http://www.originalsims.com/Objects/Personalized/Simslice-HalloweenTombstone_157654232546.html"><img src="https://web.archive.org/web/20070512220916im_/http://www.originalsims.com/Objects/Personalized/Simslice-HalloweenTombstone_157654232546.bmp" width="220" alt="Mark Weiser's tombstone"></a>

> **Mark Weiser** — On April 27, 1999, at 8:31PM, Dr. Mark Weiser, Chief
> Technologist at the Xerox Palo Alto Research Center, passed away. Mark
> was world-renowned as an innovative computer scientist, with a unique
> vision of the future. He has often been described as the father of
> "ubiquitous computing," a term he coined in 1988 to describe a time when
> invisible computers will be embedded in every day objects. He was also
> an avid drummer, and a member of Severe Tire Damage, the first band to
> ever perform on the Internet.

*The father of ubiquitous computing, memorialized inside a game about
invisible computers embedded in everyday objects. Somebody on a Sims
fansite understood exactly what they were doing — because it was Don,
testing his own software. Can't a guy test out his own tombstone
generator? He gave the man who made computers disappear a Halloween
stone with ghosts on it, which the drummer of Severe Tire Damage would
surely have preferred anyway.*

<a href="https://web.archive.org/web/2006/http://www.originalsims.com/Objects/Personalized/Simslice-HalloweenTombstone_081287714438.html"><img src="https://web.archive.org/web/20061111143450im_/http://www.originalsims.com/Objects/Personalized/Simslice-HalloweenTombstone_081287714438.bmp" width="220" alt="EA Employees' tombstone"></a>

*The ea_spouse crunch scandal, engraved as a mass grave for EA's

<a href="https://web.archive.org/web/2006/http://www.originalsims.com/Objects/Personalized/Simslice-HalloweenTombstone_551792839846.html"><img src="https://web.archive.org/web/20061111144224im_/http://www.originalsims.com/Objects/Personalized/Simslice-HalloweenTombstone_551792839846.bmp" width="220" alt="lost numbers tombstone"></a>

> **lost numbers** — 4 8 15 16 23 42 35 46 53 75 6 3 1 9 86 46 35 46 78

*The Numbers from LOST, engraved mid-broadcast, padded with extras for

## Wing IV — The coroner of Elm Street

Someone engraved the entire *Nightmare on Elm Street* filmography as a
series of deadpan coroner's reports — Tina Grey, Rod Lane, Nancy
Thompson, Coach Schneider, the Thompsons, a dozen more — each death
described by a medical examiner who cannot see Freddy Krueger.
Found-footage fan fiction, distributed across tombstones, years before
"ARG" was a household word. Selected files:

> victim's body, according to witnesses, and he began to bleed vigorously.
> Cause of death: massive hemorrhaging. Holes were of different sizes at
> top and bottom, suggesting the types of wounds found when a body is
> dropped onto spikes. In this case, however, the spikes seemed invisible.
