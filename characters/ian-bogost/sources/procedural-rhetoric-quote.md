# "An unholy blend of Will Wright and Aristotle" — the citation chain

*Where "procedural rhetoric" comes from and where the famous line was
actually said. Machine-readable source data:
[procedural-rhetoric-quote.yml](procedural-rhetoric-quote.yml).*

## The quote

> I developed my own design philosophy that I called procedural
> rhetoric, an unholy blend of Will Wright and Aristotle.

— Ian Bogost,
["Video Games Are Better Without Characters"](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2015/03/video-games-are-better-without-characters/387556/),
The Atlantic, March 16, 2015.

**Get the attribution right:** the exact Wright-plus-Aristotle phrasing
is from the 2015 Atlantic essay, not the 2007 book jacket. The book
defines the term; the Atlantic line is Bogost's memorable
self-summary. Will Wright's SimCity lineage (and Bogost's own
Persuasive Games studio work on politics, nutrition, and immigration)
is the practical half of the blend; Aristotle supplies the rhetorical
tradition of persuasion toward correct judgment.

## Primary sources

- **The book:**
  [*Persuasive Games: The Expressive Power of Videogames*](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262026147/persuasive-games/)
  (MIT Press, 2007, ISBN 978-0262026147) — the first systematic coinage
  and definition: argument through processes and rule authorship.
- **The essay chapter:** "The Rhetoric of Video Games," in *The Ecology
  of Games: Connecting Youth, Games, and Learning* (ed. Katie Salen,
  MIT Press, 2008, pp. 117–140;
  [PDF mirror](https://se4n.org/papers/bogost-rhetoric.pdf)) — defines
  procedural rhetoric alongside verbal and visual rhetoric, with the
  Spore astrobiology example.
- [Procedural rhetoric on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procedural_rhetoric)

## Related theory

- **Gonzalo Frasca**, "Simulation versus Narrative: Introduction to
  Ludology" (2003) — the precursor dialogue; Frasca called it
  *simulation rhetoric*.
- **[Janet Murray](../../janet-murray/README.md)**, *Hamlet on the
  Holodeck* — agency, immersion, transformation. Murray is the
  storytelling half; Bogost is argument in rules. The pairing carries
  the QGCon paper's
  [theory spine](../../don-hopkins/sources/qgcon-inclusivity-paper/procedural-rhetoric-theory.md).
