# Ian Bogost — current affiliation and published books

Sourced record for the portrayal. Checked **2026-09-06** against Bogost's own pages, because this
room previously said "Georgia Tech," which has been out of date since 2021.

**Primary sources:** [bogost.com/about](http://www.bogost.com/about/) ·
[CV (PDF)](http://bogost.com/downloads/I.%20Bogost%20CV.pdf) — both self-published by the subject.

## Affiliation

**Washington University in St. Louis**, since **2021**.

- **Barbara and David Thomas Distinguished Professor** (title held since 2022)
- Professor of **Film & Media Studies** — School of Arts & Sciences
- Professor of **Computer Science and Engineering** — McKelvey School of Engineering
- Professor of **Design and Visual Arts** — Sam Fox School of Design & Visual Arts
- **Co-Executive Director, Office of Public Scholarship** (Provost's Office)
- Provost's Faculty Fellow for Interdisciplinary Initiatives; also described as Assistant Vice Provost

Appointed in **three colleges** — the arrangement is itself the point, for a scholar whose subject is
the seam between engineering and criticism. Chaired the Film & Media Studies program 2021–2024.

*Not* appointed in theatre, despite some third-party listings.

### Previously

**Georgia Institute of Technology** — **Ivan Allen College of Liberal Arts Distinguished Chair in
Media Studies**, with a joint professorship in the School of Literature, Media & Communication and in
Interactive Computing (College of Computing). Also Adjunct Professor (not in residence), Centre for
Digital Humanities, Brock University, 2019–2024.

### Outside the university

- **Founding Partner, Persuasive Games LLC** — game studio and design consultancy
- **Contributing writer / Contributing Editor, The Atlantic**
- Before academia: technology industry, advertising, and Hollywood — including Hans Zimmer's media
  group

### Degrees

Ph.D. **Comparative Literature**, UCLA, 2004 · M.A. Comparative Literature, UCLA, 2001 · B.A.
Philosophy & Comparative Literature, USC, 1998.

Worth noting for this room: procedural rhetoric comes out of a **comparative literature** doctorate,
not a computer science one. The Aristotle half of "an unholy blend of Will Wright and Aristotle" is
the trained half.

## Series he edits

- **Platform Studies** (MIT Press) — series editor with **Nick Montfort**. The series that reads a
  design off its substrate: the Atari VCS, the Amiga, the NES, the BBC Micro, Minitel, Flash, the Wii.
  Directly the method this repo applies to NeWS, PostScript and SimAntics — and
  [Montfort has his own room here](../../nick-montfort/README.md).
- **Object Lessons** (Bloomsbury, 2013– ) — co-edited with Christopher Schaberg. Short books, each
  about **one ordinary object**: barcode, sewer, swimming pool, air conditioning, bicycle, pencil.
  A whole series built on the premise that an object rewards sustained attention, which is not far
  from what a Sims object's [`Motive.c`](../../will-wright/sources/2008-02-10-soul-of-the-sims/Motive.c)
  is doing from the inside.

## Games

*Cow Clicker* (2010) and *A Slow Year* (2010) among them; work held in permanent collections
including the **Smithsonian American Art Museum**.

Also on record: an encyclopedia entry **"Procedural Rhetoric,"** with Simon Ferrari, in Mark J.P.
Wolf's *Encyclopedia of Video Games* (Greenwood, 2012) — the compact definitional statement, distinct
from the *Persuasive Games* book-length argument.
