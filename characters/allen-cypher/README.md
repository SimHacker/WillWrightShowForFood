## Who he is

Allen Cypher studied mathematics at Princeton and took his PhD in computer science at Yale in 1980.
He was a cognitive scientist in Don Norman's group at UC San Diego, where he wrote "The Structure
of Users' Activities" for *User Centered System Design* (1986), then worked on expert systems at
IntelliCorp before joining Apple in 1988.

At Apple he built [Eager](http://acypher.com/Eager/) (CHI '91), one of the first intelligent agents.
Eager watched what you did in HyperCard, and when it noticed you repeating yourself it showed what
it expected you to do next by turning that menu item, button or text green. If it guessed right,
the thing you wanted was already highlighted. If it guessed wrong, your actual choice corrected it.
Only when you clicked its icon did it take over and finish the task. The icon began as a man in a
chair who clapped; testers didn't see what he had to do with repetitive tasks, so it became a cat,
drawn with its paw on the mouse when it takes over.

In 1993 he edited [*Watch What I Do: Programming by Demonstration*](http://acypher.com/wwid/)
(MIT Press), with Daniel Halbert, David Kurlander, Henry Lieberman, David Maulsby, Brad Myers and
Alan Turransky, and a foreword by Alan Kay. It collected the field's systems in one book, and he
has kept the full text online since.

With David Canfield Smith he created KidSim, in which children program agents by showing them
before-and-after pictures instead of writing code. It became Cocoa in 1994 and, after Apple's
Advanced Technology Group was closed, Stagecast Creator, from Stagecast Software, which Cypher,
Smith and Larry Tesler founded in 1997. At IBM (2002-14) he invented CoScripter, which records
what you do on the web as readable steps that others can share and rerun, and he co-edited
*No Code Required: Giving Users Tools to Transform the Web* (2010).

His [home page](http://acypher.com/) says that with large language models his dream of end-user
programming has come true.

## Why he's here

Eager shows the system's guess before it acts and leaves the action to the user, which is the
combination the 1997 Shneiderman–Maes debate argued about. Don's
[essay on that debate](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/designs/AGENTS-DEBATE-1997.md)
treats Eager as the answer nobody named. Richard Potter's "Triggers" chapter in *Watch What I Do*
is one of the sources of [Screen Angel](https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore/blob/main/apps/screen-angel/SCREEN-ANGEL.yml).
