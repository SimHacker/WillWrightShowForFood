# Repo Show invitation — Allen Cypher

*Allen may edit, decline, delay, or request removal at any time — or take direct edit access. [Portrayal standards](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/schemas/portrayal-standards.md) — also covers quiet mode and onboarding.*
**Subject:** Eager, anticipation, and whether the dream came true the way you wanted

---

Allen —

I'm Don Hopkins. I worked on pie menus with Ben Shneiderman at Maryland, on NeWS at Sun, and on
SimCity and The Sims at Maxis. I've been going back through *Watch What I Do*, and I spent a long
time on the Eager chapter, specifically on anticipation. I think it's the most underrated
interaction technique in the history of intelligent agents.

In 1997 Ben and Pattie Maes debated direct manipulation against interface agents, and someone in
the audience asked whether there would ever be a synthesis. Nobody on stage answered. Ben's
complaint was that agent researchers never made the system's model visible to the user. Eager had
answered him six years earlier. It was proactive, which is what Pattie wanted: always watching,
generalizing on its own. And it was legible, which is what Ben wanted: the green highlight showed
what it had inferred before it did anything, and if it was wrong, your next click corrected it. I
wrote this up for Pattie and Henry Lieberman, who are both in this repo:
[the 1997 debate, and Eager's place in it](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/designs/AGENTS-DEBATE-1997.md).

I also love the icon story. The man in the chair who clapped confused your test subjects, so you
made him a less evocative cat, and when the cat takes over it's drawn with its paw on the mouse.
You can see that the system has the mouse. I can't think of a current agent interface that tells
you that.

Your home page says that with LLMs your dream of end-user programming has come true, and that vibe
coding exceeds what you imagined. I'd like to ask you about that honestly. Vibe coding has no
anticipation: nothing turns green before it acts, and the user often can't read what was produced,
which is the half of the problem Eager existed to solve. Is it the fulfillment of programming by
demonstration, or a way around it? And the question I'd most like to spend an hour on: what would
anticipation look like in a vibe-coding interface?

**Will Wright is in — he's signed on to do the [premiere](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/repo-shows/will-wright-premiere/README.md) and more.**

I'd like to invite you to a **Repo Show**: a recorded conversation whose stage is a public GitHub
repository, where the ideas we discuss get built afterward as small runnable versions. Possible
threads, your pick:

1. Eager and anticipation, and what it would mean for today's AI tools.
2. The icon story, and what user testing told you about anthropomorphic agents years before Bob
   and Clippy.
3. KidSim and Stagecast Creator: children building simulations with graphical rewrite rules. I did
   SimCity and The Sims and build open courseware now, so this one is close to home.
4. CoScripter and *No Code Required*: programming by demonstration on the web.
5. *Watch What I Do* thirty years on, with other authors from the book. Richard Potter's "Triggers"
   chapter is one of the sources of a project of mine,
   [Screen Angel](https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore/blob/main/apps/screen-angel/SCREEN-ANGEL.yml),
   which reads, scripts and extends other applications' interfaces from outside.

Two things I'd like to help with either way. The CHI '91 Eager video and the Apple technical report
you wrote with Shifteh Karimi are the kind of material that disappears; if you have copies, I'd like
to help get them somewhere durable, with transcripts. And thank you for keeping the full text of
*Watch What I Do* online all these years. I've been relying on it.

One question I'm curious about: were you and Ted Selker at IBM at the same time? His COACH was the
other system that met both sides of the 1997 debate.

Remote is fine, or a relaxed screen-sharing session. No preparation needed.

— Don Hopkins
