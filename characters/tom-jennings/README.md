## Who he is

Tom Jennings wrote Fido, a bulletin board system, in 1984, and FidoNet, which let BBSes pass mail
and messages to each other over phone lines at night. FidoNet grew into an international network of
thousands of boards, run by their owners, years before most people could get on the Internet. Jason
Scott's
*BBS: The Documentary* tells its story in Tom's words.

In September 1992 he took over running The Little Garden, a shared Internet connection that John
Gilmore, John Romkey and Steve Crocker's Trusted Information Systems had started, which Tim Pozar
had extended to friends by modem; it was named after the Palo Alto restaurant where they met. Tom
built it into TLGnet, Inc., one of the first Internet providers that would sell a connection to
anyone, allowed resale, and put in its terms that it "exercises no control whatsoever over the
content of the information passing through TLGnet." It ran from his bedroom, then from 16th Street
in San Francisco, and hired from local punk and queer communities, training and paying people as
they learned ([his history of TLG](https://www.sensitiveresearch.com/Archive/TLG/TLG.html)). Wired
magazine's first connection to the Internet went through TLG.

He also worked on the portable BIOS at Phoenix Technologies, published the queer punk zine
*Homocore*, and writes and builds: a novel, *The Delivery*; essays on living with craft and
technology, "wabi tek sabi"; and embedded code and objects. His archive is
[Sensitive Research](https://www.sensitiveresearch.com/); his current work is at BLEEVE.ME.

## Why he's here

He replied to Don in April 2020 about a Wired ad for TLG, in the week of John Perry Barlow's
memorial ([digest](sources/2020-04-11-wired-tlg-ad-reply.md)). FidoNet and TLG are part of the history
this project is about: networks built by the people who used them, before the companies arrived. He
appears in Brewster Kahle and Tim Pozar's 1996 Computer History Museum interview
([digest](../brewster-kahle/sources/chm-interview-1996.md)).
