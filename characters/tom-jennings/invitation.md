# Repo Show invitation — Tom Jennings

*Tom may edit, decline, delay, or request removal at any time — or take direct edit access. [Portrayal standards](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/schemas/portrayal-standards.md) — also covers quiet mode and onboarding.*
**Subject:** FidoNet, TLG, and a stage that works like a BBS

---

Tom —

Last time we wrote, in April 2020, I'd sent you the Wired ad for TLG, and you pointed out it was
from year two, and that Wired started more or less before the Internet. I've been working on
something you might like, because it borrows from what you built.

I'm producing a show with Will Wright called **Will Wright Show For Food**, as a **Repo Show**: a
recorded conversation whose stage is a public git repository. The guests, the shows and what gets
built afterward all live in the tree; the audience asks questions as issues and pull requests,
asynchronously, and everything stays, with its history. It's closer to a BBS echo than to a podcast:
store and forward, every copy complete, nobody's server in charge.

The other thing I've been doing is old machines. David Rosenthal's story of the PDP-7 he used at
Cambridge led me to build one that runs in a web page, with its Teletype and paper tape and vector
display, and to restore a 1969 graphics program from its printed listing
([the cabinet](https://hyperties.org/cabinet/) ·
[a 23-minute tour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lo8kdY-5i6c)). Your writing about obsolete
technology and the social world it came from is the same interest from a different direction.

**Will Wright is in — he's signed on to do the [premiere](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/repo-shows/will-wright-premiere/README.md) and more.**

I'd love to record a conversation with you, on whatever you'd like. Possible threads:

1. FidoNet: how a network with no owner organized itself, and what that says to people building
   federated systems now.
2. TLG: the terms that said you control no content, resale welcome, and hiring and training people
   from your own communities.
3. Making things: *The Delivery*, "wabi tek sabi," and your embedded work.
4. With Jason Scott, whose *BBS: The Documentary* told FidoNet's story, if you'd both like that.

Remote is fine. No preparation needed.

— Don Hopkins
