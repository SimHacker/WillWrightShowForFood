## Who he is

Blair MacIntyre started working on augmented reality in 1991, in Steven Feiner's group at Columbia,
on KARMA, which overlaid maintenance instructions on a laser printer through a see-through display.
With Feiner and others he wrote
["Windows on the World: 2D Windows for 3D Augmented Reality"](https://graphics.cs.columbia.edu/projects/wotw/wotw.html)
(UIST 1993), which placed ordinary X11 windows into the space around the user; it later received
the UIST Lasting Impact Award.

At Georgia Tech from 1999 he founded and directed the Augmented Environments Lab, built the Argon AR
browser, which treated AR content as web content, and directed the Qualcomm Augmented Reality Game
Studio. At Mozilla (2016-20) he worked on WebXR, bringing AR and VR to the open web, and co-chaired
IEEE VR 2020 when it moved online into Mozilla Hubs. He then led immersive technology research at
JPMorgan Chase, and is a professor at Northeastern University
([home page](https://blairmacintyre.me/)).

## Why he's here

When Ben Shneiderman forwarded Don's aQuery proposal around 2013, Blair replied with the objection
that shaped the design: interfaces built by drilling down to specific objects break when the
application updates, so bind to patterns, and build libraries of capabilities with a layer for each
application. He also saw that the same layer could serve AR and speech, not only HTML.
[Screen Angel](https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore/blob/main/apps/screen-angel/SCREEN-ANGEL.yml)
credits both by name.
