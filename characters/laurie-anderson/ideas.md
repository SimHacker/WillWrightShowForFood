# Ideas to explore with Laurie Anderson 🎻🗣️

*Conversation hooks for a dream Repo Show — **Don's proposed topics**, each grounded in Laurie's own
publicly documented work. Things Don would love to riff on **with** her; **not** quotes from Laurie and
**not** claims about what she thinks.* [Portrayal standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md) ·
dream guest

## The hooks

### 1. Invented instruments as performance-hacks
The **tape-bow violin** — bowing recorded tape across a playback head — is a perfect Repo Show object:
a cheap, legible hack that turns *playback into an instrument*, seams showing. Sibling to Don's
**pie-menu-as-instrument** and the show's vocal-tract toys (**Pink Trombone**, **Phoneloper** —
[`../../schemas/language-cow.yml`](../../schemas/language-cow.yml)).

**Pink Trombone — built for Laurie.** [Neil Thapen's Pink Trombone](https://dood.al/pinktrombone) is a
browser vocal-tract simulator: drag a tongue, shape the tract, a voice comes out. No claim she knows it
yet — but it's *her* register: the body exposed as instrument, seams showing, audience can jam along from
chat ([`pink-trombone-jam`](../../apps/performance-space/pink-trombone-jam.md)). Sibling to the
**Voice of Authority** (machine shifts the voice *after* the tract; Pink Trombone makes the tract
*visible before* the shift) and to Nina Hagen's "she **is** the instrument" vocal work. A live segment:
Laurie + Don duet on Pink Trombone; or rebuild the tape-bow scrub-by-gesture idea in the browser and
let the audience fork it.

**The Interval bridge — the Talking Stick.** Anderson built the **Talking Stick** (a ~6-foot MIDI baton
that granularizes and replays audio in real time) with **Bob Bielecki and Interval Research** (c. 1999)
— **the same lab where Don worked**, so this is a *shared-history* door, not just a fan's admiration.
In her own words: wireless MIDI, **Max**, granular synthesis, harpoon-shaped, mostly speed/volume control
— after earlier work with [STEIM](https://steim.org/) on an "invisible instrument" (1992). Primary sources:
[Miya Masaoka, *SF Bay Guardian*, Oct 1999](https://miyamasaoka.com/writings-by-miya-masaoka/1999/examining-obsession/) ·
[Laurie's *Moby Dick* program notes, ©1999](https://www.jimdavies.org/laurie-anderson/performances/mobydick/programnotes.html)
(*"a new wireless instrument that can access and replicate any sound… a physical representation of the
disembodied voice… an extremely physical and digital descendant of turntables"*).
It's also literally a **resampling** instrument: chop audio into grains, replay them any which way —
the show's [**riff loop**](../../bits/theme-the-riff-loop/theme-the-riff-loop.md) in the palm of a hand.

**Don's granular-CA companion piece — "Musical Gas."** Don built a **granular cellular-automata
synthesizer**: colliding gas-lattice CA; each collision fires a grain. A **voice** = 16 samples (scat,
farts, laughs — e.g. David Levitt); **pitch** = a number + 16-note index. **Frozen particles** = mics;
**moving particles** = voice banks — hit a mic, play that note from the mover's voice. Two people
braided into every grain. Same granular instinct as the Talking Stick — great with **DLA** too. Web
reimplementation target: [`../don-hopkins/musical-gas-granular-ca-synth.md`](../don-hopkins/musical-gas-granular-ca-synth.md).

### 2. The "Voice of Authority" as a voice puppet
Laurie's pitch-shifted male persona is a **voice puppet** — hiding the performer to speak *as* a
character — decades before the show's own puppet taxonomy
([`../../repo-shows/will-wright-premiere/performance-and-culture.yml`](../../repo-shows/will-wright-premiere/performance-and-culture.yml)
`#puppet-kinds`). A natural thread on **voice, persona, and authority** — who gets to speak, and in
whose voice, when a machine can shift yours.

### 3. Spoken-word storytelling, live, in a repo
*United States I–IV* is storytelling as architecture. The Repo Show is storytelling that **commits to
git** as it's told. A conversation about narrative that is **live, forkable, and time-shifted** — and
what changes when the audience can edit the story.

### 4. Generative systems on stage (the Eno bridge)
The same conversation the show wants with **Brian Eno** — rules, time, surrender, and what AI changes —
runs straight through Laurie's work. A dream **three-way** (Laurie / Brian / Don) on **generative
performance**: systems you set running and then *play against*, live.

### 5. VR / immersive worlds as repos
*Chalkroom* and *Aloft* build inhabitable story-spaces. Where does an **immersive world** meet a
**repo you can walk into and edit** (the MicropolisCore/venue direction)? What persists, what's
authored, what's grown.

### 6. Songs from the Bardo — the illusory city
On ***Songs from the Bardo*** (2019, with **Tenzin Choegyal** and **Jesse Paris Smith**) — a guided
meditation through the **Tibetan Book of the Dead** — the track **"Moon in the Water"** recites the
classic catalog of illusions: *"like dreams, like illusions, like echoes, like **cities of
Gandharvas**, like mirages… like the moon and water."* The **Gandharva city** is Sanskrit's ancient
term for a **mirage city** — an illusory metropolis hanging in the air. For a show whose stage is a
**city simulator**, that's not decoration, it's thesis: every Micropolis is a Gandharva city — a
city that is *only* image, conjured and dissolved at will, real in its effects on the mind of the
player. Laurie spent an album in the bardo, the **between-state**; the show lives in one too —
between code and performance, human and AI, live and committed-to-git. A conversation about
**illusory cities, between-states, and what simulations do to the minds that inhabit them** —
Buddhism and city simulators on one stage.

### 7. Art vs. censorship
Laurie was a clear voice in the **NEA culture wars**. Ties directly to the show's
**getting-away-with-it** lineage (Bowie's video puppet slipping past the NBC censors) and its
[`unnecessary-censorship`](../../bits/tradition-unnecessary-censorship/tradition-unnecessary-censorship.yml) tradition —
voluntary comedy, never timid euphemism; we don't bowdlerize real names.

### 8. STEIM, Max/MSP, and the NATO/Jitter scene
Laurie worked at **[STEIM](https://steim.org/)** on an **"invisible instrument"** (1992) — years
before she built the **Talking Stick** with Interval (1999, **Max**, granular synthesis). The same
Amsterdam institution later hosted **Image/ine** (Tom Demeyer + Steina Vasulka, 1996–2001) and, from
2001, **Netochka Nezvanova** as **Artistic Co-Director** (*Director of Leaves and Petals* —
[IMA bio](https://archiv.ima.or.at/en/rebekah-wilson-aka-netochka-nezvanova/)). Don used **Image/ine**
for live video performance.

**For a Laurie show — not a paired episode — this is a Max/MSP conversation:**
- Laurie and **NATO.0+55+3d** (1999) both lived inside **Max** — patch-cord programming, performance
  instruments, real-time synthesis. NATO added live QuickTime RTP net jams ("operators" remixing video
  hops); Laurie's Talking Stick added granular resampling via wireless MIDI.
- When Cycling '74 shipped **Jitter** (2002), NN called it a rip-off of NATO's ground. Did Laurie follow
  that fight? What did she think of **Max/MSP** as a stage medium beyond her own instruments?
- **AntiORP / nettime** — licensing as performance, trolling as art, the turn-of-the-millennium net-jam
  scene Don documents in [real-time net jamming](../don-hopkins/realtime-net-jamming-scene.md). Institutional
  overlap at STEIM is verified; personal overlap is Laurie's to confirm if she wants to go there.
- **Image/ine** — Don's hands-on experience; parallel real-time video lineage in the same building.

**Questions to offer Laurie (optional, her pace):**
- STEIM 1992 — what was the "invisible instrument"? Who else was in the building?
- Did you know the **NATO operators** / **AntiORP** era or the **Jitter** controversy from the inside
  of the Max world?
- Granular synthesis on the Talking Stick vs granular *video* on the net — same instinct, different medium?
