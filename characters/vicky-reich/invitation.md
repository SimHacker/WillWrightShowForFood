# Repo Show invitation — Vicky Reich

*Vicky may edit, decline, delay, or request removal at any time — or take direct edit access. [Portrayal standards](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/schemas/portrayal-standards.md) — also covers quiet mode and onboarding.*
**Subject:** Mark's kittens, still online, and a conversation about keeping things

---

Vicky —

While reading David's 2016 talk on emulation I found Mark's page for Nijinsky and Pavlova again, the
one he put up in January 1995 with the puzzle about which kitten is which. The Wayback Machine still
has it, photos and all, and I've kept a copy with Mark's memorial in this repository, alongside the
SDI game he wrote in 1987 with its blizzard of pie menus:
[Nijinsky and Pavlova](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/mark-weiser/media/ubiq-kittens-1995/README.md) ·
[Mark's memorial](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/mark-weiser/README.md).
If you'd rather any of it be different, or not there, tell me and it will be.

I've also been working on preservation, from the other end. David's story of the Cambridge PDP-7 led
me to build an emulator of it that runs in a web page and to restore Heinz Lemke's 1969 PIXIE from its
printed listing ([the cabinet](https://hyperties.org/cabinet/)). I wrote up what David's 2015 report
for the Mellon Foundation means for it, and the answer keeps coming back to LOCKSS's idea: many
copies, kept by the people who care about them
([PRESERVATION.md](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/packages/cabinet/PRESERVATION.md)).

**Will Wright is in — he's signed on to do the [premiere](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/repo-shows/will-wright-premiere/README.md) and more.**

If you'd enjoy it, I'd like to invite you to a **Repo Show**, a recorded conversation whose stage is a
public GitHub repository, on whatever you'd like to talk about. Possible threads:

1. LOCKSS and CLOCKSS: how libraries came to keep their own copies of the web's journals, and what
   twenty-five years of it taught you.
2. HighWire Press and the move of scientific journals to the web in the 1990s.
3. Libraries as more than information: your 1994 paper with Mark, read thirty years on.
4. Remembering Mark, if and how you'd like to, perhaps with David, Ben Shneiderman and me. That is
   entirely your call.

Remote is fine. No preparation needed.

— Don Hopkins
