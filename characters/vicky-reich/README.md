## Who she is

Vicky (Victoria) Reich is a librarian who spent her career on keeping scholarship readable as it
moved from paper to screens. She worked at the Library of Congress as a science reference librarian
and planning librarian, and in the 1980s on one of its first tests of optical disks for storing
printed material. She led acquisitions at the National Agricultural Library and worked at the
University of Michigan before moving to Stanford University Libraries, where she was chief of the
serials and acquisitions departments.

At Stanford she was assistant director of HighWire Press, which from 1995 put scientific and medical
journals on the web for the societies that published them, and was the Libraries' expert on copyright
and fair use. She worked on the Stanford Digital Library Project with Terry Winograd and others. In
1994 she and her husband, Mark Weiser, wrote "Libraries Are More Than Information: Situational
Aspects of Electronic Libraries" ([her 1999 home page, summarized](sources/highwire-home-1999.md)).

In 1999 she and David Rosenthal founded the LOCKSS Program at Stanford Libraries: "Lots of Copies
Keep Stuff Safe," in which libraries each keep their own copies of the journals they subscribe to and
check them against each other, so no single failure loses anything. Their 2000 paper, "Permanent Web
Publishing," credits the Long Now Foundation's clock as one of its inspirations
([paper](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/publications/library/proceedings/usenix2000/freenix/full_papers/rosenthal/rosenthal.pdf)).
She was also a founder of CLOCKSS, the archive that publishers and libraries run together. She and
David stepped away from managing LOCKSS in 2016 ([LOCKSS history](https://www.lockss.org/about/history)).

Her awards include the ALCTS Outstanding Collaboration Citation (2007) for CLOCKSS, the ALCTS/Ulrich's
Serials Librarianship Award (2008), and, with David, the LITA/Library Hi Tech Award (2014) and the
2025 Paul Evan Peters Award from CNI, ARL and EDUCAUSE
([LOCKSS news](https://www.lockss.org/news/and-2025-paul-evan-peters-award-goes)).

## Why she's here

She was married to Mark Weiser, Don's teacher and friend at Maryland and co-author of his first pie
menu paper, until Mark's death in 1999. Mark's 1995 web page for their kittens, Nijinsky and Pavlova,
linked to hers, and is cached in [his memorial](../mark-weiser/media/ubiq-kittens-1995/README.md). And
LOCKSS is the other half of what this project's preservation work is about: keeping many copies, run
by the people who care about them ([PRESERVATION.md](../../packages/cabinet/PRESERVATION.md)).
