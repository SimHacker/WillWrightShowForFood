## What I'd love to talk about (your pick, any subset)

- **Workflows, and what a step should be.** On the desktop a step has to be
  keystrokes and delays, because that's all the OS lets a utility do. Where
  the app on the other end has named commands, a step can be a command that
  answers, and a delay can be a wait for an event. Your new
  `send-websocket-message` action is the door. I'm designing the same model
  into the webtop I'm building
  ([WORKFLOWS.md](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/designs/webtop/WORKFLOWS.md)),
  and I'd love to wire a Kando slice on your desktop to a page in this repo,
  live on the show.
- **The first pie menus, running again.** PIXIE's lightbutton menus from
  Cambridge now run in the browser: Heinz Lemke's 1972 SYMELEC listing,
  transcribed from 128 pages and running on an emulated PDP-7 and Type 340
  with a light pen you drive with the mouse
  ([PIXIE live](https://hyperties.org/databases/pixie/pixie-live/)). You
  can draw a circuit with the ancestor of Kando.
- **A dozen-plus years of iteration.** Trace-Menu's preview nubs and
  roll-back slice, Coral-Menu's tree, Gnome-Pie, Fly-Pie, Kando: what each
  one taught you, what survived, and what you cut. Nobody else has iterated
  on pie menus in public for this long.
- **The center, the keys, and the hover.** You made the center
  programmable and the back key a setting, and added arrow-key walking and
  key overlays. What should the center mean, and what should a hover be
  allowed to do?
- **Keeping it alive.** Free, open source, no telemetry, sponsors,
  translators, and more than ten code contributors in this release. How a
  solo maintainer keeps a cross-platform desktop utility going, and still
  fun, through Wayland and everything else.
- **Possibly an ensemble.** Heinz Lemke (PIXIE, 1969) and Ben Shneiderman
  (the CHI '88 pie menu study) are invited too
  ([pie menu history show](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/repo-shows/ben-and-heinz-pie-menus/README.md)).
  The whole lineage in one room would be something.
