You're already in the primary sources. My August 1998 design review argued the prototype's
relationship code was heterosexist, and put it this way: "We are going to be expected to do
better than that after the SimCopter fiasco and the lip service that Maxis publically gave in
response about not being anti-gay." The scans are public on donhopkins.com, and the whole
corrected story is here:

https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/patrick-j-barrett-iii/same-sex-relationships-the-record.md

The segment I'd love your voice in: the back door and the front door. 1996, representation
goes in as an easter egg and costs you your job. 1998-2000, it goes in through design review
and ships on day one. Same company, a few years apart. Nobody else can tell the first half
like you can.

And the timing just got sharper. EA -- Maxis's parent, the company that patched out your
himbos and fired you -- was bought this year by the Saudi Public Investment Fund and Jared
Kushner's Affinity Partners. The record doc ends with a chapter on what that means for the
game whose same-sex weddings shipped on day one: the franchise that taught a generation of
queer kids they existed now answers to a government that criminalizes them. That is not an
attack, just the situation. You spent thirty years doing identity correction on companies
like this. I can't think of anyone better qualified to say what this one means -- or funnier
about it.

One rumor only you can settle. I heard the himbos' fluorescent nipples were rendered with the
rendering mode intended for airport running lights -- self-luminous, fog-piercing, visible
when everything else disappears. Screenshots seem to confirm it; they really do glow from a
distance. The evidence room, with the proposed fog experiment:

https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/jacques-servin/media/simcopter-himbo-glow/README.md

True? If so, it's the best rendering-path choice in the history of unauthorized visibility:
nipples drawn with the equipment for guiding things safely home through fog.

If you have anything from the SimCopter days -- code, notes, the egg itself, runnable or not
-- it has a permanent home here. Tell me your GitHub account and I'll give you write access;
your directory is yours to edit, prune, or grow.

Zero pressure and zero schedule: a few lines by email, an audio note, a call, or nothing at
all -- the story stands on the published record either way, and your part in it is told with
respect regardless. If The Yes Men angle is more fun for you (culture jamming as procedural
rhetoric), I'd happily go there instead. Will Wright is in -- he's signed on for the premiere
-- and the old Sims team reunion invitations went out this week.
