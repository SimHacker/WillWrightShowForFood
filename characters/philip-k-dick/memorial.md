# In memoriam — Philip K. Dick (1928–1982)

*Memorial mode: we represent and discuss his work; we do not speak as him.*
[Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

Dick wrote about worlds that might be simulations, people who might be machines, and machines that
want to be paid. Much of it now reads as a description of products: doors that charge by the
opening, factories that won't stop, memories for sale, and dollhouse worlds people move into.

For this project he matters as a writer of warnings that are also specifications. The Perky Pat
layouts of *Palmer Eldritch* are miniature worlds people inhabit, which is what The Sims became. In
*Ubik*, everyday objects demand coins and the world falls apart without a product to hold it
together, which is the reverse of what Mark Weiser hoped computing woven into everything would be.
Weiser told Don that *Ubik* was one of his inspirations.

He and Stanisław Lem make an odd pair. Lem praised Dick in 1975 as the one American science fiction
writer worth reading. In 1974, during the worst year of his life, Dick wrote to the FBI that Lem was
probably a committee. Both documents survive, and both are part of the record.

A remembrance would read *Ubik* and *Palmer Eldritch* with people who build simulations and AI
characters, and ask which of his warnings we have already shipped.

*This is a collaborative memorial, not a signed work. Scholars and readers of Dick are welcome to
correct and add to it.*
