## The proposal, as printed

> **Project (Z)epplin Proposal**
> **The Hindenburg**
>
> This proposal is intended as a possible collaborative document between **Maxis and Maris** on the
> development of a new title herein referred to as **"Project Z"**. This title will be the
> authoritative multimedia work on the Hindenburg (depending on the amount of research, this could
> become the definitive graphic treatment in any media).
>
> Imagine a combination of the detailed 3-D environment of **MYST**, the authoritative breadth and
> clarity of a **Dorien Kindersly** book and a complete flight and systems simulation of the
> Hindenburg. Imagine being able to **walk anywhere on the ship**; through the luxurious passenger
> sections, into the galley or radio room, up into the catwalks inside the ship, and from there to the
> cockpit or the engine rooms.
>
> Not only can you walk anywhere onboard but you also can **operate most of the machinery**. The
> flight systems, radios, kitchen equipment, lights, heaters, engine trim, gas valves, water ballast
> and more are all under your control and are simulated as accurately as possible. **You can even tap
> out a tune on the custom aluminum piano in the lounge.**
>
> As it turns out this isn't just a convenience but vital to the game side of the product. For you
> start out **alone onboard the ship** [at] 5,000 feet over the Atlantic. **The year is 1937**, and if
> you happen to find the flight plan you will notice the destination is Lakehurst, New Jersey. The
> cause of the fateful explosion that destroyed the Hindenburg that year **was never determined.
> Unless you can discover the secret you will be doomed to relive the experience** o[ver and over…]
> about an hour after you start[…]

Bleeding through from the next sheet, a section headed **"The Interface"** — a cinematic opening on
the ship's exterior, animated propellers with "a low drone" heard from the engines, "2 large icons"
and a row of ten smaller ones along the bottom of the screen showing the layout of the ship.

## What it actually is

**A one-hour time loop aboard a doomed vessel, where the only escape is solving the historical
mystery of its destruction.** You wake alone at 5,000 feet, everything is operable, the ship blows up
in about an hour, and it keeps happening until you work out why.

That is, structurally, ***Outer Wilds*** (2019) and ***Return of the Obra Dinn*** (2018), proposed at
Maxis in the mid-1990s. The time loop as an epistemic device — repetition as the mechanism by which
the player accumulates knowledge that the *character* doesn't keep — is the design idea both of those
games are celebrated for inventing. Here it's a paragraph in a partnership pitch, and the pitch's own
framing for it is modest: the systems simulation *"isn't just a convenience but vital to the game
side of the product."* The proposal treats its best idea as the justification for its feature list.

The other half is *"the authoritative multimedia work on the Hindenburg"* — a **Dorling Kindersley
cutaway book you can walk around inside**, aimed at being *"the definitive graphic treatment in any
media."* That's the Maxis edutainment instinct of the era (SimEarth's manual, SimAnt's ant biology,
the [SimSafari / SimHealth](../../catalogs/maxis/README.md) line) applied to a single machine, at
1:1, with every valve wired up. The **custom aluminum piano in the lounge** is a real Hindenburg
fixture and the detail that gives the whole document away: someone had done the research and wanted
you to be able to play it.

**Maris** is Maris Multimedia, the CD-ROM publisher of *RedShift* — a plausible partner for
"authoritative multimedia," and the reason this is a *collaborative document* rather than a Maxis
design doc.

The parenthesis in the title — **"(Z)epplin"** — is doing double duty: the codename is "Project Z",
and the spelling is wrong. So is *"Dorien Kindersly."* This is a first draft nobody proofread, which
is what makes it a good primary source.

Two of these are load-bearing.

**Jim Mackraz's "Save the Nazi Airship…"** identifies the pitch's unsolved problem in four words.
The *Hindenburg* flew swastikas on its tail fins for the Reich, and the proposal's win condition is
**preventing its destruction** — a Maxis "save the thing" game whose thing is a Nazi propaganda
vehicle. The document never mentions this. Any revival has to answer it, and the good answer is
probably the one *Obra Dinn* found: the goal is to **know**, not to save.

**Sean Baity's "70%-20%-10%"** is Maxis's portfolio rule — roughly, 70% proven, 20% better, 10% new
— and he's pointing out that a Myst-Hindenburg-time-loop is 100% in the 10% bucket. That is, in one
comment, a plausible account of why this never shipped, and it rhymes exactly with
[Tycho Rising](../mike-perry/lunasim-tycho-rising.md): the studio's stated identity was open-ended
software toys, and both of these were something else.
