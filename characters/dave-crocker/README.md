## Who

**David H. Crocker** (born 6 August 1949, Santa Monica) is an American network engineer whose work
on **networked email** began in the early 1970s on the **ARPANET** — while he was still an
undergraduate at UCLA. His brother **Steve Crocker** introduced him to the project: Steve created
the ARPA Network Working Group and wrote **RFC 1** in 1969, seeding the Request for Comments series
that would become the Internet's standards literature. By 1974 — a year before Dave finished his
B.A. — he was already named on **at least nineteen RFCs**, most of them on email or Telnet.
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Crocker_(engineer)))

Dave earned a **B.A. in Psychology** from UCLA (1975), then researched at **RAND Corporation**
while pursuing graduate work. There he designed the **"MS" Personal Messaging System** (1977). He
took an **M.A. in Communication** from USC Annenberg (1977). At the **University of Delaware**
(1978–1982, co–principal investigator) he built **MMDF** — the Multi-purpose Memo Distribution
Facility, a message-transfer agent that became a telephone-based ARPANET gateway for CSNET, a
forerunner of NSFNET. He was **first listed author of RFC 733** (1977), the first standard format
for ARPANET text messages, and **author of RFC 822** (1982), which remains the core syntax reference
for Internet mail. A mid-1970s ARPANET email message and a message sent today share almost the same
technical core — a continuity Dave has documented publicly at
[emailhistory.org](https://emailhistory.org/) and on
[dcrocker.net](https://www.dcrocker.net/).

After Delaware he was co-architect and Director of System Development at **MCI Digital Information
Services**, building **MCI Mail** with **Vint Cerf** into a national email service (1983–1985). He
held engineering leadership roles at Ungermann-Bass, The Wollongong Group, and Digital Equipment
Corporation, served as an original **IETF Area Director** (1989–1995), and in 1991 founded
**Brandenburg InternetWorking**, where he remains principal. The [IEEE Internet Award
(2004)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Crocker_(engineer)), shared with **Ray Tomlinson**,
recognized their work on the conceptualization, first implementation, and standardization of
networked email. He has authored **45+ RFCs** and contributed to MIME, Internet fax, EDI, DNS,
and transport standards. ([ETHW](https://ethw.org/David_Crocker))

**What makes him rare for an Engelbart tribute:** he is not only adjacent to augmentation history —
he **used NLS**. Visiting his brother at ARPA, he learned Doug Engelbart's system on the **text
command interface** (not the mouse). ARC monitored access and **invited him to a class in Menlo
Park** on the mouse-and-keypad system. He remembers the team's tone as profoundly collaborative — a
version of *"Come on in, the water's fine."* He was fascinated by NLS's **paragraph-oriented model**
and emulated it as a **PL/1 programming exercise** to build a Counselor's Manual for a **student
crisis hotline** he ran. That is augmentation as student-run practice, not demo mythology.

His public site places the **Mother of All Demos** (1968) beside Licklider's *Man-Computer
Symbiosis* and *The Computer as a Communication Device* — the intent frame, not the gadget list. He
attended **Ted Nelson's Engelbart tribute** and, in a July 2026 Facebook thread with Don Hopkins,
offered an honest contrast: Ted's performance felt at odds with Doug's style and substance.
