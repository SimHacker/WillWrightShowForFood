## Washington Hilton — "Where in the room?" (Fall 1972)

Second anecdote Dave added (same thread): hired into the **UCLA ARPANET** project just before the
first public demonstration in D.C. (Fall 1972) — documentation and user support; scrambling around
the ballroom where terminals surrounded the TIP (56 Kbps lines).

Needed help from Engelbart's team late evening. System status showed no one he knew online —
only **Doug** (PI), whom he'd never met. Lowly drone vs Principal Investigator. Experience with
the group said they were helpful collaborators, so he connected via **text chat**, explained where
he was, asked for help.

Doug asked *where* he was. Dave repeated (ballroom, Washington Hilton, ARPANET demo…). Doug asked
again: **where in the room?**

Doug was sitting across from him. And yes — he helped with the question.

**On interfaces (reply to Dirk van Nouhuys):**

> Given the joys of learning the sequential alphabet encoding of the keypad, I'm not sure I agree
> [that mouse+screen was easier than text], in terms of just the surface user interface. The more
> interesting challenge, for both, was learning the underlying data and association models.

> Having used 'classic' text formatting tools, I was fascinated by the **'paragraph' oriented model
> of NLS**. And even used emulating it as a programming exercise for learning **PL/1**, using it to
> create a **Counselor's Manual** for the student crisis hotline I ran.
