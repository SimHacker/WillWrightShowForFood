# Grig Bilham — conversation hooks

*Menu, not homework. Pick any, skip the rest.*

## 1. Circular menus in the open metaverse

You were building a circular menu and thought of Don's study — so let's finish the
thought on air. Pie menu ergonomics (distance/direction, muscle memory, the Fitts's law
study) meet spatial standards: what should Web of Worlds say about radial interaction in
3D, where every direction is real?

## 2. The Thousand-Year Repo — two preservation religions

EverArchive: 200–1000 year storage, self-sovereign publishing. Repo Shows: preservation
by RUN — Temkin's OL 5.0, the PIXIE PDP-7 code from 1967 heading for an emulator. Storage
without runnability is a museum; runnability without storage is a mayfly. What does the
thousand-year *executable* archive look like?

## 3. Web of Worlds × Mesa × MOOLLM rooms — where does shared context live?

Three bets on the same question: your W3C-track spatial fabric standard, Temkin's spatial
canvas you share with an AI, MOOLLM's directories-as-rooms that humans and LLMs stand in
together. Interop between *worlds* is the new interop between *documents* — what did the
web get right the first time, and what must not be repeated?

## 4. The Laszlo alum arc — before-its-time as a career

OpenLaszlo was the RIA before its time; the open metaverse may be the metaverse before
its time. What does a Laszlo alum know about surviving being early that the standards
bodies need to hear?

## 5. Atomic swaps for culture — Chia, offer files, no intermediary

on standards, and the Internet Archive in the loop. Can creators' economics be
infrastructure instead of platform policy? (Universal Manifest as the missing
permissions layer.)

## 6. Forbes Island — preservation as community

Amsterdam (Don's backyard). Preservation isn't a vault, it's a scene. How do you keep a
