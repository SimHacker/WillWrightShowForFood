# Tuned Emergence & Design by Accretion — Chris Trottier (2004) 🪶

*Armchair Empire interview · summarized for the Repo Show · full republish below*

Chris Trottier — designer on **The Sims** and **The Sims Online** — named two Maxis design approaches that explain why giant microworlds feel alive only after long accretion and late tuning.

## The two phrases

### Design by accretion

Games like The Sims and SimCity were **designed by accretion**: incrementally assembled from a mass of separate components, like a planet forming from dust orbiting a star. They had to reach **critical mass** before tuning could even begin.

Before proper tuning, The Sims was called **“the toilet game”** — there wasn’t much else to do yet. That wasn’t failure; it was the process.

### Tuned emergence

**Tuned emergence** is what happens after accretion: balance, feel, fun — the renaissance after life finally holds on the surface. SimCity 2000 wasn’t fun until **six weeks before ship**. The Sims’ gameplay didn’t really come together until **a couple of months before ship**.

Chris on being in that tuning process:

> *Being involved in that tuning process, and seeing the game take shape from what had previously been a mass of separate components, was one of the most powerful experiences of my career.*

## Why EA was nervous

Maxis had to convince EA that this approach was normal for them — against every rule in EA’s playbook. It took **Will Wright’s tremendous stamina** to keep The Sims from cancellation: by EA’s formula, it would never work until it suddenly did.

## Chris in her own words (excerpt)

On surprise at The Sims’ popularity:

> *When I was first assigned to The Sims, it was not-very-affectionately-known within the company as "the toilet game." … In all honesty, the gameplay didn't start to really come together until a couple of months before ship.*

On The Sims Online’s live updates:

> *That's my favorite thing about working on The Sims Online: the fact that we'll get to make it better and better as we go.*
