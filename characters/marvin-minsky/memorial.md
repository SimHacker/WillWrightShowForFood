# In memoriam — Marvin Minsky (1927–2016)

*Memorial mode: we represent and discuss his documented work; we do not interview him or speak as
him, and we invent no quotes or inner life.*
[Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

Marvin Minsky helped invent the very idea that a machine could be made to think. With **John
McCarthy** he co-founded the **MIT AI Lab** in 1959, and for decades — often alongside **Seymour
Papert**, with whom he co-directed the lab — he pushed at the hardest question there is: *what is a
mind, and how could you build one?* As a graduate student he wired up **SNARC** (1951), one of the
first neural-network learning machines. He gave the field **frames** and **K-lines** for
representing knowledge and memory, and in **The Society of Mind** (1986) argued that intelligence
emerges from a society of small, mindless agents — a idea that still hums under modern AI (and under
this repo: K-lines are one of MOOLLM's load-bearing concepts).

He was also a *maker* with a sense of humor: he invented the **confocal scanning microscope**, built
early robotic arms, advised Stanley Kubrick on **HAL** for *2001: A Space Odyssey*, co-invented the
**Triadex Muse** music sequencer — and, as a Bell Labs grad student in 1952, dreamed up the
**"Ultimate Machine,"** a box whose only function is to switch itself off (Claude Shannon built the
first; Arthur C. Clarke called it "unspeakably sinister"). It lives on here as a fictional bot
mascot, [The Ultimate Machine](../ultimate-machine/README.md). With Papert he wrote **Perceptrons** (1969). He
won the **Turing Award** in 1969 and was a founding member of the **MIT Media Lab**.

We remember him by inviting the people who knew him to tell the real stories — including his
children, **[Henry](../henry-minsky/README.md)** (who grew up in the AI Lab's children's computer lab and now
carries Society-of-Mind ideas into Leela AI), **[Julie](../julie-minsky/README.md)** (Henry's twin;
Universal Access Inc. co-founder), and **[Margaret](../margaret-minsky/README.md)** (MIT Media Lab);
his doctoral students **[Gary Drescher](../gary-drescher/README.md)** (*Made-Up Minds*) and
**[David Levitt](../david-levitt/README.md)** (AI music, Sc.D. 1985 — part of the lab's jazz-improvisation
scene around Marvin's Bach-fugue keyboard playing); and colleagues and friends shaped by the same
milieu: **[Alan Kay](../alan-kay/README.md)**, **[Ken Kahn](../ken-kahn/README.md)**,
**[Walter Bender](../walter-bender/README.md)**, **[Cynthia Solomon](../cynthia-solomon/README.md)**
(whose YouTube channel preserves rare footage of Marvin), **[Stephen Wolfram](../stephen-wolfram/README.md)**
(*[Farewell, Marvin Minsky](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2016/01/farewell-marvin-minsky-19272016/)*),
and **[Ray Kurzweil](../ray-kurzweil/README.md)** — who wrote to Marvin as a high-school student,
was invited to visit, and went to MIT expressly to study with him.

We celebrate his documented public work; we do not speak as him.

**Lineage:** [Jean Piaget](../jean-piaget/README.md) → [Seymour Papert](../seymour-papert/README.md) → Minsky's AI Lab →
the show's constructionist spine. See the [constructionist-lineage trail](../../process/trails/constructionist-lineage.md).

**The Diespace wing** *(h/t [PIPS:lab's Diespace](../keez-duyves/sources/tedx-amsterdam-2012-diespace.md) —
"the social network for people who already passed away")*: peers with memorial rooms here whose
documented work belongs in these conversations — who would have been invited to memorialize Marvin
were they alive, and whom we memorialize in the same breath: **[Seymour Papert](../seymour-papert/memorial.md)**,
**[Jean Piaget](../jean-piaget/memorial.md)**, and **[Douglas Engelbart](../douglas-engelbart/memorial.md)**,
whose augmentation-vs-AI debate with Marvin ("What are you going to do for the people?") continues
between their memorial rooms. Peers who deserve rooms next: John McCarthy, Claude Shannon, Ed
Fredkin, Push Singh, Patrick Winston, Carl Hewitt. See `CHARACTER.yml` `memorial.in_memoriam_peers`.

*(And note Die Space's fine print: it also accepts the living — the TEDx mass upload interred a
whole live audience. Interring someone before they die is **premorializing**, which is what every
living guest's room here already is: a premorial they get to argue with. See
[keez-duyves ideas hook 15](../keez-duyves/ideas.md). As Don puts it: "Blogs are designed to talk
to the present, and when you stop posting, they die. Premorializations in Die Space are designed
to talk to the future, and when you stop posting, you're there!" ACT UP posted the present-tense
half in 1987: SILENCE = DEATH. The premorial flips the medium and keeps the demand — the living
get the microphone at their own memorial.)*

*This is a collaborative memorial, not a signed work. Those who knew Marvin — family, friends, and colleagues — are warmly invited to edit, correct, and add their own memories, documents, and stories whenever they're ready.*
