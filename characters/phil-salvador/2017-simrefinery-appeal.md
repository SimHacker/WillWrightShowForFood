# The appeal that started the SimRefinery hunt — 25 April 2017

*Source:* [Phil Salvador on Maxis Alumni](media/2017-04-25-simrefinery-appeal-maxis-alumni.png) —
2 reactions (one of them 😢), 8 comments.

**This is the origin document of the SimRefinery recovery.** Three years before the game surfaced,
here is the ask, posted cold to a group of ex-employees by someone who had to explain he didn't
belong there.

> Hello! **Jeff Braun** suggested that I post here. (I'm not a Maxoid…)
>
> If you haven't heard, **John Hiles**, who ran **Maxis Business Simulations from 92-94**, died
> suddenly of **brain cancer in December**. I'm working with **the Hiles family** to archive his
> software, and we need your help.
>
> We're trying to track down a copy of **SimRefinery**. If anyone has a copy – or knows someone who
> might – we'd be in your debt. **I can cover the costs of shipping the game, and I'll make a
> donation to a charitable group of your choice.** Thank you!
>
> (I know Mr. Hiles developed other software with Maxis as well – **SimEnvironment and SimSite**?)

Worth noting how the request is built: a referral from a co-founder to establish standing, an
admission of outsider status, a named family he's working with, a specific artifact, **an offer to
pay shipping**, and **a donation to a charity of the holder's choosing** — no claim on the object
beyond a copy. That is the whole method of the recovery, in one paragraph, before it worked.

Two things this pins down.

**The interview happened first.** Phil talked to Hiles in **2016**, published it, and then Hiles died
in December of that year — so the appeal is being made by someone who had already spent hours with
the subject and knew what was being lost. The line *"died from what he did"* is Phil's: Hiles worked
on **cognition and agent-based modeling**, and died of brain cancer.

**Mike Perry is in this thread too**, asking for the interview link, and named by Jim Siefert as
someone who might have a copy. The same Mike Perry who
[kept his own unreleased game's binder for twenty years](../mike-perry/lunasim-tycho-rising.md) and
whose [press sheet Phil later found in a museum](../mike-perry/lunasim-tycho-rising.md#it-was-announced-the-e3-1997-press-fact-sheet)
without either of them apparently noticing. **They have been circling each other in this group since
at least 2017.** Put them on the same show.

And *"where Will Wright was wrong"* is an interview headline sitting unclaimed in this repo's own
subject matter — Hiles publicly arguing against the Maxis house model of simulation, from inside the
Maxis family of companies.

## What happened next

SimRefinery was eventually found and published — see
[simrefinery-recovery](../will-wright/sources/simrefinery-recovery/README.md) and Phil's own
[SimRefinery](https://obscuritory.com/sim/simrefinery/) and
[Maxis Business Simulations](https://obscuritory.com/sim/maxis-business-simulations/) articles. This
page exists to keep the *failed* first attempt, because the three-year gap between a good-faith
public appeal and a result is the part that usually goes unrecorded.

## Note on the two John Hileses

This is the **American John Hiles** — Maxis Business Simulations 1992–94, later Thinking Tools,
agent-based simulation, died December 2016. The repo also has a room for
[**J.O. Hiles**](../john-hiles/README.md), a 1969 PIXIE co-author at the Cambridge Mathematical
Laboratory, where the coincidence is logged as a question to rule out. **The details here — the 2016
death, brain cancer, the Hiles family, MBS 92–94 — are the material to rule it out with.** The Maxis
Hiles needs his own room under a distinct slug.
