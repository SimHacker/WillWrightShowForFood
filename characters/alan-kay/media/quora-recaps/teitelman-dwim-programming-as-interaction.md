## Programming as interaction — Smalltalk had it; Teitelman had it earlier

Kay on Petricek's "programming as interaction" blog: **Smalltalk 50 years ago** had most of what Petricek wants
— but Petricek may not know **Warren Teitelman's** earlier, **larger along some dimensions** work in Lisp at
MIT, BBN, and PARC. Get Teitelman's **(1965?) "Pilot" PhD thesis** from MIT CSAIL historical collection.

## DWIM — "Do What I Mean"

Teitelman named one of the earliest great attempts to support tension between **"whats" and "hows"**: **DWIM**
(Do What I Mean) — series of Lisps he designed and implemented.

Also:
- One of the **earliest and deepest forms of UNDO** for human errors
- Early/best designs toward McCarthy's **"Advice Taker"** — programmer could **"Advise"** the Lisp system in
  progress

Kay's UI canon: learn classical great designs — JOSS, Sketchpad, GRAIL, **Teitelman's DWIM**, PARC work,
HyperCard (YouTube presentations on "Parc GUI" precursors).

## Pilot thesis — Advice Taker via metaprogramming, not AI

In Lisp history answer: Teitelman's **"Pilot"** thesis = heroic pass at McCarthy's Advice Taker via **meta
programming rather than AI**. Ideas incorporated into **BBN Lisp** when Teitelman joined → **Interlisp**.

Kay's principle: Lisp let you **learn more by trying more and changing your mind more** — meta-reflective
recourse when the bottom of the system lacked what you needed. "Almost nothing was known about programming —
even by the best programmers." Smalltalk instantiated intercommunicating processes **using Lisp techniques**;
if redoing, Kay would keep much of that but replace **"Lisp sharing"** with a different design.

## UI design lesson — learning environments, not control panels

Breakthrough: move from **expert control panels** (nuclear-reactor UIs) to **learning environments** for the
general public — like teaching literacy while people live in society.

**First rule:** "The End Users Are Not Like Us!" Introspection is one of the worst approaches (also true in
theater). **Including children as users** helps sympathy + drive to learn.

Kay laments POV diminished: poor UIs today (e.g. where are **UNDO and HELP** on iPhone/iPad?).
