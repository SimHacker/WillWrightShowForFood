## The answer

Alan Kay (byline: *"Still trying to learn how to think better"*), 8 Aug 2026:

> I find it easy to identify Newton, Maxwell, Einstein, etc., in their respective centuries, but
> don't have an instant answer to this question for computing. (This could partly be about
> computing itself ...)
>
> However, a category I do have for a few computer people is "magic" (where they did something
> important to advance the field, and it is difficult to see just how they were able to do it
> given when they did it and what they were starting with).
>
> Just to pick a few "magic" people: Turing, Shannon, Hamming, McCarthy, Sutherland, Barton,
> Lampson, etc.
>
> My "not-really-a-list" of "magic" people is longer than this, and there are also many worthy
> people who have advanced the field, yet not in a way I would call "magic". But I think we have
> enough to answer the question.

Turing, first on that list, now has his own memorial room:
[characters/alan-turing/](../../../alan-turing/) -- including the Morphogenesis prefatory texts
Don typed in, and the 1923 daisy drawing. Von Neumann, nominated in-thread below, has
[characters/john-von-neumann/](../../../john-von-neumann/).

**Alan Kay:** "I think it's possible to draw lines between good and great (as in music, etc) but

## Don's note: the line and the Atlantic

*Commentary below is Don's, not Alan's.*

"But Dijkstra is on the other side of the good/great line?" chimes with a 49-year-old exchange
about a different divide. Dijkstra wrote
[EWD611, *"On the fact that the Atlantic Ocean has two sides"*](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~EWD/transcriptions/EWD06xx/EWD611.html)
(1977) -- Europe's mathematical computing science vs. America's, from the side of the ocean where
full professors are appointed by the Queen.

Kay's reply came in the [OOPSLA 1997 keynote](https://tinlizzie.org/IA/index.php/Alan_Kay_at_OOPSLA_1997:_The_Computer_Revolution_has_not_Happened_Yet):

> "I don't know how many of you have ever met Dijkstra, but you probably know that arrogance in
> computer science is measured in nano Dijkstras. [Laughter] He once wrote a paper -- of the kind
> that he liked to write a lot of -- which had the title *On the fact that the Atlantic has two
> sides*. [...] So, I wrote a rebuttal paper, just called *On the fact that most of the software in
> the world is written on one side of the Atlantic*."

(The rebuttal likely existed only as its title -- the dig *was* the paper.
[Lambda the Ultimate went looking](http://lambda-the-ultimate.org/node/2087) and couldn't find it;
one commenter asked for the conversion rate to micro-Kays.)

The 2026 punchline assembles itself:

- In 1997 Kay planted the flag on the American shore: most of the world's software is written on
  *one* side of the Atlantic.
- In 2026 he answers the thread [from London](why-alan-kay-moved-to-london.md) -- living on
  *Dijkstra's* side of the Atlantic, in the country where they appoint professors by the Queen
  (well, King now).
- Asked which side of the good/great line Dijkstra falls on, Kay declines the linear order
  entirely -- "these are all subjective categories" -- and instead twice files *himself* outside
  his own "magic" category.

Arrogance is measured in nano-Dijkstras. Don hereby proposes the complementary SI unit: the
**pico-Kay (pK)**, measuring *modesty with respect for others* -- a deliberately double-jointed
definition, since the quantity is measured **with respect to** others and is made **of respect
for** others. Kay's modesty is never self-deprecation; it's calibration: he rules himself out
of the magic category while calling Dijkstra "one of the most influential computerists" in the
same breath. Note the prefix: pico is a thousand times finer than nano. It takes three more
orders of magnitude to register on the Kay scale than on the Dijkstra scale, and the rest of us
should be grateful the instrument reads in trillionths at all. (Lambda the Ultimate estimated
in micro-Kays back in 2007; measurement precision has improved since, and the base unit kept
growing -- he has ruled himself out twice more just this month.)

The man crossed the Atlantic, but wouldn't cross his own line.

And Dijkstra? He'd have filed himself under "magic" without breaking stride -- self-assessment
was never his bottleneck; he remains the only computerist whose confidence comes in SI units.
If his shore of the Atlantic shipped less of the world's software, it isn't that he couldn't:
he co-wrote the **first ALGOL 60 compiler** and built the **THE operating system**, then treated
the theorem as proved -- once you've shown it can be done, doing it again is mere engineering.

The tender coda: the two men settled the two-sides argument by **exchanging sides**. Dijkstra
finished his career in **Austin, Texas** (Schlumberger Centennial Chair, UT, from 1984), where
the software gets written; Alan writes his Quora answers from **Marylebone**, where professors
are appointed by the Crown. The Atlantic still has two sides -- they just both preferred the view
from the other one.

**Show hook:** ask Alan whether the rebuttal paper ever existed beyond its title -- and whether
he'll ratify the **pico-Kay (pK)** as the SI unit of modesty with respect for others, now that
the nano-Dijkstra has held the arrogance standard unchallenged for three decades. (He'll refuse
the honor, which is the point: the refusal recalibrates the unit. First measurement instrument
in metrology whose base standard shrinks when you try to certify it.)

## Nominations from the show roster (Don's, to bring back to Alan)

Applying Alan's test strictly -- *"difficult to see just how they were able to do it given when
they did it and what they were starting with"* -- candidates from [`characters/`](../../../README.md),
cross-checked against
[**People.md**](https://github.com/YootTowerManagement/YootTower/blob/main/Yoot_Saito_Alan_Kay_Interview/People.md)
-- Don's ledger of who Kay himself credits, compiled from Alan's writing and the
[1993 Yoot Saito interview](https://github.com/YootTowerManagement/YootTower/tree/main/Yoot_Saito_Alan_Kay_Interview)
in the YootTower repo.

Links below go to each person's top-level character directory, not to files inside, so they
survive reorganization. Four names on this slate have no room yet: Alonzo Church, Niklaus Wirth,
Konrad Zuse, Craig Chambers.

**Seconding the thread's own nominators first, by name:**

- **Jon Dron** nominated [Douglas Engelbart](../../../douglas-engelbart/) -- seconded without
  reservation. NLS by 1968 from a 1962 framework and batch-era hardware; Kay saw it at Utah and
  cites it as a direct influence.
- **Paul Myers** nominated **Alonzo Church** -- seconded. The strongest form of the criterion:
  with Church it stayed difficult to see how he did it *even after he wrote it down* (Alan's own
  McCarthy story, above, is the proof). No character room yet.
- **Robert Swartz** nominated **von Neumann, Skolem, Church, and Post** -- the logicians' bench;
  seconded, with Church now nominated twice in one thread. Turing is already on Alan's own list,
  so a salute and no extra paragraph. **[John von Neumann](../../../john-von-neumann/)** gets the
  paragraph: three kinds of universal constructor at three levels of reality (deterministic
  mathematical, physical mechanical, unfinished probabilistic quantum mechanical), then he
  *designed the first kind* on paper, without computers, because he was busy inventing them.
  It's a decidedly *non*-von Neumann architecture -- meaning he doesn't get nominative credit
  for his other, more parallel architectures. The serial stored-program machine kept the name.
  Sources live in that directory.
- **Jm Taranca** nominated **Niklaus Wirth** -- seconded on the strength of Kay's own history:
  Euler's radical simplification of Algol, and the way it mapped onto a bytecode-style
  implementation, fed straight into Smalltalk. No character room yet.
- **Thoughtaslight** nominated [Ole-Johan Dahl](../../../ole-johan-dahl/) and
  [Kristen Nygaard](../../../kristen-nygaard/) -- seconded easily; Kay names Simula one of
  Smalltalk's two roots. Also [Steve Wozniak](../../../steve-wozniak/) (seconded) and **Konrad
  Zuse**, who deserves more than a courtesy nod: a working programmable computer built from
  telephone relays in his parents' Berlin living room, 1941, cut off from everyone. No character
  room yet for Zuse.

**Additions:**

- [Wesley Clark](../../../wesley-clark/) -- TX-2 (the machine Sketchpad ran on) and the LINC in
  1962, a personal computer a decade before the concept. Upstream of Sutherland, who made the list.
- [Maurice Wilkes](../../../maurice-wilkes/) -- EDSAC running 1949, microprogramming 1951, from
  mercury delay lines.
- [Lynn Conway](../../../lynn-conway/) -- dynamic instruction scheduling in 1965, decades before
  "superscalar" was a word -- then Mead-Conway VLSI. Passes the test twice.
- [L Peter Deutsch](../../../l-peter-deutsch/) -- a Lisp interpreter on the PDP-1 at age twelve.
- The Self team -- [David Ungar](../../../david-ungar/), [Randall Smith](../../../randall-smith/),
  Craig Chambers (no room yet), [Urs Hoelzle](../../../urs-hoelzle/): born at Xerox PARC in 1986,
  before Stanford and Sun. They made a radically simple prototype language fast, then with
  dynamic deoptimization (PLDI '92) made fully optimized code answer to a source-level debugger.
  Simple AND fast AND honest to the debugger: hard to see how, given 1992. Their tricks (maps,
  polymorphic inline caches, adaptive optimization) now run most of the world's software.
- [Vanessa Freudenberg](../../../vanessa-freudenberg/) -- SqueakJS: a bit-compatible Squeak VM in
  the browser that never implements a garbage collector; it maps Squeak objects onto JavaScript
  objects and lets the JS GC collect the Smalltalk garbage. Judo, not force.
- [Will Wright](../../../will-wright/) -- Don is on his team and honored to be, so one sentence:
  SimCity, then The Sims, still looks like magic given when and what they started with.
- [Dan Ingalls](../../../dan-ingalls/) (Smalltalk-72 from one page of Alan's notes, in BASIC, on
  a bet) and [Diana Merry-Shapiro](../../../diana-merry-shapiro/) (co-wrote BitBlt, hired as a
  typist -- the "what they were starting with" clause read personally): Alan's modesty evidently
  extends to his own team and lineage, so Don nominates them on his behalf.

**Also arguable, not in the Quora draft:** [Sophie Wilson](../../../sophie-wilson/),
[Terry Winograd](../../../terry-winograd/), [Donald Michie](../../../donald-michie/),
[Bill Atkinson](../../../bill-atkinson/), [Ray Kurzweil](../../../ray-kurzweil/),
[Bret Victor](../../../bret-victor/).

And the roster's funniest entry: [`alan-kay`](../../README.md) -- nominated twice in-thread,
ruled out twice by the only person applying the criterion strictly.
