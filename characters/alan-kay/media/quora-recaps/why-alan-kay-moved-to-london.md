# Why did Alan Kay move to London? — Bonnie, Baker Street, and "not a plan"

## The short answer

"Short answer is that my wife — **the amazing [Bonnie MacBird](../../../bonnie-macbird/)** —
decided she wanted to write a Sherlock Holmes novel in the authentic voice of Doyle's Watson."
A superfan since childhood, she thought it would be "fun, motivating, inspiring" to write some
of it at the **Sherlock Holmes Hotel on Baker Street** — "nicely funky, an establishment on
different levels that had been stitched together into a hotel many years before" — in the
middle of Marylebone, surrounded by Victorian flat blocks built in the 1890s, "Holmes'
fictional era."

## It compounds

The writing went well; they kept going back. Alan built a **Hauptwerk organ keyboard and
pedals** that the hotel stored for him so he could practice on visits. Then the arithmetic:
"If we were to live in London half-time we could break even on a flat rental for what the
hotel was costing us." They found a small Victorian flat nearby; Bonnie fixed up her half of
the double sitting room **as the 221B sitting room of Holmes and Watson**.

On London itself: "a palpable **'spirit of kindness'** from most people… Teenagers will
actually give up their seats on the underground to older people… The 'feel' of London is a
good feel." Plus "some of the very best English language theatre in the world, and in great
profusion."

Then Trump's election, then the pandemic ("we decided that both the UK and the US would botch
it, and that we'd rather try to sit it out in London than in LA. This turned out to be a good
choice"). During all this Bonnie wrote her Sherlock Holmes novels for Harper-Collins from the
flat — five at the time of the answer, **six** as of Alan's 2026 update.

## The kicker

"So: **'London' was not a plan, but just gradually happened**, and we are both happy how it
turned out."

## Gems from the comment thread

- **How they met** (reply to Jacob Daniels): "When you've found the right wife/lover/partner
  you follow her around. Bonnie was working in the movie business when we met…" And to James
  McLean ("the writer of TRON is your missus?"): "As I mentioned, she's amazing. We met when
  she came to Silicon Valley looking for technical advice about computers for her script, and
  I was at Xerox Parc, on her list. We like to say that **'The marriage turned out better than
  the movie.'**" (Full origin story — Ted Nelson's *Computer Lib* as matchmaker — in
  [`../../../bonnie-macbird/`](../../../bonnie-macbird/).)
- **On staying legally**: friends wrote letters; an extended visa category fit; after five
  years, the wonderfully-named "**Indefinite leave to remain**." ("'Leave to remain' is such a
  British thing." — commenter)
- **On a book of his Quora answers** (to Matthew Belge): 608 answers, mostly 500+ words —
  "more than 300,000 words of answers — but with no larger organization, and that would make
  for a too long and too kaleidoscopic book." (Belge counters with Eno's *A Year with Swollen
  Appendices* as the model. See the
  [corpus index](quora-corpus-2023-index.md) — this repo's recaps are quietly doing the
  organizing.)

## Show fodder

- The London story is **instance-first living**: no plan, follow the concrete thing (a novel,
  a hotel, a flat), lift later. Same shape as play-learn-lift.
- Bonnie's 221B sitting-room recreation ↔ this repo's rooms-with-memory: she built a
  character room in physical space.
- The Hauptwerk organ at the hotel — Alan's music thread (Apple Hill chamber music, pipe
  organ) as a recurring counter-melody to the computing lineage.
