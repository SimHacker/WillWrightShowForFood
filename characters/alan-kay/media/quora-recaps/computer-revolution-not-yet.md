## Two different "computer revolutions"

In Kay's ARPA/PARC community (60s–70s), **"computer revolution"** meant the next qualitative leap after the
**writing, printing, math, and science** revolution — scale of **hundreds to thousands of years**.

Today's public sense: pervasiveness and convenience of tech that **imitates old media** and distributes them
more widely.

A revolution **has** happened for the public — with consequences — but the one they were "sure was possible
— and partially invented — **hasn't happened for the public**" (for whom much of the writing/printing/math/science
revolution **hasn't happened yet either**).

Kay's operational test elsewhere: where is **"the equivalent of HyperCard in 2020?"** — see
[`hypercard-personal-computing-breakthrough.md`](hypercard-personal-computing-breakthrough.md).

## Illich as problem-finder — Deschooling and Convivial Tools

Kay rates Ivan Illich **"quite high as a 'problem finder'"** — anyone interested in education past/present/future
should read **Deschooling Society** (deep critique of standard schooling) and **Convivial Tools** (tool-based
species perspective).

Kay quotes Illich on schooling confusing:
- teaching with learning, grade advancement with education, diploma with competence, fluency with saying
  something new;
- imagination "schooled" to accept **service in place of value**;
- health, learning, dignity, independence, creative endeavor defined as performance of institutions — improvement
  = more resources to hospital/school management.

*(Connects to Kay's Quora answer on public libraries / Carnegie library vision — open stacks, children's room,
Whole Earth Catalog as "systems of systems" — displaced by "subsets, sometimes disastrous subsetting.")*

## School limits potential — and pop computer culture can't see systems

On why school & college limit children: Kay argues good answers need **revolution in public schooling** *and*
revolution away from **pop culture around computers** that "can't see the systems for the bits."

"Lots of real deep work ahead."

On **children vs. education**: Kay was focused on **how children actually think** — what should **"reading" and
"writing"** become in a computational world? (Dynabook as "teacher for every learner" — not classroom technology
alone.)

## Media that amplify cognition

Kay distinguishes **Whole Earth Catalog** as **"counterculture" medium** rather than **"hippie"** one — points
to his dedicated Quora answer on WEC (Bertalanffy, Waddington, classics — systems thinking for civilians).

**McLuhan territory:** why some media amplify cognition more than others — ties to **misspent youth** threads
(theatre, anthropology, molecular biology degree "lurking") used to think about representation and learning.

## Whole Earth / hippie influence on personal computing

Personal computing was influenced by counterculture/WEC sensibilities — not as fashion but as **access to
powerful ideas** and tools for independent thought. Kay's line elsewhere: ARPA-PARC images of the future were
**Carnegie libraries** and **Whole Earth Catalog** — extend literacy, not imitate old media on screens ("none
of the screens available now are as good to read from").
