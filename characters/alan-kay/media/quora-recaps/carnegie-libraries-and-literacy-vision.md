## ARPA-PARC's two images of the future

In 60s–70s personal-computing research, two compelling futures were:
1. **Carnegie Libraries** — open stacks, **special room for children**, **room where reading was taught** in
   evenings.
2. **Whole Earth Catalog** — interlinked "systems of systems" of ideas.

Goal: extend what **literacy, reading, writing, understanding** could be — **not** imitate old media on screens.
Today: "none of the screens available now are as good to read from" (for good readers).

## Carnegie's origin story

~Half of US libraries by ~1920 funded by **Andrew Carnegie** — immigrant **child laborer** from Scotland;
first boss opened **home library to laborers on Saturdays**; Carnegie educated himself there.

Carnegie grants required **two special rooms**: teaching reading + children only. Libraries create **social
and amplifying roles** in communities — not just information access.

## Disastrous subsetting — what we lost

Displacement by electronics is often **subsets, sometimes disastrous subsetting**:
- Search engines → fragments, not whole books; telegraph made currency beat content; TV replaced newspapers
  with less depth.
- Smaller screens, smaller viewing angles, non-keyboards → barriers to extensive reading and real prose.
- **Open stacks' serendipity** — seek one thing, find many you didn't know to look for — poorly replicated
  online (Amazon's catalog huge but UI weak on serendipity).

Web building by people who **(a) don't know Carnegie libraries** and **(b) don't supply modern equivalents
of the two special rooms** → web as **"the context of no context."**

## What libraries could do — and what AI should do

Physical libraries erode unless they enhance **teaching + children's functions** (people learning together),
better browsing, "take books out" — consumer companies do these poorly; room for **community + philanthropist**
supported higher value.

Online gap Kay finds shocking: **no free system that teaches people to read.** "If semi-AIs are to have any
real value at all, **this** is what is needed from them" — not generic chat.

Libraries today often serve **homeless** populations; Kay: maybe too big a task, but Solon — judge a society
by how it treats its least fortunate.

## Powerful ideas — the meta-list

Top of civilization's list:
1. **There are powerful ideas**
2. Human brains aren't great for thinking but **can be taught to think much better** (IQ needs Knowledge
   needs Perspective)
3. **Science**
4. **Look for powerful ideas** — try to figure what the phrase means …

(Papert + Kay tried to organize a full list — hard; #4 may beat any single entry.)

Education's main goal in the large: progress in **(b) ideas-about-ideas** + deep habits — "real thinking" vs.
genetic "reacting and rationalizing."
