## Engelbart — tool ≠ augmentation

Reasonable **"augmented human"** is *not* created by handing someone a powerful tool.

Engelbart's points Kay repeats:
- **(a)** Augmentation requires **non-optional deep training/education** for deep skills with new powers
- **(b)** Most work is in **groups** → must **"augment collective IQ"** with collaborative-tool education

Available powers + impulse to compete → poor/bad uses. Image: **cave people with atomic weapons** ("pretty
much what we have today"). Deep prep turns crafts into civilization; without it → trivial/dangerous pop culture
weapons.

**Same answer needed today** for *"What happened to Doug Engelbart's real insights and goals?"* as for
schooling/Dynabook — confusions **worse than 1968**.

## Media that amplify cognition — literacy hurts before it helps

All media **amplify cognition** but in different directions — some **pro-civilization**, some **anti-**.

Easy media (movies, recordings) need little new learning. **Difficult media** (literacy, diagrams, graphs) require
method, skill, repetition, "psychic pain" — fluency builds **new mechanisms for thinking**. Literate +
schooled societies think qualitatively differently from oral ones (McLuhan, Postman, Ong, Innis, Eisenstein…).

Non-conformal visuals (graphs, schematics) — Ron Thornton's Tufts work: graph-reading fluency **predicted
physics grades**; teaching graph fluency **improved overall performance**.

**Simple answer:** learn which media do what — and **make choices**.

## Human communication retrograded

Modern tech: important discourse **retrograded** by untrained use; competitive impulses overwhelm cooperation.

McLuhan's **"global village"** (early 60s) — Kay notes McLuhan was **not in favor**: networked world → everyone
knows what everyone else is doing → loss of identity, war to reclaim it, demands to **participate** without
interest in **cooperate**.

24/7 embedded environments **re-norm "reality"** — worth pondering.

## WWW vs. Engelbart NLS — Berners-Lee's apology didn't help

Historic/literary CS approach: compare Engelbart 60s goals to **almost non-existent notions for WWW**.
Berners-Lee apologized for not knowing Engelbart — didn't fix ~30 years. Engelbart OS: conferencing = shared
face/voice/screen **plus simultaneous interaction with shared content** — not just documents.

## Inverse vandalism — making because you can

**Inverse vandalism:** making things just because you can. Industrial revolution → oversupply; things without
genetic "curbs" (sugar, stories, social media) become **"legal drugs"** when mass-produced.

Tinkering ≠ problem (useful); problem = confusing tinkering with engineering, **overproducing underthought
things** that **"redefine 'normal' lower"** (Portobello flea market: everything was once mass-produced and cared-for).

Communications tech: telegraph made **currency > content**; electric/electronic media let **oral/visual modes
replace literate modes** from writing/printing — Kay: **bad** (marketing/authoritarians disagree).

Thoreau: **"We become the tools of our tools."** Society losing battle to understand what's happening;
technology progresses (physical world) while **humanity regresses** (refuse to understand ourselves + new
powers).
