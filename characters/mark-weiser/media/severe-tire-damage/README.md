# Severe Tire Damage

Mark Weiser was the drummer of Severe Tire Damage, a band that called itself "the first band on the
internet, the first band on the MBone." On 24 June 1993 it played live over the Internet, and then
kept broadcasting most Wednesday nights "to demonstrate that technology can be both loud and
annoying." Its web site, `www.std.org`, survives in the Wayback Machine (600 captures, 1997–2026).

## The band

From the site's own pages ([band members](site/text/bandmembers.html), [friends](site/text/bandothers.html)):

- **Russ Haines**, guitar and vocals
- **Mark Manasse**, bass and vocals
- **Steve Rubin**, vocals
- **Mark Weiser**, drums

Friends of the band: Hania Gajewska (band manager emeritus), Ken Beckman (founding member; drums,
live audio engineering), Anna Karlin (founding member; guitar and vocals), Amy Lansky (vocals),
Dexter Kozen (guitar, composer), Ethan Robertson (sax and keyboards), Bruce Donald (guitar), Daniel
Scharstein (keyboards and drums), and Paul "Shag" Walmsley (remote operator).

The first performance on the MBone is written up on the site with Mark's 1994 exchange with Steve
Casner, who remembered watching it: "It looked like everyone was having a good time out on the
patio. I recall that there were dancers" ([report](site/text/firstMCastReport.html)).

## The recordings

The audio and video are in an Internet Archive item, **[severe-tire-damage-std-org](https://archive.org/details/severe-tire-damage-std-org)**,
about 195 MB:

- *Who Cares*, the album, 12 tracks in MP3, and song clips in WAV;
- *Trial Starter Kit*, *More Crap From STD*, and the Karin Ann Phillips tribute, in MP3;
- RealAudio and RealMedia streams of the songs, and live recordings from 1999 and 2000;
- the H. Ross Perot clip, Nightline and other video, and the band's VRML and PostScript logos.

[MANIFEST.csv](MANIFEST.csv) lists every file with its size, SHA-1, original `std.org` URL and the
Wayback capture it came from. 275 files the site linked to couldn't be retrieved from the Wayback
Machine. One file, `movies/doubtfire.data` (a Macintosh QuickTime movie's data fork), is in the
item as `doubtfire.data.mov`, because the Archive won't take it without a video extension.
