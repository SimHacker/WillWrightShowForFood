INTERACTIVE SEVERE TIRE DAMAGE

On June 24, 1993, Severe Tire Damage became the first band to perform live on the internet. Performing from the patios of Xerox PARC, they were broadcast onto the Internet Multicast Backbone (the "MBone"). They were seen and heard live as far away as Australia. Anna Karlin was with the band then. MBone engineering was by Ron Frederick and Steve Deering of the Computer Science Lab at Xerox PARC.

The second Severe Tire Damage Internet broadcast occured on November 18, 1994, from 6:30-6:50pm PST. Broadcasting from the Digital Systems Research Center in Palo Alto, Severe Tire Damage opened on the Internet for the Rolling Stones Internet broadcast, which began on the same MBone channel at 7pm PST. Severe Tire Damage also played some tunes after the Stones, at 8:05pm PST, on the same channel. Audio engineering was by Ken Beckman, MBone engineering was by Lance Berc, both of Digital's Systems Research Center. The press was generous, and we received many other comments about our 'cast. If you heard us, we want to hear from you!

As if this wasn't enough, Severe Tire Damage did a series of live weekly MBone internet broadcasts (every Wednesday at 9pm PST). We hope to return soon so that you can once again tune into the Severe Tire Damage channel for a short burst of music, silliness, and lost packets. Catch our advertisements on the MBone all week long. Or check out this simulated MBone experience.
While watching, you can control the camera and grab images. Up, down, left, right, zoom in and out, or just click on the picture of your favorite band member. But be warned, only those watching live on the MBone will see the results of your twidling.
. While listening, you can also control the audio mix. Adjust the levels of each instrument. Add reverb. Turn up the guitar for the solo. And of course, this one goes to eleven. And of course, only those listening live on the MBone will hear the results of your twidling.
. While watching, you can also control our fog machine. Stage effects are good, and a room full of fog makes the picture much clearer. Again, only those performing live on the MBone will fail to see the results of your twidling.
