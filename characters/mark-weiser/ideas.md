## What Mark did

Mark Weiser (1952–1999) — father of **ubiquitous computing** and **calm technology**, CTO of
**Xerox PARC**. His 1991 "The Computer for the 21st Century" imagined computing woven invisibly
into everyday life (tabs, pads, boards). He co-authored the **CHI'88 empirical pie-vs-linear menu
study** with Jack Callahan, Don Hopkins, and Ben Shneiderman — and drummed for **Severe Tire
Damage**, the first band to play live over the Internet. He argued for measuring **instructions
per joule**, pushing the industry toward low-power portable computing.

## The hooks

### 1. The PKD·Lem·SF·AI masterpiece — *Ubik* → ubiquitous computing (flagship)
Mark **named his field after a Philip K. Dick novel** and inverted its metered-everyware dystopia
into calm technology. That one fact opens the whole reading group: PKD, Lem, and sci-fi read as
literal specs for the AI we're building now.
**Draw in:** David Rosenthal (knew Mark; LOCKSS; anchors the thread), Will Wright (Palmer Eldritch
/ The Sims), Don (primary source — heard the Ubik story from Mark).
[`pkd-lem-ai-sf.md`](pkd-lem-ai-sf.md) · [`pkd-lem-ai-sf.yml`](pkd-lem-ai-sf.yml) ·
concept: [`../../process/artifactory.md`](../../process/artifactory.md)

### 2. Calm technology vs the attention economy
"The most profound technologies are those that disappear" — the computer as "a quiet, invisible
servant." Did the field keep the calm, or ship the opposite (notifications, dark patterns, the
everyware Mark warned about)? An honest, argumentative tribute.
**Draw in:** Ben Shneiderman (direct manipulation vs calm framing), Don, Brad Myers.

**Living-room proof (Don + David Levitt):** [**ConnectedTV**](../don-hopkins/connectedtv-touch-tuning-finger-pies.md)
— Touch Tuning + Finger Pies on Palm; task-oriented UI in the dark. Honda ditching touchscreens
([HN 22738415](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22738415)) and Norman's elderly-UI threads
([37902412](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37902412)) are the same debate — show seed:
[`connectedtv-calm-technology.yml`](../../repo-shows/connectedtv-calm-technology.yml).

### 3. The ubicomp prophecy — tabs, pads, boards → IoT / ambient
Read the 1991 Scientific American piece as the map it turned out to be. What came true, what
didn't, and what got twisted on the way to smartphones and smart speakers.
**Draw in:** PARC-era peers, David Rosenthal, Don.
Source: "The Computer for the 21st Century" (Scientific American, 1991).

### 4. CHI'88 pie menus — the table Mark sat at
The empirical pie-vs-linear menu study (Callahan, Hopkins, Weiser, Shneiderman). Remember Mark
through the co-authors who ran the numbers with him — the most personal, on-the-nose hook for this
repo.
**Draw in:** Don, Ben Shneiderman, Brad Myers, Jack Callahan (documented co-author).
Source: [`../don-hopkins/career/work-history.yml`](../don-hopkins/career/work-history.yml).

**1991 mail — tape, alphabet pies, stylus:** Don's demo tape (*"work of art"*) shown to **Bill Buxton**;
Mark's **6×6 / 6×8 alphabetic pie trees** and **26-quadrant stylus word shapes** (PARC, no handwriting
recognition); bill demos as **artworks** not tech pitches.
→ [`../don-hopkins/sources/1991-03-mark-weiser-pie-menu-tape-stylus.md`](../don-hopkins/sources/1991-03-mark-weiser-pie-menu-tape-stylus.md)

### 5. Instructions per joule — the low-power argument
Mark pushed measuring performance by energy, not MIPS — prescient for the battery-bound,
carbon-bound computing we live in. A short, sharp segment.
**Draw in:** systems / hardware peers, Don.

### 6. Severe Tire Damage — the first band live on the Internet (1993)
Mark drummed for the band that beat the Rolling Stones to the first live Internet concert. Calm
technology, loud music — the human, joyful side of the PARC visionary.
**Draw in:** bandmates / PARC colleagues (friend-phone clips welcome).

### 7. PARC CTO — the research culture
What it was like to run research at Xerox PARC in the ubicomp years; systems work (including
garbage-collection research); how a lab turns a sci-fi hunch into a field.
**Draw in:** John Seely Brown (calm-tech co-author, PARC Director — candidate), PARC alumni, Don.

### 8. The memorial itself — async clips, cut together
Format note: record separate 1-on-1 remembrances whenever convenient for each person, interlink
their material, and edit into one tribute in post. No synchronized call required.
[`../../repo-shows/remembering-mark-weiser.yml`](../../repo-shows/remembering-mark-weiser.yml)

### 9. The living legacy — the award and the torchbearers
Mark's name reaches new people every spring: the **Mark D. Weiser Excellence in Computing
Scholarship** at UC Berkeley EECS, where recipients post short thank-you clips (Angelina Wang '18,
Alex Lee '19, Ethan Yue Guo & Jason S. Wang '22, Meiqi Sun '23, and more). And his ideas are still
carried forward — **Gregory Abowd** (read Weiser in '94; his *Internet of Materials* is the vision
still compounding) and **Amber Case** (built calmtech.com and wrote *Calm Technology* after Mark
died). A hopeful, forward-looking segment: not just what Mark did, but who he keeps touching.
**Draw in:** Gregory Abowd, Amber Case (their consent). Honor roll + citations:
[`legacy.yml`](legacy.yml) · Abowd talk: [`abowd-internet-of-materials.md`](abowd-internet-of-materials.md)
