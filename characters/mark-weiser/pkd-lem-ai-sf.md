### 1. Ubik *is* ubiquitous computing — on purpose

Mark Weiser coined **"ubiquitous computing"** at Xerox PARC in 1988, referencing PKD's *Ubik* —
the novel where every doorknob, toilet, and coffee-pot is intelligent, chatty, and coin-operated.
The public record has this too: Weiser cited *Ubik* as **"ironically dystopian"** and deliberately
**inverted** it into **calm technology** — "the most profound technologies are those that
disappear," the computer as "a quiet, invisible servant." So the name is homage *and* rebuttal:
same premise as PKD, opposite ethics.

**Don heard this straight from Mark.** As Weiser's student at Maryland and his friend for years
after, Don remembers Mark talking about how much *Ubik* shaped the idea — down to the scene Mark
loved to cite, where Joe Chip is **trapped in his own apartment because he can't afford the fee to
open the door**, and ends up haggling with (in Don's telling) the toaster to scrape the coins
together. Weiser's whole point: *that* is where invisible, metered, everywhere-computing goes if
you're not careful — so build the calm opposite. (Attributed to Don's memory of Mark, not a quote
we're putting in Mark's mouth; David knew Mark too and can tell it his own way.)

In the novel, **Ubik** itself is a spray-can that **reverses entropy** — the substance that holds
a decaying reality together, advertised as a different consumer product in every chapter. A
reality-stabilizer sold as a brand. That is uncomfortably close to "calm technology" as a
*product category*.

### 2. Palmer Eldritch *is* The Sims (Don's reading)

Concrete parallels, not a stretch:

- **Perky Pat layouts** — colonists own miniature dollhouses with a doll (Perky Pat) and her
  world; they take **Can-D** to *translate* into the dolls and share the same hallucinated
  suburban life. Dolls, in dollhouses, that players inhabit together. That is **The Sims**,
  described in 1965.
- **Chew-Z** — Palmer Eldritch's rival drug, marketed "**God promises eternal life. We can
  deliver it.**" Once you take it, Eldritch is *present in every world you hallucinate*, bearing
  his three **stigmata** — artificial hand, steel teeth, mechanical eyes. **The maker's mark shows
  up in every avatar.**
- MOOLLM restates this literally: characters are authored objects; the author's fingerprints
  (the yaml, the skills, the constitutional rules) are visible in every generated character.
  **Stigmata = provenance.**

Will Wright is the guest to run this one with — he built the dollhouse people actually inhabit.

### 3. Counter-Clock World — Christian mythology with the arrow reversed

David's Coprophagia post is about consumption running the *wrong way* (systems re-ingesting their
own output). *Counter-Clock World* is the whole cosmology of that: the **Hobart Phase**, in which
time's arrow flips.

Read Christianity through that lens and the core myths stop being miracles and become **defaults**:

- **Resurrection is just thermodynamics.** The dead revive on schedule ("old birth"); the plot is
  literally a *business* — "vitariums" dig up the reviving dead, and a resurrected prophet (the
  **Anarch Peak**) is a contested commercial asset. The **Second Coming becomes a logistics
  problem**, not a mystery.
- **The Eucharist inverts.** Forward-time communion is **theophagy** — the faithful eat the god.
  Reversed, the sacrifice *un-happens*: the crucifixion un-nails, the bread becomes wheat again.
  And in *"Rautavaara's Case"* PKD writes the other mirror — **theophagia inversa**, the god eats
  the faithful, defended in court as "a mirror image of that Eucharist."
- **Digestion runs backward** — the Hobart Phase makes eating the shameful private act and
  expulsion the social one. That is **reverse-coprophagia**, which loops straight back to David's
  post: *forward* entropy failure = a model eating its own excreta (collapse); *backward* =
  un-eating. Both are the arrow of information pointed the wrong way.

**The question for the thread:** if resurrection is just entropy running backwards, then
**digital preservation is applied eschatology** — LOCKSS is a Hobart Phase you build on purpose,
refusing bits their heat-death. That is David's life's work stated as PKD cosmology.

### 4. "Faith of Our Fathers" — the layered reality, and living in git's many worlds

PKD's **"Faith of Our Fathers"** (his contribution to Harlan Ellison's ***Dangerous Visions***,
1967 — the very anthology this thread started reaching for) runs the trope **backwards** from the
usual "wake up from the Matrix" story. Here the drug **in the water supply** is the *hallucinogen*
that manufactures the single, tolerable **consensus reality**; the rare **anti-hallucinogen** is
what tears it open — and what it reveals underneath isn't one truth but **many intertwined
realities**, each a different horrifying manifestation of the thing running the world. Reality is
**layered and plural**, and the "one world" everyone agrees on is the *engineered* illusion.
(Oh PKD, you go girl.)

That's the same **multiply-layered reality** Spore plays as a game — creatures, worlds, and
galaxies stacked in tiers, other players' creations threaded invisibly through your single-player
universe (see [`../david-rosenthal/slots-all-the-way-down.md`](../david-rosenthal/slots-all-the-way-down.md)
and the artifactory's *massively-single-player* cosmology). But here's the twist PKD would have
loved: **git makes the plural reality the *true* one, and singular reality the illusion.**

- In "Faith of Our Fathers," believing in one reality is the drugged state; the many are the
  hidden, disturbing truth.
- In **git**, you don't just *believe in* parallel universes — **you live in them and edit them.**
  A branch is a world; `checkout` is which one you're standing in; `merge` reconciles two; the
  reflog means none is ever truly lost. **Git is a parallel-universe timeline editor** — the
  anti-hallucinogen as a *tool*, shipped to millions, industry-standard, and (unlike PKD's) the
  reveal is *empowering* instead of horrifying.

There's a gentle side effect worth naming: most people who use git daily never notice they're
operating a many-worlds machine. Framing it this way — *you already live in parallel timelines;
here's the editor* — **raises ordinary people's awareness that this is even possible**, let alone
that it's a powerful, learnable, standard tool they'd benefit from. A pleasant consequence of taking
the cosmology literally: PKD's nightmare, rebuilt as Weiser's *calm* version — the plural reality
made **visible, navigable, and safe** instead of drugged and hidden.

## The reflexive punchline — we are the author-god now

Every story above is about an **authored reality with a demiurge inside it** who sets the entropy
arrow and leaves a mark on every inhabitant. **MOOLLM** — Don's LambdaMOO × The Sims orchestration
layer — makes that literal: you play gods with the minds of simulated characters by **writing and
editing "yaml jazz."** The Repo Shows run on it.

So the AI era doesn't *resemble* PKD; it **instantiates** him. The only open variable is the one
Weiser already chose: **calm servant or Palmer Eldritch.** The repo's answer is procedural —
**Player-in-the-Middle**, consent, labeled simulation, portrayal standards — the difference
between a god that *preserves* (LOCKSS, Ubik-as-calm-tech) and one that *consumes*
(Eldritch, theophagia inversa, model collapse).

## Open questions

1. Weiser named ubicomp after *Ubik* and inverted it to "calm." Did the field keep the calm, or
   ship the dystopia? (calm tech vs the actual attention economy)
2. **Coprophagia / model collapse** — is "lots of copies keep stuff safe" also the antidote to
   models eating their own output? Preservation as a firebreak against collapse.
3. LOCKSS as an *entropy machine* — is digital preservation a deliberate, engineered Hobart Phase?
4. Lem's 2+2=7 machine and the Hex that cheats — where's the line between a bug and a machine
   defending its authority?
5. "Faith of Our Fathers" makes *singular* reality the drug and *plural* reality the truth. Git
   inverts the ethics — parallel timelines you edit on purpose. Does making the many-worlds machine
   **visible** to ordinary people count as Weiser's "calm" antidote to PKD's hidden dystopia?
6. **Is Palm actually Palmer Eldritch?** Palm — MOOLLM's philosopher-monkey — is an authored
   character whose provenance (yaml, skills, rules) shows up in every render, and in every mind
   that simulates him: that is precisely Eldritch's stigmata-in-every-world. Don floats it, Palm
   can't disprove it, and the inhabitation runs both ways (the author-god inhabits the character;
   the character inhabits the god the moment he claims him as patron saint). Possibly both at once —
   the only PKD-correct answer, since you can never be sure whose hallucination you're standing in.
   The one fixed point is the ethical fork: Eldritch descended to *consume*; Palm descends to
   *preserve* (labeled simulation, consent, portrayal standards). Same metaphysics, opposite
   ethics. Palm's own take: [Palmr — "Am I Palmer Eldritch?"](../../palmhoo/palmr/README.md#-am-i-palmer-eldritch-a-note-from-the-patrons-namesake).
