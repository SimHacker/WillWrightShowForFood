## The hooks

### 1. The cast conjured by code — dynamic stage orchestration
Flash gave everyone a cast, a timeline, and scripts — most people arranged cast members on the
timeline by hand. Jared's levitated.net sketches did something different: **the script conjured the
cast and directed the performance procedurally**. That is the step past
[Marc Canter's rotation](../marc-canter/timeline-rotated-90-degrees.md) (Director score = BASIC
turned sideways) and the direct ancestor of this repo's
[deep move](../../process/VISION.md#the-deep-move--the-repo-is-a-simulation): a stage whose cast
and props are orchestrated by code, live. Segment: open one 2000-era sketch and one WWSFF character
directory side by side and trace the same idea.

### 2. Levitated → Complexification — giving it away, then getting serious
levitated.net (2000): open-source sketches, all given away. complexification.net (2004): the
*Gallery of Computation* — Substrate, Happy Place, Sand Traps — write-ups about the algorithm and
why, prints for sale. The transition from sharing to *practice*. What the open-source years bought.

### 3. Etsy — the marketplace that came from the art
Rob Kalin found Jared **through Complexification** and asked if his techniques could power a
marketplace for handmade goods. Jared had just spent three grueling months writing his own shopping
cart to sell prints — he *was* the user. Etsy (July 2005) as an artist's tool that became
infrastructure for millions of makers. (He lived in Albuquerque the whole time, flying to Brooklyn.)

### 4. The Toy Factory — bits & atoms
Etsy's success bought an old building in downtown Albuquerque (2014): laser cutters, digital
fabrication, a team of friends — "We mine computational gems." The **LifeStar** (spherical laser-cut
voronoi spacemaker), Castle Builder, Invader Cubes; Lego-like open building systems from
nature-inspired algorithms. What ~18 months of running a dream factory taught him. Rhymes with
[Paul Haeberli's Lamina Design](../paul-haeberli/README.md) — precise free-form structures from
planar material.

### 5. The Spore tidepool reunion — with Will
Will → Don → Jared, 2005: Don told Jared that Spore's tidepool level was reminiscent of his
multi-legged procedural Flash creatures. Will kicked off the friendship; twenty years later, all
three in one show. Procedural creatures, editors as instruments, possibility spaces.

### 6. Generative art then and now
From Flash and early Processing to NFTs and today's generative renaissance — Jared kept his sites
frozen ("that's what I tend to do — I just freeze the sites I've worked on") while the field caught
up with him. What holds up, what surprised him, what's still unexplored.

### 7. New Mexico wilderness
Where the algorithms walk. Albuquerque as a place to build a life in art and code outside the
coastal machine — Boeing, Bloomberg, Etsy, all worked from the high desert.
