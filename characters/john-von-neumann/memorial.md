# In memoriam -- John von Neumann (1903-1957)

*Memorial mode: represent and discuss; do not speak as him.*
[Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

**John von Neumann** gave his name to the architecture almost every computer uses -- and then,
for his last great work, designed a machine that pointedly wasn't one: a massively parallel
29 state cellular automaton hosting a universal constructor that could build and boot a copy
of itself. He worked it out on paper, on graph paper, without the help of the computers he had
just invented, and he was still extending the theory -- toward probabilistic, mutating,
evolving machines -- when he died at 53, likely of cancer from radiation exposure at atomic
bomb tests.

He was there at the founding of more fields than most people ever visit: the mathematical
foundations of quantum mechanics, game theory, the stored program computer (the EDVAC report),
numerical weather prediction, Monte Carlo methods, and the theory of self-reproducing automata
that seeded artificial life, nanotechnology dreams and nightmares, and every "von Neumann
probe" in science fiction.
