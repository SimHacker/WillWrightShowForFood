## The line

> One of my challenges is often I'll have three ideas in my head at the same time, and then I have a
> **serial port** — like which is a mouse, or hands which is typing — and I can't get all three ideas
> out.

That is the webtop's problem statement, said better than we have said it, by a stranger, in 2021.
Parallel thought, serial output channel. Ted's complaint about single-column text is the same complaint
from the document's side; hers is from the body's side, which is the side the webtop lives on.

## What her questions got out of Ted

She asked the two questions in that Q&A that produced quotable positions rather than anecdotes.

**On his own invention:** "I don't know. It seemed an obvious idea to me when I published it in 1965."

**On mind mapping**, which nobody had cleanly pinned him on before: hypertext is "the extension of
literature, especially parallel hypertext side by side," and then the criterion this repo now builds
against —

> a document is a package of information with a point of view, and it has to be portable, and that has
> to be extendable to somebody else. And mind maps don't seem to fill a bill.

Then she proposed the refinement that thebrain.com is "a collection of fairly short memes" and "a
little bit too **atomized**," and Ted said "I love that distinction." Getting Ted Nelson to adopt your
sharpening of his own position, live, is not common.

"Too atomized" is an objection MOOLLM has to answer about its own YAML, and she is the one who framed
it.

## Why invite her

The webtop has a vocabulary for structure — paths, masks, tours, rooms, ranks — and **no vocabulary for
how any of it feels.** Whether a reading surface is expressive is a different question from whether it
is any fun, and which kind of fun. Curiosity-driven exploration is precisely what room-strolling bets
on, and she has done the observational research on that rather than the arguing.

Also, and not incidentally: she is a practitioner who studies what players *actually* do instead of
what they say afterward. That is the same methodological stance as measuring a skill's behavior rather
than trusting its description.
