## Question one: the serial port

> One of my challenges is often I'll have, you know, three ideas in my head at the same time, and then
> I have a **serial port** -- like which is a mouse, or you know hands which is typing -- and I can't
> get all three ideas out. So hypertext is kind of an interesting way around that, if you will. But I
> was curious, how does that reflect at all about your inner workings, like how you think, how you
> create?

**Ted's complete answer:**

> I don't know. It seemed an obvious idea to me when I published it in 1965.

That is the whole answer. He does not introspect about it, and a few minutes later, when Selker
credits him with inventing hypertext, he says:

> Okay, now I don't think I invented it. I thought it was obvious and just needed a name.

## Question two: mind mapping

> I was curious about what you thought about mind mapping software such as, you know, thebrain.com.
> How might that relate to your work on hypertext?

**Ted:**

> I've looked at it and fiddled with it and didn't find any use for it. Hypertext I see is the
> extension of literature, especially parallel hypertext side by side. But mind mapping doesn't create
> -- okay, **a document is a package of information with a point of view, and it has to be portable,
> and that has to be extendable to somebody else.** And mind maps don't seem to fill a bill.

**Her refinement, which he accepted:**

> It sounds like a mind mapping software like thebrain.com, it's really a collection of fairly short
> memes, fairly short ideas, where your vision is more constructs of ideas put together.

**Ted:** "Well, writing is constructs of ideas put together. That's exactly -- yeah."

**Her:** "So the mind map in terms of like thebrain.com is often might be just a little bit too
**atomized** in a way."

**Ted:** "I love that distinction. Thank you very much."

## Why both questions matter here

**The serial port** is the webtop's problem statement in one sentence, from the body side: parallel
thought, serial output channel. Ted's objection to single-column text is the same complaint from the
document side. Nobody in this repo had phrased it that well.

**The mind map answer** gives us Ted's three criteria for a document — sendable, expresses a point of
view, made by a person — which is the test the whole path-and-mask argument is built to pass, and the
test Roam and the rest of the current crop fail. Load-bearing in
[moollm `designs/PAIRED-LINKS.md`](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/designs/PAIRED-LINKS.md).

"Too atomized" is also a sharp piece of criticism that she, not Ted, supplied: the failure of a note
graph is not that it has links, it is that the units are too small to carry an argument. That is an
objection MOOLLM has to answer about its own YAML.
