# Donald Michie — Duckmouse

🕯️ **11 November 1923 – 7 July 2007**

He is a character. The Repo Show already orbits him whenever PIXIE, Glasgow rain, Robot Olympics, or Bletchley silver bars come up. This room is the memorial: rich, detailed, and loving — not a ventriloquist act.

### How to say the name (inheritance along pronunciation)

Say **Donald** as in **Donald Duck**. Say **Michie** as in **Mickey Mouse** — **MICK-ee**, never MISH-ee, never MY-kee, never mi-SHEE.

That is not soul-stealing from cartoons. Pronunciation axis only:

```python
# from <module> import <dimension.aspect> as <local>
from donald_duck import pronunciation.donald as donald
from mickey_mouse import pronunciation.mmickey as michie

given_name = donald   # DON-ald
surname = michie      # MICK-ee
nickname = "Duckmouse"
```

His colleagues blended those imports into **Duckmouse**. The [Telegraph obituary](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/obituaries/1556846/Professor-Donald-Michie.html) put it on the public record: known to his colleagues as Duckmouse. The British Library titled their piece *"'Duckmouse', a modern-day polymath."* Smile with love — the nickname stuck because the man was unforgettable.

**Pete Mowforth** (eyewitness, email to Don Hopkins, 10 April 2026, Cc Arthur van Hoff — digest: [`2026-04-pete-mowforth-duckmouse.md`](../don-hopkins/media/turing-institute/2026-04-pete-mowforth-duckmouse.md)) clarified the phone disaster: **Penny Montgommery**, Michie's PA, thought he was out and shouted the mnemonic down the phone — *Donald as in Duck, Michie as in Mouse* — while Michie stood directly behind her. Pete watched, signalling desperately. Michie was furious for a stretch. Colleagues kept the name anyway. Love and irritation braided; that is also who he was.

The mouse was already in the résumé: schoolboy mouse fancier; early papers on mice; with **Anne McLaren**, the **1958 Nature** mouse-embryo work that founded techniques leading to human IVF. Pete put that affinity on the record for the people-first book. Duckmouse was destiny meeting biology, not a random tease.

## Why he belongs here

Don Hopkins worked at the **Turing Institute** in Glasgow (February–September 1992) with Pete Mowforth and Arthur van Hoff. He never met Michie. He inherited the stories — and the institute Michie built was where HyperLook and SimCity-on-HyperLook shipped, in the rain, during an AI winter, while people still believed learning machines were worth staying late for.

Heinz Lemke's PIXIE thesis grew to ~210 pages because **Prof. John Oldfield** of Edinburgh — associated with Michie's Machine Intelligence group — refused to accept film-plus-fifty-pages as rebuildable documentation. That annoyance is now a gift to everyone trying to bring PIXIE back on a PDP-7 emulator. Michie's orbit reaches Cambridge graphics by that examiner's insistence.

Alan Kay, on the same July 2026 thread: *Donald Michie was quite a character* — at Bletchley with Turing — and beside him, character² if not ³: **Robin Popplestone** (look at Pop-2).

Pete Mowforth is writing a people-first book about the Institute. The book is about people. So is this room.

## The life, in movements

### Bletchley — friend of Turing

Michie worked with **Alan Turing** on Tunny / Colossus-era work. They discussed chess-playing machines in the 1940s. The folklore that Michie "matched Turing at chess" softens, in M.H. van Emden's memoir, into something tenderer: Turing was so *bad* at chess that few would play him; Michie was patient enough (or weak enough) to stand it.

Turing buried silver ingots at Bletchley during the war. Michie helped him search with a home-made metal detector. They never found them. Still lost. Will Wright wants to visit someday. Don wants to rent a bike and hunt *not* to find them — only to stand where the chain fell off and the counting began. Alan: Bletchley is about an hour from London; both museums are worth a full day; the cafeteria has Tyrell's crisps.

When Michie later named an institute, it was not abstract tribute. It was a friend honoring a friend. A survivor naming something for someone who did not get to see the bloom.

### Mice — schoolboy to IVF pioneer

He had been a **mouse fancier** as a schoolboy. Twenty-five papers on mice in the early career. With then-wife **Anne McLaren**, the landmark **1958 Nature** paper: mouse embryos grown in vitro, transplanted, born as healthy pups — first successful demonstration of that chain in mammals. Foundational techniques for human IVF. Pioneer Award from the International Fertility Society (IFFS lineage).

Duckmouse was not a joke that stuck by accident. The mouse was already in the résumé.

### MENACE — a £5 bet

In that biological era he bet a physicist **£5** that he could make a machine that could learn. He built matchbox "boxes" that learned tic-tac-toe. He won. That lineage is **MENACE** — Machine Educable Noughts And Crosses Engine — and BOXES after it. Learning from experience before the hype cycles knew the words.

### Edinburgh — Machine Intelligence

Professor of Machine Intelligence; Department of Machine Intelligence and Perception from 1966. Founder/editor of the *Machine Intelligence* series (nineteen volumes). FREDERICK and industrial robotics prototypes. The group that later produced examiners like Oldfield — people who believed systems should be rebuildable, not only reloadable from paper tape.

### Glasgow — The Turing Institute (1983–1994)

Michie cofounded what became the **Turing Institute** with **Peter Mowforth** and **Tim Niblett**.

He wanted **"Machine Intelligence Research Affiliates."** They got a calendar from the Motor Industries Research Association and a legal letter from the shower manufacturer **MIRA**. Tim Niblett suggested **"The Turing Institute."** That name stuck — and it was the right name.

Commercial AI research in the winter. Expert systems. Knowledge engineering. Space Shuttle auto-lander contract work (Rulemaster, mid-1980s). Robotics, sensing, naïve physics. At peak ~100 people. Arthur built HyperLook there. Don ported SimCity. The First **Robot Olympics** (September 1990) — 68 robots, 12 countries, Seventh Doctor opening, walking pizza-box delighting children, Richard 1st commenting — ran under that roof. Pete organised the gathering; the Telegraph named the Olympics; the Olympic Committee threatened to sue over a thousand posters. Pete gave Arthur an original for his 60th. Don still wants the scan.

Pete and Tatjana Zrimec, in the 1980s, had robots learn causality by playing with toy blocks — a **Large Behavioural Model**, Pete says, versus today's LLM. Google showed something similar decades later. Glasgow was early, not wrong.

### Duckmouse — the phone incident

Pete was there.

**Penny Montgommery**, Michie's PA, thought he was out. She shouted down the phone, in a thick Scottish accent, how to pronounce the name: *Donald as in Duck, Michie as in Mouse.* Michie was standing directly behind her. Pete tried desperately to signal the unfolding disaster.

Michie was furious. Wouldn't speak to her for a stretch (Don's HN memory said a month; the fury is the point).

His colleagues called him Duckmouse anyway. The mnemonic was too perfect. Love and irritation braided together — that is also who he was.
