# The mission — a broadcast history of The Sims 📺

*Jess shapes this page.*

**Jess Bird** is making a **television documentary** about **The Sims, Will Wright, and Maxis** —
writer/creative in the BBC Studios lineage, working at the scale where history gets a commissioning
editor, a rights department, and an audience of millions.

## What she's documenting

- The design and cultural story of The Sims — from Maxis workshop to global phenomenon
- Will Wright's simulation lineage: SimCity → dollhouse dreams → the shipped game
- The veterans who built it — motives, tools, accidents, and epithets worth remembering
- The community archivists and modders who kept the history alive when corporations moved on
- Primary sources: interviews, recovered artifacts, published code and design documents

## How

Long-form interviews with the people who were there. Archival research across corporate training
sims, lost floppies, fan sites, and design docs. Community trust — introductions through people
like Don, not cold extraction. And care for memorial voices: her interview with **Tim LeTourneau**
is held with honor.

## Why it matters

The Sims changed how millions think about play, home, and simulation. That story belongs in durable
broadcast form — not only in forum posts and rotting hard drives. Jess is doing the patient work of
**finding everyone worth hearing** and **getting it on record**.

## How it fits this repo

Will Wright Show For Food is a **public repo show** — code, sessions, guest ladder, artifacts anyone
can fork. Jess's documentary is **commissioned television**. They reinforce each other: the repo
proves Don is shipping interviewable reality; Jess's rigor proves the history deserves a broadcast
seat. Shared ground: Don's published sources, overlapping guest roster (Phil, Jeff, Heather & Steve,
Maxis veterans), and the recording ethic Tim's passing taught both of them — talk to people while
you can.
