# The Documentarian's Tour 📺 — this repo, through Jess's eyes

*Written for Jess Bird — and for anyone who wants to see this repo the way a documentary
producer sees it: as **story arcs, interviewees, primary sources, and footage leads**, not code.*

No GitHub account needed. Every link below is a page you can read in a browser. No code literacy
required — the code is the *subject* here, the way a violin is the subject of a film about a
violinist.

---

## The 90-second orientation

You asked: *"tell me what I'm looking at here???"*

You're looking at a **show that lives in a repository**. Instead of shooting footage and locking a
cut, Don is building the show's world in public: every **character** gets a room
([characters/](../README.md)) — guests and hosts, people whose life and work the show celebrates,
memorials to the departed, and the occasional historical, fictional, or game-born persona (SimEarth
has opinions, and speaks as Gaia). Every episode idea gets a seed
([repo-shows/](../../repo-shows/README.md)), and every primary source — 1996 lecture transcripts,
Will Wright's original Sims motive code, design documents, community archives — gets **filed where
anyone can cite it**.

For a documentarian, that means this repo is three things at once:

1. **A research archive** — sourced, dated, linkable primary material on The Sims / Maxis / Will Wright.
2. **A warm-contact roster** — people already engaged, portrayed with their consent process on record.
3. **A parallel production** — a code-first show you can point cameras at, quote, and draw from,
  which never competes with a broadcast documentary because it's a different animal entirely.

If you read nothing else, read the **evidence room** and the **people roster** below.

## Part 4 — How the documentary and the Repo Show fit together

You already have the short version from Don's email; the filed version is
[**Two documenting endeavours**](synergy/willwrightshowforfood.md). The core:

- **You document at broadcast scale.** Commissioning, rights, veteran access, the long patient cut.
- **Don ships code and interviews in public.** The repo is a living artifact your film can point
at — and its guests arrive already on record, already consenting, already sourced.
- **Neither waits on the other.** The repo show is not a rival documentary; it's the thing a
documentary about this world would want to *film happening*.

What to lift freely: primary sources, the community landscape, the consent-tracked roster.
What to ask Don for: introductions, scans, and interview footage as it accumulates.
