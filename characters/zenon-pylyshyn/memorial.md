# In memoriam — Zenon Pylyshyn (1937–2022)

*Memorial mode: we represent and discuss his work; we do not interview him or speak
as him.* [Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

Zenon Pylyshyn asked, for a living, what it means to say the mind computes. Son of
Ukrainian immigrants to Montreal, he came up through engineering and control
systems into experimental psychology, and spent his career on the boundary between
**cognition and computation** — arguing against the idea that mental images are
literally pictures, and developing **visual indexing ("FINST") theory** to explain
how attention latches onto and tracks objects. He taught for nearly three decades at
**Western University** before founding the **Rutgers Center for Cognitive Science**.

For this show he is remembered through his student **Roy Eagleson**, who carries his
questions into the classroom where the history of computer graphics — and now PIXIE
— gets taught. Roy places Pylyshyn in a striking lineage: his postdoc advisors were
**Allen Newell** and **John McCarthy**, and he later directed the CIFAR program that
hired **Geoffrey Hinton**.
