## Leo Joskowicz — 23 July 2026, 08:06 (the bottleneck is the human)

- Conversational AI won't replace graphical interfaces; "they will evolve together, as they have
  done since the PIXIE days."
- New technology is oversold, then finds actual use later, sometimes in unexpected fields.
- Generative AI lowered the entry barrier for creating UIs and visuals — welcome — but **"the
  'bottleneck' of generative AI is actually the human user"**: human understanding and learning
  is slow and cannot be dramatically accelerated.
- Teaching anecdote: Data Structures course, AI tools allowed, but closed-book handwritten exam;
  students need as much study time as 10 years ago. "Eating 5 meals in a day after not eating a
  week is not the same as eating a meal every day for a week."

## Mario Cypko — 22 July 2026, 01:16 (PIXIE as inspiration for clinical modelling)

- Pushes back on an AI company's advice to stop investing in desktop applications: "We humans are
  visual beings, and we need structured and inspectable forms of communication," especially when
  taking responsibility for decisions.
- Heinz was his **doctoral supervisor**; **PIXIE became an important inspiration** — "perhaps
  today more than ever." Key reading: PIXIE didn't just display a formal model graphically; it
  connected digital modelling with **visual and manual ways of constructing and inspecting
  circuits already familiar from the non-digital world**.
- His field: clinical knowledge modelling with probabilistic graphical models. Clinicians find
  constructing graphs in desktop apps difficult and counterintuitive; **LLMs as powerful but
  fallible translators between representations** — clinician speaks familiar language, AI helps
  transform it into a formal model the clinician can inspect, discuss, correct.
- But conversation alone is insufficient: "Drawing, pointing, arranging, comparing, and jointly
  inspecting a visual object are also fundamental parts of human communication." Structured
  models make assumptions, sources, uncertainties, and responsibilities visible.
- Offers to develop this as a **Human-AI Model Interaction** contribution for the proposed
  **CARS 2027 session**. → [`../../mario-cypko/`](../../mario-cypko/)

## Roy Eagleson — 20 July 2026, 22:39 (OOP in assembly; question for Alan)

- On Heinz's attendance list for the 1967 seminar: proof Sketchpad and PIXIE/Rainbow were "much
  more 'contemporary' than the history books reveal."
- Recalls laughing with Heinz "at all the kids who think that you can't do object-oriented
  programming in Assembly Language!"
- His lecture paragraph: building an interactive graphical editor in pure assembly instills a
  discipline — **modular → data first, algorithms later → "data are entities" → emergent
  behaviours mediated by "message passing"** — and user events and computer outputs are messages
  too. On LLM/text interfaces: "the end of the desktop metaphor? don't sell it short too soon!"
- Asks **Alan Kay** for favourite "eureka" anecdotes: Sketchpad + headsets → OOP, interactive
  graphics, desktop metaphor at PARC, the Apple site visit, inspiring Norman, Shneiderman,
  Baecker. *(Unanswered as of 24 Jul — standing question for the show.)*
