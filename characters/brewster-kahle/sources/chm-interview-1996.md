# Tim Pozar & Brewster Kahle — CHM interview (29 October 1996)

**Video:** [YouTube — u2h2LHRFbNA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u2h2LHRFbNA)  
**Interviewer:** Marc Weber (Computer History Museum)  
**Published to YouTube:** 16 March 2020 (Asilomar Microcomputer Workshop channel)

## Who speaks

- **Tim Pozar** — The Little Garden (TLG), Bay Area ISP history, Fido→Internet glue (K9Q), Tom Jennings hire, Wired magazine bandwidth, pirate radio / Ways emergency-broadcast server.
- **Brewster Kahle** — WAIS release and freeware spread; Alexa-era thinking; **Internet Archive** crawler and preservation (1996 — ~600 GB gathered, Wayback vision); early web dreams vs technician-defined stagnation; three-phase plan (open protocols → collection infrastructure → hard questions).

## Topics (from transcript)

### The Little Garden (Pozar)

- Resale-friendly ISP model; co-op origins with John Romkey, John Gilmore, Steve Crocker (~1988–89).
- Tom Jennings hired to run operations; slip accounts from Toad Hall (Haight-Ashbury); graduated to colo at 444 Market / MFS.
- Wired's first web presence saturated TLG lines; IMA and Wired were highest-usage customers before direct Sprint links.
- Restaurant in Palo Alto named **The Little Garden** — meeting place that outgrew the restaurant.

### WAIS / early web culture (Kahle)

- Alpha release posted to `wais-talk`; exponential porting to many platforms; compile-from-source norm before Mosaic binaries.
- **Ethics of digital librarianship** shipped in WAIS distributions — usage logs and librarian responsibility.
- Ted Nelson note when Internet Archive announced: "another piece of Xanadu" — Brewster's reply: fulfilling suggestions from decades prior.

### Internet Archive (1996 state)

- Robot crawling ~1M pages/day on donated Sun hardware; cache → tape; goal of comprehensive web snapshot.
- "If it's not digital it won't be saved" / "if it's digital it won't be saved without people doing what you're doing."
- Endowment intent: bits donated to IA get funding to migrate every ~10 years.
- Early search logs preserved as valuable record of what people dreamed the net could answer.
