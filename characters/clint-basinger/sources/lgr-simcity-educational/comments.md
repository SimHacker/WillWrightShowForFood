# The Comments — Wisdom of the Crowd on LGR's SimCity Educational Version

> Curated from the public YouTube comment thread on
> [LGR — SimCity Educational Version Unboxing & Overview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=edXRNtuAGTg)
> (164 comments). Public statements by real people, quoted faithfully; grouped, not editorialized into
> anyone's mouth. Companion to [`README.md`](README.md) · [`transcript.md`](transcript.md) ·
> girder [`comments.yml`](comments.yml).

**Why this page exists.** Fifteen years of comments on one retro-unboxing turn out to be a small
oral history of *SimCity in the classroom* — teachers, students, and collectors saying, unprompted,
"we played this in school," "please scan the catalogs," "I'd take that class today." That's exactly
the evidence base for the Repo Show's pitch to educators, libraries, and institutions: the appetite
is documented, in the crowd's own words. And Clint Basinger (LGR) answers in the thread himself.

---

## 3. Clint Basinger (LGR) in his own words

His replies in the thread — the guest, on the record, warm and funny:

- On the soundtrack: *"I love elevator muzak. Or in this case, The Sims 1 soundtrack. As for room, I just stash them wherever I can! Closets, shelves, wormholes..."*
- On where it came from: *"It was liberated from an old school computer lab several years ago."*
- On the collection: *"Many of the sim games I've owned for years, some of them since 1992 or so. But yeah, most of them came from eBay and local thrift stores."*
- On the price: *"About ten bucks with shipping included."*
- On the hardware: *"This Mac is from 1994 - as such it has a 3.5" floppy drive :D"* and *"Macs do crash! In fact I've probably had more Macs crash than PCs in recent years... maybe I'm doing it wrong."*
- On the sticker-melted disk: *"The problem was that it was stuck to another label. Plus heat and old diskettes do NOT mix well, like, at all."*
- On the SimCity 2000 educational edition: *"yes, there was an educational version of SimCity 2000. You can see it in the Maxis catalog shown in the video ;)"*
- On wanting to be a student again: *"I'd sign up for classes again just to take a course that used this!"*
- Identifying the mystery game at 2:14 — *"Rome: Pathway to Power!"* ... *"Maxis published it in the US, but it was developed by Firstlight."*
- On finding things: *"I just search from time to time and happen to find something cool now and then."*
- Self-deprecating, late-night editing: *"Haha, whoops, totally didn't catch that! Making videos late at night FTW."*

## 4. Maxis's educational line — facts the crowd (and Clint) pinned down

- Educational editions shown/named: **SimLife, SimCity 2000, Widget Workshop, SimTown, SimEarth, SimFarm, SimAnt, SimCity Classic, SimHealth.**
- **The "education hat" is a mortarboard** — corrected repeatedly and fondly (**@DeinonychusCowboy**, **@Markell1991**, **@Hellwyck**: *"They're Mortarboards, Clint."*).
- **Zurk's Learning Safari** (**@someperson42**) — *"Almost all the Macs at my school had it installed"*; came bundled with **Microsoft Bob**.
- **Rome: Pathway to Power** — Maxis-published, Firstlight-developed (per Clint).
- **800-99-MAXIS** — **@sergeantpanther2000** (16 likes) dares Clint to call it; **@cpufreak101** reports back: *"I just tried, it's now some dating hotline :/"*
- **Pricing** from the sheet: Lab Pack **$80**; **$600 for 50 units**; Clint paid **~$10** on eBay.
- **@AhmetwithaT**: *"I didn't know they assigned ISBN to software"* (1-56754-090-2) — a preservation/cataloging detail.
