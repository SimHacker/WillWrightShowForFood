I work on advanced packet-switching data systems.

A single communications link that collects information into datagrams and then transmits them onto an attached network.

Sort of like a handshake that introduces distant computers to each other in virtual space.

A code, exactly.

And is the post office in Japan?

Japan, Johannesburg, Bangkok, Rio — anywhere and everywhere.

In heaven.

Exactly.

Simultaneously!

And that's where what happens?

Creation.

But not quite.

The information generated by the beast still needs to be disseminated, sort of like pollen in the wind.

And that happens, well, in another room.

Think of the beast as your disembodied brain.

The machine that services your brain translates a postcard written in any one of 116 languages known to man into a universal code instantaneously.

It keeps it communicative.

It lives here.

The Interface Message Processor.

What kind of information?

Anything.

Scientists and mathematicians, [universities?], military.

For now.

So the IMP is like a traffic cop?

Traffic cop, facilitator, translator.

Keeping information moving with no snafus, no tie-ups, on an endless ribbon of virtual highway.

Going where?

To the future.
