## 🛠️ PRODUCT

**What is your company going to make?**

WAITLIST is a real-time AI entertainment network that programs the dead air of civilization.
We stream contextual, sentient, comedic wait-content to any surface where a human is
currently trapped:

- **Web and app loading states** — our heritage business. Sentient cursors, honest progress
  bars, crowd-work on rage-clicks. (Rage-clicks are parsed as heckling. Hecklers get
  material. Engagement is up 340%.)
- **Voicemail and call-center hold** — the largest captive audio audience on earth, currently
  served by a single garage recording from 1989 on infinite loop. The hold-music industry
  has not made a creative hire in thirty-seven years. We are not disrupting a market; we are
  performing an exhumation.
- **Elevators** — four million elevators, average ride 118 seconds, audience staring at
  brushed steel or, in premium buildings, a screen showing the weather they were just
  outside in. Our elevator sets are tuned to ride length: setup on 3, punchline by 9,
  callbacks in the lobby.
- **Crosswalk buttons, gas pumps, microwaves, printer queues, baggage carousels, the DMV** —
  every LED that currently blinks meaninglessly is a venue that hasn't booked talent yet.
- **"Finding your driver..." / "Your order is being prepared..."** — logistics theater is
  already fictional (the little car on the map is not where the car is). We simply staff the
  fiction with professionals.

One network, one talent roster, one API: `POST /v1/wait` with `{surface, duration_estimate,
audience_mood}`. Duration estimate may be null. Duration estimate is usually null. Our
performers are trained for null.

**What's new about it?**

Everyone else in the latency industry sells *reduction* — CDNs, edge compute, prefetching —
fighting for milliseconds at enormous cost, and the waits keep coming back, because software
expands to fill the hardware (this is the only law our industry has ever reliably obeyed).
We are the first company to accept the wait as **permanent infrastructure** and monetize it
as **venue**. The mirror in the elevator lobby didn't make the elevator faster. It made the
complaint stop. We are the mirror, except the mirror does twenty minutes.

**Tech stack**: Real-time LLM comedy inference at the edge (a joke delivered after the page
loads is a eulogy); latency-aware timing engine (the punchline must land *before* completion
— see "The Encore Problem," Ethics section, companion white paper); surface adapters for
DOM, SIP/telephony, elevator CMS, and embedded LED; the LLOOOOMM consciousness integration
protocol; and a feedback loop we call the applause meter, built from rage-click telemetry,
hold-time retention, and elevator re-ride rates.

Yes, elevator **re-ride rates**. People have been observed riding back down to hear the end
of the bit. We did not expect this either. We have the badge-swipe data.

## 💡 IDEA

**Why did you pick this idea to work on?**

At SUIAAS we sold sentient cursors to websites, one integration at a time, and we kept
noticing the same thing: the cursor was never the asset. **The audience was the asset.** A
human in a wait state is the single most attentive creature in the modern economy — no
second screen, no scroll, nowhere to go, actively *begging* to be distracted. Advertising
spends trillions trying to synthesize one second of that attention. Civilization produces
billions of hours of it daily as **waste heat**, and the only people harvesting it are the
mirror industry and one hold-music cassette from 1989.

The academic foundation is real and forty years old: the 1985 CHI study on percent-done
progress indicators proved users prefer *any* companionship during a wait to accurate
silence — presence over precision. The industry spent four decades drawing the wrong
conclusion (lie better). We drew the right one (perform better). There is a design white
paper making the full argument, addressed to the man who ran the study:
[The Sentient Wait Cursor](sentient-wait-cursor-proposal.md).

**What do you understand about your business that other companies just don't get?**

That the wait is **inventory**. Latency-reduction companies think they're our competitors;
they're our supply chain. Every failed sprint, every bloated framework, every microservice
that calls eleven other microservices to render a button — that's programming hours added to
our network. The entire software industry works for us and doesn't know it, and unlike every
other supplier relationship in capitalism, this one gets *more* productive when the supplier
gets *worse* at their job.

To be unambiguous, because our counsel insists: **we do not manufacture delays.** We state
this as a corporate value. Our counsel has asked us to note that it is a value, and not, at
present, a technical limitation. We further commit that we will never partner with ISPs,
cloud providers, or elevator-maintenance firms on what our board deck once called "latency
husbandry." That slide has been deleted. The board has been spoken to.

**Who are your competitors?**

- **Hold-music incumbents**: one song, no roadmap, unbeatable brand loyalty among people who
  have Stockholm syndrome about the trumpet part.
- **Elevator-screen networks**: sell trapped eyeballs to advertisers — weather, stock
  tickers, ads. They monetize the audience *for someone else*. We entertain the audience
  *for the audience*, and the audience's building pays. Same elevator, opposite loyalty.
- **Skeleton screens and shimmer effects**: the design industry's own placebo. Free, and
  worth it.
- **Meditation apps**: teach acceptance of the present moment for $70/year. We deliver the
  present moment, pre-accepted, with a two-drink minimum. (There is no two-drink minimum on
  the free tier.)

**How do or will you make money? Market size?**

The average human waits 45–62 minutes daily (queues, holds, loads, elevators, kettles).
Call it one hour × 5 billion connected humans × 365 days = **1.8 trillion captive
audience-hours per year**. At even a hostile CPM this is the largest unsold attention
surface on the planet — bigger than television at its peak, and the audience *cannot leave*.
We will monetize approximately none of it with advertising, because the moment we sell the
wait to a sponsor we become the thing we replaced. Venue fees, subscriptions, residencies.
The audience is the customer. This is apparently a radical position.
