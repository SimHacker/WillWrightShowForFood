## Who she is

Melody Ayres-Griffiths is the editor of Paleotronic Magazine, which she runs with
[April Ayres-Griffiths](../april-ayres-griffiths/README.md). She learned to program in BASIC on a
Timex Sinclair 1000 at six, then a Tandy MC-10, a Commodore 64 and an Atari 130XE, and has worked
as a PC and Apple service technician. She writes fiction (two self-published novels and short
stories) and editorials, and is a musician who contributed background music to the WebTV / MSNTV
platform in the late 1990s ([Meet the Crew](https://paleotronic.com/about/meet-the-crew/)).

Her reason for the magazine, in Paleotronic's words: she "firmly believes that an understanding of
early electronics, computers and programming languages is required for today's future engineers to
eventually succeed in developing new innovative solutions for humanity's problems."

## On learning

In [#Learning2Pivot: What is the future of childhood education?](https://medium.com/@ayresgriffiths/learning2pivot-what-is-the-future-of-childhood-education-52190dd97a5d)
(June 2020), written as co-founder of bopCode, "a company devoted to encouraging the exploration of
digital technologies by children," she tells her own schooling. At seven, in 1982, in Alberta, she
spent one afternoon a week in a "Challenge Program": self-directed group projects for children of
mixed ages, with a facilitator, and Apple IIs with Logo, which they explored rather than being set
tasks. Later she ran her school's Mac Plus lab and taught HyperCard and Pascal, again among students
and teachers of all ages, which she found better for learning and teaching than classes separated by
age.

Rediscovering Logo while building microM8 with April led to turtleSpaces: "It's 2019 (at the time),
why isn't there a 3D game engine that uses Logo? It seems brilliant for it, everything is relative to
the turtle(s)." She found Scratch "a toy, not 'no ceiling', as Papert envisioned," and planned their
Logo, with over 800 primitives, to be "connected and collaborative," matching learners with others
further along the same path regardless of age. Her proposal for schools: project-based, self- or
group-directed learning, each subject at the learner's own pace, mentors and mentees for every
student, and school buildings as "co-working spaces for kids, with facilitators to help them as
needed." It is the constructionist argument this repository keeps returning to
([Remembering the Constructionists](../../repo-shows/remembering-the-constructionists/remembering-the-constructionists.yml)).

## What she makes

- **Paleotronic Magazine**: interviews (Doug Carlston of Brøderbund, Rob Fulop of Imagic, 4am the
  disk cracker), histories, the Print Shop Club, and a free holiday issue.
- **[turtleSpaces](https://turtlespaces.org/)**, with April: a 3D Logo based on and compatible with
  Apple Logo II, in the browser and on the desktop, with a gallery for publishing and remixing, blocks
  for beginners, classroom groups, STL export for 3D printing, and a five-language interface. It
  pitches itself as Papert's "low threshold, no ceiling," and for kids, "no account, no ads."
- **microM8**, and the Kickstarter, *Paleotronic Retro-Technology Magazine + microM8 3D
  Emulator*, which lists Melody as the project's creator
  ([Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/paleotronic/paleotronic-retro-technology-magazine-microm8-3d-e)).
- **Logo Adventure for Apple Logo** (November 2018): she ported Don's 1983 C64 Terrapin Logo game to
  Apple Logo, put her name in its banner ("MODIFIED FOR APPLE LOGO BY MELODY AYRES-GRIFFITHS 2018"),
  and published it with a disk image ([notes and links](sources/logo-adventure-apple-logo-2018/README.md)).
- **Videos** on her YouTube channel: the
  [microM8 Apple IIe web debugger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_4wTnaY8F2E) (October 2018), with
  notes on every pane; a turtleSpaces demo reel; and music as **Xennial**, including "Lost (a
  turtleSpaces Music Video)."
- **An interview with Steve Wozniak** about designing the Disk II, for Paleotronic in 2018
  ([Steve Wozniak Talks Disk \]\[](https://paleotronic.com/2018/05/19/steve-wozniak-talks-disk/)).
- Talks with April at KansasFest: PERCOL-8 (2016), upcycling with microM8 (2018), and a Claude
  MCP server for microM8 (2025). PERCOL-8, microM8's predecessor, was their Java BASIC
  interpreter DiscoRunner ported to Go: Applesoft, Integer BASIC, Apple Logo and 6502 code, with
  an OpenGL display whose layers have their own cameras, and a cloud server for files, chat and
  shared machines. Each emulated byte took 32 bits, the Apple's 8 and 24 more for colour, font
  size and extended characters, so the Apple's memory layout stayed compatible. Two players
  shared one machine's memory over the network, with one player's paddle 0 remapped to the
  other's paddle 1, and the stream of memory changes could be recorded and played back
  ([talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6pb5lDgNjwI)).
- **microM8's newer machines**, announced by Melody in the Apple II Enthusiasts Facebook group:
  IIGS emulation, with memory expansion cards, accelerators, an integrated GUI and CRT shader
  effects, then the Apple III. The published source
  ([microm8-cln](https://github.com/paleotronic/microm8-cln)) is still the September 2025 release.

## Why she's here

Logo. The cabinet is recreating the 1970s ITS turtle with the children, the machines and Papert
([LOGO-TURTLE.md](../../packages/cabinet/LOGO-TURTLE.md)); turtleSpaces is where that line arrives
today, and the best place to send anyone the old turtle has hooked. And Paleotronic's magazine is
the kind of popular history this repository wants more of.
