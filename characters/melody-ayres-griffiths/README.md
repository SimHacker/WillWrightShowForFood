## Who she is

Melody Ayres-Griffiths is the editor of Paleotronic Magazine, which she runs with
[April Ayres-Griffiths](../april-ayres-griffiths/README.md). She learned to program in BASIC on a
Timex Sinclair 1000 at six, then a Tandy MC-10, a Commodore 64 and an Atari 130XE, and has worked
as a PC and Apple service technician. She writes fiction (two self-published novels and short
stories) and editorials, and is a musician who contributed background music to the WebTV / MSNTV
platform in the late 1990s ([Meet the Crew](https://paleotronic.com/about/meet-the-crew/)).

Her reason for the magazine, in Paleotronic's words: she "firmly believes that an understanding of
early electronics, computers and programming languages is required for today's future engineers to
eventually succeed in developing new innovative solutions for humanity's problems."

## What she makes

- **Paleotronic Magazine**: interviews (Doug Carlston of Brøderbund, Rob Fulop of Imagic, 4am the
  disk cracker), histories, the Print Shop Club, and a free holiday issue.
- **[turtleSpaces](https://turtlespaces.org/)**, with April: a 3D Logo based on and compatible with
  Apple Logo II, in the browser and on the desktop, with a gallery for publishing and remixing, blocks
  for beginners, classroom groups, STL export for 3D printing, and a five-language interface. It
  pitches itself as Papert's "low threshold, no ceiling," and for kids, "no account, no ads."
- **microM8**, and the Kickstarter, *Paleotronic Retro-Technology Magazine + microM8 3D
  Emulator*, which lists Melody as the project's creator
  ([Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/paleotronic/paleotronic-retro-technology-magazine-microm8-3d-e)).
- Talks with April at KansasFest: upcycling with microM8 (2018), and a Claude MCP server for
  microM8 (2025).

## Why she's here

Logo. The cabinet is recreating the 1970s ITS turtle with the children, the machines and Papert
([LOGO-TURTLE.md](../../packages/cabinet/LOGO-TURTLE.md)); turtleSpaces is where that line arrives
today, and the best place to send anyone the old turtle has hooked. And Paleotronic's magazine is
the kind of popular history this repository wants more of.
