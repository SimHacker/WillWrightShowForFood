Butler —

I'm **Don Hopkins**. With **Will Wright** and a growing cast of people who built interactive computing,
I'm producing **Will Wright Show for Food** — a **Repo Show**: recorded conversations whose stage is
a public GitHub repository. Sources and credit land in the open.

**Alan Kay** has already written the blurb. Asked what a computer scientist is, he answers that he's
not sure how to define it, but **"Butler Lampson is the quintessential example of a great one."**
In his Quora sketch he calls you brilliant in the sense that "the term seems to have been coined for
him" — thinker, doer, and the person who "almost literally dragged the whole field forward." We'd
like that not as legend on a wiki, but with you on the record: Genie / SDS-940, the Alto bet with
**Chuck Thacker** and Kay's kiddicomp budget, Mesa, Ethernet-era systems, and the design rule that
*"in computing, design rules change dramatically every few years."*

## What I'm asking

One or more **recorded conversations** (video call, your schedule):

1. **What "computer scientist" meant at Berkeley and PARC** — capabilities, fail-safe OS design, CAL.
2. **The Alto deal** — Lampson's "$500 PDP-10," Thacker's three-month bet, Kay's KiddiKomp memo.
3. **Minimal standardization** — disk format, later Ethernet packets — and what you refuse to freeze.
4. Optional: how that ethos travels to Microsoft / SRC, or what you'd tell a lab starting now.

**Async is fine.** Pair segments with Kay or Dan Ingalls welcome, not required.
