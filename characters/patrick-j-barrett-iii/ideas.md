## The integrated story (Don + Patrick)

### 1. The slap in the prototype
Early tree code: two women try to kiss → **violent negative interaction**. Don's Draft 3 review
(8/7/98): heterosexist/monosexist; proposed 0–100 scales per sex (Don now prefers Patrick's simpler model).

### 2. Which design doc did you have?
Team had debated removing same-sex romance; production DB may not have reflected Don's reviews.
Patrick worked from an **older doc** that still allowed it — implement first, politics later?

### 3. How you programmed it
Romantic interactions **tagged**; game tracks same-sex vs opposite-sex over time; preference
**emerges from behavior** — not a fixed personality enum. Don's retrospective: happier with this
than his 1998 model (gameplay, role-play, no false essentialism).

### 4. Will's approval
Barrett: Will liked social interactions, glad same-sex support was back. What was said in the room?

### 5. E3 1999
Two female Sims kiss during live demo — "stole the show." Expected EA to kill it; they didn't.

### 6. "Did The Sims make you gay?"
Alexander Avila's essay (he is **openly trans masc**) + **1,700+ comments** analyzed in MOOLLM —
retrospective queer recognition, hiding gameplay from parents, safe microworld. Patrick's code as
precondition for all of it.

### 7. Sims audience solidarity
Builders, modders, storytellers, creator-network — the show welcomes the congregation that grew
up in this engine. Tie to post-Collection exodus, custom content ethic, Simprov Wedding Playset.
