# Chuck Blanchard's authorship, corroborated in public sources

*Assembled for the [public portrayal](../README.md). Everything here is already
public — a personal website, a 1989 newspaper, and a mailing list post. Nothing
in this file came from private correspondence.*

## Jaron Lanier, on his own site

The clearest public statement, from the person whose name the story usually
carries:

> "Body Electric is a rare example of a successful visual programming language
> that is seductive at first approach, but also scales and has been used for
> industrial strength applications. **It was created by Chuck Blanchard.**"

He goes on, in the same place, to rate it in both directions at once:

> "The VPL software is **both the best and the worst VR software ever made.** It
> has infuriating limitations and wasn't maintained during VPL's long dark ages
> of French stupor (1992-1998), but it is also the most inspiring, seductive
> virtual world creation toolkit I have ever seen (though of course I might be
> biased)."

And on the thing almost nobody claims about a development tool:

> "The worst thing about it is that the only viable version runs on… the
> Macintosh. On the other hand… **It is absurdly fast — an incremental
> compiler.** It is also the only programming tool of which I can say that
> **debugging is as much a pleasure as making new stuff.** If you are familiar
> with programming tools, and VR tools in particular, you know this is almost a
> supernatural claim, yet it's true."

`Source: jaronlanier.com/vpl.html.`

**Read the split as a claim about which axis matters**, not as ambivalence.
Portability, maintenance and data structures: bad, and he says so without
softening. Depth of live interactivity: unmatched, and still unmatched years
later. The two verdicts are about different properties, and he is arguing that
the field optimised the ones that were easier to fix.

## Contemporaneous press, 1989

Quoted as "Chuck Blanchard, a VPL programmer" in Simson Garfinkel's *Christian
Science Monitor* piece on VPL, describing a plan to put a system in a children's
hospital:

> "…so they can leave their disabilities behind and interact with others on the
> same level."

`Source: csmonitor.com/1989/0815/d1sig.html.`

**Worth keeping for what it is**: the earliest public quotation of him is not
about the technology, it is about who it was for.

## Don Hopkins, stating it on a list

In March 2014, on a thread about visual programming languages whose recipients
included Jaron Lanier, David Levitt, Ben Shneiderman and Brad Myers, Don wrote
the attribution plainly: **"Chuck Blanchard (who wrote Body Electric)."**

Three independent public sources, three decades apart, from the founder, the
press and a colleague.
