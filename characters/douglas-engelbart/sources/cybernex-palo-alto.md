# Cybernex (Palo Alto) — who made the production mouse

Not a character room. Company notes for the hardware on [`../engelbart-mouse-and-keyset.md`](../engelbart-mouse-and-keyset.md).

**Cybernex of Palo Alto / Stanford is not Cybernex Ltd. of Ottawa.** Ottawa Cybernex (founded 1974: James Gadzala, Colin Turner, Bruce Douglas, David Londry) made video terminals (D1600, XL-80, Dasher emulators). Different company, later listed as Cybernex/Zavitz, Ottawa. Do not merge them.

---

## What the label on Don's mouse says

Underside photo: [`../media/don-cybernex-mouse-underside-sn-m026-1976-08.jpg`](../media/don-cybernex-mouse-underside-sn-m026-1976-08.jpg)

Printed factory label on the black base plate:

| Field | On the label |
|-------|----------------|
| Name | **Cybernex** (stylized blue wordmark) |
| Place | **PALO ALTO CALIFORNIA** |
| Serial | **SN M026** (handwritten in black) |
| Date | **8/76** (red stamp — August 1976) |

That is the evidence the mouse is Cybernex hardware. Not a guess from style. The word is on the object.

This is a **production-era wheel mouse** (two orthogonal metal discs, three-button, beige pebbled shell, black base, three screws, ball foot). Not the 1964 SRI redwood first mouse.

## Contemporary paper: ARC bought mice from Cybernex

1974 SRI ARC "Line Processor" paper — mice and keysets were commercial products, not only lab one-offs:

> Mice and key sets can be purchased from Imlac, Cybernex and Computer Displays.

Scan: https://dougengelbart.org/pubs/papers/scanned-original/1974-augment-20184-Line-Processor-Amplification-of-Display-Capabilities-for-Text-Manipulation.pdf

So by 1974 Engelbart's own lab named **Cybernex** as a vendor of the mouse and the keyset, alongside Imlac and Computer Displays.

## Cybernex Corporation, Stanford (1971)

*IMLAC Users Forum* Vol. 1 No. 3 (1 November 1971) lists **Cybernex Corporation** selling a PDS-1 / PDP-11 memory-and-control interface plus graphics software, hardware housed in the Imlac PDS-1.

Contact on that page:

- Robert Simmons
- Cybernex Corporation
- P.O. Box B
- Stanford, California 94305

PDF: https://bitsavers.org/pdf/imlac/IMLAC_Users_Forum_Vol1.3_110171.pdf

Same issue: Imlac itself had just added **tablet, mouse, and joystick** as PDS-1 options. Cybernex was in that graphics-terminal neighborhood (Stanford PO box; Don's label says Palo Alto — adjacent, same company is the economical reading).

The PDS-1 workstation photo kept in the private dump as *not Engelbart hardware* is the right *industry*, wrong *object*.

Christie's "1968" is MOAD-year catalog dating. Don's label is **8/76**. Believe the label for manufacture date. The 1964 wooden mouse and the 1968 demo units are earlier.

### Serial collision — do not paper over

Christie's Allen lot and Don's underside label both say **M026**.

Possible readings, none proven here:

1. Same physical mouse (then the "Don still has it / Lottor sold it to Allen" stories need a recount)
2. Christie's copied **M026** from a public photo of Don's mouse (Facebook Old Farts 2022 or similar) onto the Allen lot
3. Two units got the same handwritten serial (weak)

Look at Christie's lot photos vs Don's underside before treating them as one object. Ask Lottor which serial he sold.
