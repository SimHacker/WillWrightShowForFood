## Provenance (catalog)

Engelbart gave this pair to **Bill Daul**, a colleague on the SRI Augmentation Research Center team. RR's catalog is the public source for that chain. No character room for Daul in this repo unless Don asks.

Headline says "as used in the 'Mother of All Demos.'" Body text says "**like those used**." Treat the headline as auction copy. The underside stamp **05 - 81 4** (if a date) is 1981, not 1968.

## Catalog dimensions

| Device | Size |
|--------|------|
| Three-button mouse | ~4″ × 2.75″ × 2.5″ |
| Five-key coding keyset | ~5″ × 1.25″ × 1.5″ |

Both cords end in large black serial-style connectors (DB-25 family). Mouse connector housing is molded **ITT Cannon**.

This construction is a different unit and likely a later generation than the 1976 Cybernex-labeled mice (three screws, black base). Do not collapse them into one object.

## Catalog encoding (NLS-era usage)

RR's description of how the pair worked together matches the published NLS account:

- Keyset: five keys → **31** non-empty chords. Letters A–Z plus comma, period, semicolon, question mark, space
- Keyset supplements a middle keyboard; mouse stays in the right hand
- Middle mouse button: shift (uppercase + more punctuation)
- Left mouse button: numbers + further symbols

## Catalog history claims (RR's text, not independently checked here)

- Apple licensed Engelbart's mouse patent from SRI for around **$40,000**
- Xerox mice ~$300, three buttons; Jobs wanted a $15 single-button mouse
- IDEO designed Apple's rollerball mouse; Lisa 1983, Macintosh 1984
- 1979 PARC visit as the Jobs/GUI story

Use these as auction-house narrative, not as citations of Apple or SRI archives.
