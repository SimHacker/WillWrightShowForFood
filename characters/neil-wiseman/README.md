## Who

**Neil Ernest Wiseman** (19 May 1934 – 13 June 1995) founded and led the Cambridge
Computer Laboratory's **Rainbow Group** for thirty years. He designed the
**high-speed data-link that joined the PDP-7 and its Type 340 vector display to the
Titan** — called, in his Cambridge obituary, *"probably the world's first
distributed system."* He invented a **pen-following screen menu** (an ancestor of
the pop-up menu), held three patents and 70+ publications, and supervised **40+
PhDs**. Cambridge made a personal **Readership in Computer Graphics** for him in
1986; he turned down chairs elsewhere to stay.

## Why he's in this cast

He is **Heinz Lemke's PhD supervisor** and the lead author of both the **PIXIE**
(1969) and **RAINBOW** (1972) papers — the two documents at the center of the PIXIE
show. The machine PIXIE ran on was, in large part, the machine *he built*. Heinz
began under him on 1 October 1967 (see [Heinz's memoir](../heinz-lemke/sources/1967-back-to-the-roots-part1.md)).

Roger Needham, breaking the news of his death to the lab, called him *"of the
fabric of the laboratory."*
