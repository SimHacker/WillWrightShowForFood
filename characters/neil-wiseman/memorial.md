# In memoriam — Neil Wiseman (1934–1995)

*Memorial mode: we represent and discuss his work; we do not interview him or speak
as him.* [Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

Neil Wiseman spent thirty years quietly building the foundations of interactive
computer graphics at Cambridge. When one of the world's first minicomputers, the
**DEC PDP-7** with its **Type 340** vector display, arrived, he designed a
high-speed data-link to connect it to the **Titan** — a setup his obituary calls
*"probably the world's first distributed system"* — and around it grew the
**Rainbow Group**, the integrated CAD project that produced **PIXIE** and
**RAINBOW**. He also anticipated the pop-up menu with a pen-following screen menu,
built early screen text editors, and even connected a television camera to the
PDP-7, anticipating multimedia.

For this show he is remembered as the supervisor who took on a university-less
research student in 1967 and set him writing his first paper by year's end —
**Heinz Lemke**, who can remember him on air. Peter Robinson's funeral appreciation
described a man whose *"door was always open,"* whom Roger Needham called *"of the
fabric of the laboratory."*

We remember him by inviting those who knew him — see `CHARACTER.yml`
`memorial.who_can_discuss` (documented connections only; listing is not an
invitation): **Heinz Lemke** (his student and PIXIE/RAINBOW co-author) and
**David Rosenthal** (Cambridge, same stack, ~1970).
