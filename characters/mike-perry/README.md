# Mike Perry — *Michael Perry*

**Producer and designer at Maxis, 1996–1998.** Designer of **LunaSim**, which became **Tycho
Rising** — a lunar colony simulator that got a 49-page design document, paid focus groups, an E3
demo disc, rendered animation, **a press conference, a $40–50 price, and a Q1 1998 ship date** — and
was then shelved after the EA acquisition, in favor of SimMars, which was also cancelled.

He kept the binder. In 2019 he photographed it for the 50th anniversary of Apollo 11 and posted it
to Maxis Alumni. A year earlier, [Phil Salvador](../phil-salvador/) had photographed the game's
**press fact sheet** in the collection of The Strong / Museum of Play without knowing the designer
was in the same Facebook group. Between them, an unreleased Maxis game has a paper trail from
focus group to shelf.

## On the record

Confirmed by name in the [March 1996 Maxis internal newsletter](../will-wright/sources/1996-03-what-newsletter/README.md),
in Bryan Magers' new-hire bio — whose stated job was

> "to undo all the damage that Communications Specialist Karen Fagen, and **Producers Michael Perry,
> Andy Larson and Chris Weiss** have caused over the last year and a half."

"Over the last year and a half" puts him at Maxis by roughly **late 1994**. The same page reports
that 4% of Maxis's 203 employees were named Mike or Michael.

## Why invite him

- **A complete unreleased Maxis game, documented from both sides.** Design doc, market research, E3
  build, animation, *and* the public announcement with a ship date and a price. Most cancelled games
  leave nothing; this one has an internal draft and a press sheet that disagree about what it was.
- **Two sources who should meet.** Phil Salvador found the fact sheet in a museum in 2018; Mike
  posted the binder in 2019. Putting the designer and the archivist in one conversation over the
  same object is the show.
- **The robot-programming mechanic.** Tycho Rising's stated central innovation was that *the player
  cannot act directly* — you instruct robots and they carry it out. That is a real, unexplored
  branch of the Sim lineage, and it is the most show-worthy idea in the binder.
- **How a sim toy grew an RTS.** LunaSim (1996, open-ended) → Tycho Rising (1997, with combat) is a
  documented case of genre pressure deforming a design, and he says outright he no longer remembers
  why: *"for reasons I don't recall, but I'm sure it seemed cool at the time."*
- **What producers actually did at Maxis.** Focus-group reports, revision control on paper, art
  lock, E3 discs, the shelf.
- **The disc question.** A **Tycho Rising E3 disc (6-17-97)** and **LunaSimX Resource Files
  (4/29/98)** exist in his hands. Whether they still read is a preservation question worth asking
  on camera.
