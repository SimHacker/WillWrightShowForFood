## Who he is

Peter Korn has worked on accessibility for more than thirty years. At Berkeley Systems he and Ben
Drees developed outSPOKEN for Windows (1994), a screen reader. At Sun Microsystems he was on the
team that created the Java Accessibility API and implemented it in Swing, wrote the original Java
Access Bridge for Windows, and as Sun's Accessibility Architect brought accessibility to the Unix
and GNU/Linux desktop, largely through GNOME, whose accessibility framework, AT-SPI, screen readers
on Linux still use. After Oracle acquired Sun he was its Accessibility Principal and led the
technical work of the European AEGIS project.

At Amazon he became Director of Accessibility for Amazon Devices, where he led VoiceView, the
screen reader for Fire TV, Fire tablets and Kindle. He has represented Sun, Oracle and Amazon at
the W3C and speaks often on building accessible products
([TDIConf21 talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JgysDOR3SPU)).

## Why he's here

Don first described the idea that became
[Screen Angel](https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore/blob/main/apps/screen-angel/SCREEN-ANGEL.yml)
in an email to Peter Korn around 2013: a scripting layer that is to accessibility trees what jQuery
is to the DOM, for reading, scripting and extending applications from outside. The
[thread](https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore/blob/main/apps/screen-angel/sources/2013-email-thread.md)
is the earliest record of the design.
