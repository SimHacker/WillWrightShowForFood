## What Don's comment holds

He accidentally calls them **Grateful Dead Conferences**: the same people from Silicon Valley, the free-software community, and the conference circuit turned up at Dead shows.

**Raster Masters** hauled million-dollar SGI machines across North Shoreline Boulevard from SGI headquarters to Shoreline Amphitheatre, packed them into trucks, and toured with the Dead. Live improvisational graphics on the screen behind the band, an ensemble of their own software, several workstation video feeds mixed and fed back in real time. The software was an instrument, performed with the music, and the source plus recorded performances went out the way Dead tapes did.

At one show a concert-goer on mushrooms pointed at the screen and said he could not believe what they were making him see. Don had taken nothing, and could see the same thing.

Wired's 1994 profile, quoted in the comment: [Raster Masters](https://www.wired.com/1994/06/raster-masters/). Creon Levit: "free-form mind blowing," "live computer-graphics ensemble performance," "virtual hallucinations," "the visual equivalent of the first synthesizers." Rig: two Onyx RealityEngine2s and two Indigo2 Extremes, a 12-foot truck, five hours to set up. Levit and David Tristram from NASA Ames; Tristram then SGI, where he met Ron Fischer. Maggie Hoppe mixed video. Jonathan Nelson designed sound. Fischer: performance graphics are "puppets, directly reacting to many operator actions at once."

**Infrared Roses** — Dead live compilation of Drums and Space — carries Electropaint and other Raster Masters pictures. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_Roses) · [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gkhr23asO-M).

The comment's contrast, in Don's words: this was long before a club VJ could show up late, open a playlist, press space, and stop performing.

## Panel Library and Electropaint, as posted

David Tristram and Eric Raible, NASA NAS (Numerical Aerodynamic Simulation): GL widgets with real bevels, sometimes attached to animated 3D objects. Everybody thought they were crazy. Scheme Panel Editor. Electropaint's live UI was banks of automatic bouncing sliders — value, min, max, speed, wrap — scrubbed in performance. The Indy screensaver hid the sliders and played the recordings back. "The Indigo without the go."

Tristram's own description, from the archived history page Don quoted: Electro-Paint "is a string of bytes… It has controls… It will reward attention… It has subtle bugs. It's free." Try a little "wrst", some "zoom", maybe "flip" or "spin". Viviography — art in motion, jazz spontaneity, synaesthesia — is the 31 Mar 2019 archive of tristram.com linked from the comment. [Electropaint history](https://web.archive.org/web/20110814074647/http://darkside.cometway.com/content.agent?name=001&page_name=Article).

Ron Fischer, on that page: written at NASA before SGI; source was an SGI demo for a while, then withdrawn. Raster Masters' "guitars" were Onyx RE/2s. SGI did not believe in a live-music market. Fischer did not keep a copy of the source.

Matevz Bradac (2002) found the 1989 Panel Library posting, wrapped IrisGL as **IGL** rather than porting call by call, and called the Windows rebuild **GLectric**. Don's mirror:
