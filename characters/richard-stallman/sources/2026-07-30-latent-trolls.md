## 1. Don → RMS — 30 July 2026, 08:53 GMT+2

> Hi Richard!
>
> Remember how I snail mailed you a Copyleft (L) sticker in 1984:
>
> https://www.gnu.org/graphics/copyleft-sticker.html
>
> I'm glad I decided not to patent pie menus, because then I was free to use them in
> subsequent jobs like The NeWS Toolkit at Sun, products like SimCity and The Sims at
> Maxis/EA, and many other free software user interface components and projects like
> Micropolis City (the GPL-3 version of SimCity).
>
> But people still get confused about copyrights and patents.
>
> So we need a term like "Copyleft" that refers to patents.
>
> And the opposite of "Patent" is "Latent", so there should be a Latent Office that protects
> inventions from the people who claim to have invented them, and issues warnings to patent
> trolls:
>
> **LATENT (L)** — All rights unreserved. This invention may already exist, but hasn't
> become obvious yet.
>
> **Latentleft** — publish the invention, conceal the monopoly.
>
> **Latent Pending** — the idea is out there somewhere.
>
> **Latent Troll** — remains dormant until an industry becomes profitable.
>
> -Don

## 2. RMS → Don — 1 August 2026, 20:15 GMT+2

> Patents are unlike copyright in almost every detail, and I concluded that there is
> nothing in patent law that is analogous to copyright.
>
> This conclusion is based on some knowledge of how patents are applied for, judged, and
> if granted, subsequently enforced -- and how that is nothing like copyright.
>
> See https://gnu.org/philosophy/not-ipr.html.
