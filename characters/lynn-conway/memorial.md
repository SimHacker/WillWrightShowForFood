# In memoriam — Lynn Conway (1938–2024)

*Memorial mode: we represent and discuss her documented work and publicly shared
advocacy; we do not interview her or speak as her, and we invent no quotes or inner life.*
[Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

Lynn Conway was the "hidden hand" of modern microchip design. At Xerox PARC, with
**Carver Mead**, she co-led the **Mead–Conway VLSI** revolution: scalable design
rules, a textbook (*Introduction to VLSI Systems*) that trained thousands of students,
and multiproject-wafer infrastructure (MPC79 → **MOSIS**) that helped invent the
fabless-design + silicon-foundry industry. Earlier at IBM she contributed to advanced
computer architecture — including multiple out-of-order dynamic instruction scheduling
— before being fired in 1968 during gender transition. She rebuilt her career under a
new identity (Memorex, then PARC), later served at DARPA on Strategic Computing, and
taught as Professor of EECS / Associate Dean at the University of Michigan. CHM Fellow
(2014).

After her history became public in the late 1990s she used her platform for
**transgender rights and visibility**, documenting successes and resources so others
would not face the career erasure she had. Friend-phone clips and colleague memories
are welcome when people who knew her choose to share them.

We celebrate her documented public work; we do not speak as her.

*This is a collaborative memorial, not a signed work. Those who knew Lynn — family,
friends, students, and colleagues — are warmly invited to edit, correct, and add their
own memories, documents, and stories whenever they're ready.*
