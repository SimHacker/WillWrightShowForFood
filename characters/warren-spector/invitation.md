# For Warren Spector

Warren —

Following up on my note. Years ago you asked if I had Sims design notes or materials, and we talked about donating what I have — design documents, notes, and code — for academic research. You were collecting when most of the industry was shredding. I meant to follow through and didn't.

Here's where things stand:

- I published [94 design-document PDFs](https://donhopkins.com/home/TheSimsDesignDocuments/) — the internal design bible chapters, Jefferson-era prototype specs, the SimAntics VM and Edith tool docs, my marked-up review drafts — plus a [Scribd collection](https://www.scribd.com/collections/4050497/The-Sims) from 2012.
- [**The Soul of The Sims**](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/will-wright/sources/2008-02-10-soul-of-the-sims/README.md) — Will's January 1997 `Motive.c` prototype, the earliest glimpse of the Sims needs loop. I kept the printout, scanned it, and it's now OCR'd back into [code that compiles again](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/will-wright/sources/2008-02-10-soul-of-the-sims/Motive.c).
- [**The Sims Steering Committee demo**](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/will-wright/sources/1998-06-04-sims-steering-committee-demo/README.md) — the June 4, 1998 pre-release build shown to EA's steering committee, with Edith and SimAntics live-debugging switched on. I kept the installer for two decades; it's now [on the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/the-sims-steering-committee), and I recorded a [narrated playthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zC52jE60KjY).
- I still have fat folders of paper: printed bug reports, object-database printouts, handwritten notes and diagrams, some of which I believe are Will's.
- I still have Maxis-era email archives (`.pst` and friends), mostly unprocessed.
- In 2020, Henry Lowood confirmed Stanford's interest, and Chaim Gingold offered to connect me with Jon-Paul Dyson at the Strong Museum. Your name came up in that thread too — I'd emailed you about it once and never heard back, which I chalked up to a busy inbox, not disinterest.

**What I'd like your advice on:**

1. **Routing.** Your [papers at the UT Videogame Archive](https://repositories.lib.utexas.edu/communities/4bf6571f-a6c3-4cd2-8258-1fa5f0d76355) were the model I had in mind when we first talked. My instinct is *all of them* — Lots Of Copies Keep Stuff Safe, as David Rosenthal would say: digital scans to every archive that will take them, and the Internet Archive besides. The real question is where the physical originals should live, and how to do the handoff so each institution gets a proper finding aid instead of scattered uploads. Do deed-of-gift terms ever fight with spreading copies everywhere? You've been through this with UT.
2. **The scan pipeline.** I'm using LLMs to classify and summarize thousands of pages without flattening what makes them interesting. You've dumpster-dived for game history; I'd value your judgment on what to prioritize and what researchers actually need. This is squarely in the consulting you said you're open to.
3. **Permissions.** In 2012 I asked Will whether the material was OK to publish after all that time; the EA/MOMA questions from then may have cleaner answers now.

**And an invitation.** I host Repo Shows — recorded conversations staged on a public GitHub repo, where the artifacts under discussion are right there in the tree. Will Wright is in for the premiere. The shows I'd want you on are the ones with the archivists:

- **Game preservation panel** — with people already in the fold: Phil Salvador (Video Game History Foundation; he found and wrote the history of SimRefinery), David Rosenthal (LOCKSS founder), Brewster Kahle (Internet Archive). Your UT archive and dumpster-diving stories belong in that room.
- **PIXIE** — a preservation story you'd appreciate: I traced pie menus back to PIXIE, a 1969 Cambridge system by Neil Wiseman's group. The Cambridge University Library archivists dug up and digitized the original demo films for me. Now we're restoring PIXIE itself on an emulated PDP-7 with Heinz Lemke (who worked on it in 1969), Lars Brinkhoff, and David Rosenthal — plus educators and students in the loop: Roy Eagleson teaches the history of computer graphics at Western University, swapped PIXIE into his HCI lectures alongside Sketchpad, and his students are lined up to help with the reincarnation. Archive request → digitized film → running code, 57 years later. That's the arc I want for the Sims material too. I cut the long film down into a short music video — [**Flight of the PIXIE**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jDrqR9XssJI), set to Yuja Wang — to make the story accessible to people who'd never sit through raw archival footage.

Zero homework, remote is fine, async is fine, and "just route my archive questions and skip the camera" is also a completely acceptable answer.
