# Thread — archiving Sims development materials (2020)

Condensed from Don Hopkins ↔ Chaim Gingold ↔ Henry Lowood ↔ Terry Winograd mail, May 2020. Full nested forwards omitted; Warren Spector references preserved.

## Don to Chaim (15 May 2020)

Context: Chaim asked about audio for Don's [Will Wright talk notes](http://art.net/~hopkins/Don/simcity/WillWright.html).

Don appended Terry Winograd's confirmation that Will's **"Interfacing to Microworlds"** CS547 talk was **26 April 1996**, recorded (DVD in Stanford archive Box 12).

Then:

> I just ran across an old fat folder of papers I saved from the development of The Sims, including a whole bunch of printed out bug reports, design documents, object database printouts, hand written notes and diagrams, including some that I think are by Will, etc. I'll scan some of the good stuff — but there's a whole lot! I'd like to put them on archive.org or somewhere like that.
>
> Who do you think might be interested in archiving computer game design notes like that? **Wasn't Warren Spector working on that a while ago, and put out a request for game design notes and memorabilia? I emailed him about it once a while ago, but never heard back.** Do you know him or anybody else who might be interested?

## Chaim (16 May 2020)

- **Jon-Paul Dyson** at the Strong Museum of Play would be interested; Strong already has some of Will's notebooks.
- **Henry Lowood** (cc'd) might be interested; Chaim connected Henry with Jeff Braun previously.
- Chaim writing MIT Press SimCity book; wants Will's PCD talks transcribed.

## Henry Lowood (18 May 2020)

> I am confirming my definite interest in these materials for the Stanford collection, as it fits well with other materials we have. Of course, the Strong Museum would also be a good place for the materials. In either case, we will take good care of these documents.

## Josh Schneider, University Archivist (20 May 2020)

On CS547 recordings: only **3 lectures** cleared rights for online access so far. SCPD transferred ~**75 boxes** of recordings without a listing — processing queue.
