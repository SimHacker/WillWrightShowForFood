## Who he is

Mark S. Miller has spent his career on one question: how can programs written by different people
cooperate without being able to harm each other? His answer is object capabilities. A program can
affect only what it holds a reference to, so authority travels with references and each part of a
system gets only the authority it needs, the Principle of Least Authority.

He started on Project Xanadu. In the 1979 summer at Swarthmore he and Stuart Greene developed
General Enfilade Theory, and he was one of the lead architects of the Udanax designs through the
years Autodesk funded Xanadu (1988-92). In 1988 he and K. Eric Drexler wrote the
[Agoric Open Systems papers](https://papers.agoric.com/papers/), on markets as a way to allocate
computational resources. He worked at Xerox PARC on concurrent logic programming, co-founded
Agorics, and with colleagues from Electric Communities designed [E](http://www.erights.org/), a
language for secure distributed programming. In his words, "E's Promises are derived from Joule's
Channels and from the Promise architecture of the Udanax system"
([history of E](http://www.erights.org/history/index.html)).

His 2006 Johns Hopkins thesis, [*Robust Composition*](http://www.erights.org/talks/thesis/),
treats access control and concurrency control as one problem. At Google (2007-17) he designed the
JavaScript side of Caja and worked in TC39, the ECMAScript committee, on strict mode, Proxies,
Promises and the foundations of SES, which lets JavaScript code be confined. He is Chief Scientist
at Agoric, whose [Endo](https://github.com/endojs/endo) project carries that work forward as
Hardened JavaScript, for plugin systems and resistance to supply-chain attacks.

## Why he's here

Don is building systems where LLM agents act on behalf of people: MOOLLM's characters with
[declared permissions](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/designs/MOOAM.md), and
[Screen Angel](https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore/blob/main/apps/screen-angel/CAPABILITIES.yml),
which drives other applications only with grants the user can see and revoke. Those are exactly the
problems capabilities were designed for, and Don would like his critique. He also connects to the
Xanadu thread in this repo through Roger Gregory and Ted Nelson.
