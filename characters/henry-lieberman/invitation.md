## What I'd love to explore with you — your pick

1. **Watch What I Do, re-read in light of LLMs.** Allen Cypher now writes that "with the advent of
   Large Language Models, my dream of End User Programming has come true." I'd love to go through
   the 1993 chapters with you and ask which problems LLMs solved (inferring intent from one or two
   examples) and which they made worse (you can't see what was inferred before it acts). My take is
   [An interface to agency](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/designs/INTERFACE-TO-AGENCY.md):
   programming by demonstration and by dictation, both running two ways through one shared
   interface.
2. **The debate's missing synthesis.** You were in the room. Maybe with Pattie, if she's up for it.
   I've been discussing this with Brad Myers and Ben Shneiderman too.
3. **Tinker → today.** Programming by demonstration with multiple examples, conditionals and
   recursion, and what MOOLLM and LLM microworlds owe the lineage.
4. **NPUC Reunion (group show).** You opened the **First Annual NPUC** at IBM Almaden in 1993 with
   *Smart Interfaces to Smart Machines*, on the same program where I spoke on self-revealing
   gesture interfaces and [Ken Kahn](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/tree/main/characters/ken-kahn)
   presented *Sketches and Animations as Programs*. [Ted Selker](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/tree/main/characters/ted-selker)
   organized those workshops and says he still has **seven years** of NPUC recordings:
   [NPUC Reunion](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/repo-shows/npuc-reunion/README.md).
   Virtual IBM Burgers on Zoom optional but encouraged.

**Format:** remote is fine; in person if we overlap. **Zero homework — as much *homefun* as you are up to.**
