# Bonnie MacBird — show hooks

Public-work hooks only; [standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md).

## Segments

- **Ted Nelson, matchmaker** — three-guest arc: [Ted](../ted-nelson/) wrote *Computer Lib*,
  Bonnie found Alan in its pages, TRON and a marriage followed. Replay the
  [Intertwingled tribute](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnrlSqtpOkw), then ask both sides
  what the book meant in 1979 vs. now. "The marriage turned out better than the movie."
- **The first word-processed movie script** — she edited TRON on a Xerox Alto at PARC.
  Pair with the repo's live-objects thread: scripts as live documents then, repos as live
  shows now. What did the Alto teach a screenwriter that Final Draft never did?
- **Authorship and credit** — story credit vs. screenplay credit; the WGA arbitration
  after Disney's rewrites; how it feels when "none of your dialogue survives" but the
  characters do. Alan Bradley/Tron **is** her surviving dialogue, in a sense — a character
  based on a living computer scientist, still headlining sequels forty years on.
- **Writing Watson's voice** — six novels of disciplined pastiche for HarperCollins. Voice
  fidelity as a craft: what "writing in the authentic voice of Doyle's Watson" shares with
  this repo's portrayal standards (represent faithfully, never fabricate). She's the
  professional at the thing the repo attempts.
- **Holmes at the source** — the research move: write the book *from* Baker Street. The
  London story ([Alan's Quora answer](../alan-kay/media/quora-recaps/why-alan-kay-moved-to-london.md))
  as instance-first methodology: no plan, just following the work.
