## Who

**Bonnie MacBird** — in her own site's words, "a polymath artist" ([macbird.com](https://macbird.com/)).
Born and raised in San Francisco; BA in music and MA in film at Stanford; thirty years in the
Los Angeles entertainment business — four years as feature-film story editor at Universal,
**original writer of the movie TRON**, three Emmys for documentary work, extensive live
theatre as actor, playwright, and director, and ten years teaching screenwriting at UCLA
Extension's Writers' Program. A Sherlock Holmes devotee since reading the canon at age ten,
she now lives in London with her husband, computer scientist [Alan Kay](../alan-kay/), where
she writes, acts, paints, sings in a choir, and volunteers for the Crime Writers' Association
and the Sherlock Holmes Society of London.

## TRON — the authorship story

Bonnie **wrote the first several drafts of TRON** (1982) and shares the **story credit** with
director Steven Lisberger; the screenplay credit went to Lisberger alone after what she has
called "a rather bitter credits dispute" following Disney's extensive revisions
([her MediaMikes interview](https://mediamikes.com/2011/09/interview-with-trons-bonnie-macbird/),
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tron_(film))). She developed Tron and Kevin Flynn
from conceptual figures into characters — her Flynn was more comedic (she suggested Robin
Williams) — and she created **Alan Bradley, the creator of Tron, based on Alan Kay**, whom she
met at Xerox PARC on a research interview that stretched from thirty minutes to four hours and
ended with her hiring him as the film's technical consultant.

She then edited the script **on a Xerox Alto at PARC — the first movie script ever edited
with a word processor** (her account, in the tribute below).

## Ted Nelson, matchmaker

The repo's favorite lineage beat: [Ted Nelson](../ted-nelson/)'s *Computer Lib/Dream Machines*
introduced them. Researching the film in 1979, Bonnie found the book in LA's one computer
store, read it "cover to middle, then upside down, and other cover to middle," hit the article
about Alan Kay, "so I went up to Xerox PARC and met the guy." Alan and Bonnie recorded a
[video tribute for Ted's "Intertwingled" festival](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnrlSqtpOkw)
thanking him "for his role in us meeting up, falling in love, and getting married" (published
as ["The Two-Eyed Man"](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-16925-5_3) in the Nelson
festschrift). Their standing line: **"The marriage turned out better than the movie."**

The London move was Bonnie's doing too — she wanted to write Holmes at the source, starting at
the Sherlock Holmes Hotel on Baker Street; the rest "just gradually happened" (Alan's public
Quora answer, recapped in
[why-alan-kay-moved-to-london.md](../alan-kay/media/quora-recaps/why-alan-kay-moved-to-london.md)).
