**"70-20-10" is the most useful thing anyone has said in these threads.** A studio rule of thumb —
roughly 70% proven, 20% better, 10% new — applied by an employee, from memory, as a postmortem on a
cancelled pitch. It explains [Tycho Rising](../mike-perry/lunasim-tycho-rising.md) and
[Project Z](../byrt-martinez/project-zeppelin.md) in one line each, and it is worth asking him to
state properly on air.

### The whiteboard is the artifact

Behind them, taped across the whiteboard, are **printed spec sheets for The Sims' social interaction
menus.** Legible structure: a title ending in **"Interaction Menus"**, then four stacked blocks, each
split into two columns headed **"LTR HIGH"** and **"LTR LOW"** — *long-term relationship* high and
low — with a list of interactions under each.

That is the **social menu availability matrix**: which interactions a Sim may offer another,
conditioned on relationship score. The readable entries are the shipped social verbs — *Talk, Hug,
Kiss, Cheer up, Dance, Flirt, Entertain, Tickle, Joke, Brag, Apologize, Insult, Tease, Scare, Slap,
Attack, Fight, Play, Push, Serenade, Give Backrub, Check out, Say Goodbye* — and several rows carry
inline parameters, e.g. a *Say Goodbye* row expanding to *hug / kiss / kiss cheek / shake hands* and a
*Give Night(?)* row with the same. **Individual entries are at the edge of legibility at this
resolution**; the columns and the LTR split are not.

Two reasons this matters:

1. **It is the design of the pie menu's contents, on paper.** The Sims' social pie menus are the
   canonical example of a menu whose *items* are a simulation output — you cannot Kiss until the
   relationship permits it, so the menu is a readout of social state as much as a control. The sheet
   on this wall is the table that made that true, printed and taped up where the team argued about
   it. It belongs next to
   [`TheSimsPieMenus.pdf`](../will-wright/sources/the-sims-design-documents-zip/README.md) in the
   design-documents bundle.
2. **This is a working wall, incidentally photographed.** Nobody took this picture of the whiteboard.
   The same is true of the whiteboards in
   [Patrick's Christmas 1998 photos](../patrick-j-barrett-iii/media/README.md) — the substance of the
   archive keeps arriving in the background of jokes.
