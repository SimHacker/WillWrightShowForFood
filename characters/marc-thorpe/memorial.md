# In memoriam — Marc Thorpe (1946–2023)

*Memorial mode: we represent and discuss his work; we do not interview him or speak
as him.* [Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

**Marc Allen Thorpe** (9 November 1946 – 24 November 2023) invented **Robot Wars** —
robot combat as a live sport, an art installation, and a merchandising property — at
Fort Mason, San Francisco, August 1994. He called it an **"art sport."** The crowd
cheering for Spiny Norman at the first featherweight match is the moment he knew the
idea would prosper.

Before the arena: **ILM** model maker (1979–1990) on *Empire Strikes Back*, *Raiders*,
*Jedi*, *Temple of Doom*, and more; **LucasToys** senior designer; dolphin **behavior
sculpture** at Marineland (NEA fellowship, 1974); later **Stupid Fun Club** conceptual
designer with **Will Wright** and **Mike Winter**; **EA** mechanical engineer; BattleBots
Hall of Fame (2022).

At **Robot Wars '94** the pit held legends in embryo: **Mark Setrakian** (ILM colleague),
**Caleb Cheung** (later Furby), **Mike Winter** (X1; Lisa Winter's dad), **Charlie Tilford**
(The Mauler), **Scott LaValley** (DooLittle), and the quiet genius **Will Wright** with
**JulieBot**.

Will's [Newsweek profile (2002)](sources/stupid-fun-club-newsweek-2002.md) quotes Marc
on what Stupid Fun Club was really for: *"Will has an interest in things that test
people's tolerance. He has an amazing appetite for understanding the human condition."*
The 250-pound **Reality Robot** on Berkeley's University Avenue — Sad Robot, gender
differences in who helps vs who strips parts — is the same research program as The Sims,
in steel.

We remember Marc by carrying the sport forward: [**Repo Wars**](../../process/repo-wars.yml)
— gladiatorial *code* combat on a public GitHub stage, judges and declared rigs,
efficiency and cost beside speed. Robot Wars proved strangers will build mechanical
athletes for love of the craft; Repo Show can do the same for resurrection, translation,
and AI-assisted programming.
