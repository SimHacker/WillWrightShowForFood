# Ideas to explore with Brandel Zachernuk 🫲

*Conversation hooks — Don's proposed topics, grounded in the public record.
Not quotes from Brandel.* [Portrayal standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md)

## 0. What was the system you offered to show Ted?

First question, because it is the only one where he has information nobody else does. Don recalls the
offer from the BayCHI session; it is not in the captions. Whatever it is, it is the nearest living
relative of the webtop that we know about, built by someone who thinks about reading surfaces from
embodied cognition.

## 1. The simplicity ceiling, which he named

> hypertext authoring is a valuable thing, but it may be challenging to get it simple enough to be
> [relevant] for most people's use cases

He said that to Ted, and Ted immediately confirmed it from the inside by explaining that Scrivener
defeated him because he is lazy. So the question to put back to Brandel is the one he raised:

**What is the actual budget?** How much structure can you ask a reader to learn before they quit, and
how much can you ask an author to declare before they stop authoring? He has shipped interaction to
consumers on a new class of device; he has numbers and instincts we do not.

Our current answer is [`designs/SONG-FORM.md`](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/designs/SONG-FORM.md):
explain every structure as music, because verse/chorus/bridge, repeat signs, and skipping the third
verse are already installed in everybody at zero cost. Worth testing that claim on somebody who would
know if it is wishful.

## 2. Epistemic action, and what a mask is for

Kirsh's Tetris players rotate the block to see whether it fits. The rotation is not progress; it makes
the thinking cheaper. Brandel publishes this on his own resources page.

The webtop's bet is that **a path plus an attention mask is an epistemic artifact you can hand to
another person** — you did the rotating, and now their thinking is cheaper. Which raises the question
Brandel is positioned to answer: does epistemic offloading survive transfer? Kirsh's players were
helping *themselves*, in the moment, with their own hands. Does a recorded route do anything for a
stranger, or does the benefit live entirely in the doing?

If it does not survive transfer, the whole path-and-mask argument needs to be about **authoring** rather
than about sending, and that would be worth knowing early.

## 3. Reading surfaces in space

The webtop vocabulary is textile: authored cloth, the reader's rebound frame, tours as embroidery
couched over the top and changing nothing underneath
([`TOURS-AND-PLAYLISTS.md`](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/designs/webtop/TOURS-AND-PLAYLISTS.md)).
That was worked out for screens.

He builds for headsets, where the frame is the room and the body is the cursor. Does the cloth/frame
distinction hold when the reader can walk around the document? Is a tour in a spatial medium still an
overlay, or does it become a route you physically take — and if so, does that make Ted's parallel
side-by-side hypertext finally cheap, since space is the one resource single-column text was starving
for?

## 4. Origins of writing, and the long view

His stated framing goes "back to the origins of writing and beyond." That is the same move Ted makes
(hypertext as the extension of literature) and the same move this repo keeps making (song form, paste
pots, the Memex). Worth an hour on its own: what does the pre-history of writing tell you about which
interaction affordances are learnable versus which ones people have to be trained into?

## 5. Future of Text

He is in that community and Ted is adjacent to it. Ask who else is worth talking to, and whether the
community has already argued out any of the above — the fastest way to avoid rediscovering something
badly.

## 6. The honest pitch to make to him

Not "look at our thing." Rather: here is a design brief you personally wrote in 2021 in one sentence,
here is the most famous hypertext author on earth confirming it in the next breath, and here is what
we built while trying to satisfy both. Tell us where it fails.

He asked Ted a critical question instead of a reverent one. Give him something to be critical about.
