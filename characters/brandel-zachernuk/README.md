## Why him, for the webtop

He asked Ted Nelson the sharpest question in the 2021 BayCHI Q&A, and he was right:

> It strikes me that hypertext authoring is a valuable thing, but it may be challenging to get it simple
> enough to be [relevant] for most people's use cases.

Ted answered by explaining why he had bounced off Scrivener:

> Basically I was simply daunted by its complexity. **I'm lazy, and not eager to spend hours delving
> into the complexities of a particular piece of software.**

The objection and its confirmation, one minute apart, from the man who coined the word. That pair is
the webtop's acceptance test, and Brandel is the person who stated it.

## What he actually does

Builds spatial web interaction at **Apple, in the Vision Products group**, and reasons about it from
embodied cognition rather than from widgets — his own words: a long view "back to the origins of
writing and beyond," extended mind, and human neuroplasticity. He is in the **Future of Text** and
**Near Future Laboratory** communities, which is very likely how he and Ted overlap.

His [resources page](http://www.zachernuk.com/) is a short course in the theory the webtop needs, and he
curated it years before we needed it: **Andy Clark** on extended mind, **Barbara Tversky** on gesture as
thinking, **Sunny Bains** on sensory prosthesis, and **David Kirsh** on thinking with the body.

The Kirsh entry is the one that matters most here. Kirsh's 1994 study of expert Tetris players — who
rotate a block just to see where it fits — gave us **epistemic action** as distinct from **pragmatic
action**: moves you make not to advance toward the goal but to make thinking cheaper. That is the
missing citation for what an attention mask *is*. A path plus a mask is an epistemic artifact you hand
to somebody else so their thinking is cheaper. See
[moollm `designs/PAIRED-LINKS.md`](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/designs/PAIRED-LINKS.md).
