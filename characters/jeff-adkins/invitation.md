# Repo Show invitation — Jeff Adkins (SimObliterator) 🪓🧬

Hey Jeff,

You already said yes to the important part, so this is me writing it down in the open, where your
fingerprints belong.

Here's the plain version, no hype — the work speaks for itself. You built **SimObliterator** (which inspired me to make **VitaMoo**): the tools that read, write, analyze, and render The Sims' saves, objects, and
characters. The whole show turns on one hard, unglamorous problem — getting a character *in and out*
of a game: reading a 25-year-old save file, understanding the objects around it, writing it back
without breaking it. You went into the file formats, the GUIDs, the objects, and made it work. That's
the layer I've been calling the **soul mover**, and it's real because you did that.

There's a story on this show I keep coming back to, and it's why I want to say this the way I'm saying
it. **Doug Hilsinger** re-recorded Brian Eno's whole *Taking Tiger Mountain* album by hand — copying
it, mutating it, making it his — and one night, before I knew the record was Doug's, I heard it and
told him how much I loved it. Loving the work before you know whose it is: that's the realest kind of
tribute there is. **What you and I are doing with this code is the same shape.** You built
SimObliterator with AI by studying code I'd documented and put out there, plus open-source
Sims-family reimplementations — different languages, read and translated and verified by AI. Now I'm
rewriting your work into TypeScript, also with AI. Each version feeds the next and gets better. AI
copying AI copying people, and back again — Eno, cellular automata, stone soup, the cauldron,
Wolfram. Nobody strip-mined; everybody credited. That's not flattery, it's just what happened — and I
think it's beautiful.

It also puts you somewhere specific. Will Wright has talked for years about the **pyramid of players**:
a wide base of people who play, and fewer as you go up who tinker, mod, and **build tools** — the ones
who make the game better for everyone below them. You went from playing The Sims, to reverse-
engineering it, to building the tools other people use. That's the same climb **Heather (SimFreaks)**
and **Steve (SimSlice)** made. It's just true, and it's worth naming.

And it lands at the front of the show for a real reason: the first episode is **Will's 1996 Stanford
talk**, where he calls porting data between games the holy grail. Your work is a big part of how that
becomes something we can actually run.

So — I'd like you on the show: to talk, to show your stuff, and to explain how SimObliterator got this
started. **Low-pressure and time-shifted** — record a **solo video** whenever you like, and/or a
relaxed **one-on-one**, and I edit them together into a longer show. (Three busy people on one live
stream across time zones is the hard ask; one-on-one, edited, is easy on everyone.) Even if you never
record a frame, the credit stands — the code is part of what made this possible.

Here's the concrete engineering, so you can see where you'd plug in — because your work kicked off two
things. First, it got me writing another **clean-room version of the Sims VitaBoy character-animation
system** — like the clean-room VitaBoy I once did in Unity3D, but this time in **TypeScript, running in
the browser.** Second, it made me realize I could just have **Claude port your Python straight to the
browser too** — because your code is solid and well-organized and already does a ton of the hard
stuff, like **analyzing SimAntics programs.** We need exactly that for the content side: a **"file
cop" / "virus scanner"** for Sims content — **detecting missing objects and broken user-created
content, repairing it, and locating the original sources back in the catalog.** That's a big, real
chunk of work and it's squarely in your wheelhouse.

The bigger build, if you want it: a real **home for all the user-created Sims content** — SimFreaks,
SimSlice, Zombie Sims, the Simprov Wedding Playset — reviewed, analyzed, patched, and upgraded, **on
GitHub**, where anyone can file an issue on a Sims object, say what's wrong, and fix it together. I'd
love you leading a big piece of that: **the content-management features and the back-end site.**
You've been looking for technical people to build this with. So have I.

And there's a bigger role here too, if you want it — beyond the code and the interview: help me
**produce and manage the show behind the scenes.** Wrangling the repo, prepping and coordinating
guests and segments, keeping the pipeline of interviews and edits moving, handling the day-to-day so
the whole thing doesn't live or die on me alone. You've got the web chops and the organizing instinct
for it, and honestly I need the hands. We can work out the shape of that — and anything beyond the

And thanks — you've offered to help however's useful, and there's real work here to do. It's a
