The man behind the **TrackPoint** (that red nub between G/H/B that keeps your hands on the keys). He ran IBM Almaden's **NPUC — New Paradigms for Using Computers** workshops — the same "new paradigms" venue where **Don demoed Multiplayer SimCity** — championed **considerate computing** at the MIT Media Lab, and co-directed the **Caltech/MIT Voting Technology Project** after the 2000 election. Input efficiency and attention-respecting design are right in this show's wheelhouse.

The TrackPoint's actual design content is the **pressure-to-speed transfer function** — two plateaus,
one for predictable slow positioning and one just below eye-tracking speed, because above that you
lose the cursor. A plateau is a detent one derivative up, which makes it the ancestor of the friction
strips in the geometry-manager work. [Written up with Don's accounts as
source](sources/trackpoint-transfer-function.md), along with the father who designed the non-skid
rubber, the bag of spare Joy Buttons in the car, and the two-TrackPoint prototype that OS/2 could not
cope with.

He gave **six lectures in Terry Winograd's Stanford CS547 seminar** (1993–2007) — the same series that holds Will Wright's 1996 *Interfacing to Microworlds* talk. Five stream from Stanford; the 1993 **TrackPoint** lecture is still tape-only in Box 1. [The lectures + rescue notes](sources/cs547-lectures.md).
