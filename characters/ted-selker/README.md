Ted Selker invented the TrackPoint, the red pointing stick between the G, H and B keys that lets you point without leaving the keyboard. He ran IBM Almaden's NPUC (New Paradigms for Using Computers) workshops, where Don demoed Multiplayer SimCity. At the MIT Media Lab he worked on considerate computing, and after the 2000 election he co-directed the Caltech/MIT Voting Technology Project. His work on input efficiency and on design that respects the user's attention connects to several topics planned for this show.

The core of the TrackPoint design is its pressure-to-speed transfer function. It has two plateaus:
one for predictable slow positioning, and one just below the speed at which the eye can track the
cursor, since above that you lose it. A plateau works like a detent one derivative up, and Don sees
the same idea in the friction strips of the geometry-manager work. [Written up with Don's accounts as
source](sources/trackpoint-transfer-function.md), along with the father who designed the non-skid
rubber, the bag of spare Joy Buttons in the car, and the two-TrackPoint prototype that OS/2 could not
cope with.

He gave six lectures in Terry Winograd's Stanford CS547 seminar (1993–2007), the same series that holds Will Wright's 1996 *Interfacing to Microworlds* talk. Five stream from Stanford; the 1993 TrackPoint lecture is still tape-only in Box 1. [The lectures + rescue notes](sources/cs547-lectures.md).
