## Who

**Sir Maurice Vincent Wilkes** (26 June 1913 – 29 November 2010) ran the
**Cambridge Mathematical Laboratory** (later the Computer Laboratory) from 1946 to
1980. He built **EDSAC** (1949) — the first practical stored-program computer in
regular service — invented **microprogramming** (1951), wrote the first paper on
**cache memory** (1965), and co-wrote the first programming textbook (Wilkes,
Wheeler & Gill, 1951), which introduced subroutine libraries. His lab built the
**Titan** and the **Cambridge Ring**. Turing Award 1967; FRS; knighted 2000.

## Why he's in this cast

He is the reason PIXIE happened at all. In [Heinz Lemke's own account](../heinz-lemke/sources/1967-back-to-the-roots-part1.md),
Wilkes received a university-less 22-year-old mathematician on a colleague's
introduction, insisted he give a talk to the whole lab, and then — as a member of
the Academic Senate who knew of a 13th/14th-century statute permitting exceptions —
arranged a **PhD with no prior degree and no funding**, convincing Plessey it was a
good investment too. The **Titan** and the **PDP-7 ↔ Titan** link his lab hosted are
the machine PIXIE ran on.

Roy Eagleson's half-joke — *"does Maurice Wilkes have an avatar in the SIMs
milieu?"* — is answered here: he does now.
