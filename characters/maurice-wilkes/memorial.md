# In memoriam — Maurice Wilkes (1913–2010)

*Memorial mode: we represent and discuss his work; we do not interview him or speak
as him.* [Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

Maurice Wilkes built the machine the whole field grew from. **EDSAC**, running in
Cambridge in May 1949, was the first practical stored-program computer in regular
service — and Wilkes immediately turned it toward *use*: subroutine libraries, the
first programming textbook, **microprogramming**, the first paper on **caches**, and
the **Titan** time-sharing system. He led the Cambridge Mathematical Laboratory for
34 years and shaped generations of computing in Britain.

For this show he is remembered less as a monument than as the man who, on the
strength of a single afternoon talk, found a medieval loophole to admit a
penniless German mathematician to a Cambridge PhD — and so made **PIXIE** possible.
That story is told first-hand in [Heinz Lemke's memoir](../heinz-lemke/sources/1967-back-to-the-roots-part1.md).

We remember him by inviting people who knew him or work in his lineage to share
memories and context — see `CHARACTER.yml` `memorial.who_can_discuss` (documented
connections only; listing is not an invitation): **Heinz Lemke** (whose PhD Wilkes
arranged), **David Rosenthal** (Cambridge, same PDP-7/Titan stack ~1970), and
**Roy Eagleson** (who teaches this history).
