You built the heart, brain, and bladder of **The Sims** — the behavior engine, smart objects, the motive system, the blue puddles — and I'd love you at **Sims Fest**, our Maxis reunion Repo Show. The stage is a public GitHub repo; we talk; ideas can become runnable artifacts on stream. **Show, don't tell.**

You were Will's faithful and original mentee through the long **Project X** years — the ones that only shipped as

I've fully adopted first class advertisements into my latest project MOOLLM, because they're the missing magic ingredient for orchestrating AI driven character simulations!

**One thing to review first, because it names you with love.** I wrote up
[the record](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/patrick-j-barrett-iii/same-sex-relationships-the-record.md)
correcting the "outdated design doc" myth about how same-sex relationships got into The Sims.
The early prototype's kiss-then-slap reaction was a zeroth-order sketch, probably yours, and
the record says exactly what I believe: zero blame, a brilliant engineer's quick first pass to
get Sims interacting at all, and a textbook case of why diverse eyes on a team catch what no
one person can. Ian Bogost calls it procedural rhetoric -- code makes arguments nobody wrote
down or meant. You built behavior machinery so expressive it convinced the world that Sims had
inner lives; the slap was just the one argument nobody meant it to make. Please read it and
correct anything I got wrong -- your memory of that code beats my archaeology. The 1998
marked-up design docs and the SimAntics tree-code screenshots are
[archived in the repo](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/don-hopkins/sources/sims-design-documents/README.md) too.

**Want the keys?** Tell me your GitHub account and I'll give you write access -- no PRs, we're
in push-to-main mode. Your directory is yours to edit, prune, or grow. And if you have ANY old
code or documents from the Maxis days -- prototypes, SimAntics trees, design notes, even
SimCircus -- upload them, runnable or not. LLMs make dead code walk now. That's the whole point
of the repo: get the great designs and prototypes out where people and machines can learn from
them and bring them back to life.

- **Then and now** — modern tools, AI, and what you'd still refuse to automate.
