# Randall B. Smith 🪄

*Invitation guest — consent not yet asked. [Portrayal standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md).*

**The other designer.** Self has two names on *The Power of Simplicity* (OOPSLA 1987):
David Ungar and **Randall B. Smith**. Ungar's public credit for the Self years runs through
Randy every time; so does Don's — "you (and Urs, and Randall)."

**Before Self: the Alternate Reality Kit.** At Xerox PARC, Smith — a physicist — built
**ARK**, an animated world where simulation objects have mass and velocity and you build
physics experiments by grabbing, throwing, and wiring things with a hand cursor. His CHI
1987 paper named the **tension between literalism and magic**: make the world behave like
reality and it's learnable; give it magic and it's powerful; every interface picks its spot
on that line. UI designers have been standing on that sentence ever since — Don included:
a pie menu is a magic act wearing a literal costume.

**With Ungar at Sun Labs.** *Programming as an Experience: The Inspiration for Self*
(ECOOP 1995) is the design half's manifesto — Self as an *experience* of working among
live objects, not a syntax. **Us** explored subjective programming: one object, many
perspectives, layers as first-class context. **Kansas** put everyone in one huge flat
shared world (so flat they named it Kansas) for collaborative programming and
distance-learning experiments. And **Morphic** — built with John Maloney for Self 4.0 —
became the direct-manipulation UI framework that jumped to **Squeak**, powered **Etoys**,
and seeded the liveness lineage (Scratch's ancestors, Lively Kernel, Squeak-descended
worlds) that the live-objects trail on this show keeps returning to.
