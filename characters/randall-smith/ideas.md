# Randall B. Smith — conversation hooks

*Menu, not homework. Pick any, skip the rest.*

## 1. Literalism vs magic — the sentence UI design still stands on

ARK, CHI 1987: make the world literal and it's learnable; give it magic and it's powerful.
Where did you land then, and where would you land now — when LLM interfaces are almost pure
magic with a chat-shaped literal costume? Don's corollary: a pie menu is a magic act
wearing a literal costume (distance and direction are literal; the powers are spells).

## 2. The Alternate Reality Kit — physics as programming

A physicist builds a world where objects carry mass and velocity and students discover
laws by throwing things. ARK next to Etoys, next to The Incredible Machine, next to every
"physics sandbox" since: what did ARK get that its descendants dropped?

## 3. The Power of Simplicity — the design half of the story

Two designers wrote that paper. What did you and David argue about? What almost didn't
make it in — and what got cut from Self that you still miss? (Urs at the table can answer
what the cuts cost the compiler.)

## 4. Programming as an Experience

The ECOOP '95 manifesto: Self wasn't a syntax, it was the feeling of working *among* live
objects. That feeling is the target MOOLLM chases with directories-as-rooms and objects
you walk into. What makes the experience — liveness, directness, uniformity, or the
company you keep in the world?

## 5. Us — subjective objects, thirty years early

One object, many perspectives; context as a first-class layer. Us reads today like a
design for personalization, feature flags, and multi-agent views done honestly. Why did
subjectivity never ship, and should it now?

## 6. Kansas — everyone in one flat world

A shared space so big and flat they named it Kansas; distance learning by living in the
same world as your students. Twenty-five years before "metaverse" discourse, no avatars
needed — just shared live objects. What actually worked?

## 7. Morphic — the UI framework that outlived its language

Built with John Maloney for Self 4.0; jumped to Squeak; powered Etoys; echoes in every
liveness-first framework since. Direct manipulation all the way down — every visible thing
grabbable, inspectable, live. What's the Morphic test that modern toolkits still fail?

## 8. Trio beat — two designers and the implementer

Ungar & Smith wrote the simplicity; Hölzle paid for it in compiler engineering. The
three-way argument every language team has — how much magic can design demand of
implementation? — with the people who settled it once, in public, in a spiral-bound manual.
