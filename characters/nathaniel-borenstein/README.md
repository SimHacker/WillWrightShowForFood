## Why he's here

**Nathaniel S. Borenstein** co-created **MIME** with Ned Freed — RFC 1341 (1992) and its
successors — the standard that turned email from plain text into a carrier for images,
audio, and every attachment since. The first multimedia MIME message carried a photo of
his barbershop quartet, the **Telephone Chords**, and a recording of them singing about
it. Email that sings is not a metaphor here; it is the founding artifact.

Before MIME he built the **Andrew Message System** at CMU's Information Technology
Center — the same Andrew project where **James Gosling** and **David Rosenthal** built
the window system. After it he wrote **metamail**, then designed **ATOMICMAIL** and
co-designed **Safe-Tcl**: programs delivered inside email messages, *behavior on the
wire* years before Java applets — with all the security lessons that implies. His 1991
book *Programming As If People Mattered* is a humane-software manifesto that has aged
better than most software. In 1994 he co-founded **First Virtual Holdings**, the first
internet payment company. Later: IBM Research, then chief scientist at Mimecast.

His most-quoted line:

> "The most likely way for the world to be destroyed, most experts agree, is by
> accident. That's where we come in; we're computer professionals. We cause accidents."
