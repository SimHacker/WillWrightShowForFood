# Conversation hooks — Nathaniel S. Borenstein

1. **The email that sang.** The first multimedia MIME message: a photo of the Telephone
   Chords and the quartet singing. What it took to get a song through 1992's mail
   infrastructure, and what it felt like to press send on the format the whole world
   would use — and abuse — forever after.

2. **MIME as constitutional law.** Designing a format knowing you can never revise it
   once deployed. What he got right, what he'd change, and the boundary between
   liberal-in-what-you-accept and security hole (Postel's principle, stress-tested by
   thirty years of spam and malware).

3. **Behavior on the wire, round one.** ATOMICMAIL and Safe-Tcl put programs inside
   email before Java put them in browsers. The sandboxing lessons active mail learned
   first — and Java relearned in public. Cross-examine with Gosling: NeWS sent
   PostScript programs to your display; Safe-Tcl sent Tcl programs to your inbox.
   Three answers to "send a program, not a data structure."

4. **Andrew reunion.** CMU ITC, early 1980s: Borenstein on the message system, Gosling
   and Rosenthal on the window system. What Andrew got right a decade early —
   multimedia, distributed fileystem-backed campus computing — and why the pieces that
   escaped (MIME, NeWS-by-way-of-SunDew) outlived the whole.

5. **Programming As If People Mattered (1991).** A humane-software manifesto from before
   "UX" was a job title. Which of its noble delusions came true, which are still
   delusions, and what "friendly programs" means when the program can talk back.

6. **First Virtual (1994).** Internet payments before SSL, before the web could really
   hold them. Selling trust on a network built for openness — and what today's crypto
   and payment stacks still haven't solved that they had working in 1995.

7. **We cause accidents.** His most famous line is about computer professionals
   destroying the world by accident. He now describes his mission as helping people
   "envision working together with intelligent machines to build a better world."
   Between those two sentences is an entire show about AI.

8. **Retirement as freedom.** "No one tells me what to do and I rarely get paid much,
   if anything" — the same energy as this repo. What an Internet Greybeard chooses to
   build when nothing is required of him: the SIMH lightpen and AM radio drivers are
   formally on offer. PDP-7 code you can dance to, conducted with a light pen baton
   through PIXIE pie menus.
