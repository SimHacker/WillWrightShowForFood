# Ideas to explore with David Crane 🕹️🏠

*Conversation hooks for a Repo Show — **Don's proposed topics**, each grounded in Crane's public work
and his own published statements. Things Don would love to follow **with** him; not quotes from him
about any of this, and not claims about what he thinks now.*
[Portrayal standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md) · invitation guest

## What Crane has done

One of the four Atari programmers who founded Activision in 1979, and the author of Pitfall! (1982)
and Ghostbusters (1984). In 1985 he took Rich Gold's passive Pet Person concept, rewrote about half of
it to make the character respond to the player, and shipped Little Computer People — which Will Wright
names as an ancestor of The Sims. He co-founded Absolute Entertainment in 1986.

## The hooks

1. **Where is the Apple ][ brain block?** He documented the C64 version's 256-byte personality block in
   an email to the Software Preservation Society, and he programmed that version himself. The Apple ][
   release (December 1985) has never been publicly documented, and it is the platform this project's
   [retro stack](https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore/blob/main/documentation/designs/apple-ii-floppy-bridge.md)
   targets first. Best case: he remembers. Good case: he says who would. Either beats a disassembly.

2. **Reading a person off a disk, live, on camera.** Boot a real serialized 1985 floppy in a browser
   emulator, find the block, and show him his creature's accumulated state as editable text — forty
   years of nothing having touched it.

3. **The fishbowl argument with Gold.** He rewrote half the program to add interactivity that Gold
   thought contradicted the premise. That is the agents-versus-direct-manipulation argument, run to
   completion, in 1985, on one house. What would he do now — and does he think Gold was wrong, or just
   early?

4. **Identity stamped at the duplicator.** Modifying the disk duplicator's code to serialize every
   copy, then seeding a personality from that number, is a design nobody has repeated: uniqueness by
   manufacture rather than by database. What else could have been done with it, and why did it stop?

5. **The Turing-test remark, taken seriously.** He said the ambition was impractical given the software
   business's time constraints. Both halves of that constraint are gone. What does he want the little
   person to be able to do now, and what should he still not do?

6. **The sequel that got dropped on the floor.** Producer Sam Nelson's LCP apartment complex —
   watching people interact, working several houses at once — was prototyped and shelved. Moving
   persons off individual disks into one modern town is that design executed from outside. Does it
   match what they had running in the office?

7. **Where the person goes.** [Tiny Life](https://tinylifegame.com/), made by one developer with
   household export and open modding: an homage cover of The Sims played in the key of The Sims 0. It
   has in soul what it lacks in resolution. What survives a move like that, and what should be
   refused?

8. **Pitfall! and the room, not the person.** Optional and separate: 256 rooms from a polynomial
   counter, the same generative trick used one year later to make people instead of jungles. Same
   mechanism, different noun.
