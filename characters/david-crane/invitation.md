Dear David,

I'm Don Hopkins — I worked on The Sims at Maxis, doing the pie menus and content tools. I want to
talk with you about the 256 bytes on a floppy disk that held a person.

**Ask:** a **Repo Show** — a friendly recorded conversation whose stage is a public GitHub repo, where
the ideas we talk about get built and credited in the open. **Will Wright is in — he signed on for the
[premiere](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/repo-shows/will-wright-premiere/README.md) and more.**

**Why you, specifically, and not just for the anniversary.** We are building bridges that carry a
character out of one game's save file and into another's. Little Computer People is the headwater
target: the first commercial computer person, whose personality persisted to disk fifteen years
before The Sims. And the only public account of where that state lives is your own email to the
Software Preservation Society — the 256-byte block holding the LCP's brain, rewritten by the user's
drive as he aged, all zeros on a virgin diskette. That paragraph is doing a lot of load-bearing work
in our design documents, and it documents the C64 version you programmed yourself. **Nobody has
documented the Apple ][ layout.** We would like to find it, and we would rather ask you than
brute-force a disassembly.

**What we would actually do on the show.** Boot a real serialized 1985 disk in a browser emulator,
read the brain block live, and show you the little person's state as editable text — the personality
your serial-number-seeded polynomial counter generated at the duplicator, forty years later, still
there. Then move him somewhere he has never been able to go.

**Where he goes.** [Tiny Life](https://tinylifegame.com/) is a modern life sim made by one developer,
Ell, with household export and open modding — an homage cover of The Sims, played in the key of The
Sims 0. It has in soul what it lacks in resolution. Ell has already said the bridge idea sounds
interesting, and his export format is nearly the right shape.

Which gets us to the thing I most want to tell you: **your producer's dropped sequel is what a bridge
does.** Sam Nelson described it as an LCP apartment complex — *"more interesting to watch the
interaction between the people, study the relationships that form... able to do things in several
houses at once"* — and it never shipped because Activision wasn't sequelling things then, so it got
*"dropped on the floor."* Moving persons off individual 1985 disks into one modern town is that
design, built from outside, by strangers, four decades late. Your person gets neighbours.

**And the question you raised in 2005.** You said part of you wanted to make him the smartest thing
in computing, maybe pass the Turing test, but the constraints of the software business made it
impractical. That constraint is gone. I would like to hear what you would do with it now, from the
person who had the idea when it cost everything.

**Rich Gold.** He died in 2003. He conceived the thing and you made it interactive, and the two of
you disagreed about exactly that — which is a real design argument worth having on the record rather
than smoothing over. There is a room for him in the repo and the reincarnation is the tribute.
