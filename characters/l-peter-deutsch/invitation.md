Peter —

I'd like you on a **Repo Show**: a friendly recorded conversation whose stage is a public GitHub
repo, carried through to working code and credited ideas in the open.

The hook is your **Basic PDP-1 LISP**. I'm building a live-coding emulator room for old machines,
the "cabinet", where every word in core is linked up through its source to the scan of the page it
was printed on. It already runs Heinz Lemke's 1972 PIXIE on a PDP-7 and Type 340, assembled from
the transcribed lineprinter listing and checked word for word against it, and Mitch Bradley's new
PDP-7 Forth. A PDP-1 is next, and your Lisp is the program I most want to show on it: the REPL
running, and for any instruction you stop on, its line in your and Edmund Berkeley's 1964
listing, the rectangle on the scan, the paragraph that explains it, and what people have written
about it since. I'd love to walk through it with you and hear how it really worked.

From there, your pick:

- **From the PDP-1 to PS**: an interpreter in 4K words to translating Smalltalk bytecodes to native
  code on the fly, and the JIT line after it.
- **PostScript from both sides**: you wrote Ghostscript; I wrote PostScript in NeWS and HyperLook
  at Sun.
- **The Deutsch limit**, revisited against today's visual and block languages.
- Or anything else, including your music, if you'd like.
