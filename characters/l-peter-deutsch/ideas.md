# Ideas to explore with L Peter Deutsch ⚙️

*Conversation hooks for a Repo Show: **Don's proposed topics**, each grounded in Peter's
public work. Things Don would love to follow **with** Peter, not quotes, not claims about what
he thinks.*
[Portrayal standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md) · invitation guest

## What Peter has done

Basic PDP-1 LISP (1963); TRAC's first implementation (1964); the Berkeley Timesharing System on
the SDS 940; Interlisp and Smalltalk at PARC, including PS, the dynamically translating
Smalltalk-80; the Eight Fallacies of Distributed Computing at Sun; Ghostscript; the zlib, DEFLATE
and gzip RFCs; and now music. Sources: [CHARACTER.yml](CHARACTER.yml).

## The hooks

### 1. Basic PDP-1 LISP, live, with its own listing beside it

Bring up a PDP-1 in the cabinet, load Basic PDP-1 LISP, and type at its REPL. Stop on any
instruction and follow it up the layers: the disassembly, the line in the 1964 listing, the
rectangle on the scanned page, the paragraph of the Deutsch & Berkeley document that explains
it, and later commentary. What did a 17-year-old leave out of Lisp 1.5 to fit it in 4K words,
and how did the REPL work?

### 2. From the PDP-1 to PS: dynamic translation

The line from an interpreter on a 4K machine to PS translating Smalltalk bytecodes to native code
as they ran, and on through Self, HotSpot and V8. David Ungar would be a natural co-guest.

### 3. PostScript from both sides

Peter wrote Ghostscript; Don wrote PostScript programs in NeWS and HyperLook at Sun. A PostScript
interpreter as a live programming environment: what Ghostscript's internals know about the
language that its users never see.

### 4. The Deutsch limit, revisited

Peter's adage about visual programming (more or less: you can't have more than about 50 visual
primitives on the screen at once) against today's block languages, node editors and the
cabinet's own panels. Has anything beaten it?

### 5. The Eight Fallacies, in a room of emulated machines

The cabinet's machines share a bus with messages and shared memory (tiny-titan). Which fallacies
does an emulated network let you stage on purpose, and which does it let you forget?

### 6. Music

What Peter composes now, and how it does or doesn't feel like programming. His pick whether this
belongs on the show at all.
