## Who he was

Timothy Leary took his PhD in clinical psychology at Berkeley in 1950. His dissertation and his
book *The Interpersonal Diagnosis of Personality* (1957) described personality as a circle, the
interpersonal circumplex, with dominance and affiliation as its axes. Psychologists still use the
model. In 1960 he started the Harvard Psilocybin Project, and Harvard dismissed him in 1963. The
rest of the decade made him famous and put him in court: the Supreme Court overturned his first
conviction in *Leary v. United States* (1969), and after a second he escaped from prison in 1970,
lived abroad, and was recaptured in 1972 and released in 1976.

In the 1980s he turned to computers, which he saw as tools for thinking about yourself. His company,
Futique, made [*Timothy Leary's Mind Mirror*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Leary%27s_Mind_Mirror),
published by Electronic Arts in 1985 and 1986. It turned his circumplex into a game: you rate
yourself and others on his scales, see the results as a shape, and play scenarios that test your
choices. The disk credits read "Design and script by Timothy Leary. Program and Design by Peter Van
den Beemt and Bob Dietz." He went on to promote virtual reality, wrote *Chaos & Cyber Culture*
(1994), and when he learned he was dying in 1995, he made the process public on his website. He
died in 1996. His papers are at the [New York Public Library](https://archives.nypl.org/mss/18400).

## Why he's here

Mind Mirror is an early game about modeling personality, and The Sims later modeled people with
traits too. Don extracted Mind Mirror's text and data from the Apple II disk
([text](https://donhopkins.com/home/mind-mirror.txt), [data](https://donhopkins.com/home/mind-mirror.json)),
and MOOLLM's [mind-mirror skill](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/tree/main/skills/mind-mirror)
uses Leary's scales beside the Sims traits to describe its characters.
