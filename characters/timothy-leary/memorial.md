# In memoriam — Timothy Leary (1920–1996)

*Memorial mode: we represent and discuss his work; we do not speak as him.*
[Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

Before he was famous for anything else, Timothy Leary was a research psychologist who drew
personality as a circle. His interpersonal circumplex (1957) placed behavior on two axes, dominance
and affiliation, and it is still in use.

Thirty years later he put it in a computer game. *Mind Mirror* (Electronic Arts, 1985) let players
rate themselves and other people on his scales, see the result as a shape, and try out choices in
scenarios. It was an attempt to make a personality model into something you play with instead of
something done to you. The Sims later built its people out of traits, and MOOLLM's
[mind-mirror skill](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/tree/main/skills/mind-mirror) uses his
scales beside Will Wright's to describe its characters. Don extracted the game's text and data from
the original Apple II disk so the model can be read directly.

A remembrance would run *Mind Mirror*, read the 1957 model against how games and AI characters model
people now, and talk with people who worked with him about the software years: what he hoped
personal computers would do for self-knowledge, and what came of it.

*This is a collaborative memorial, not a signed work. Those who knew him are welcome to correct and
add to it.*
