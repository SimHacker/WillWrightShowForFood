## Don's comments (verbatim)

> I vividly remember your TV set full of performances with the giant heavy spinning crank on the
> side that controlled TIME ITSELF! You invented physical inertial scrolling. If you put all the
> lost souls from Die Space into that TV, would that be DOOM SCROLLING?

> It worked so well with your body of work because it compells you to freeze frame, rewind, scrub
> back and forth, and spin fast forwards and backwards! And it's physically concrete fun to spin
> the actual wheel with your whole arm and hand, the way mobile phone doom scrolling can only
> simulate at the most abstract shallow fingertip level.

> Have you considered second and third order extensions like a brake and motor, for direct
> manipulation and force feedback, like Margaret Minsky's sandpaper?
> https://interfacemuseum.com/exhibits/minsky-sandpaper/

## The claims, unpacked

- **The crank TV**: Keez built a TV installation whose giant heavy side-crank scrubs recorded
  performances through time — freeze frame, rewind, spin fast forward/backward. Physical
  **inertial scrolling** with the whole arm, years before touchscreen momentum scrolling shrank
  the gesture to a fingertip flick.
- **DOOM SCROLLING**: put the lost souls of Die Space inside the crank TV and the pun completes
  itself — doom-scrolling the departed with a flywheel.
- **The Sandpaper extension**: add a **brake and motor** to the crank — second and third order
  direct manipulation with **force feedback** — and the crank becomes a haptic display in the
  lineage of [Margaret Minsky's Sandpaper system](https://interfacemuseum.com/exhibits/minsky-sandpaper/)
  (MIT Media Lab, SIGGRAPH 1990): programmable lateral forces synthesizing texture. Imagine
  feeling the *texture of time* — grit at the sad parts, velvet at the good parts, stick-slip at
  scene boundaries.
