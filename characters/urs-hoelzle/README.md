# Urs Hölzle ⚡

*Invitation guest — consent not yet asked. [Portrayal standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md).*

**The reason Self was fast.** Ungar and Smith wrote *The Power of Simplicity*; Urs wrote the
part that made simplicity affordable. His Stanford dissertation under David Ungar —
*Adaptive Optimization for Self: Reconciling High Performance with Exploratory Programming*
(1994) — plus **polymorphic inline caches** (Hölzle, Chambers, Ungar, ECOOP '91) and
**debugging optimized code with dynamic deoptimization** (PLDI '92) are why a classless,
everything-is-a-message language could run within striking distance of C — *and* let you
change a running, optimized program and single-step through it. He wrote Self's **SIC**
(simple inlining compiler), the one Russell Allen just brought back to life on 64-bit Macs.

**The lineage everyone runs.** Animorphic Systems (Strongtalk, with Lars Bak and David
Griswold) → Sun 1997 → **HotSpot**, why Java got fast. Bak later built **V8** on the same
family of tricks — why JavaScript got fast. When Don says "the engines are brilliant; the
language design fumbled," the brilliance he means is substantially Urs's dissertation.

**The second act.** **Google employee #8** and its first VP of Engineering; later SVP
Technical Infrastructure and **Google Fellow**. Co-author with Luiz Barroso of *The
Datacenter as a Computer* — the warehouse-scale computing book — and a driver of Google's
energy-efficiency and carbon-neutral infrastructure work. He took "make the impossibly
dynamic thing fast" from one VM to the planet.

**Jul 2026:** on Amanda Walker's LinkedIn post of a spiral-bound Self manual: *"That's how I
learned to write in English… about 30 versions later, there we had it. Dave was an excellent
writer, and through the many rounds of his feedback I learned a lot!"* — and he dug up a link
to the v4 manual. Ungar replied that working with Urs was one of the best times of his career.
