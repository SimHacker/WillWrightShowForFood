## Who

**Upmanu Lall** is one of the world's leading water scientists. Since January 2024 he is the
inaugural director of the **Water Institute** at Arizona State University's Julie Ann Wrigley Global
Futures Laboratory, and a professor in ASU's School of Complex Adaptive Systems. Before that he was
the Alan & Carol Silberstein Professor of Engineering at **Columbia University** and director of the
**Columbia Water Center**, leading global initiatives on water sustainability, flood prediction, and
America's water infrastructure. IIT Kanpur B.Tech (1976), UT Austin PhD (1981); AGU Fellow, AAAS
Fellow, EGU Henry Darcy Medal; on Reuters' 2021 "Hot List" of the world's top 1,000 climate
scientists.

## The precedent: SimCity as a classroom data lab

Decades ago at Columbia, Lall ran **Project OPTIMUS** — using simulation games, SimCity included, to
teach science and engineering. As Don recalls it, the design was sly and brilliant: use SimCity to
**draw students from other departments into environmental engineering** classes. Offer a fun
multidisciplinary course where the game is the laboratory — students run **controlled experiments**
on a city, **gather and export the data as spreadsheets**, and come out the other side knowing basic
data analysis and spreadsheet wrangling. The game is the bait; the data literacy is the catch.

That is the founding precedent of the show's
[educators' track](../../repo-shows/ideas/themes/educators-track.yml), and the direct ancestor of
its `data_lab` format: Micropolis metrics streaming into **Google Sheets**, which sits high on the
enable-educational-uses unlock path. What Lall's students did by hand, every classroom could do live.

The paper trail survives. OPTIMUS — the **Open Platform for Teaching Integrated Modeling and Urban
Simulation** (Columbia CEEM/EEE + CCNMTL, prototype released September 2003; the anchor course
*Resources & Environment* launched spring 2002) — is documented in Garvin, Smyth, Sommer & Lall's
conference paper, and the prototype code sits in a public GitHub archive
([`ccnmtl/optimus`](https://github.com/ccnmtl/optimus)). The paper names the exact blocker of its
era:

> "Currently, SimCity lacks the open architecture to add modules and extract state variables."

That sentence, written around 2002, is the requirement MicropolisCore + MOOLLM finally fulfills:
open architecture, addable modules, extractable state. It even asked for **multiple roles** so
student teams could play a city's departments (the federation/multiplayer design), called computers
"a **conversational environment** in which the learner can apply knowledge to problems" (now
literal, with an LLM), and wanted **cellular automata** as the early teaching formalism (Micropolis
*is* a CA city). His 2002 wishlist is the 2026 feature set.

## The correspondence

When Don wrote him in December 2008 about the GPL'd Micropolis and the Kennisnet/IBM education push,
Lall replied the same week:

> "Fantastic to hear from you and learn what you are working on -- sounds very cool …
> perhaps i can be listed as a collaborator? and I could then say I will use in my classes"

He has been in the Micropolis manual credits (**Special Thanks**) ever since the OLPC era.

## The approach

Back into the fold **with love and respect** — as a guide, not a grant ornament. Even if he has no
current funding, no students, and no bandwidth for this, his guidance is critical: OPTIMUS was the
rubber hitting the road on applying constructionist theory in a real classroom, inside a
traditional university, with real curriculum committees. Standing invitation, first look, zero
obligation — and one concrete ask that doubles as the reconnection: *your 2002 blocker is fixed;
may we see the original proposals?*

## Why invite him

- **He proved the pedagogy before the tooling existed.** A conversation about what he wanted SimCity
  to do for his students then, versus what an AI-instrumented, spreadsheet-exporting Micropolis can
  do now, writes itself.
- **Water is a Micropolis system.** Reservoirs, floods, drought, infrastructure — his life's work is
  the real version of the simulation's subsystems. A "SimCity vs. real hydrology" segment with an
  actual Darcy Medalist is a dream episode.
- **The interdisciplinary draw is the whole point.** His trick — use a game to pull outsiders into a
  technical field — is exactly what Repo Shows do with open source development.
