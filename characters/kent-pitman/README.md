# Kent Pitman 🦥📖

*A portrayal about Kent Pitman, not Kent Pitman. He may correct, add to, or
remove anything here. [Portrayal standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md).*

**Kent M. Pitman (KMP)** learned Lisp in a class taught by Joseph Weizenbaum,
wrote `:TEACH;LISP`, which taught MACLISP to MIT-AI's tourists, wrote the
manual for MACLISP, designed the Common Lisp condition system, edited the
ANSI Common Lisp standard, and turned it into the Common Lisp HyperSpec, so
a programming language standard could be read as hypertext
(his [Wikipedia biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_Pitman) and
[his own list of papers](https://www.nhplace.com/kent/Papers/)).

He also taught Don Hopkins Lisp, over the ARPANET, when Don was a high school
kid. This room collects the stories.

## His work, in his words and others'

- **The Pitmanual.** *The Revised Maclisp Manual (Saturday Evening Edition)*,
  1983; on the web as the Sunday Morning Edition, 2007:
  [maclisp.info/pitmanual](https://www.maclisp.info/pitmanual/). Still the
  reference for MACLISP, and full of jokes, including the
  [funnies](https://www.maclisp.info/pitmanual/funnies.html) page.
- **The condition system.** Signals, handlers and restarts, separated so the
  code that notices a problem doesn't have to decide how to fix it:
  [Condition Handling in the Lisp Language Family](http://www.nhplace.com/kent/Papers/Condition-Handling-2001.html).
  phoe, author of *The Common Lisp Condition System*, calls him "the father
  of the condition system as it exists nowadays" in
  [an HN comment from 2020](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=23845935).
- **ANSI Common Lisp and the HyperSpec.** Project Editor of the standard and
  author of the [Common Lisp HyperSpec](http://www.lispworks.com/documentation/HyperSpec/Front/index.htm);
  the story of how the language came to be is
  [Common Lisp: The Untold Story](https://www.nhplace.com/kent/Papers/cl-untold-story.html).
- **Lisp1 vs Lisp2.** [Technical Issues of Separation in Function Cells and Value Cells](http://www.nhplace.com/kent/Papers/Technical-Issues.html),
  with Dick Gabriel, 1988. Kent on HN: "RPG and I disagreed completely on
  whether Lisp1 or Lisp2 was a good idea. Most readers read this like a
  Rorschach" ([12801023](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=12801023)).
- **[Special Forms in Lisp](https://www.nhplace.com/kent/Papers/Special-Forms.html)**,
  1980, the paper that argued fexprs out of mainstream Lisp.
- **[The Best of Intentions: EQUAL Rights—and Wrongs—in Lisp](http://www.nhplace.com/kent/PS/EQUAL.html)**,
  on why no single equality or copy function can be right.
- **[Kent M. Pitman Answers On Lisp And Much More](http://developers.slashdot.org/story/01/11/03/1726251/kent-m-pitman-answers-on-lisp-and-much-more)**,
  Slashdot, 2001.
- His [LispNYC](https://www.youtube.com/@lispnyc1039/videos) talk on how he
  became a Lisper, which he recommends for the full origin story.

## What he gave Don

Patience with a kid on a borrowed account. A program that taught Lisp
without getting tired. A duel that ended in Roman numerals. A game that made a teenager
apologize to a program, which is the first lesson in designing characters
people care about. And, forty-four years later, on Facebook, the name of a
1982 command nobody else remembered, exactly right.
