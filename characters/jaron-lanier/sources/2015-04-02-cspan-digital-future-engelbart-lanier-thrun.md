# Digital Future — Lanier & Thrun on Engelbart (C-SPAN, April 2, 2015)

Jaron Lanier and Sebastian Thrun, moderated by Henry Lowood, on
[Douglas Engelbart](../../douglas-engelbart/README.md) and the Mother
of All Demos, before a Stanford performance of *The Demo*.

## Why this transcript matters — hooks to discuss with Jaron

- **The Engelbart ↔ Minsky story, told firsthand.** Jaron: "When I was
  a teenager, probably my most important mentor was
  [Marvin Minsky](../../marvin-minsky/README.md). They used to have
  arguments. Marvin would say we are going to do this, this and this
  for machines, and then Doug would say: but what are you going to do
  for the people?" — the same story Jaron told in his
  [2005 American Scientist review](2005-american-scientist-early-computings-long-strange-trip.md)
  of Markoff's *Dormouse* (see also
  [the Dormouse/Engelbart/Minsky notes](2023-01-24-dormouse-engelbart-minsky.md)).
  Jaron heard it from both sides: mentored by Minsky, befriended by
  Engelbart — who used to wander by and pick flowers in the fields
  outside the cottage where Jaron did the first VR work.
- **Engelbart's standard: virtuosity, not convenience.** "For Doug,
  progress meant expecting more and more from people... He expected
  them to gain virtuoso capabilities with technology." Under Moore's
  Law "we never get the chance to become a virtuoso with technology."
- **Musical instruments as the best interfaces** — violins and
  clarinets took centuries to co-evolve with their playing cultures;
  "maybe in 200 years we'll have interfaces that we use that are as
  good as a violin or a piano." Direct feed for the
  [patch-cords show](../../../repo-shows/patch-cords/README.md)
  and [Rebounce](../../../repo-shows/rebounce/README.md) — Jaron the
  rare-instrument collector said Engelbart agreed, in his living room.
- **Measure your effect or you're failing.** "If you are creating a
  freer society, measure it... If you are saying at the same time the
  middle class is declining, then you are failing." The empirical-loop
  demand that becomes *Who Owns the Future* — and the
  [Soul City repossession thesis](../../../catalogs/soul-city/README.md).
- **Original meaning of "virtual reality" = social.** "The term
  virtual reality originally meant having a social version of virtual
  worlds where people would see each other as avatars" — VPL's RB2 and
  [David Levitt's](../../david-levitt/README.md) shared-reality
  prerequisite, firsthand (see
  [Body Electric / Bounce VR stack](../../don-hopkins/body-electric-bounce-vr-stack.md)).
- **Oculus deja vu.** "The stuff looks and feels so much like what we
  were doing in the 80s... it is very strange actually." "$2 billion."
- **Credit where due:** "The first glove was made by Tom Zimmerman. I
  should give him credit. He is currently a researcher at IBM."
- **The autobiography teaser** — "I'm working on an autobiography,
  trying to reconcile my current view of the world with the one I had
  in my 20s" — became *Dawn of the New Everything* (2017).
- **The SAIL manager's dissent** ⟦likely Les Earnest — confirm⟧: an
  audience member who "was managing the Stanford Artificial
  Intelligence Lab" argues the Mother of All Demos contained "one new
  idea... it didn't work" and that point-and-click predates it
  (Whirlwind, light pens, SAGE). Jaron's answer: Engelbart's
  achievement was "a wholistic sensibility... levels of achievement
  that can't be described in terms of molecules."
- Also present in the conversation: [Alan Kay](../../alan-kay/README.md)
  and [Ted Nelson](../../ted-nelson/README.md) as the archetypes of
  inventors who "end up as research types" rather than moguls.
