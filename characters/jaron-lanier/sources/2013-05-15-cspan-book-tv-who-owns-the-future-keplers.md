# Who Owns the Future? — Lanier at Kepler's (C-SPAN Book TV, May 15, 2013)

Jaron Lanier arguing the thesis of *Who Owns the Future?* a week after
US publication, a few miles from the Googleplex: whoever owns the
biggest computer gathers the benefits and radiates the risks; the fix
is monetized information — universal micropayments flowing back to the
people the data comes from. "Information is people in disguise."

## Why this transcript matters — hooks to discuss with Jaron

- **Data dignity is the Soul City argument.** Lanier: creators locked
  into hub networks get "a tall tower and an emaciated neck and a long
  tail"; the middle class needs to own and monetize what it creates.
  [Soul City](../../../catalogs/soul-city/README.md) makes the same
  argument about Sims families and player-created content — repossess
  the value from the walled garden. Lanier's "you could see the real
  middle class come out of something like Facebook if it were
  monetized" is the
  [repossession thesis](../../../characters/ian-bogost/roles-not-characters.md)
  in economic form.
- **Micropayments every time code runs.** "There is a third
  alternative: every time code runs, micropayments go back to whoever
  wrote it. That has never been given a fair chance." Compare the
  [GitHub distribution model](../../../catalogs/soul-city/github-distribution-model.md)
  — attribution-preserving forks as the non-monetized half of the same
  design; [Ted Nelson](../../ted-nelson/README.md)'s Xanadu
  transclusion-royalties are the ancestor Lanier credits: "the
  original design for networking was monetized, starting in 1960,
  before we knew how to make networks."
- **"I was present in the room when open source was born."** Lanier's
  firsthand account of [Richard Stallman](../../richard-stallman/README.md)
  "being so upset about the machine being held up because the company
  went bankrupt" — the Lisp machine fight that produced GNU, told as
  origin story and critique in one breath.
- **"Sergey and Larry would make money from the search code and would
  not have to start this spying empire."** The counterfactual that
  makes the micropayment argument concrete.
- **"Machines don't exist. It is only people pretending they don't
  exist."** Against the AI-futurist framing — the same argument Don
  makes about [LLM personas and Minsky's Society of Mind](../../marvin-minsky/README.md):
  there's no little person inside; there are people all the way down,
  in disguise.
- **Surveillance priced, not prohibited.** "If you have to pay money
  when you recognize somebody on a camera, all of a sudden you can't
  do it without restraint" — no taxation without representation as an
  engineering constraint. Regulation-as-prohibition loses to
  programmers with big computers; costs create balance.
- **The informal economy warning.** Airbnb-style schemes: "the people
  running the computers stay away from the risk... the people in the
  informal economy have to take the risk themselves." Relevant to any
  creator-economy design [Soul City](../../../catalogs/soul-city/README.md)
  ships.
- **The kindergarten infinity story.** Little girls arguing whether
  infinity is a number, making excellent arguments — and the teacher
  says "no, it's addition." Engelbart-grade indictment of squandered
  curiosity; pairs with the math-intuition demo Jaron wished for in
  the [2015 Engelbart conversation](2015-04-02-cspan-digital-future-engelbart-lanier-thrun.md).

high-frequency trading machine is that it's making trades so fast that
there isn't time for information from the real world to get in. So
it's technically impossible for these things to be optimizing based on
events in reality. Then there's the other argument: they're optimizing
the function of the market itself. I actually think that could be

collectively melt money out of the world, radiating the risk to

picture it never does, because there is never a free lunch.

gone down all of those roads, and I don't think they go to pretty

says: why are you trying to keep markets at all? Why not just get rid

also screws people over if it's allowed to function on its own terms.

occur in a purely political way — we see communist parties use power,
or mullahs, or whatever it is, again and again. People are difficult

are just ornery. So I tend to believe a purely political solution is
not going to happen. I think the use of markets, the use of money,
should be understood as something that balances the tendencies of
human society to be dysfunctional. Money also can be terrible — money

politics and economics can balance each other's worst failure modes

capitalism. One of the trends concerning me — and I think a lot of

benefit from having cameras on every corner in populated areas, and

people by face or gait or all kinds of things, and all of a sudden you

the people constrain the government — or, for that matter, other
