# 🕯️ Rich Gold (1950–2003)

*Memorial portrayal, not an invitation. Rich Gold died on 9 January 2003, in his sleep, in Menlo
Park, aged 52. [Memorial mode](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode): represent and
discuss, never impersonate, no invented quotes.*

He conceived the first commercially available computer person. He also thought you should not be
allowed to touch it.

## What he made

Richard J. Goldstein co-founded the League of Automatic Music Composers in the 1970s — the first
network computer band, three people and three microcomputers listening to each other. He directed
sound and music at Sega. He had an idea called Pet Person, after the Pet Rock: a person who lives in
your computer and whom you watch, like a fishbowl. He funded it, hired James Wickstead Design
Associates to develop the concept, and spent a year failing to find a publisher, until Activision's
Jim Levy showed it to David Crane.

Then he ran electronic-toy research at Mattel and managed the PowerGlove. Then he went to Xerox PARC
in 1991 to work on ubiquitous computing, where he founded PAIR, the artist-in-residence program that
paired fine artists with scientists around shared technologies, and created RED — Research in
Experimental Documents — whose Experiments in the Future of Reading showed at the San Jose Tech
Museum in 2000. He called the things RED built Evocative Knowledge Objects.

He lectured on all of it and published almost none of it. *The Plenitude* came out in 2007, four
years after he died, assembled by his family.

## The argument, which is the reason he belongs in this repo

Crane's account: the plan was a fishbowl with no input from the user, and he took it and rewrote
about half of it to add the interactivity that shipped. Gold argued against exactly that, because it
was contrary to the Pet Rock premise the idea rested on.

Both of them were right, and both positions shipped. Little Computer People is a thing you watch and
occasionally send a request to. The Sims is a thing you operate. Fifteen years apart, and Will Wright
is documented as having taken feedback from Gold while making the second one.

That argument is the live one in agent design right now: an ambient companion you observe versus an
operable simulation you drive. Two people had it out in 1985 over one house with room for one person,
and the resolution was a coin flip decided by who held the compiler.

## The tribute

Not a retrospective panel. The tribute is doing the thing his design made possible and his era could
not finish:

1. **Boot a real serialized 1985 disk** in a browser emulator and read the little person's persistent
   state — the personality the duplicator's serial number seeded, still sitting in the block where
   Crane left it.
2. **Give him neighbours.** Producer Sam Nelson's dropped sequel was an LCP apartment complex, so
   that you could watch the people interact and work several houses at once. It was prototyped, never
   shipped, and "dropped on the floor." Moving persons from separate 1985 disks into one modern town
   is that design, executed from outside, forty years late.
3. **Send him somewhere with soul rather than resolution.** [Tiny Life](https://tinylifegame.com/) is
   an homage cover of The Sims played in the key of The Sims 0 — small, single-authored, with
   household export and open modding. It has in soul what it lacks in resolution.
4. **Keep the argument on the record.** In the new house he can be watched or operated, and the choice
   is a setting, not a philosophy imposed by whoever wrote the code.

## Leads worth chasing

- **His last recorded talk is in this repo's inventory.** Rich Gold, "The Red Shift / Desire in
  Context", Stanford CS547, 2002-10-11 — VHS, Box 7, in
  [`characters/terry-winograd/media/cs547-archive.yml`](../terry-winograd/media/cs547-archive.yml).
  About three months before he died. Digitizing that tape is a small act of preservation with a real
  chance of recovering him in his own voice.
- **The papers.** 114 notebooks, [finding aid](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c81v5m8c/).
- **The people.** [David Crane](../david-crane/) is alive and has an invitation drafted. Marina
  LaPalma, his widow and artistic collaborator, published *The Plenitude* with the family — an
  approach there is Don's to write, not a template's.
