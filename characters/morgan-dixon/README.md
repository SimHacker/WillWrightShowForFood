## Who he is

Morgan Dixon built [Prefab](https://prefab.github.io/) at the University of Washington with James
Fogarty. Prefab looks only at the pixels on the screen and recovers the structure of the interface,
its buttons, sliders, tabs and text, many times a second, so that any application can be modified
without access to its source. With that structure it could add target-aware pointing to every
program on the desktop, translate interfaces, add accessibility, and let people restyle or rearrange
software they didn't write. The first Prefab paper won a Best Paper award at CHI 2010, and its
workshop paper asked, "What if every GUI were open-source?" His 2015 dissertation,
"Pixel-Based Reverse Engineering of Graphical Interfaces," gathers the work; the
[videos](https://prefab.github.io/videos.html) show it running.

He was a Microsoft Research PhD Fellow, a research scientist at Adobe, and then a UX engineer at
AppSheet, which Google acquired in 2020 ([home page](https://morgandixon.net/)).

## Why he's here

When Ben Shneiderman forwarded Don's aQuery proposal around 2013, James Landay pointed Don to
Prefab, and Don has cited it ever since as the proof that pixel-based recognition works on real
interfaces. [Screen Angel](https://github.com/SimHacker/MicropolisCore/blob/main/apps/screen-angel/ACCESSIBLIFY.yml)
starts its widget recognition from Prefab's prototype matching, and the PDP-7 cabinet's
[manifesto](../../packages/cabinet/MANIFESTO.md) credits it.
