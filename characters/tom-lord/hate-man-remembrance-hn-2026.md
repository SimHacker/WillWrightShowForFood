# I HATE YOU!!!! — the Hate Man remembrance (HN, July 2026)

Don's public remembrance of Tom, told through Hate Man, the 2010 Facebook exchange, and one
minute of a shirtless guy riding a cow through Berkeley. Two pieces, both in the Haskell/Lisp
thread where [The Part Where It Compiles](../don-hopkins/the-part-where-it-compiles-hn-2026.md)
ran as a partial tribute to Tom.

**Thread:** [After 7 years in production, Scarf has reluctantly moved away from Haskell](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48859673) (Jul 2026).

**Piece 1** — appended to Don's live reply to pjmlp ("You provoked me by being right..."),
downstream of comment [48870736](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48870736).

**Piece 2** — the standalone follow-up comment, the "will publish more later" payoff.

Primary source behind both: [Tom and Don on Hate Man, April 2010](sources/2010-04-hate-man-facebook-exchange.md),
with the original screenshot in [`media/`](media/2010-04-hate-man-facebook-exchange.png).

## Piece 1 — the reply to pjmlp

> Heatedly arguing while strongly agreeing was exactly how Tom and I were friends for decades,
> and Berkeley had a patron saint of that. Last week I posted two words to Tom's memorial page:
> I HATE YOU!!!! Any friend of Tom's would recognize that as a love letter. It's a Hate Man
> reference: Mark Hawthorne, the New York Times reporter who quit and spent decades living on
> the streets around People's Park, preaching what he called oppositionality. He asked everyone,
> friends and strangers, to tell him "I hate you," because a connection that starts by declaring
> opposition is honest, and whatever affection survives it is real. Tom sent me this article
> about him when it ran 16 years ago, because of course he did. People's Park held great meaning
> for Tom, and so did arguing as a form of love.
>
> Homeless ex-reporter opted for Berkeley streets (SF Chronicle, 2010, via archive.org):
>
> https://web.archive.org/web/20100413124819/http://www.sfgate.com/cgi-bin/article.cgi?f=/c/a/2010/04/11/BA831COQP3.DTL

## Piece 2 — the standalone follow-up

> More memories of Tom Lord, as promised.
>
> In April 2010 Tom posted me a link out of nowhere: "That reminds me (why? I dunno) that - hey:
> hate man made the news."
>
> Homeless ex-reporter opted for Berkeley streets (SF Chronicle, April 2010, via archive.org):
>
> https://web.archive.org/web/20100413124819/http://www.sfgate.com/cgi-bin/article.cgi?f=/c/a/2010/04/11/BA831COQP3.DTL
>
> Hate Man was Mark Hawthorne, a New York Times reporter from 1961 to 1970 who opted out of
> normal society and spent decades on the streets around Telegraph Avenue and People's Park. He
> wouldn't talk to you until you told him you hated him. From the Chronicle interview: "It's a
> new way of hating. It's about being straight with people... My idea is to be straight about
> negative feelings that we all have, which is what hate is, and then you can have a real
> conversation."
>
> I replied that I loved Hate Man (but that kind of sounds weird, like I totally missed the
> point), and that I was honored he'd hated on me once and wished we'd discussed it more deeply,
> and that he seemed like one of the most sensible people walking around Berkeley.
>
> Then Tom wrote something I've been thinking about ever since. He said that back in the day
> he'd had some really, really bad days, real existential crises, and that "Hate man and several
> of the brothers in the park basically saved my life." Hate Man by getting him to play his
> "let's lean on each other game," the brothers in the park by being good listeners to mumbled
> words, often enough good counsel, and by whooping his ass at street chess. He described how,
> as things shut down for the night, Hate Man would gather the rough itinerant runaway kids of
> Telegraph up in a circle in Sproul Plaza, and they'd all chill and keep safe and share warmth
> and make peace, "all in the name of Hate. Telegraph was very much a zen temple. Hate man a
> famous monk."
>
> He also corrected the Chronicle's timeline. The article placed the start of Hate Man's street
> life in '86, and Tom called bull: Hate Man was the first resident of Berkeley he ever heard
> of, in 1983, in Massachusetts, from someone who was basically trying to encourage him to run
> as fast as he could away from the preppy high school that G. W. Bush went to: "There's this
> guy there. And he's really cool. He likes to shout 'I hate you' at people. And he won't talk
> to you unless you first tell him that you hate him, too." It made no sense to him at the time.
> Years later, he said, arriving in Berkeley was like walking into the landscape of a favorite
> children's book he'd read years before. Many of the familiar characters were there.
>
> I told him that was beautiful.
>
> He replied: "I hate you."
>
> I replied: "Go stick your head in a pig, beeeyatch!"
>
> (That's a lyric from Share and Enjoy, the Sirius Cybernetics Corporation jingle from The
> Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy:
>
> https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_wSBC5Dyds8 )
>
> That was sixteen years ago. Tom died suddenly in 2022, and last week I finally took my turn
> again, on his memorial page: I HATE YOU!!!!
>
> Hate Man would approve. Be straight about the negative feelings first, and then you can have a
> real conversation. I hate you, Tom. I miss you.
>
> And if you want one minute of the town where all of this made perfect sense, Tom's Berkeley,
> here is a shirtless guy riding a cow through the streets:
>
> Cows In Berkeley? Moooo:
>
> https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=10JSPdTDFsI
>
> I love that town. It suited him perfectly.

## Notes

- Every Tom quote is verbatim from the 2010 Facebook exchange; every Hate Man quote is verbatim
  from the Chronicle article. The whole piece is receipts.
- The Chronicle said Hawthorne had been on the streets "since 1986"; Tom's '83 Massachusetts
  story disputes exactly that claim. The piece presents both and lets Tom win.
- The private memorial-page voices (family and friends) are deliberately left out, per
  [portrayal standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md).
- The emotional structure: the 2010 conversation never ended. "I hate you" / "Go stick your head
  in a pig" was Don's turn; I HATE YOU!!!! sixteen years later is the next one.
