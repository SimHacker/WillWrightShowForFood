## Introduction (thesis)

Berkeley debates criminalize visible homelessness. Both punitive and "humane" poles seek to
remove destitute people from commercial districts — **social cleansing** without admitting the goal.

## Three historical turns

1. Feudalism → capitalism (Black Death, Enclosure) — vagrancy compels labor  
2. Post-Emancipation US — segregated employment, convict labor  
3. Great Depression — labor oversupply; vagrancy loses compulsory-work role  

## Edwards v. California (1941)

*"Poverty and immorality are not synonymous."* — end of "moral pestilence of paupers" framing.

## Modern Berkeley

Hyper-specific ordinances (feet from parking kiosks, dog density, anti-reclining). Main defense
shifted from **having a job** to **spending money**.

## Conclusion

Penalizing homeless behavior won't fix surplus-labor crisis. *We're all in this together.*
