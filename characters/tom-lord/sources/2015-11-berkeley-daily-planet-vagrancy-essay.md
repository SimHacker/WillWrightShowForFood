# The Moral Pestilence of Paupers — Thomas Lord (2015)

**Author:** Thomas Lord  
**Venue:** Berkeley Daily Planet, Public Comment  
**Date:** 2015-11-12  
**URL:** https://berkeleydailyplanet.com/issue/2015-11-13/article/43898

Tom was proud of writing this. Long-form historical essay on **vagrancy law** from medieval England
through Berkeley commercial-district policing — how both punitive and "humane" poles pursue
**social cleansing** of visible poverty; shift from compelling labor to compelling consumption.

---

## Lord's arc (for show / character)

| Period | Vagrancy role |
|--------|----------------|
| Black Death → Enclosure | Compel labor; peasants → proletariat |
| Post-Emancipation US | Segregated employment; convict labor |
| Great Depression | Labor oversupply; *Edwards v. California* — "poverty and immorality are not synonymous" |
| Post-Papachristou | Hyper-specific ordinances; "spend money or move along" |
| Berkeley 1980s–2010s | Feet from parking kiosks, dog density, anti-reclining |

**Conclusion:** *"We're all in this together"* — penalizing homeless won't fix surplus labor crisis.
