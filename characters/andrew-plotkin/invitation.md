# Repo Show invitation — Andrew Plotkin

*Draft — not yet sent. Andrew Plotkin may edit, decline, delay, or request removal at any time — or take direct edit access.*

[Portrayal standards](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/schemas/portrayal-standards.md) — correct, expand, replace, or remove your directory anytime; also covers the quiet-mode publication plan and easy onboarding.

**Ask:** I'd like you on a **Repo Show** — a friendly recorded conversation whose stage is this public GitHub repo, carried through to working code and credited ideas in the open.

**The occasion:** I recently archived my TinyMUD files, and there we both are, December 1989: you announcing MIDgaard — object-oriented rooms, programmable object behavior, everyone a builder — and me replying at 1:37 AM with the "what could be..." letter telling you not to invent a language, because "the biggest nastiest bitch of them all is writing the manual." You then spent the following decades shipping the VM, the I/O standard, and the packaging format — and writing the manuals, which became the field's specs. I want to reread that thread on air and find out who won the argument. Also: I wrote "I'd do just about anything for original muddle Zork sources" — to you, in 1989 — and the wish came true; I keep a checkout. We could open the muddle together.

**Two things I only just noticed.** Reading Jimmy Maher's interview with you: *Praser 5* started as a puzzle living in the CMU filesystem, directories as rooms, symlinks as exits, `ls` as `look`, permissions as the progression gate. That is exactly the premise I am building on now, twenty-five years after you did it, and in those same months I was building the parallel version inside Emacs instead of inside the filesystem. Same seam, different substrate. And the other one is sharper: your account of Glk starts from the front-end-times-VM matrix and the realization that the machine only accepts text in and emits text out. In my 20 December 1989 letter to that same list I was arguing for typed output packets and wrote, "Idealy I'd like to be able to download PostScript procedures to the mud server that tell it what format to send messages back to me. The standard text-based user interface would be just one such procedure." I reached for NeWS; you shipped the version that lasted. I would like to put those two paragraphs side by side on screen and talk about the seam we both found and only you built.
