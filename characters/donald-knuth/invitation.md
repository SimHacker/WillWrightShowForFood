## Letter draft (for paper)

```
Prof. Donald E. Knuth
Computer Science Department
CoDa Building room W208
389 Jane Stanford Way
Stanford University
Stanford, CA 94305-5008
USA

Dear Professor Knuth,

I am Don Hopkins, a longtime student of your work — TeX, literate programming, and the idea that
a program should be written primarily for human readers. I work with Will Wright (SimCity, The Sims)
producing Will Wright Show for Food, a "Repo Show": a recorded conversation whose stage and lasting
product is a public GitHub repository — rooms, characters, and props as files you can walk through,
closer to Adventure and LambdaMOO than to a disposable video file.

We recently sat with your CWEB recasting of Crowther and Woods's Adventure (advent.w). It is a
gift: the founding interactive-world schema — locations, travel instructions, objects, dwarves —
explained in the order a curious reader needs, then tangled for the machine. That is exactly the
discipline we are trying to practice with MOOLLM (a microworld OS in the spirit of MOO + LLM) and
with these show rooms.

I would be honored — no pressure, any silence respected — if you would consider a short recorded
conversation someday about why Adventure deserved a literate treatment, and what "programs as
literature" still means when the audience includes both people and language models. Async is fine:
you speak when convenient; we assemble with sources and credit. You would of course control how
you are portrayed.

A public portrayal directory (correctable or deletable by you at any time) lives here:

  https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/tree/main/characters/donald-knuth/

Our study notes on advent.w:

  https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/tree/main/characters/donald-knuth/sources/adventure-knuth/

(Also: https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/tree/main/characters/donald-knuth/sources/ )

Thank you for decades of making programs worth reading.

Respectfully,
Don Hopkins
Amsterdam
```
