David —

I read *The Wedding Album* when it came out, and I've never put it down — I worked at Maxis on
The Sims, and I've spent the decades since building tools for exactly the creatures you wrote
about: simulated people with interiority of unknown status, owned by people who click.

I host a show that lives in a GitHub repository —
[WillWrightShowForFood](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood) — about developing
and playing games and tools, with Will Wright as flagship guest. The show *manufactures your
premise on purpose*: portrayals of real people (with consent machinery), memorial rooms for the
dead (discuss, never impersonate), and characters moved between games as portable souls. We wrote
our rulebook with your novella open on the desk, and I've published the acknowledgment:

**[The Wedding Album and the rights of digital souls](the-wedding-album-and-digital-souls.md)** —
your story's issues mapped onto today's games and AI, with credit where it's due.

I'd love to have you on — as a science fiction author, a collaborator, and frankly as the
conscience of the subject matter. Conversation could include:

- **The Album, 27 years on** — what you'd write differently now that the gift-shop banality
  arrived (grief-bots, voice clones, LLM keepsakes).
- **Sims that know they're sims** — our disclosure rules vs. Anne's awakening; is a copy that
  knows it's a copy actually better off?
- **Save files as souls** — the show moves characters between games as conserved data; what is
  a portable soul owed at the border?
- **Counting Heads' world** — mentars, clone castes, the future of work when personhood is
  manufactured.
- **Writing from Fairbanks** — the slow, precise, world-first method, as the antidote to the
  AI moment's hype cycle.

Format is low pressure: a solo video whenever you like, a relaxed one-on-one edited together, or
just correspondence — the repo grows either way. Accepted, delayed, too busy, declined, or no
reply: all honored.
