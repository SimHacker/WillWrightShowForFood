Lumpinou —

I'm Don Hopkins. I worked on The Sims at Maxis, wrote the same-sex relationships into the design
document in 1998, and made the object-building tools that a lot of the Sims 1 modding community
ran on. I'd like to invite you onto a **Repo Show** — a live conversation whose stage is a public
GitHub repo people can clone, run and build on, instead of a video with a comment section.

I want to talk about your **LGBTQIA+ / Gender & Orientation Overhaul**, and I want to be precise
about why.

The Sims 4 shipped the wardrobe. Gender-independent clothing in 2016. Custom pronouns in 2022. An
orientation setting in July 2022. Binders and top surgery scars in the January 2023 patch. All
real, all worth having, and all of it sitting in the character creator. You shipped the **rules underneath** — identity that a Sim lives through in moodlets, sentiments, interactions and other Sims' reactions; romantic and sexual orientation as two separate axes; self-discovery and coming out as systems rather than flags; queerplatonic partners; aromantic Sims who can WooHoo without romance; and an auto-assign knob that decides how queer the neighborhood is by default. Then you made every bit of it switchable, which is a design position about players and not about Sims.

That is systems design on a shipped product you don't have the source to. It's also, structurally,
the same thing that happened in 1998: our first same-sex implementation was a programmer
[simply not checking sex](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/characters/patrick-j-barrett-iii/same-sex-relationships-the-record.md),
with preference emerging from behavior rather than an attraction number. You split those axes
apart twenty-four years later, from outside the company. I would very much like to get you and
**Patrick Barrett** into the same discussion and let the two of you philosophize about how each model works.

## Shows I'd love to do with you

- **Solo** — play through the overhaul and talk about the design decisions out loud.
- **You and Patrick** — 1998 and 2022, two people who implemented inclusion without being asked to.
- **You, Heather and Steve** — three generations of Sims modding. They built behavior out of
interlocking objects in 2000; you build it in Python in 2022. Same job, different century.
- **You and Ell** — what a life sim should model natively versus what belongs in a mod.



## Two things I'd want your read on

**A Sims 4 soul bridge.** I'm building tooling that reads households out of a save — names, relationships, identities, album pages — and writes them to a portable format so characters can move between games instead of being stranded when a platform dies. Sims 1 is the focus because it just came back on Steam, and I know it well. Sims 4 matters because that's where the players are, and I don't know that game well. You do. Your mod already reasons about most of the state a bridge would need to carry, and I'd rather build it with you than reverse-engineer around you.

**Sustaining the work.** Mods have to be free under EA's policy. I'd like to understand honestly
how that works out for you, and what breaks when a patch lands, because I'm trying to figure out
how to fund open-source bridges without pretending the economics aren't real.

No pressure and no deadline. Live, async, written Q&A, or nothing at all — any of those is a fine
answer. If you'd rather just correct the pages I've written about your work, that's welcome too;
they're yours to edit.

— Don Hopkins
