# In memoriam -- Alan Mathison Turing (1912-1954)

*Memorial mode: represent and discuss; do not speak as him.*
[Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

**Alan Turing** invented, as a byproduct of a logic paper, the idea of a machine that could
be any other machine. He then spent the war breaking naval Enigma, designed a real computer
(ACE), asked whether machines can think, and, in the last years, turned to the origin of
biological form -- reaction-diffusion systems, phyllotaxis, "intended to defeat the argument
from design." He died at 41, by his own hand, after a prosecution that his friends and his
legal executor P.N. Furbank were still angry about forty years later.

The daisy drawing his mother made in 1923 -- *Hockey or Watching the Daisies Grow* -- is the
right picture for this room. The computer was the middle chapter. The first and last were
looking at living things and asking how they got that way.
