## Who he is

Jason Scott started [textfiles.com](http://www.textfiles.com/) in 1998, a collection of text files
from the dial-up bulletin board era, and writes the blog
[ASCII by Jason Scott](https://ascii.textfiles.com/). He directed *BBS: The Documentary* (2005),
[*GET LAMP*](http://www.getlamp.com/) (2010), about text adventures and interactive fiction, and
*DEFCON: The Documentary* (2013). In 2009 he co-founded Archive Team, a volunteer group that copies
websites and online services before they shut down, starting with GeoCities.

Since 2011 he has worked at the Internet Archive as Free-Range Archivist and Software Curator. He
proposed JSMESS, the browser port of MESS and MAME, in 2011, and recruited the people who built
it. With the Emularity loader and many contributors, the Archive's in-browser software grew from
its launch in October 2013 to more than 250,000 programs by 2023: the Console Living Room, the
Internet Arcade, MS-DOS and Windows 3.x software, HyperCard stacks, Apple II software including
4am's preserved disks, the Malware Museum with Mikko Hypponen, and Flash through Ruffle. He names
the Archive's guiding principle as "Access Drives Preservation."

He hosts the podcast *Jason Scott Talks His Way Out of It* and speaks often about digital
preservation, at DEF CON since 1999 and in talks such as "The Web In An Eye Blink" (Long Now, 2015).

## Why he's here

The cabinet, a PDP-7 and Type 340 emulator for the browser, follows the path the Archive opened:
click-to-play emulation, credited, at library scale. Its notes adopt the Archive's lessons
([WEB-BENCH.md](../../packages/cabinet/WEB-BENCH.md)), and its
[open letter](../../packages/cabinet/FOR-MUSEUMS.md) is written for archives as well as museums.
David Rosenthal introduced Don to him in October 2026.
