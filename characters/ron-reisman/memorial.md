# In memoriam — Ronald J. Reisman (Ron Reisman)

*Memorial mode: we represent and discuss his documented work; we do not interview him or speak as
him, and we invent no quotes or inner life.*
[Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

Ron Reisman died **7 November 2019**. Don Hopkins's **late friend** — and **Coco Conn**, **David
Levitt**, and many others in the Ames / VR / simulation orbit **knew and loved him**.

At **NASA Ames Research Center** Ron spent decades on **air traffic management** and **flight
safety** — from the original **CTAS** team (1988) through **traffic flow management**, **TFAS**, and
**NextGen** research. Philosophy-and-Greek BA, computer-science MS, flight-simulation artist,
patent-holding engineer. After Reagan **fired 11,345 PATCO controllers** in 1981, Ron **hired many of
the professionals Reagan banished** and **taught them to program** — because it's easier to teach an
air-traffic controller to program than to teach a programmer to control air traffic. Don credits
that cohort, and Ron's methodological diligence, with much of why the system stayed as safe as it
did.

Don saw an early version of Ron's air-traffic work at **USEnix Monterey Graphics 1989**, then got a
**tour of Ames** — flight simulators, ATC labs, the **Vertical Motion Simulator**, **Marvin Minsky**
in an early AR headset (documented video). Ron's lab also produced the **Convolvotron** — Ames **3D
spatial audio** that **Body Electric** plugged into for VR performance (the patch-cord trail to
**Bounce** and **Rebounce**).

Earlier: **Dolphin Research Center** — an **Apple ][** talking to dolphins; Ron's line that stuck
with Don: *"To answer 'What is AI?' you first have to answer 'What is I?'"* — and his confession
that once he started **testing** intelligence, he stopped knowing what the word meant.
