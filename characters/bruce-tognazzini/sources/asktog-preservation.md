## The site

[asktog.com](https://asktog.com) — *"Interaction Design Solutions for the Real World"* — decades
of columns: the [Fitts quiz](https://www.asktog.com/columns/022DesignedToGiveFitts.html), First
Principles of Interaction Design, Keyboard vs. The Mouse, Magic and Software Design, The Third
User, Intuitive Equals Familiar, the Self-Destruct Switch, What Happened to John Denver.
Regularly rediscovered on [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/from?site=asktog.com).

## The strandedness

Built in **Adobe GoLive** — discontinued; Tog lost all reasonable edit access. In his own words
(2023):

> "I no longer have any reasonable access to my site since Adobe discontinued GoLive."

> "I also tired of having to spend ten times as much time formatting new material as I did
> writing it."

> "I'd happily spend a considerable sum if someone could rebuild the site in a framework that
> could enable me to easily add new content. Just haven't found a competent, reliable person to
> do that task."

## The plans

### The living repo — THE plan (2026)

Interview Tog about the site and his life's work, then scrape asktog.com into a GitHub repo:
every column in clean Markdown with a YAML metadata skeleton, organized MOOLLM-style as a
navigable world — rooms, characters, documents, mind maps, memory palaces, adventures, dragons,
treasures. Live, simulatable, forkable. The same treatment Scott Adams and Don want for their own
life's accumulated data — exactly what a Repo Show is meant to produce. Static hosting and an
archive.org package fall out of the repo for free; Google search replaces the broken search box;
Jim Paradis remains the competent, reliable builder Tog gave up looking for.

### Forget training an LLM

The 2023 "asktog.ai" idea (train an LLM on the corpus) is obsolete. The goal is to publish ALL
the information in a GitHub repo that WILL be scraped by every LLM — formatted so models can
learn from it AND operationalize it. You don't train your own model; you feed them all, forever,
with attribution baked in.

### The HCI history site (Ben Shneiderman)

> "A more comprehensive approach would be to grab more of the websites related to the early days
> of HCI and provide some kind of index to them… a nicely done HCI history page would be helpful."

Wayback has pieces, but it's awkward for a site DEDICATED to usability.
