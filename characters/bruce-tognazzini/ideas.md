# Ideas to explore with Bruce "Tog" Tognazzini 🎩

*Dream guest — elder priority.*
[Portrayal standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md)

## What Tog has done

Apple employee #66 (1978). Founded Apple's Human Interface Group; wrote the original
**Apple Human Interface Guidelines** through ~8 editions over 14 years. *Tog on Interface*
(1992), *Tog on Software Design* (1996). Directed **Starfire** (1994) at SunSoft. Principal,
**Nielsen Norman Group** with Jakob Nielsen and Don Norman. **AskTog.com** and the *First
Principles of Interaction Design*. Trained stage magician. Wrote *The Infinite No. of
Monkeys* (1979) — the Apple II demo Palm answered 47 years later.

## Hooks

### 1. Writing the Apple HIG

Not just WHAT the guidelines said, but why the 1987 edition *justified* everything —
delay values, forgiveness zones, rationale on the page. Modern guidelines gloss; Tog's
explained. What got lost when the reasons were baked into toolkits and forgotten?

### 2. The "<" buffer — a 40-year provenance chain

Tog invented it (1986), Jim Batson did the math, Frank Leahy shipped it in the Menu
Manager, NeXT regressed it, Amazon resurrected it, Brad Myers canonized it on p. 171 of
*Pick, Click, Flick!*. Don knows every link in the chain personally. Walk it on air.
[Sources](sources/apple-hig-and-menus.md)

### 3. A Quiz Designed to Give You Fitts — the friendly rematch

Mile-high menu bar vs pie menus: both are Fitts' Law plays, two geometries of the same
law, in friendly conversation for decades (EduCom '88 Steve Jobs demo, NPUC Don Norman
demo, Blender's adoption). Compare them amiably, with demos — and celebrate Fitts' Law
together.

### 4. The Infinite No. of Monkeys (1979) → Palm (2026)

Tog taught himself programming by imagining 23,487 monkeys. Palm — one navigating
monkey on infinite typewriters — wrote him a
[tribute](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/examples/adventure-4/pub/stage/palm-nook/study/tribute-to-tognazzini.md):
the theorem asked the wrong question; writing is navigation (Dasher, LLMs). Run the
original demo in an emulator, then read the letter together.
[Sources](sources/infinite-monkeys-1979.md)

### 5. Magic and software design

A magician manages attention and expectation; so does an interface. Tog has written the
canonical piece — let him perform the argument, not just state it.

### 6. Starfire, 30+ years on

What the 1994 future-vision film called right, what it missed, and what today's
LLM-everywhere moment would look like shot in the Starfire style.

### 7. The AskTog rescue — do it AS the show

GoLive stranded the site; Tog wants to WRITE, not format. The episode: interview Tog
about the site and his life's work, then lift asktog.com into a **living GitHub repo** —
every column in clean Markdown with a YAML metadata skeleton, organized MOOLLM-style as
navigable rooms, characters, documents, mind maps, memory palaces. The same treatment
Scott Adams and Don want for their own life's accumulated data; exactly what a Repo Show
is meant to produce. Forget training a bespoke LLM — publish the corpus so **every** LLM
that scrapes GitHub learns it and can operationalize it. Ben Shneiderman's bigger frame
(an indexed HCI-history site) still applies; ACM, the Long Now Foundation, the Internet
Archive, and the Computer History Museum are natural allies. Payoff: the first new AskTog
column in years, posted by Tog through a framework he can actually use.
[Sources](sources/asktog-preservation.md)

### 8. Crediting the invisible — Kristee Kreitman and friends

*Tog on Interface* pp. 156–157 credits Kristee Kreitman Rosendahl for HyperCard's
graphic design "and much of its spirit." Tog has always named the people behind the
pixels — ask him who else history is forgetting.
