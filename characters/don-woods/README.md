# Don Woods 🏔️

Co-creator of **Adventure** — the Crowther/Woods 350-point cave of 1977 that founded
interactive fiction and taught two generations that a repository can be a place you go.
Co-designer of **INTERCAL** (1972, with James M. Lyon), the first great parody programming
language. Co-author of **The Hacker's Dictionary** (1983). And — the reason this room has a
personal key — Don Hopkins's colleague at **Sun Microsystems**, where Woods hacked on
**NeWS** and **The NeWS Toolkit**. A great PostScript hacker.

## The four acts

### 1. Adventure (1976–77)

Woods, a Stanford AI Lab grad student, found Will Crowther's unfinished cave crawl on a lab
machine. No author contact, no README, no license. So he emailed `crowther@` at **every host
on the ARPANET** until one landed — Crowther answered from Xerox, sent the source, and gave
his blessing. Woods expanded it into the canonical 350-point game: the pirate, the volcano,
the endgame, the scoring — including the infamous **last lousy point**.

Every Zork, every MUD, every graphical adventure descends from this repo. Knuth thought it
was literature: he recast it in CWEB as [`advent.w`](../donald-knuth/sources/adventure-knuth/advent.w),
and the copyright line reads **"Don Woods and Don Knuth"**. The full study lives in
[the Knuth room](../donald-knuth/sources/adventure-knuth/).

### 2. INTERCAL (1972)

At Princeton, Woods and James M. Lyon designed the **Compiler Language With No Pronounceable
Acronym** — a complete parody of language design where the compiler enforces politeness:
too few `PLEASE`s and the programmer is insufficiently polite; too many and the program
grovels. Executable humor theory, thirty-odd years before anyone wrote it down as such.

### 3. The Hacker's Dictionary (1983)

Co-author, with Guy Steele, Raphael Finkel, Mark Crispin, Richard Stallman, and Geoff
Goodfellow — the book form of the Jargon File, the culture writing its own dictionary.

### 4. Sun, NeWS, and the two Dons

At Sun, Woods worked on NeWS and **The NeWS Toolkit (TNT)** with Don Hopkins. The NeWS
server's `/etc/motd` read: *"Welcome to Adventure. Would you like instructions?"* — the host
machine **was** the well house. He also appears in the 1991
[NeWS/TNT vs ICCCM interoperability threads](../don-hopkins/sources/1991-09-news-tnt-icccm-death-match.md).
After Sun he turned up at **General Magic** in Communicating Applications — per the
[1994 org chart Don found on HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44540509)
([archive](../don-hopkins/sources/zork-troll-flag-adventure-lineage-hn.md)) — before
Postini and Google. Adventure's co-author kept landing at the industry's most storied
communicating-applications shops.

## The stamp is already issued

The MOOLLM microworld's kitchen holds a
[matchbook collection](https://github.com/SimHacker/moollm/blob/main/examples/adventure-4/kitchen/matchbooks.yml)
honoring the founders of interactive fiction. Mail the *Spelunker Today* matchbook and you
receive the **One Lousy Point Donald Woods Stamp** — a tribute to Infocom's real mail-in
brochure. Worth one lousy point. Priceless historically. The invitation, in a sense,
predates itself.

## Forty years of crossed mail

The two Dons have been correspondents since **1986**, when Don Hopkins typed `:mail don`
instead of `:mail don@mimsy.umd.edu` and the message landed at Woods.pa@Xerox.COM.
Woods's complete reply: *"I'll bite: To whom did you intend to send this?"* The full arc —
SUG tapes, Spider, TNT memos, tnt-geeks reunions, the 2025 motd myth-check, and the
invitation sent 31 July 2026 — is in
[sources/correspondence-digest.md](sources/correspondence-digest.md). His Sun-era
PostScript and toolkit artifacts are in
[sources/sun-news-artifacts.md](sources/sun-news-artifacts.md).
