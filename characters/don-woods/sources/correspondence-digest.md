# Don Woods ↔ Don Hopkins — forty years of crossed mail (1986–2026)

Curated digest of the two Dons' correspondence, from a misdirected 1986 email to the
Repo Show invitation sent 31 July 2026. Provenance: Don Hopkins's mail archive.
Subject may request correction or removal anytime
([portrayal-standards](../../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md)).

## 1986 — First contact was an accident

**9 April 1986.** Don Hopkins (A2DEH@MC.LCS.MIT.EDU) typed `:mail don` instead of
`:mail don@mimsy.umd.edu` while sending himself a note about a civic-tech idea (net mail
printed and posted to congressional representatives). `DON@MC` forwarded to
**Don Woods — Woods.pa at Xerox.COM**. Woods's complete reply:

> I'll bite: To whom did you intend to send this?
>
> — Don.

Don confessed it wasn't even the first time — he'd previously mis-sent Woods the database
for his "what" program. The two Dons' mail has been crossing wires ever since.
(Also establishes: Woods was at **Xerox** in 1986, matching Spider's port to the Xerox
Development Environment.)

## 1988–1991 — Sun, NeWS, and TNT

- **26 Nov 1988** — Don's SUG-tape solicitation lists **Woods's PostScript quicksort**
  among the collection ("PostScript sorts (leffler (bubble), owen (heap), woods (quick))").
  See [sun-news-artifacts](sun-news-artifacts.md).
- **17 Jan 1990** — Woods posts **Spider solitaire for X11/NeWS** to NeWS-makers:
  a complete, beautifully documented card game in pure NeWS PostScript, with a
  40-page rules-and-conundrums document. Its header records the provenance:
  *"The author's first computer implementation was on the Stanford Artificial
  Intelligence Lab system (SAIL). It was later ported to the Xerox Development
  Environment."* SAIL → Xerox → Sun, the same arc as its author.
- **25 May 1990** — Don, recruiting a friend to Sun: *"There's Owen Densmore, Don Woods,
  Brian Raymor... other neat people!"* Rafael Bracho was forming the NeWS toolkit group
  in Advanced Development.
- **8 Nov 1990** — Woods's **pinned-menus design memo** ("I'll see your 6 cents and raise
  you 20..."): a model of API design deliberation — four issues, a tentative proposal,
  a radical extension, and honest "this all strikes me as very thin ice."
- **4 Dec 1990** — the follow-up memo settling the pinned-menu design.
- **31 May 1991** — the focus-wars message, opening with the immortal correction:
  *"Throughout the original message... my name was misspelled. **I'm Woods with an `s'.**
  The former Windows person with no `s' is Tom Wood."* Then a masterclass on X11/NeWS
  focus race conditions. (Thread context:
  [1991-09-news-tnt-icccm-death-match](../../don-hopkins/sources/1991-09-news-tnt-icccm-death-match.md).)

## 2007–2008 — tnt-geeks

- **Aug 2007** — the *Somewhere Nearby is Colossal Cave* thread (Dennis Jerz's DHQ
  article). Woods: the salvaged **Stanford backup tapes** — from which Crowther's
  original source was recovered in 2005, out of **Woods's own student account backup** —
  also revealed *"I'd been getting the year wrong in all my previous interviews on the
  history of Adventure."* Plus the v2 oyster endgame hint (*"this initial hint is all you
  get"* — with a bonus hint that the hint contains a pun), and Jeff Dinkins: *"Don, how
  does it feel knowing they still have an archive of your student account from 1977?"*
- **Jul 2008** — Steve Drach circulates the
  [Computerworld A-Z of Programming Languages: INTERCAL interview](https://web.archive.org/web/20080723072756/http://www.computerworld.com.au/index.php/id;528456667).
  Established there: Woods was then at **Google (via the Postini acquisition)**;
  INTERCAL was designed **freshman year at Princeton with James Lyon**, post-finals and
  "more than a little giddy"; the compiler emitted **SPITBOL** with datatypes as strings
  of "0"s and "1"s; they did *not* specifically spoof FORTRAN/COBOL ("Even though
  Wikipedia seems to claim we did"); the 17-hour Sieve benchmark is "not criticism,
  that's a boast!"; and — the crown jewel — **in 2003 Knuth wrote an INTERCAL program
  and found "a really cool hack" in the standard library's division routine**, writing
  to Woods to ask whether he or Lyon deserved credit in TAOCP volume 4. (Neither could
  remember.)

## 2025 — THROW AXE AT DWARF

Don wrote (12 Jul 2025) with "one burning question that's been eating away at my soul
for decades": did anyone ever actually fall for the `/etc/motd` Adventure trick?
Woods's reply (23 Jul 2025):

> Wow, that's the question that's been eating at your soul? Poor soul! And alas, I cannot
> answer for certain, but I do not recall anyone ever telling me they had done so.

**Myth-check on record:** the motd is real (Don's firsthand memory, and Woods didn't
dispute it); *confirmed victims: none reported.* Also in that thread:

- Woods **retired July 2022**; spends it "playing games and solving puzzles."
- Saw **Knuth at a SAIL reunion** — "getting frail but is still plenty sharp."
- On AI: simulations are "surprisingly (disturbingly?) good at generating
  fake-but-convincing dialogues... Of course, they're still terrible at actually
  understanding anything." (Exhibit: Google offering "stag" as a 7-letter word for deer.)
- On walled gardens: no HN or Facebook accounts, on principle — "if they won't let me
  look... how am I to decide whether their content is worth spending my time on?
  Easy answer: it's not; drive on!"
