# Repo Show invitation — Catherine Plaisant

*Catherine may edit, decline, delay, or request removal at any time — or take direct edit access. [Portrayal standards](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/schemas/portrayal-standards.md) — also covers quiet mode and onboarding.*
**Subject:** HyperTIES runs again, and a conversation about HCIL

---

Catherine —

HyperTIES runs again. I've restored it in the browser from the original databases, so you can
browse the old articles the way we did at HCIL:
[the reading room](https://hyperties.org/archive/reading-room/). The story of the restoration, and
my letter to Ben about it, are in the
[Hyperties repository](https://github.com/SimHacker/Hyperties/blob/main/docs/README.md). Our 1991
look-back paper with Ben, Rodrigo and Bill is one of the documents I keep returning to; it says
more in a few pages about pie menus and browsing than most of what came after.

Since you and Ben have been gathering HCI history, I wanted you to know what else has come out of
that period. I built a PDP-7 emulator in the browser to restore Heinz Lemke's 1969 PIXIE, the
earliest pie menus we know of, which grew out of the evidence I sent you and Ben in 2019
([try it](https://hyperties.org/databases/pixie/pixie-live/) ·
[23-minute tour](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lo8kdY-5i6c)). The same renderer that draws The Sims
characters in the browser could someday show Ben, you and Rich Potter at a touchscreen IBM PC
demonstrating lift-off and the toggle switches, if you'd want that and would guide it.

**Will Wright is in — he's signed on to do the [premiere](https://github.com/SimHacker/WillWrightShowForFood/blob/main/repo-shows/will-wright-premiere/README.md) and more.**

I know you've retired, and I'm not asking for any work: no reviews, no writing. Just a
conversation, if you'd enjoy one, on a **Repo Show**: a recorded talk whose stage is a public
GitHub repository. Possible threads, your pick:

1. HCIL in the late 1980s: HyperTIES, *Hypertext on Hypertext*, and what it was like to arrive from
   Paris into that lab.
2. The touchscreen work, from high-precision selection to push-or-slide toggles, decades before
   phones.
3. Thirty-four years of HCIL, and how to keep its history, videos and technical reports alive.
4. Visualization: LifeLines, SpaceTree and EventFlow, and evaluating what visualizations are for.

Ben could join, or it could be just the two of us. Remote is fine.

— Don
