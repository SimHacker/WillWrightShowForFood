## Who she is

Catherine Plaisant trained as an engineer in Paris, earning a Doctorat d'Ingénieur from the
Université Pierre et Marie Curie, and worked at the Centre Mondial Informatique et Ressource Humaine
before coming to the University of Maryland's Human-Computer Interaction Lab in 1987. Her first
project there was hypertext: HyperTIES, Ben Shneiderman's hypertext system, which became the basis
of the 1988 *Hypertext on Hypertext* disk published with the ACM and of a 1991 paper looking back at
its workstation browser.

She went on to shape much of HCIL's work for more than three decades. With Andrew Sears and Ben
Shneiderman she showed that touchscreens could be precise (1990), and her study of touchscreen
toggle switches, push or slide (1990, with a CHI '92 video), was later cited in the litigation over
slide-to-unlock. In information visualization she worked on LifeLines and EventFlow, for seeing
personal histories and sequences of events; on SpaceTree, for browsing large trees, which won the
InfoVis Test of Time award in 2022; and on treemaps. She co-edited BELIV 2006, the first workshop on
how to evaluate visualizations, and co-chaired the first IEEE Visual Analytics Challenge.

She co-wrote *Designing the User Interface* with Ben Shneiderman from its fourth edition (2005)
through its sixth (2016), edited the HCIL video reports from 1991 to 2006, and was the lab's interim
director and then associate director. She is a member of the ACM SIGCHI Academy (2015) and received
the IEEE Visualization Career Award and the ACM SIGCHI Lifetime Service Award in 2020. She retired
in 2021 and is a Research Scientist Emerita at UMIACS
([profile](http://hcil.umd.edu/catherine-plaisant)).

## Why she's here

She and Don worked together at HCIL on HyperTIES; they co-authored the 1991 paper with Ben
Shneiderman, Rodrigo Botafogo and Bill Weiland, which describes using pie menus without looking.
Don has since restored HyperTIES in the browser at [hyperties.org](https://hyperties.org/archive/reading-room/),
and she and Ben have been gathering HCI history.
