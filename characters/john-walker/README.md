## Who he was

John Walker was born in Baltimore in 1949 and studied electrical engineering at Case Western
Reserve. In 1974, on a UNIVAC, he wrote [ANIMAL](https://www.fourmilab.ch/documents/univac/animal.html),
a Twenty Questions game, and gave it a routine that copied it into every directory it could reach,
so that it spread from site to site. In 1976 he founded
[Marinchip Systems](https://www.fourmilab.ch/documents/marinchip/) from his house in Mill Valley,
selling a 16-bit computer for the S-100 bus with its own operating system and compilers.

In 1982 he and Dan Drake gathered friends at his house to start a software company. Fourteen
founders pooled their money, and Autodesk shipped AutoCAD that year. It went public in 1985. He
wrote the company's history as he went, in internal memos he called Information Letters, and later
published them with the rest of the record as
[*The Autodesk File*](https://www.fourmilab.ch/autofile/), a rare history of a company told in its
own documents. Under his leadership Autodesk invested in Ted Nelson's Xanadu and in AMIX, and
divested both in 1992.

After meeting Rudy Rucker at the Hackers conference in 1987, he hired him, and together they made
[CelLab](https://www.fourmilab.ch/cellab/) (1989), cellular automata on an ordinary PC, with a
manual that taught the rules and their history. Its rule catalog credits the CAM-6 of Toffoli and
Margolus. He later ported it to JavaScript as WebCA, so it still runs in a browser.

In 1991 he moved to Switzerland with his wife, Roxie Smail, and published from
[fourmilab.ch](https://www.fourmilab.ch/) for over thirty years:
[*The Hacker's Diet*](https://www.fourmilab.ch/hackdiet/), which treats weight as a signal to be
filtered; [HotBits](https://www.fourmilab.ch/hotbits/), random numbers from radioactive decay;
an [Analytical Engine emulator](https://www.fourmilab.ch/babbage/); the
[Earth and Moon Viewer](https://www.fourmilab.ch/earthview/); Home Planet; and years of book
reviews in his [Reading List](https://www.fourmilab.ch/documents/reading_list/). He gave nearly all
of it away.

He died on 2 February 2024, in Neuchâtel, after a fall at home
([family announcement](https://scanalyst.fourmilab.ch/t/john-walker-1949-2024/4305)).

## Why he's here

CelLab is one of the foundations of the cellular automata work in this repository, beside the
CAM-6. Rudy Rucker's [invitation](../rudy-rucker/invitation.md) and the cabinet's courseware plans
draw on it, and *The Autodesk File* is a model for keeping a project's history in its own documents.
