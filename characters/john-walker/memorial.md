# In memoriam — John Walker (1949–2024)

*Memorial mode: we represent and discuss his work; we do not interview him or speak as him.*
[Memorial standards](../../schemas/portrayal-standards.md#memorial-mode)

John Walker co-founded Autodesk and wrote much of its early software, then spent the rest of his
life giving work away. His site, [Fourmilab](https://www.fourmilab.ch/), holds decades of it:
programs, essays, book reviews, a diet manual written by an engineer, and a full history of the
company he started, told in its own memos.

For this project the most important thing he made is [CelLab](https://www.fourmilab.ch/cellab/),
with Rudy Rucker. In 1989 it put cellular automata on an ordinary PC, with a manual that explained
each rule and where it came from, and it credited the CAM-6 of Tommaso Toffoli and Norman Margolus
as its inspiration. Walker kept it alive by porting it to JavaScript, so anyone can still run it.
The [origins chapter](https://www.fourmilab.ch/cellab/manual/chap5.html) records how it began, and
his short video on billiard-ball computing shows the rules at work
([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=esgn0Dz8SOA)).

[*The Autodesk File*](https://www.fourmilab.ch/autofile/) is the other gift. It is the history of a
company in its own documents, with the arguments, plans and mistakes left in, including Autodesk's
investment in Xanadu. Few founders publish that much, and fewer keep it online for thirty years.

A remembrance here would bring together people who worked with him: Rudy Rucker on CelLab, and the
Xanadu team from the Autodesk years. We would run CelLab, read from *The Autodesk File*, and talk
about what he built.

*This is a collaborative memorial, not a signed work. Those who knew John — family, friends and
colleagues — are welcome to edit, correct and add their own memories, documents and stories.*
