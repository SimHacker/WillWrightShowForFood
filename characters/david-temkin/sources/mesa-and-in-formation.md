### The tagline's ancestry, and its amplifier

The tagline is the compressed form of a question first asked at the founding of AI, and the chain is
documented in public at all three points.

**Douglas Engelbart, to Marvin Minsky**, as Jaron Lanier reported it in his review of John Markoff's
*What the Dormouse Said* (*American Scientist*, July–August 2005,
[archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20110312232514/http://www.americanscientist.org/bookshelf/pub/early-computings-long-strange-trip)):

> Engelbart once told me a story that illustrates the conflict succinctly. He met Marvin Minsky — one
> of the founders of the field of AI — and Minsky told him how the AI lab would create intelligent
> machines. Engelbart replied, "You're going to do all that for the machines? What are you going to
> do for the people?" This conflict between machine- and human-centered design continues to this day.

Engelbart asked which side of the interface would be adapted to the other. **The *In Formation*
tagline is that question answered in the past tense**, forty-odd years later, with the results in.

**Ted Nelson tweeted it**, crediting Temkin by name, on 2018-08-10 —
[@TheTedNelson](https://x.com/TheTedNelson/status/1027975562671734784) — where it became one of his
most-circulated posts. Temkin's reply on the thread: "That was the tagline of In Formation magazine,
circa 1998, but unfortunately far truer today."

Also on that thread, Conrad Barski (*Land of Lisp*) gave the honest counter-position in two clauses:
"part of me shudders at the brutal truth of this… the other part of me thinks 'is it really so bad
that we're making people more user-friendly'?" Which is the same objection Kevin Kelly raised to the
Engelbart anecdote in *Out of Control* — "I'm squarely on Minsky's side… but what are we going to do
for the machines?" The counter-argument is stable across seventy years and keeps being made by
thoughtful people, which is the reason to state the position as a question about allocation of
effort rather than as a verdict.

Lanier's sentence immediately preceding the anecdote is also the fairest published assessment of
Nelson's situation: the pioneers "labor on to this day, building the computer as they feel it should
be, even as the world at large has adapted on a massive scale to a computer that to those pioneers is
only half-born."

**Show hook:** the tagline as the through-line — Engelbart's question, Temkin's answer, Nelson's
amplification — and *Mesa* and *Declare* as attempts at the missing half. Related:
[`../../ben-shneiderman/agents-debate-1997.md`](../../ben-shneiderman/agents-debate-1997.md), where
the same argument gets a stage and two moderators in 1997.

**Show hook:** Repo Show as the *git* answer to enshittification; In Formation as the *print* answer; Declare as the *UI language* answer. Paper punk ↔ constructionist education.

## 1991 Mac game → web (Claude Code)

Stereoscopic 3D glasses game: C + 68000 asm, custom renderer, fixed-point, macro OOP, ~15K LOC for 16 MHz / 2 MB Mac. Claude Code rewrite to JS in 3–4 days; "not vibe coding — directing an impossibly fast engineer." Excited + dread. Nudged by Adam Wolff (Claude Code @ Anthropic), Oliver Steele, Adam Bosworth.

**Show hook:** Same agentmaxxing muscle as OL 5.0 + Declare; Adam Wolff on both Claude Code *and* "laszlo reunion wen?"
