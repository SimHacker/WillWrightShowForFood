# The pitch and the lineage — Construction is king

> Two things worth keeping intact from the review: (1) the **Primeagen pitch** — the hook that gets someone

> new idea. Extracted so they can be reused verbatim in a deck, a README, or an actual pitch.

## The pitch (keep this wording)

> Everyone says **"content is king."** I think that's wrong. **Construction is king.**
>
> YouTube made the video the product. Podcasts made the audio the product. Blogs made the text the product.
> GitHub accidentally invented something richer: a living, forkable, reviewable, collaborative artifact.
>
> We've been treating GitHub like a filing cabinet. It's actually a **stage.**
>
> A Repo Show doesn't stream the *making* of software. **The evolving repository is the show.**
> Every commit is part of the narrative. Every issue is a question from the audience. Every pull request is a
> guest appearance. Every fork is an alternate timeline. Every AI session is another character entering the
> world. Video isn't replaced — it's just one camera angle.
>
> The jump: not *"watch me code"* — **"build this world with me."**

**Who it's for.** The review names the target audiences precisely, and they're the right ones:
- **Primeagen** — the hook that gets a streamer to stop scrolling: your show is already more than a video;
  the real product is the evolving repo. Make your show *participatory and interactive* instead of broadcast.
- **Will Wright** — the simulator/StoryMaker lineage is literally his (see below); confirmed participant per
  `WillWrightShowForFood/characters/INDEX.yml`.
- **Brian Eno** — generative/systems art; a world that evolves under rules.
- **Drew Carey** — *especially*, for his improv (*Whose Line*) and audience-participation history; Repo Show
  is "audience co-authors the world" done as improv with a version-controlled stage.

The pitch's honesty is its strength (the review calls this out): it **doesn't claim to invent collaboration
from scratch.** It argues GitHub "has always been a remarkably rich social medium — we've simply been looking
at it through the narrow lens of source control."

## The lineage (Repo Show is step N, not step 1)

This is what makes the idea credible rather than hype: it's the next node in a chain Don has personally been
walking for decades.

```
StoryMaker      →  Audience influences stories        (stories were the artifact)
   ↓
CurrentTV       →  Audience influences television
   ↓
Bar Karma       →  Audience co-authors television      (episodes were the artifact)
   ↓
Urban Safari    →  Audience explores & documents reality
   ↓
GitHub / Repo Show → Audience co-builds the world itself   (worlds are the artifact)
```

- **Bar Karma** was explicitly an *online community-developed television series*: viewers proposed scenes and
  twists using **Will Wright's StoryMaker tool**, and selected ideas became part of later episodes. That's
  strikingly close to TicketPR — and it *predates* it, which is the point.
- **The jump Repo Show makes:** nobody submits "an episode." They submit code, documentation, simulations, AI
  prompts, object definitions, room descriptions, experiments, conversations, design docs, bugs. **The
  artifact stopped being a story or an episode and became a world.**

The lineage does double duty: it's a **credibility argument** (this person has been iterating toward this for
decades) and a **design argument** (each prior step tells you which participation mechanics worked and which
didn't).

## The culture note worth preserving

The review captures a values marker that belongs in the show's ethos: **artisanal programmers** (humans
coding by hand, no AI — "TextEdit to Emacs to VS Code and beyond") "earn extra respect here," alongside
**intentional** and **conscientious** coders (deliberate craft + show-your-work ethics). Don coined
*"conscientious objectors"* at a Kaleida meetup with **David Ungar** (Self's co-creator), with a proposed
show: **Self × MOOLLM.** Keep this: it signals the show honors hand-craft *and* AI collaboration rather than
replacing one with the other — which is the honest posture for a show *about* building with AI.
