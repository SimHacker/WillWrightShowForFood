# Soul City, North Carolina — the namesake (not to be confused with, but inspired by)

Our **Soul City** — the liminal city between games where souls travel, meet, and mate — is **not to
be confused with, but inspired by, Soul City, North Carolina**: the anti-racist planned town that
civil rights leader **Floyd B. McKissick** built on former plantation land in the 1970s, with —
surprisingly — **Richard Nixon's support**.

Watch this first: [The anti-racist town in the American South, explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R2karaKGgkk)
(Vox, *Missing Chapter*, 2023 — with McKissick's son, former NC Senator Floyd McKissick Jr., and
Jane Ball-Groom, one of the town's first residents, who lives there still).

## What happened, truthfully and briefly

**The man.** Floyd B. McKissick: first Black law student at the University of North Carolina,
attorney for arrested protesters, head of CORE, one of the organizers of the 1963 March on
Washington. He'd seen European cities rebuilt from rubble after WWII and asked: what if Black
Americans could build and lead a city of their own?

**The dream.** A planned, multiracial city — Black-built and Black-led, open to everyone — on
thousands of acres of former plantation land in Warren County, North Carolina. "A city without
prejudice, without poverty." Black economic power through Black capitalism: jobs ten minutes from
home, industry feeding community, a reverse migration bringing people back to the rural South.
Reclaiming the land mattered: a community representing the future, on ground representing the
worst of the past.

**The surprise ally.** McKissick pitched Johnson, then Robert Kennedy — who was assassinated. So
he made a calculated, controversial move: he took the idea to Nixon, who backed it (Black
capitalism fit his coalition math — support came from strategy, not conviction). In July 1972,
Soul City became one of only 13 projects funded under HUD's New Communities program — $14 million,
the **first new town in America built with federal backing by a Black-led development company**.

**What they built, from red clay, in seven years.** The largest regional water system in North
Carolina at the time — *it still operates today*. Roads, electricity, a fire department, a health
clinic, a pool and park that still serve the community, the first residential village, and
**Soultech 1** — a 73,000-square-foot industrial incubator meant to seed 24,000 jobs. "From
nothing came... a lot. We rose up."

**What killed it.** Not incompetence — the audit is the record. Senator **Jesse Helms**, a
segregationist who fought federal money flowing into his own state, attacked the project
relentlessly; in 1975 he and an ally demanded a federal audit that froze funding for over a year.
The audit **cleared Soul City of major wrongdoing** — but the smear campaign (amplified by
hostile press) had already scared away residents and industry, in an oil-shocked economy where no
anchor employer would commit to a freestanding rural new town. HUD pulled out in 1979. McKissick
had to foreclose.

**The coda.** Soultech 1 — the building meant to be the engine of Black economic second chances —
today houses a **prison work complex**. McKissick never left; he lived in Soul City until his
death in 1991 and is buried there. And Warren County made history again anyway: the 1982 protests
against a toxic landfill there — McKissick among the hundreds arrested — are considered the birth
of the **environmental justice movement**.

His family's verdict, and ours: a place that embodies **possibility, not failure**. "It's about
hope. It's about dreams."

## Why our Soul City carries the name

An homage, carried carefully:

- **A city built for souls to thrive.** McKissick built a city where people written off by the
  world around them could live, work, and be somebody. Our Soul City is the city where every
  game's souls — including the stranded ones in abandoned saves nobody can visit anymore — get to
  live, meet, and continue.
- **Second chances, inverted coda.** The real Soul City's incubator became a prison. Ours runs
  the arrow the other way: souls locked in dead formats and orphaned games get drained, carried,
  and given new lives in living worlds ("repossessing lost souls" —
  [`game-bridge-soul-hydraulics.yml`](game-bridge-soul-hydraulics.yml)). Nobody's soul belongs to
  a defunct platform.
- **No senator can defund it.** GPL-3 and a git repo: infrastructure that can't be foreclosed on,
  smeared into insolvency, or audit-frozen. The failure mode that killed the real Soul City is
  the exact failure mode free software was built to survive.

**House rule:** whenever the show introduces Soul City, we say the namesake sentence — *not to be
confused with, but inspired by* — and point at the history (link the Vox video). The name is a
tribute; tributes name their sources.
