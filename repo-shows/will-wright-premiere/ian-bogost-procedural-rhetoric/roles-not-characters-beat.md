# Show beat — Roles, not characters (Bogost essay on air)

**Guests:** Ian Bogost, Will Wright (minimum); Don Hopkins hosts  
**Essay:** ["Video Games Are Better Without Characters"](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2015/03/video-games-are-better-without-characters/387556/) (The Atlantic, March 2015)  
**Don's response:** [roles-not-characters.md](../../../characters/ian-bogost/roles-not-characters.md)

The essay says games about systems lost. The rebuttal isn't an argument; it's a table with more chairs.

## Act 1 — Read the SimCity passages aloud

Ian and Will read Bogost's essay — especially the SimCity / Maxis method passages.

**Opening beat — B. Dalton / Software Etc.**

The essay opens at the B. Dalton Software Etc. with *Dark Castle* — "a 1986 Macintosh game
about manipulating the then-unfamiliar mouse to throw rocks at bats" — and drops the Jonathan Gay
→ FutureSplash → Flash lineage as a parenthetical.

**Response beat:** Don performs the greeting. First time Marc Canter introduced Don to Jonathan Gay
at CGDC as Dark Castle's creator, Don held his hands above his head and shouted in monster-voice:
"Nya nya nya nya nya! Yow! Uuuurh! Yeah! Yeah! Yeah!"

Receipts: [hn-dark-castle-nya.md](../../../characters/ian-bogost/sources/hn-dark-castle-nya.md) ·

Same game, two ends of the fourth wall — Ian played it in a mall bookstore; Don greeted its creator
in the game's own voice.

## Act 3 — The card in the middle of the table

Save one quote for the center. Read it aloud. Let Ian and Will argue.

**Will Wright, April 2009** ([CNET, leaving EA for Stupid Fun Club](../../../characters/will-wright/sources/2009-cnet-stupid-fun-club/README.md)):

> Most people are very narcissistic. The more you can make the game about that person, the more
> interested, the more emotionally involved they will get.

The man who built system protagonists diagnosing the identification hook — **six years before**
Ian's essay argued against indulging it. Both true at once. Good show question.

---

## Act 4 — The closing, and the exit ramp Bogost didn't have

Read Bogost's last paragraph aloud — the billionaires rationing makeshift tools:

> We'll sign away anything, it would seem, so long as we're still able to "express ourselves"
> with the makeshift tools we are rationed by the billionaires.

Diagnosis with no exit ramp. Then show the ramp.

**Soul City / Soul Saver** — Nina Hagen as patron saint
([the attitude](../../../catalogs/soul-city/README.md#the-attitude)): when the wall's owner starts
rationing your tools, you don't petition the wall — you walk out with your voice intact and get
louder. She did it to an actual walled state in 1976; Soul Saver does it to the silos.

The mechanism: an **underground railroad** — character souls onto **underground rails** nobody can
expatriate you from. Plain files, git, federation spokes. Not a support ticket. A route out.

Optional live demo: turnstile count ticking, a soul-file committed to a public repo, a bridge export
to Tiny Life.
