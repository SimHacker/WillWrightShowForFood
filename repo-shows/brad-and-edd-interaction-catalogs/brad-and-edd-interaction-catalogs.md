**Topic.** *Pick, Click, Flick!* meets the Game UI Database: the academic taxonomy of interaction
techniques and the industry's 73,000-screen visual archive, cross-referenced live. Where the
literature and the shipping record agree, where they contradict, and what each catalog is missing
that the other one has.

## Why a great pair

- **Brad A. Myers** — [*Pick, Click, Flick!*](https://www.ixtbook.com/) is the
  interaction-techniques bible: the whole history of how we point, select, and gesture, with
  citations. *All the Widgets* (CHI '90) was the video catalog before video catalogs.
- **Edd Coates** — the [Game UI Database](https://www.gameuidatabase.com/) (Guinness World
  Record; 73k+ screens) is the largest curated catalog of shipped game interfaces: what the
  industry actually built, taxonomized and searchable.
- **Same territory, opposite methods:** Brad catalogs from the literature outward; Edd catalogs
  from shipped screens inward. Games ship interaction techniques years before papers describe
  them — and papers describe techniques games never discover.
- They probably already know of each other; the show makes the crosswalk explicit and public.
- **Don bridges:** pie menus live in BOTH catalogs — the *Pick, Click, Flick!* section (p. 171
  territory) and GUIDB's radial-menu taxonomy — and he shipped the Sims pies both catalogs cite.

## Beats

1. **Show and tell** — each guest walks the other through his catalog: taxonomy design, what
   counts as an entry, what gets rejected.
2. **The crosswalk** — pick one technique (radial menus is the obvious first) and trace it
   through both catalogs: literature lineage vs shipping record.
3. **What games knew first** — techniques GUIDB has that the academic literature missed or
   lagged (and vice versa).
4. **All the Widgets (1990) → GUIDB 2.0 video (2024)** — the 34-year arc of cataloging
   interfaces in motion.
5. **Second editions** — what belongs in the next *Pick, Click, Flick!* from the GUIDB record;
   what GUIDB taxonomy could borrow from the VPL/interaction-techniques literature.
6. **Ethical archives** — GUIDB's no-AI-scraping stance meets academic citation culture; who
   gets credit and who gets paid.

## Format

Live with audience Q&A — **and time-shifted works too.** Real-time is ideal (Pittsburgh + UK +
Amsterdam all exist on the internet), but the repo is the shared stage, so Brad and Edd can
exchange annotated notes asynchronously: Brad marks up a GUIDB taxonomy branch, Edd marks up a
book section, each records a reaction pass, and the splice becomes the episode. The
exchange-of-notes IS the show; live conversation is the bonus.
