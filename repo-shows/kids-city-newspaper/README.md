## Premise

Kids playing a shared city publish their own newspaper — INTO the game, ABOUT
the game. Not a feed bolted on the side: the paper is a civic organ of the city
itself, written by the players, archived in the save, read by the citizens
(and the advisors, and the next class to load the city).

## Origin

Multiplayer X11 SimCityNet had a scrolling chat window. Watching kids argue,
brag, negotiate, and report in that little strip of text — "who bulldozed my
airport", "vote YES on the stadium" — Don saw a newsroom trying to be born.
The chat window was already the city's paper; it just had no masthead, no
bylines, no archive, no front page. The dream has been compelling him ever
since: promote the chat into journalism.

## Why it matters

It's constructionist literacy hiding inside a city builder: reporting teaches
evidence (the save file is the source of record), editorials teach argument,
the advice column teaches empathy, and the masthead teaches roles and
deadlines — a civics class that thinks it's a game. The paper is also the
city's MEMORY: load an old save and read what its citizens were fighting
about. Games mostly simulate space; a player-published newspaper simulates
HISTORY.
