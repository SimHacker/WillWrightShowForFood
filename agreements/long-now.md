# Ask: The Long Now Foundation — clip permission

**Status:** draft, not sent.
**What we need:** permission to publish short excerpts from SALT seminar recordings with
commentary.
**What we do not need permission for:** linking, embedding, and using Long Now's **own
shareable clip feature**, which they built for exactly this and which should be the first
tool reached for.

The rights position, checked rather than assumed: Long Now seminars are **not** released under
a Creative Commons license. The videos are free to watch, members get HD and the full archive,
and the seminar pages offer embeddable and shareable clips -- but free to watch is not free to
reuse, and a re-upload needs permission. Media is produced in association with Shoulder High
Productions, so there may be a second rights holder in the chain; ask who else needs to say
yes.

Long Now's stated reason for opening the archive is outreach -- seminar recordings are
described as among their best forms of it. An ask framed as outreach is aligned with their
mission rather than an imposition on it.

```text
Subject: Permission request: short clips from a SALT seminar, credited and linking back

Hello,

I am building a free, non-commercial archive and companion guide to Will
Wright's talks and design thinking, with Will's written permission for his
own material. His Long Now seminar is one of the essential pieces, and since
the recording is yours rather than his, I would like to ask properly.

What I would like permission for: short excerpts, typically under ninety
seconds, published with original commentary, credited on screen to The Long
Now Foundation and linking to your seminar page and podcast.

What I am already doing, which I believe needs no permission: linking to your
seminar pages, embedding your player, using your own shareable clip feature,
and threading your talks into curated playlists with context. All of it sends
people to you for the full seminar.

I would also like to check whether Shoulder High Productions or any other
party needs to be part of this permission, and I am glad to write to them if
so.

Conditions are welcome -- maximum length, specific credit wording, a required
link, or excluding particular seminars. If the answer is no, I will keep to
linking, embedding, and quoting.

The archive itself may interest you on its own terms: it is transcripts,
timestamped indexes, and citations designed to make talks from 02003 onward
findable and searchable decades from now, which feels like the sort of
long-term thinking the seminars are about.

Thank you,

Don Hopkins
[contact, and a link to the repo]
```
